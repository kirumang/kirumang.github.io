Research Landscape

The strongest cross-cutting theme today is safety-constrained contact interaction, showing up almost identically in manipulation and medical robotics. URF has a policy predict not just a virtual target but the impedance-admittance switch ratio itself, letting the controller move between compliant tracking and safe rigid contact in real time; FORGE-plus reaches a similar division of labor from the opposite direction, keeping a frozen LLM entirely out of direct force control and delegating a per-object force ceiling to a hard-constrained low-level controller. The same pattern appears in surgery: RL-MACRO and Human-Inspired Craniotomy — sister papers sharing two authors — both fuse force and acoustic signals to detect an otherwise-unobservable event (bone breakthrough or unsafe cutting temperature) and act before a safety bound is crossed, while the catheter interaction-dynamics paper makes the underlying tension explicit and quantitative: an unconstrained controller drives contact force to 0.60 N against a penetrating target, while an explicit predictive constraint holds 0.47 N at identical tracking accuracy. Across five papers, the emerging consensus is that safety cannot be left to compliance or reward shaping alone — it needs an explicit constraint or switch sitting between the learned policy and the actuator.

A second thread is about diagnosing, not just scaling, generalization in manipulation. Scale Up Strategically is the sharpest example — it localizes compositional-generalization failure to specific instruction factors (color, verb, object, size, spatial) and finds the same shortcut hierarchy across six different foundation policies, then shows that redirecting a fixed data budget toward under-grounded factors beats naive scaling with half the demonstrations. That finding sits in tension with AXIS and TableVerse, two large data engines (50K+ community trajectories and 100K Real2Sim environments, respectively) that scale volume and diversity but don't yet target bias the way Scale Up Strategically argues for. GuidedAttention and TransBiolab approach the same generalization problem from the interpretability and perception-data side — one making a policy's attention correctable at rollout, the other supplying real annotated data for the notoriously hard cluttered-transparent-object regime.

Finally, navigation and multi-agent control are both being pushed toward continuous, resource-constrained, real-world operation rather than clean episodic benchmarks. Beyond Episodic Evaluation sets the tone by showing that spatially grounded 3D memory — not occupancy maps or short-horizon episodic context — is what lets an EQA agent answer a sequence of questions in the same scene without trading off accuracy against navigation cost; VoLN pushes in a related direction by stripping externally supplied route instructions out of VLN entirely, and its resulting 1.8–7.4% success rates on hard splits suggest prior VLN benchmarks were partly measuring instruction-following rather than visual navigation. On the control side, the defensive-racing paper, the GNEP intersection paper, MAPS, and Distributed Model-Based Diffusion each pair a fast structured optimizer (MPC, PSO, hierarchical RL, or decomposed diffusion) with a learned or game-theoretic strategic layer, converging on hybrid architectures rather than either pure optimization or pure end-to-end learning for real-time multi-agent decisions.

Beyond Episodic Evaluation: Memory Architectural Bottlenecks in Sequential Embodied Question Answering

2026-07-23 cs.RO Mintong Kang (h=13)
Zikui Cai, Kaushal Janga, Tan Dat Dao, Seungjae Lee, Shivin Dass
Core Contributions
  • Breaks with the standard episodic EQA protocol (reset state between questions) to evaluate agents across a sequence of queries in the same scene with memory carried forward — a much closer match to how a deployed robot would actually operate.
  • Diagnoses two distinct architectural failure modes rather than treating "more memory" as monolithic: pure occupancy-map memory retains where the robot explored but not the visual-semantic evidence later questions need, while episodic-trained agents suffer temporal mismatch when short-horizon training meets continuous multi-query deployment.
  • Shows that mapping persistent visual observations onto metric 3D geometry — rather than 2D occupancy or raw episodic context — breaks the usual accuracy-vs-navigation-efficiency tradeoff, improving both simultaneously instead of trading one for the other.
  • Validates the finding beyond simulation on a real mobile robot, an important check given how much EQA work stays purely in simulated benchmarks.
Show abstract

Embodied question answering (EQA) is traditionally evaluated under an episodic formulation, where agents solve each task independently and reset internal state between episodes. However, real-world robots operate continuously and must accumulate, retain, and selectively reuse information acquired from prior interactions. Despite this practical requirement, the architectural mechanisms needed to support sequential memory in EQA remain underexplored. In this work, we investigate how different memory architectures behave when EQA agents are evaluated sequentially, with multiple questions answered in the same scene while memory is carried forward across queries. We find that simply preserving existing memory is often insufficient. Agents that retain only traversability information, such as 2D occupancy maps, remember where the robot has explored but not the visual-semantic evidence needed for later questions. Agents trained on short-horizon episodic data face a different challenge: when exposed to continuous, multi-query histories, their inherited context suffers from severe temporal mismatch, rather than forming a reusable scene representation. To overcome this architectural bottleneck, we highlight the necessity of structured, spatially grounded memory: architectures that map persistent visual observations onto metric 3D geometry preserve visual-semantic evidence in a coherent scene representation. Extensive experiments in simulated environments reveal that this form of memory breaks the accuracy-efficiency tradeoff in sequential settings, simultaneously achieving higher answer accuracy and lower navigation costs. We further validate these findings on a real-world mobile robot, demonstrating that spatially grounded visual memory is critical for enabling continuous, intelligent operation in physical environments.

GLAM-SLAM: Real-time Gaussian Large-scale Mapping via Flow Densification and Spatial Decomposition

2026-07-23 cs.RO, cs.CV Petros Maragos (h=5)
Panagiotis Mermigkas, Argyris Manetas, Petros Maragos
Core Contributions
  • Targets a specific gap in Gaussian-splatting SLAM: existing systems are real-time only on short sequences or blow past GPU memory budgets on long ones, so GLAM-SLAM decouples tracking from mapping so each can scale independently rather than sharing one representation.
  • Uses a structured, sparse anchor-grid map representation to keep memory bounded over long-horizon outdoor sequences, instead of dense per-frame Gaussian accumulation.
  • Introduces geometry-based flow-densification anchoring via epipolar constraints to satisfy 3D Gaussian Splatting's dense-initialization requirement, without needing a separate dense depth sensor.
  • Treats large-scale mapping as a multi-scene problem with a spatial-partitioning strategy using MLP initializations for localized Gaussians, reporting a 15% reconstruction-quality improvement over the second-best performer on KITTI, Oxford RobotCar, and Málaga while staying real-time.
Show abstract

Existing Gaussian-splatting-based monocular Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) systems are either tailored to short sequences, are not real-time, or suffer from prohibitive GPU memory requirements, limiting their applicability in realistic, long-horizon scenarios. To address this, we present GLAM-SLAM, a real-time, decoupled Gaussian-splatting SLAM system designed for large-scale outdoor scenes. We ensure lightweight tracking using a robust, feature-based SLAM frontend, while for mapping, we adopt a structured, sparse anchor grid representation that ensures scalable operation and maintains scene coherence across long-term sequences. To satisfy the dense initialization requirements of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), we introduce a geometry-based flow-densification anchoring strategy using epipolar constraints. Furthermore, by treating mapping as a multi-scene problem, we propose a scene-partitioning strategy that introduces a strong spatial inductive bias via MLP initializations to generate localized Gaussians. We evaluate our system on the challenging, long-sequence KITTI Odometry, Oxford RobotCar, and M'alaga datasets. Extensive ablations and comparisons demonstrate a 15% improvement in reconstruction quality over the second-best performer, while maintaining real-time performance and the ability to scale to longer sequences. Code is publicly available for the benefit of the community.

ZONDA: Zero-shot Object Navigation with Dynamic Avoidance in Multi-floor Environments

2026-07-23 cs.RO Xuanhong Liao (h=1)
Shaomin Liang, Xuanhong Liao, Shiyao Zhang
Core Contributions
  • Existing Object Goal Navigation work is largely restricted to static, single-floor environments; ZONDA specifically targets the two things that break in real deployment — cross-floor topology and dynamic pedestrians — rather than incremental gains on the single-floor setting.
  • Heuristic multi-floor planning derived from height-difference traversable maps enables stair traversal and cross-floor exploration without a platform-specific learned controller, keeping the navigation stack portable across robot embodiments.
  • Cross-checks multi-scale visual observations against a vision-language model for multi-view target verification, aimed explicitly at reducing false-positive goal detections rather than trusting single-view VLM grounding.
  • Explicitly tracks and predicts moving pedestrians to generate anticipatory avoidance behavior, and is validated on a real biped robot (Direct Drive Tech TITA) as well as HM3D/MP3D simulation, including the dynamic-specific HM3D-DYNA benchmark.
Show abstract

In Object Goal Navigation task, existing methods are typically restricted to static and single-floor environments, ignoring cross-floor topologies and dynamic pedestrian, which limits their real-world deployment. To address these limitations, we propose ZONDA, a zero-shot object navigation with dynamic avoidance framework. In particular, ZONDA integrates three core components: (i) Heuristic multi-floor planning: from height-difference traversable maps, enables stair traversal and cross-floor exploration without a platform-specific learned controller; (ii) Multi-view target verification: cross-checks multi-scale observations with a vision-language model, significantly reducing false positives; and (iii) Dynamic pedestrian avoidance: explicitly tracks and predicts moving pedestrians to generate anticipatory behaviors. Evaluated on a real Direct Drive Tech TITA biped robot and extensive simulations on HM3D and MP3D, ZONDA achieves significantly improved results. Moreover, ZONDA can maintain robust navigation on the dynamic benchmark HM3D-DYNA compared to the existing baseline.

VoLN: Vision-Only Long-Horizon Navigation—Paradigm, Benchmark, and Method

2026-07-23 cs.RO, cs.AI
Jiabin Lou, Haopeng Wang, Yuanshuai Wang, Xinyu Liu, Xuxin Lv
Core Contributions
  • Standard VLN benchmarks implicitly reward agents for exploiting spatial priors embedded in route-level language instructions (orientation, distance, layout); VoLN strips this out by supplying only a goal view and forcing agents to find route information from locally observable in-scene cues instead.
  • Instantiates the paradigm for aerial navigation via VoLN-UAV, a 7,210-episode benchmark combining long-horizon flight, continuous 3D motion, large viewpoint changes, and context-dependent beacon selection — conditions route-instruction-based VLN benchmarks don't stress.
  • The reference baseline (VoLN-MLLM) aligns self-supervised visual features with a structured semantic space and predicts short-horizon waypoints from observation history, goal views, and proprioception, with no externally supplied route description at all.
  • Reports success rates of just 7.4%, 4.5%, and 1.8% on Easy, Normal, and Hard Test-Unseen splits — numbers that expose how much of prior VLN performance may have been riding on instruction-supplied spatial priors rather than genuine visual navigation competence.
Show abstract

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) enables embodied agents to follow natural-language instructions. However, route-level instructions commonly encode spatial priors, such as orientation, distance, and layout, that are not explicitly available from onboard sensing at deployment in open, GPS-denied environments. Benchmark performance under such interfaces therefore jointly reflects visual navigation ability and the use of route structure explicitly supplied by the task description. As a complementary formulation, we propose Vision-Only Long-Horizon Navigation (VoLN), which shifts route-relevant information from externally supplied instructions and global guidance to locally observable in-scene cues. In VoLN, goal views specify the destination, while route-relevant information is available only through locally observable in-scene cues that the agent must detect, interpret, and select online. We instantiate VoLN for aerial navigation through VoLN-UAV, a 7,210-episode benchmark that combines long-horizon goal-directed flight, continuous 3D motion, large viewpoint changes, and context-dependent beacon selection. We further provide VoLN-MLLM as an initial reference baseline. It aligns self-supervised visual features with a structured semantic space and predicts short-horizon waypoint segments from observation history, goal views, retrieved visual--semantic tokens, and proprioception. On the five-environment Test-Unseen split, it obtains success rates of 7.4%, 4.5%, and 1.8% on Easy, Normal, and Hard episodes, respectively. These results provide an initial evaluation of VoLN and reveal substantial remaining challenges in long-horizon evidence integration, cross-view goal matching, and closed-loop stability. Project page: https://admire-ljb.github.io/VoLN-UAV/

Factorized Spatio-Temporal Convolutions for Human Pose Estimation from Planar Lidar

2026-07-23 cs.RO
Simone Arreghini, Mirko Nava, Nicholas Carlotti, Antonio Paolillo, Alessandro Giusti
Core Contributions
  • Most human pose-estimation pipelines assume cameras, 3D LiDAR, or GPU-class compute; this work specifically targets the common but under-served case of service robots equipped only with omnidirectional planar LiDAR and modest onboard processors.
  • Introduces Space-Time Blocks that explicitly factorize spatial processing along individual scan rays from temporal aggregation across scans, rather than mixing spatial and temporal reasoning in one convolution — a lightweight design suited to the sparse, per-scan structure of planar LiDAR.
  • Trains via cross-modal self-supervision from a narrow RGB-D body tracker in the sensor overlap region, eliminating the need for manual LiDAR pose labels entirely.
  • Reduces errors versus a parameter-matched baseline by 38% (distance), 28% (position), and 15% (orientation), and demonstrates real-time CPU inference on an actual service robot plus benchmarking on the public FROG dataset.
Show abstract

Localizing nearby humans and estimating their facing direction are key capabilities for safe navigation and socially aware human-robot interaction. Many pose-estimation pipelines target cameras and 3D LiDAR or assume GPU-class compute, whereas service robots are often equipped only with omnidirectional planar LiDARs and modest onboard processors. We address omnidirectional human detection and relative 2D pose estimation from planar LiDAR sequences with a lightweight network based on Space-Time Blocks, which explicitly separate spatial processing along scan rays from temporal aggregation across scans. Our network processes 360° LiDAR sequences to output per-ray human presence, distance, and relative orientation. We train it via cross-modal self-supervision from a narrow RGB-D body tracker in the sensors' overlap region, removing the need for manual LiDAR labels. Quantitative experiments show that our approach consistently outperforms a parameter-matched baseline model, reducing errors in distance (-38%), position (-28%), and orientation (-15%). We further benchmark on the public FROG dataset, report real-time CPU inference on a service robot, and validate with in-field demonstrations, supporting its suitability for spatial perception on computationally constrained service robots.

HGeo-TopoMap: Boosting Topological Mapping with Hierarchical Geometric Priors

2026-07-23 cs.CV, cs.RO, eess.IV
Siyu Li, Kunyu Peng, Di Wen, Beiping Hou, Zhiyong Li
Core Contributions
  • Centerline detection for topological road mapping is hard specifically because real-world roads lack explicit centerline markings; HGeo-TopoMap addresses this by injecting hierarchical geometric priors rather than relying on learned features alone to infer where a centerline should be.
  • A geometric adaptive learning module discretely encodes semantic and spatial features from an inverse-perspective-mapped road structure map, then a prior-mask attention mechanism focuses the network on the regions that prior actually makes informative.
  • A geometric consistency learning module enforces that centerline instances sharing the same geometric orientation get aligned features in the decoder, building spatial consistency directly into the representation rather than hoping it emerges from supervision alone.
  • Evaluated on OpenLane-V2 across centerline, lane-segment, and robustness benchmarks, reporting gains in both topological mapping accuracy and — notably — robustness under challenging conditions, not just clean-benchmark accuracy.
Show abstract

Topological maps are key outputs of autonomous driving perception systems, delivering essential road information for path planning. They identify instances such as centerlines and traffic signs, along with their connectivity relationships. Due to the lack of explicit markings for centerlines in real-world environments, the detection of centerline instances remains a significant challenge. To tackle this problem, we propose HGeo-TopoMap, which leverages an explicit prior map and implicit spatial relations to hierarchically boost topological mapping. First, a geometric adaptive learning module is designed for the road structure map obtained via inverse perspective mapping. This module discretely encodes semantic and spatial features from the map, followed by a prior-mask attention mechanism that selectively focuses on informative regions. Then, a geometric consistency learning module is devised, which leverages the geometric properties and spatial relationships of centerlines. Built on the geometry-aware decoder, it enforces spatial consistency by aligning features of centerline instances with identical geometric orientations. The proposed method is evaluated on the OpenLane-V2 dataset across the centerline, lane segment, and robustness benchmarks. Beyond substantial improvements in topological mapping accuracy, the proposed method offers the benefit of enhanced robustness, consistently outperforming baselines under both standard and challenging conditions. The source code and model weights will be made publicly available at https://github.com/lynn-yu/HGeo-TopoMap.

GuidedAttention: Interpretable and Correctable Visual Attention for OOD-Robust Robot Manipulation via Imitation Learning

2026-07-23 cs.RO Y. Domae (h=13)
Masaki Murooka, Ryoichi Nakajo, Keisuke Shirai, Tomohiro Motoda, Hanbit Oh
Core Contributions
  • Targets a specific opacity problem in end-to-end visuomotor policies: there's no way for a human to inspect or fix what the policy is "looking at," so GuidedAttention makes visual attention an explicit, correctable intermediate representation instead of an implicit byproduct of training.
  • Predicts task-relevant attention keypoints from camera images and conditions a diffusion action policy on them, so the same architecture that makes attention interpretable also makes it steerable.
  • Lets a user correct selected keypoints just once, at rollout initialization, after which a tracking module automatically propagates the correction through the rest of execution — correction cost is front-loaded rather than requiring continuous supervision.
  • Reports consistent gains specifically under positional and appearance out-of-distribution conditions, the exact regime where opaque end-to-end policies tend to fail silently.
Show abstract

End-to-end visuomotor policies provide little opportunity for humans to understand or correct the policy's visual attention. We propose GuidedAttention, a visuomotor imitation learning framework that introduces interpretable and correctable visual attention as an explicit intermediate representation. Task-relevant attention keypoints are predicted from camera images and condition a diffusion-based action policy. Users can inspect and optionally correct selected keypoints once at rollout initialization, after which the corrected attention is automatically propagated throughout execution by a tracking module. Experiments in simulation and the real world demonstrate that GuidedAttention consistently improves robot manipulation performance, particularly under positional and appearance out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions.

Scale Up Strategically: Learning Compositional Generalization via Bias-Aware Evaluation and Data Collection for Robotic Manipulation

2026-07-23 cs.RO, cs.CV Jonathan Tremblay (h=6)
Yu Qi, Zhang Ye, Xinyi Xu, Yuxuan Lu, Amitoj Sandhu
Core Contributions
  • Moves past aggregate benchmark scores to localize exactly where compositional generalization breaks: individual instruction factors like color, verb, object, size, and spatial attribute, rather than treating "language grounding" as one undifferentiated capability.
  • Introduces two concrete diagnostic metrics — Factor Dominance Rate for pairwise shortcut bias and Factor Dominance Hierarchy for a global ranking — that quantify shortcut reliance rather than just reporting that a policy fails on some instructions.
  • Finds a consistent shortcut ordering across six different foundation policies (color ≥ object ≥ spatial ≥ verb ≥ size), suggesting the bias is systemic to how these models are trained rather than an artifact of any one architecture.
  • Turns the diagnosis into an actionable fix: reallocating a fixed data-collection budget toward under-grounded factors (verb, size) beats baseline collection strategies in simulation and on a real robot using half the demonstrations.
Show abstract

Compositional generalization is essential for robot to follow diverse instructions. However, pretrained policies are known to take shortcuts, deferring to salient cues rather than grounding language. We introduce a diagnostic framework that localizes this failure to individual \textit{instruction factors}, \textit{e.g.,} reusable semantic components such as color, verb, object, size, and spatial attribute. Our framework formalizes instruction factor bias, the tendency of fine-tuned policies to over-rely on dominant factors as shortcuts, and quantifies it through two metrics: Factor Dominance Rate (FDR), capturing pairwise bias between factors, and Factor Dominance Hierarchy (FDH), aggregating these into a global ranking. Evaluation on six foundation policies reveals broadly consistent ordering, \textit{i.e.}, color $\geq$ object $\geq$ spatial $\geq$ verb $\geq$ size, with color dominant, and verb and size most under-grounded. We further show the diagnosis is actionable: a bias-aware data collection strategy that reallocates a fixed budget toward under-grounded factors outperforms baselines in simulation and on a real robot using half the demonstrations, thereby enabling more sample-efficient and generalizable policy learning.

AXIS: A Growable Community-Driven Data Engine for Scalable Robot Manipulation

2026-07-23 cs.RO Peihao Li (h=4)
Mengfei Zhao, Dihong Huang, Yikai Tang, Peihao Li, Mingxuan Yan
Core Contributions
  • Targets the scalability bottleneck of existing manipulation data pipelines directly — reliance on specialized hardware, centralized operators, or fixed task suites — by making data collection browser-based and community-driven instead.
  • Automates the parts of the pipeline that usually require manual curation: task generation and validation, success checking, quality filtering, trajectory smoothing, and both visual and physics-based augmentation, so community-submitted demonstrations become training-ready without a human curation bottleneck.
  • Organizes 207 tasks and 50K+ trajectories into task snapshots with a systematic held-out evaluation protocol, enabling apples-to-apples comparison of VLA policies rather than each paper defining its own eval split.
  • Continual pretraining on AXIS improves π_0.5's overall success rate by 5.8% and beats RoboCasa365 pretraining by 37.3%, with the largest gains specifically under layout, sensor-noise, and camera perturbations — exactly the robustness axes community-collected data should help with.
Show abstract

Learning effective robot manipulation policies requires diverse, high-quality demonstrations, yet existing data pipelines are often difficult to scale because they rely on specialized hardware, centralized operators, or fixed task suites. We present AXIS, a growable community-driven data engine and benchmark for scalable robot learning, which enables browser-based teleoperation for large-scale demonstration collection, automatically generates and validates new manipulation tasks, and transforms community-collected demonstrations into training-ready data through automated success checking, quality filtering, trajectory smoothing, and visual and physics-based augmentation. The AXIS dataset currently contains 207 diverse tasks and 50K+ trajectories. Meanwhile, AXIS organizes data into task snapshots and evaluates policies with a systematic held-out protocol. We compare vision-language-action (VLA) policies under a unified AXIS evaluation suite and analyze scaling behavior across different data volumes. Continual pretraining on AXIS substantially improves the overall success rate of $π_{0.5}$ by 5.8%, outperforms the model pretrained on RoboCasa365 by 37.3%, and exhibits consistent scaling with increasing data volume, with the largest gains observed under layout, sensor-noise, and camera perturbations.

TransBiolab: A Real-World Multi-View Dataset of Cluttered Transparent Biomedical Objects

2026-07-23 cs.CV, cs.MM, cs.RO Ke Ma (h=3)
Ke Ma, Yifei Wang, Meng Wang, Tian Xia
Core Contributions
  • Addresses a perception gap that generic transparent-object datasets don't cover: cluttered multi-object scenes where mutual occlusion and view-dependent appearance changes compound the usual difficulty of segmenting transparent plasticware.
  • Captures calibrated multi-view sequences rather than single-view snapshots, which matters because transparent-object appearance changes drastically with viewpoint — a single view alone can't disambiguate identity or pose.
  • Delivers 161,315 frames across 98 scenes with 1.03M instance annotations spanning 6D poses, full and visible masks, depth, and camera calibration for 15 lab object types — enough density to support segmentation, depth, and pose benchmarks simultaneously rather than just one task.
  • Organizes difficulty explicitly along object category, object count, and viewpoint, and backs the released annotations with a system-level robot manipulation evaluation, connecting the perception benchmark to an actual downstream manipulation outcome.
Show abstract

Autonomous biomedical laboratories increasingly rely on visual perception to recognize, localize, and manipulate transparent plasticware, yet high-quality real-world datasets for this setting remain limited. The scarcity of domain-relevant data is particularly restrictive in cluttered multi-object scenes, where mutual occlusion and view-dependent appearance changes remain challenging even for contemporary visual foundation models. Existing transparent-object datasets have advanced segmentation, depth, and pose estimation, but they usually do not evaluate the combined setting of multi-object clutter, occlusion, and calibrated multi-view capture that characterizes real laboratory manipulation scenes. To address this gap, we present TrainsBiolab, a real-world RGB-D dataset of cluttered transparent biomedical objects captured as calibrated multi-view sequences. TrainsBiolab contains 161,315 frames from 98 scenes and 1.03M instance annotations over 15 laboratory object types, including 6D poses, full and visible masks, depth, and per-frame camera calibration. The dataset is organized along three axes that reflect operational difficulty: object category, the total number of objects in a frame, and camera viewpoint. We further define dataset-centric benchmarks for segmentation, depth estimation and completion, and 6D pose estimation, and report a system-level robot manipulation evaluation enabled by the released annotations and calibrations. By focusing on repeated transparent instances, clutter, and multi-view laboratory capture, TrainsBiolab provides a resource for segmentation, depth estimation, 6D pose estimation, and multi-view reasoning in autonomous laboratory manipulation. Project page: https://dualtransparency.github.io/TransBiolab/.

TableVerse: A Large-scale Tabletop Dataset with Real-world Grounded Layouts for Generalizable Manipulation

2026-07-23 cs.RO Yu Sun (h=3)
Boyuan Wang, Yue Zhang, Xutao Xue, Xueyu Song, Yu Sun
Core Contributions
  • Breaks from the two dominant synthetic-scene generation paradigms — text-to-layout hallucination and simplified procedural generation — both of which struggle with physical implausibility and dense real-world clutter, by reconstructing scenes deterministically from unstructured internet images instead of imagining them.
  • The Real2Sim pipeline converts unscripted internet media into simulation-ready tabletop environments with accurate metric scale, authentic object topology, and verified mechanical stability, rather than relying on generative hallucination for physical plausibility.
  • Pairs an automated task-conditioned trajectory generator with the reconstructed scenes to synthesize collision-free pick-and-place demonstrations, so the dataset ships with usable manipulation trajectories rather than just static scenes.
  • Delivers TableVerse-100K, 100,000 physically consistent environments with interactive trajectories — a scale aimed squarely at the data-hungry end of generalizable manipulation policy training.
Show abstract

The development of generalizable robotic manipulation policies is inherently bounded by the availability of large-scale, high-fidelity scene data. While recent automated synthesis methods attempt to bridge this gap via text-to-layout hallucination or simplified procedural generation, they frequently suffer from physical implausibility and fail to capture the complex, dense clutter of actual human environments. In this paper, we introduce TableVerse, a fully automated Real2Sim pipeline that shifts the paradigm from imaginative layout generation to deterministic reconstruction from unstructured, in-the-wild image data. Our framework seamlessly processes unscripted internet media into high-fidelity, simulation-ready tabletop environments with accurate metric scales, authentic topologies, and verified mechanical stability. Furthermore, an automated task-conditioned trajectory generation framework is integrated to synthesize high-quality, collision-free pick-and-place demonstrations. Leveraging this complete pipeline, we construct the TableVerse-100K Dataset, a large-scale corpus comprising 100,000 unique, physically consistent environments paired with interactive manipulation trajectories. By capturing diverse asset compositions, realistic spatial distributions, and high-quality demonstrations, TableVerse-100K establishes a highly scalable and high-fidelity data foundation, providing significant value to facilitate future research in generalizable robotic manipulation tasks.

Deep Reinforcement-Learning-Guided Model Predictive Control for Preventing Overtakes in Autonomous Racing

2026-07-23 cs.RO Tulga Ersal (h=29)
Yufei Xi, Yijie Liao, Tulga Ersal
Core Contributions
  • Reframes defense in autonomous racing as spatial-occupancy regulation rather than the usual lap-time minimization objective, changing what the controller is optimizing rather than just adding a defensive term to an existing racing objective.
  • Hierarchical design keeps a Soft Actor-Critic strategic layer in Frenet coordinates for geometry-aware defensive references, then hands execution to nonlinear MPC with those references folded in as spatial regularization under friction limits — RL sets intent, MPC guarantees dynamic feasibility.
  • Nearly doubles average overtake-prevention time from 8.8s to 14.6s while cutting opponent progress, and does so while still using 83.4% of available tire force, showing the defensive behavior isn't just conservative blocking.
  • Mean solve time of 33.3ms (13.9ms std) keeps the hierarchical RL+MPC loop inside real-time budgets for high-speed adversarial driving, evidence the added strategic layer doesn't blow the control bandwidth.
Show abstract

This paper addresses defensive blocking in autonomous racing, where a vehicle must prevent a faster opponent from overtaking while operating near its dynamic limits.
