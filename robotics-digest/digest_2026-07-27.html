Research Landscape

Today's batch of 30 papers shows manipulation-focused VLA and foundation-model research converging on a shared insight: raw pixel-level video or vision-language pretraining alone under-specifies the physical and temporal structure needed for reliable robot control, so recent work is injecting explicit structure back in. FutureRTC uses robot motion as a physical prior to predict execution-time observations for asynchronous control; τ and FeelWorld both push into tactile representation from different angles—τ learns tactile latents via JEPA-style future visual supervision for VLA fusion, while FeelWorld builds a full hierarchical visuo-tactile world model for planning; DeVA decouples video and action experts with explicit affordance/depth guidance; KAI and NEO both inject structured priors (kinematic structure, edited neural fields) rather than trusting an end-to-end policy to infer them from demonstrations alone. The Data Pyramid survey functions as a useful meta-commentary on this whole cluster, framing the five-source data ecosystem these systems draw on and naming tactile-data scarcity and cross-embodiment alignment as the two hardest open problems—both of which surface directly in today's other papers (τ's TacAura dataset, KAI's human-video co-training).

A second cluster is about resilience and coordination in multi-agent systems under real-world resource constraints—energy, bandwidth, and single points of failure. Distributed Coordination for Multi-UAV inspection and Quality-Adaptive Multi-UAV reconstruction both attack decentralization from the coordination-algorithm side (re-auction mechanisms, local-plus-global imbalance correction), while SimBEV2X and the underwater monitoring paper attack it from the infrastructure side (a massive new V2X dataset; a two-tier low-power/high-power hardware split). SHARE adds a third angle specific to human-robot collaboration: treating heterogeneous agents (AR headset users vs. robots) identically in a shared SLAM pipeline is itself a design flaw, not just a scaling problem—a framing that pairs naturally with FloAff-Kitchen's point that navigation and manipulation planning have been artificially separated.

A smaller but notable thread is honest reporting of tradeoffs rather than pure benchmark-chasing. WARL explicitly documents that wrench-augmented exploration can produce behaviors that ignore the robot's physical embodiment; the note on SE(3) vs. SO(3)×R³ integration is careful to say its result doesn't automatically extend from a single rigid body to full multibody systems; and the two social-robotics field studies (episodic memory, humanoid head acceptance) report that the biggest friction points are venue-context mismatch and response latency, not raw conversational capability. Combined with two BMI papers tackling the same neural-drift problem from complementary angles—cyclic domain adaptation/generalization vs. consistency-based representation learning—this batch reads less like isolated point solutions and more like a field actively cross-checking its own assumptions.

FutureRTC: Real-Time Robot Execution with Anticipatory-Conditioned Action Chunking

2026-07-27 cs.RO Fanman Meng (h=34)
Hai Jiang, Yixian Zou, Binbin Liang, Boqian Liu, Fanman Meng
Core Contributions
  • Targets a specific failure mode in async VLA deployment—inter-chunk discontinuities from prediction-execution misalignment—rather than generic latency, focusing on the boundary artifacts that appear when a new chunk lands mid-execution of the old one.
  • Plug-and-play: wraps around a frozen VLA policy instead of retraining it, predicting what the policy will see at execution time rather than what it currently sees.
  • Two-part correction: a state-correction module fixes proprioceptive rollout error, while an observation-prediction module forecasts future visual frames using robot motion as an explicit physical prior (motion-aware feature transport) rather than a generic video predictor.
  • A policy-consistency loss ties predicted-context action chunks to what the policy would output under true future inputs—turning delay compensation into a trainable signal instead of a runtime patch.
  • Reports smoother trajectories, faster execution, and higher success under inference delay across simulated and real environments.
Show abstract

Real-time deployment of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies necessitates asynchronous execution, wherein subsequent action chunks are computed concurrently with the execution of the current chunk, leading to prediction-execution misalignment and manifesting as inter-chunk discontinuities. Existing methods either superficially smooth chunk boundaries, require costly policy optimization, or exclusively forward-predict proprioceptive states yet neglect critical visual observations. In this paper, we propose FutureRTC, a plug-and-play adaptation framework that predicts execution-time observations and states for asynchronous VLA control without modifying the underlying policy. Specifically, FutureRTC features a state correction module to compensate for the discrepancy between rolled-forward and actual execution-time proprioceptive states and an observation prediction module that forecasts execution-time visual representations by leveraging robot motion as an explicit physical prior through motion-aware feature transport and reconstruction. Furthermore, we introduce a policy consistency loss to align the action chunks generated from predicted contexts with those produced under the expected execution-time inputs of the VLA policy. Extensive experiments across simulated and real-world environments demonstrate that FutureRTC achieves superior robustness to inference delays, resulting in smoother trajectories, faster execution, and consistently higher task success rates.

PAC-DP: PAC-Bayesian Diffusion Policy Learning

2026-07-27 cs.RO M. Khadiv (h=19)
Mohammad Hasan Yeganegi, Dian Yu, Andrea Del Prete, Majid Khadiv, Matteo Saveriano
Core Contributions
  • Targets a gap most practitioners don't address: diffusion policies only minimize a denoising objective, giving no explicit lever over generalization when demonstration data is scarce.
  • Reframes the diffusion policy as a Bayesian neural network and derives a PAC-Bayes generalization bound, turning it into a concrete auxiliary loss—a KL regularizer between posterior and prior parameters—added on top of the standard denoising loss.
  • Nearly free to add: the regularizer doesn't meaningfully increase training time, unlike ensemble- or sampling-heavy uncertainty methods.
  • Reports improved denoising performance, lower variational negative log-likelihood, and higher manipulation success, with the largest gains concentrated in low-data regimes and harder tasks—exactly where a generalization bound should matter most.
Show abstract

Diffusion Policies (DPs) are able to perform complex manipulation tasks. However, DPs are typically trained by minimizing a denoising objective, which provides limited control over generalization in the finite-data regimes common in robotics. In this letter, we propose PAC-DP, an approach that increases the performance of DPs in robotic manipulation tasks. By modeling the DP as a Bayesian neural network, and defining a PAC-Bayes generalization bound, we derive a novel training objective that augments the standard denoising loss with a Kullback-Leibler divergence regularizer between the posterior and prior parameter distributions. From the theoretical perspective, our approach provides a principled approach to regularize the training of DPs without significantly increasing the training time. From the practical point of view, experimental results demonstrate improved denoising performance, lower variational negative log-likelihood, and higher success rates across multiple robotic manipulation benchmarks. Crucially, the largest improvements are observed in low-data training regimes and complex tasks, establishing PAC-DP as a theoretically grounded framework for robot policy learning.

KAI: A Kinematic-Aware Interface for Data-Efficient Articulated Object Manipulation

2026-07-27 cs.RO Dahua Lin (h=16)
Yaping Li, Zhaxizhuoma, Qiaojun Yu, Jia Zeng, Dahua Lin
Core Contributions
  • Targets sample efficiency in articulated manipulation by inserting an explicit intermediate representation (kinematic structure) rather than expecting a policy to infer joint/hinge structure implicitly from raw demonstrations.
  • Numbers: 82.9% average success across six simulation tasks while using only half the demonstration data of baselines—a direct sample-efficiency claim, not just a peak-performance one.
  • Action-agnostic design lets it co-train with human interaction videos (not just robot teleop data), achieving over 70% success under visual distractions—leveraging cheap human video data for a channel most articulated-manipulation methods can't use.
  • Generalizes from a single clean training environment to cluttered real scenes with unseen backgrounds/distractors, suggesting the kinematic prior does real transfer work rather than memorizing training-scene statistics.
Show abstract

Articulated object manipulation requires an understanding of kinematic structure that is difficult and costly to learn from robot demonstrations alone. We introduce the Kinematic-Aware Articulation Interface (KAI), a structured intermediate representation that captures the kinematic structure of articulated objects. By embedding interpretable geometric and kinematic priors into policy learning, KAI provides a strong inductive bias aligned with the underlying structure of articulated motion. This design effectively improves sample efficiency, with gains particularly pronounced in low-data regimes: across six simulation tasks, our method achieves an average success rate of 82.9%, matching or surpassing baseline performance while using only half the demonstration data. Our method also exhibits robust generalization to unseen backgrounds and visual distractors, transferring from a single clean training environment to cluttered real-world scenes. KAI's action-agnostic design further enables co-training with human interaction videos to enhance real-world robustness: under diverse visual distractions, our method with video co-training achieves over 70% average success rate.

Data Pyramid for Embodied Manipulation

2026-07-27 cs.RO cs.CV Ziang Cao (h=15)
Yifan Ye, Yankai Fu, Yaoxu Lv, Bohan Hou, Jun Cen
Core Contributions
  • A positioning/survey paper, not a new model: organizes the embodied data ecosystem into a five-layer pyramid (real-robot, UMI-style, ego/exocentric, simulation, general vision-language) framed around the tension between scalability and robot-specific alignment.
  • Analyzes recent embodied foundation models specifically through their data recipes—how sources are selected, aligned, and mixed—rather than through architecture, which is the more common lens.
  • Useful as a companion read to today's other manipulation papers (FutureRTC, τ, DeVA, KAI): gives a vocabulary for classifying what kind of data each of those systems actually depends on.
  • Names concrete open problems rather than vague future work: large-scale tactile datasets, failure/recovery data, scalable pipelines, cross-embodiment action alignment, egocentric data for dexterity, and principled data-mixing recipes—effectively a research agenda for the field.
Show abstract

Multimodal foundation models learned to see and to speak by consuming the whole internet. Embodied agents admit no such shortcut, since they require data that couple observations with physical states and actions. These signals can be provided, to varying degrees, by multiple data sources. In this work, we organize the embodied data ecosystem as a pyramid spanning five complementary sources: real-robot data, UMI-style data, egocentric and exocentric data, simulation data, and general vision-language data. We organize the pyramid around the tension between scalability and robot alignment, and further characterize each source in terms of data quality, diversity, reusability, and physical fidelity. We then analyze recent embodied foundation models through the lens of their data recipes, examining how different sources are selected, aligned, and mixed during pretraining. For embodied brain models, vision-language-action models, and world-action models alike, we relate data composition to capabilities in perception, reasoning, planning, action generation, and world prediction. We close by discussing six open challenges: building large-scale tactile datasets, collecting failure and recovery data, developing scalable data-collection pipelines, aligning actions across embodiments, leveraging egocentric data for dexterous manipulation, and designing principled data recipes for robot learning. We hope this work paves the foundation for the design of next-generation embodied systems.

NEO: NeRF It Once, Edit It Many Times for Continuous Object Manipulation

2026-07-27 cs.RO P. Moghadam (h=7)
Mikołaj Zieliński, David Hall, Dominik Belter, Peyman Moghadam
Core Contributions
  • Targets scene editing for manipulation planning: letting a robot 'imagine' an edited future scene state (e.g., object removed) before acting, using NeRF rather than pixel-space image editing.
  • Combines neural field resampling with multiview-consistent progressive inpainting for object removal, addressing the classic NeRF-editing failure mode where edits look consistent from one view but break from others.
  • Edits NeRF weights directly via knowledge distillation in a teacher-student setup (original NeRF as teacher, edited NeRF as student) rather than re-rendering and re-optimizing from scratch for each edit.
  • Introduces NEO-Dataset as the first benchmark specifically for evaluating NeRF scene-editing quality in a manipulation context, reporting gains on both the editing task and downstream pick-and-place execution.
Show abstract

In this paper, we present NEO, a unified framework providing language-guided NeRF editing for robotic manipulation. Our paper introduces (i) a language-guided object removal that combines neural field resampling with multiview-consistent progressive inpainting, (ii) a direct NeRF weight editing method utilizing knowledge distillation, composing original and edited NeRFs via a teacher-student model, enabling coherent modeling of future scene states before a robot executes an action, and (iii) the first benchmark (NEO-Dataset) for quantitatively evaluating NeRF scene editing methods suitable for robot manipulation. We show that our approach outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in scene editing tasks, including object removal and pick-and-place robotic experiments, yielding visually coherent and geometrically consistent edits that reduce artifacts commonly introduced by prior methods.

DeVA: Decoupled Video-Action Model with physical guidance for robot policy learning

2026-07-27 cs.RO cs.CV Simar Kareer (h=6)
Mengqi Zhang, Sahil Khose, Simar Kareer, Yuchen Song, Unnat Jain
Core Contributions
  • Targets an architecture tradeoff in video-conditioned VLA models: coupling video and action prediction in one shared backbone makes optimization harder, while under-utilizing video features in the guidance direction loses signal—DeVA decouples them into specialized experts instead.
  • Transfers representations from multiple video-model layers into the action expert (not just the final layer), enabling richer information flow while keeping the two learning problems separable.
  • Adds physically salient guidance (affordance/depth) as intermediate supervision on both video and action streams—an explicit inductive bias toward physically meaningful features rather than raw pixel prediction.
  • Reports strong performance with limited data and faster convergence than a unified (coupled) architecture, directly testing the claim that decoupling helps optimization, not just accuracy.
Show abstract

Generalizable robot manipulation requires policies that can anticipate how visual scenes evolve while executing language instructions. While recent Vision-Language-Action models benefit from large-scale pretraining, their predominantly static pretraining objectives provide limited supervision for physical dynamics and temporal causality, leaving control-relevant knowledge to be learned from downstream robot demonstrations. Video generative models offer a promising foundation by encoding rich spatiotemporal priors through future predictions. However, existing Video-Action Models either couple video and action prediction in a shared backbone, making policy adaptation harder to optimize, or under-utilize video information when guiding the action branch. In this work, we introduce DeVA, a Decoupled Video-Action model with specialized video and action experts, multi-level feature transfer, and physically salient guidance. DeVA transfers representations from multiple video layers to the action expert, enabling rich information exchange while making policy learning more tractable. It further supervises intermediate video features and the action stream with physically salient guidance (affordance/depth). Experiments on both simulation benchmarks and real-world deployment demonstrate strong performance with limited data, faster convergence than a unified architecture, and clear performance gains from physical guidance.

SimBEV2X: A Large-Scale Dataset and Data Generation Tool for Multi-Task Vehicle-to-Everything Cooperative Perception

2026-07-27 cs.CV cs.LG cs.MA cs.RO A. Eskandarian (h=34)
Goodarz Mehr, Sepideh Gohari, Montasir Abbas, Azim Eskandarian
Core Contributions
  • Attacks the V2X dataset bottleneck by scale: 258 scenes, up to 8 connected vehicles + 4 RSUs, 102,200 frames, 588,520 lidar point clouds, 3M+ images, and 27M+ bounding boxes—an order of magnitude larger than prior V2X datasets.
  • Built on CARLA rather than real-world capture, sidestepping the synchronization and annotation cost that has kept existing V2X datasets small.
  • Ground truth spans 3D boxes with persistent track IDs, HD maps, BEV segmentation, and semantic occupancy voxels from both vehicle and roadside-unit (RSU) viewpoints, enabling multi-task benchmarking beyond single-task 3D detection.
  • Introduces CoBEVFusion, combining CoopDet3D with fused axial attention (FAX) for context-aware multi-agent feature aggregation, and reports it beats the CoopDet3D baseline—establishing a first strong baseline rather than just releasing raw data.
Show abstract

Cooperative perception through vehicle-to-everything (V2X) communication can overcome the inherent physical limitations of individual autonomous vehicles, such as occlusions and limited sensor range. However, the development of robust V2X algorithms, particularly those relying on unified spatial representations like bird's-eye view (BEV) representation, is hampered by the lack of large-scale, multi-modal, multi-task datasets. Moreover, collecting and annotating a large set of synchronized, real-world multi-agent data is prohibitively expensive. This has resulted in a landscape where existing V2X datasets are notably limited in both size and scope. To overcome this, we introduce SimBEV2X, an advanced synthetic data generation tool built on the CARLA simulator. SimBEV2X automatically creates randomized driving scenarios to collect multi-modal sensor data alongside various types of ground truth including 3D bounding boxes with unique track IDs, HD map information, BEV segmentation maps, and semantic occupancy voxel grids from both vehicles and RSUs. We also present the SimBEV2X dataset, the largest V2X perception dataset to date. The dataset comprises 258 scenes, each involving up to 8 connected vehicles and up to 4 RSUs across a variety of road networks. The SimBEV2X dataset is an order of magnitude larger than existing V2X datasets and contains 102,200 frames, 588,520 lidar point clouds, more than 3 million images, over 27 million bounding boxes, and a comprehensive set of other annotations. Finally, we establish a strong baseline on the SimBEV2X dataset using CoopDet3D and propose CoBEVFusion, a novel architecture that combines CoopDet3D with fused axial attention (FAX) for context-aware multi-agent feature aggregation, resulting in superior performance. SimBEV2X, the SimBEV2X dataset, and CoBEVFusion are available at https://simbev2x.org and https://github.com/GoodarzMehr/SimBEV2X.

Distributed Coordination for Resilient Multi-UAV Remote Sensing: A Photovoltaic Inspection Case Study

2026-07-27 cs.RO P. Campoy (h=28)
Guillermo GP-Lenza, Miguel Fernandez-Cortizas, Martin Molina, Pascual Campoy
Core Contributions
  • Reframes three separate failure modes—initial task allocation, platform failure, and degraded-data reassignment—as instances of the same underlying problem: a re-auction over remaining targets and active platforms.
  • Removes the single point of failure baked into typical UAV inspection fleets (a ground-station coordinator) by running peer-to-peer, layered non-invasively on an existing framework (Aerostack2) with zero core modifications.
  • Validated on a concrete, economically relevant task—utility-scale photovoltaic plant inspection—where mission value hinges on complete coverage, making graceful degradation under link or platform loss directly actionable.
  • Practical significance: shows a coordination layer can be retrofitted onto existing aerial autonomy stacks rather than requiring a bespoke multi-robot framework built from scratch.
Show abstract

Deploying multiple UAVs for remote sensing enables proportional reductions in mission time, but realizing these benefits requires the fleet to coordinate at runtime: distributing sensing targets, responding to platform failures, and recovering from degraded data quality. In inspection campaigns, where mission value depends on complete coverage and the usability of every capture, a centralized ground-station coordinator is a single point of failure: a lost link or station fault leaves sensing gaps that cannot be filled without operator intervention. We propose the SwarmLink, an inter-agent communication infrastructure that non-invasively extends any existing aerial framework with peer-to-peer coordination capability, without modifying the host system. We apply it to photovoltaic plant inspection as a representative large-scale sensing campaign, extending Aerostack2 with a distributed auction that unifies initial sensing-target allocation, platform-failure recovery, and data-quality-triggered reassignment into a single runtime mechanism. All three disruption scenarios reduce to the same re-auction over remaining targets and active platforms, requiring zero modifications to the Aerostack2 core and no ground-station involvement during the mission.

Energy Constrained Hierarchical Underwater Monitoring via Local Multi-Agent RAG

2026-07-27 cs.IR cs.CV cs.MA cs.RO Laurent Gautier (h=7)
Mohamed Amine Janati, Laurent Gautier, Stéphane Barbot
Core Contributions
  • Solves a resource-constraint problem specific to marine deployments: energy is scarce, connectivity is poor, and transmitting raw multimodal data is expensive, so the system must decide locally what's worth processing and reporting.

Show abstract

Marine life monitoring is limited by strict energy constraints, poor underwater connectivity, and the high cost of transmitting raw multimodal data from remote deployments. This paper proposes a low-consumption underwater monitoring architecture that combines always-on edge sensing with selective high-performance local reasoning. The system follows a hierarchical master-satellite design in which ultra-low-power MAX78000/MAX78002 microcontrollers continuously monitor visual and acoustic signals, while an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX is activated only for scheduled processing, event-driven analysis, or researcher interaction. Quality-Adaptive Multi-UAV 3D Reconstruction with Sparse Workload Redistribution

2026-07-27 cs.RO Olivier Simonin (h=4)
Benjamin Sportich, Kenza Boubakri, Olivier Simonin, Alessandro Renzaglia
Core Contributions
  • Targets a coordination problem specific to multi-UAV mapping: UAVs must agree not just on where to look but on how much fidelity is needed where, since blindly maximizing coverage wastes limited onboard compute/energy.
  • Integrates a TSDF-confidence-based quality criterion directly into viewpoint generation and information-gain estimation, so the fidelity target actively shapes exploration rather than acting as a post-hoc filter.
  • Two-level coordination avoids both over- and under-correction: a local penalty discourages UAV clustering, while a separate global imbalance-correction step (regularized clustering + optimal task assignment) only triggers when clustering is actually detected—avoiding constant costly rebalancing.

Show abstract

3D reconstruction of unknown environments is a key application in robotics but is severely limited by the computational and energy capabilities of current aerial platforms. Deploying multiple UAVs and providing efficient and scalable path planning strategies are common approaches, but effective online coordination among UAVs remains a significant challenge. To address this problem, we propose a quality-adaptive decentralized decision-making strategy to build a 3D map with user-defined degrees of fidelity. The approach integrates a quality-oriented criterion based on TSDF confidence into view generation and information gain estimation to produce viewpoints consistent with the desired fidelity target. Additionally, we employ two levels of coordination: a penalty factor in the viewpoint evaluation to encourage local dispersion among the UAVs and a global imbalance correction mechanism. A note on the motion representation and configuration update in time stepping schemes for the constrained rigid body

2026-07-27 cs.RO A. Müller (h=29)
A. Müller
Core Contributions
  • A theory paper, not an applied system: asks which Lie group should represent rigid-body configuration in numerical DAE integration for constrained multibody dynamics.
  • Flags a subtle but consequential common practice—treating rigid-body motion on the direct product SO(3)×R³ instead of the semidirect product SE(3)—and shows this choice affects whether constraints are satisfied exactly by the integrator.
  • Key result: constraints are satisfied exactly only when they define a subgroup of the configuration space, and SE(3)'s subgroups map onto physically meaningful cases (lower kinematic/Reuleaux pairs) already implicit in multibody modeling.
  • Explicit caveat that matters for simulation/contact-integration code: the SE(3) recommendation holds for a single constrained rigid body but does not automatically extend to full multibody systems.
Show abstract

The dynamics of a holonomically constrained rigid body can be modeled by Newton-Euler equations subjected to geometric constraints. This is frequently formulated as a differential-algebraic equation (DAE) system of index 1. In multibody system (MBS) dynamics it is common (1) to numerically solve this system by means of integration schemes for ordinary differential equations, and (2) to treat the rigid body motion on the direct product Lie group SO(3)xR3, although rigid body motions form the semidirect product Lie group SE(3). It is has been observed that the constraint satisfaction depends on which Lie group is used as configuration space (c-space). In this paper the problem is considered from a geometric perspective. It is shown that the constraints are exactly satisfied by a numerical integration scheme if they define a subgroup of the c-space. The subgroups of SE(3) have a significance for modeling mechanical systems, including lower kinematic (Reuleaux) pairs and are implicitly used in MBS modeling. It is concluded that SE(3) is the appropriate c-space for numerical DAE modeling of a constrained rigid body. This result does not immediately apply to MBS, however.

Bridging Reinforcement Learning and Optimal Control via Feasible Action Mapping

2026-07-27 eess.SY cs.RO Peter Dürr (h=11)
Stefan Richter, Alberto Giammarino, Guillem Torrente, Sam Blakeman, Peter Dürr
Core Contributions
  • Targets the classic RL-vs-optimal-control tradeoff directly: RL is flexible but can't guarantee constraint satisfaction, while OC guarantees safety but needs expert-designed action/parameter spaces.
  • Core contribution is a mapping algorithm, not a new RL algorithm: transforms the RL agent's abstract action into a state-dependent feasible parameter set for the OCP, so the solver guarantees constraint satisfaction regardless of the RL policy's output.
  • Distinguishes itself from prior RL-OC hybrids by not requiring hand-designed or heuristic abstract action spaces—the mapping handles that automatically.
  • Tested on robot table tennis, chosen because it needs both fast reactive motion planning and strict feasibility; reports better sample efficiency and closed-loop performance than baselines in simulation.
Show abstract

Operating constrained dynamical systems requires controllers to efficiently solve complex tasks while enforcing recursive feasibility and safety constraints.
