Research Landscape

Four of today's papers converge on the same question from different angles: what should a world model's latent or visual representation actually encode for planning? Temporal-Distance JEPA and VisualPatchWorld (same author pair) both argue that representation-learning byproducts — Euclidean latent distance, or an unstructured neural predictor — make poor planning costs, and instead mine a directed temporal cost from trajectories or fit an inspectable, code-form dynamical model. INTACT pushes further, folding planning and representation into a single isomorphic architecture so that no test-time search is needed at all (one-epoch models already hit up to 100% success on LeWM tasks, with optional CEM refinement using 23× fewer samples). DC-WAM applies the same underlying insight to video-based World-Action Models, showing that de-emphasizing photorealistic reconstruction in favor of contact- and motion-centric supervision improves robustness to exactly the appearance shifts that photorealism was supposed to help with. Read together, these four papers make a specific, falsifiable claim: the field has been over-investing in representation fidelity (visual or geometric) and under-investing in representations shaped directly by the planning or control objective.

A second cluster targets the reactivity and data-efficiency of manipulation policies. πR² attacks the latency problem in flow/diffusion policies directly, splitting fast proprioceptive and slow visual-language conditioning so a large pretrained backbone can replan four times faster without retraining from scratch. SAM3D-Guided VLA and the causality-aware IDR framework both refine how a VLA fuses modalities — one by distilling 3D object priors into training only, the other by diagnosing vision's causal effect on actions at test time — while S2A2 extends the modality question to acoustic cues. Underneath all of this sits a data question: HiFi-UMI shows that sufficiently high-fidelity robot-free data can remove the need for real-robot fine-tuning entirely, while "When Does Legacy Data Start to Help?" gives the clearest empirical account yet of exactly when cross-hardware-generation data transfer flips from useless to essential (a grokking-like threshold effect). Together they suggest the community is shifting from "collect more data" toward "collect the right fidelity/configuration of data, at the right moment in training."

A third theme is realism as a first-class simulation concern rather than an afterthought. Pictura shows that privileged vectorized self-play for driving creates a representation gap that only closes by rendering each agent's actual egocentric view during training itself — and the resulting policy then beats privileged agents zero-shot on Waymo data. SONG makes the analogous argument for social navigation, using 3D Gaussian splatting and LLM-driven pedestrian trajectories to combine visual fidelity with behavioral plausibility, and its benchmark results (safety deficits precede etiquette failures; real data beats scale) are a useful corrective for anyone assuming bigger models alone will fix vision-based social navigation. Meanwhile a distinct swarm of multi-agent papers — nano-UAV Lévy walks, shared voxel-map MASAC guidance, and curriculum-based multi-UAV RL — all push toward decentralized coordination under minimal communication and explicit safety constraints, echoed even outside physical robotics in Untangling Co-Drift's treatment of self-driving networks as coupled macro-intents.

$πR^2$: Reactive Real-time Flow Policies
2026-07-28 cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.LG Top author: Shubham Tulsiani (h=45)
Sungjae Park, Shubham Tulsiani
Core Contributions
  • Splits policy conditioning into a fast proprioceptive channel refreshed every tick and a slow, asynchronously updated vision-language channel, so the model reacts to contact and pose changes immediately without waiting on a full VLM forward pass.
  • Treats already-committed in-flight actions as inpainting constraints inside a diffusion-forcing-style flow schedule, letting the model replan and emit a new action in a single denoising step instead of the multi-step schedule typical flow policies need.
  • Requires only lightweight fine-tuning of an existing large backbone (shown on GR00T-N1.7) rather than a new architecture, replanning roughly 4× faster than the base policy (~25Hz on an A5000) with a fresh observation folded in every 40ms.
  • Improves success rate by up to 23% in simulation and 30% in the real world over the strongest baseline, evidence that closed-loop reactivity — not just bigger backbones — is a major bottleneck for current generalist policies.
Show abstract

Generalist manipulation policies increasingly take the form of action-chunking flow policies built on large pretrained backbones. Such chunks run open-loop, so the policy cannot react to sensory input arriving mid-execution, sacrificing reactivity. Replanning more often would restore it, but the perception-to-action pipeline (a large backbone plus multiple denoising steps) is too slow: this latency forbids frequent replanning and leaves committed actions stale, making such policies ill-suited for dynamic, closed-loop control. We present $πmathbf{R}^2, which makes these policies reactive and real-time while retaining large backbones, expressive multi-modal policies, and multi-action prediction. Built on the per-position noise schedule of diffusion forcing, πmathbf{R}^2 contributes two ideas. First, it splits conditioning into a fast channel (proprioception, fresh every tick) and an asynchronously updated slow channel (vision-language features), so the policy reacts to proprioception within a chunk while tolerating stale vision. Second, a latency-adaptive flow schedule treats in-flight actions as inpainting conditioning and emits actions in one denoising step per call, letting one trained model adapt to varying hardware latency. Requiring minimal modification to existing architectures, πmathbf{R}^2 can be finetuned from a pretrained policy: applied to GR00T-N1.7 on a real xArm6+XHand platform, it replans closed-loop roughly 4× faster than the base policy (~25Hz on an A5000 GPU), acting on a fresh observation every 40ms. Across simulation and real-world manipulation tasks, πmathbf{R}^2 improves the success rate by up to 23% in simulation and 30%$ in the real world over the strongest baseline. Project page: https://pi-r2-flow.github.io/

SAM3D-Guided Object-Centric Representation Alignment for Vision-Language-Action Models
2026-07-28 cs.RO, cs.AI Top author: Chinese Academy of Sciences (h=35)
Zonghe Liu, Shanyuan Jie, Xiaoquan Sun, Chen Cao, Zetian Xu
Core Contributions
  • Distills 3D object priors from a frozen SAM3D teacher into π₀'s visual features during training only, so the deployed policy keeps a pure RGB-language-to-action pipeline with no depth, point clouds, or extra 3D modules at test time.
  • Targets alignment specifically at task-relevant objects (localized via recognition models + masks) rather than the whole scene, concentrating 3D supervision exactly where occlusion, pose variation, and scale changes hurt manipulation most.
  • Reaches 99.1% on LIBERO and an average chained-task length of 4.11 on CALVIN, both simulation benchmarks that stress precise, multi-step object interaction.
  • Real-world gains concentrate in long-horizon tasks where the robot must re-attend to different target objects across subtasks, suggesting the 3D alignment specifically helps object re-identification rather than general policy quality.
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have shown strong potential for general robot manipulation, but most existing models rely on 2D visual-language backbones and lack fine-grained 3D understanding of target objects, especially under occlusion, pose variation, scale changes, and precise spatial interaction. We propose an object-centric 3D representation alignment framework built upon π_0, using SAM3D as a frozen 3D teacher to provide target-object 3D priors during training. Specifically, we localize task-relevant objects with object recognition models, generate corresponding object masks, and use SAM3D to extract dense object-level 3D representations, which are aligned with intermediate visual features of π_0. This enables the policy to internalize target-object 3D information while preserving the original RGB-language-to-action inference pipeline without requiring depth, point clouds, masks, SAM3D, or additional 3D modules at test time. Simulation experiments show consistent improvements, achieving 99.1% on LIBERO and an average length of 4.11 on CALVIN. Real-world experiments further demonstrate that our method is particularly effective in long-horizon manipulation scenarios where the robot must focus on different target objects across multiple subtasks.

A Causality-aware Infer-diagnose-refine Framework for Test-time Modality Adaptation in VLA Models
2026-07-28 cs.RO Top author: Yuwei Wu (h=20)
Haoyu Zhang, Yuwei Wu, Jin Chen, Gao Zhi, Zhenxin Diao
Core Contributions
  • Introduces a training-free, test-time refinement layer that plugs into existing VLA architectures rather than requiring retraining for better modality fusion.
  • Diagnoses vision's causal effect on action prediction by comparing factual vs. zero-padded counterfactual visual inputs, giving a dynamic 'how much should I trust vision right now' signal instead of a fixed or purely learned fusion weight.
  • Applies gated residual fusion to refine actions only where the diagnosed visual causal effect is high, implicitly recognizing phases (long-distance transit vs. close-range contact) where visual importance shifts without being told to.
  • Shows consistent gains across multiple VLA backbones in both simulation and real-world tasks, indicating the causal-diagnosis idea is backbone-agnostic rather than tied to one architecture.
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models predict sequential actions to execute tasks specified by language instructions, conditioned on visual observations and proprioceptive states. However, how to fuse modalities in VLA models remains an open problem, since robot manipulation involves dynamic phases, such as long-distance movements and close-range interactions, in which the importance of visual observations may vary over time. In this paper, we propose an infer-diagnose-refine (IDR) framework, a model-agnostic framework that can be integrated with diverse VLA architectures for refining action predictions at test time. IDR first infers actions under factual and counterfactual scenarios of visual observations, and then diagnoses the causal effects of visual observations as the estimated dynamic importance, which is finally used to refine the action predictions in a training-free manner. We further design a causality-aware action refiner to realize the IDR framework, including zero-padding interventions for inferring counterfactual actions, norm-based quantification for diagnosing causal effects, and gated residual fusion for refining actions. Extensive experiments on both simulation benchmarks and real-world tasks show improvements in overall performance across multiple VLA backbones, demonstrating the efficacy of dynamically adjusting visual importance at test time.

When Does Legacy Data Start to Help? Emergent Transfer in Cross-Configuration Robot Learning
2026-07-28 cs.RO Top author: Yingdong Hu (h=13)
Tao Wang, Hudson Hou, Yingdong Hu, Yufeng Liu, Qinghai Li
Core Contributions
  • Challenges the default assumption that more cross-hardware-generation data always helps, documenting a grokking-like transition: legacy data (different camera + gripper) stays useless until the new hardware reaches a minimum competence on its own.
  • Quantifies three distinct regimes on real manipulation tasks: no benefit at low competence (10.0% to 10.0%), a sharp jump after crossing threshold (23.3% to 86.7% on flower insertion), and diminishing returns near saturation (85.0% to 93.3% on pen insertion).
  • Grounds the empirical pattern in a theoretical account based on gradient alignment and residual policy uncertainty, translating into a practical phase-aware rule for when to collect new-hardware data vs. reuse legacy demonstrations.
  • Confirms the same three-phase pattern on an independent task (mobile dual-arm watering), suggesting the phenomenon generalizes beyond one platform or skill.
Show abstract

Robotic hardware evolves over time, but demonstration data is often tied to a specific sensor and actuator configuration. This raises a practical and underexplored question: when does legacy data begin to benefit an upgraded robot? We study this question on a wheeled humanoid platform across two hardware generations, where both the camera and gripper are changed while the overall morphology remains fixed. Contrary to the common assumption that more cross-configuration data is always helpful, we observe a grokking-like transition: legacy data remains ineffective until the upgraded configuration acquires a minimum level of task competence, after which co-training gains rise sharply before diminishing near saturation. We hypothesize that this task-dependent transition is governed by a transfer threshold and characterize the resulting three-phase pattern. Across real-robot manipulation tasks, we observe all three phases: no measurable benefit at low competence (10.0% → 10.0%), a sharp gain after crossing the threshold (23.3% → 86.7% on flower insertion), and diminishing returns at high competence (85.0% → 93.3% on pen insertion). We provide a theoretical account based on gradient alignment and residual policy uncertainty, and derive a phase-aware rule for deciding when to collect more new-hardware data and when to reuse legacy demonstrations. We further validate this three-phase pattern on a mobile dual-arm watering task, with results consistent with our predictions.

S2A2: Audio-Visual Imitation Learning for Manipulation Tasks Using Acoustic Spatial Information
2026-07-28 cs.RO Top author: Ryosuke Kojima (h=11)
Kaneyoshi Hiratsuka, Benjamin Yen, Ryosuke Kojima
Core Contributions
  • Introduces a new task family where acoustic cues, not just vision, are necessary to identify the manipulation target — a modality imitation learning has largely ignored.
  • Fuses acoustic spatial localization and spectral (timbre) information with visual features in a framework designed as a drop-in wrapper around several existing policy classes (ACT, Diffusion Policy, VQ-BeT, π₀) rather than a new architecture.
  • Simulation results show the largest gains specifically on tasks requiring joint reasoning about sound position and timbre, evidence the benefit comes from acoustic-spatial fusion rather than just adding more sensors.

Show abstract

Acoustic information provides rich cues about object location, material properties, and changes caused by contact or motion. This paper introduces a new set of acoustic-aware manipulation tasks for imitation learning, in which robots must use auditory cues to determine manipulation targets. These tasks require sound source localization and identification for active exploration in robotic manipulation. Also, we propose a multimodal imitation learning framework, Spatial-Spectral Audio Action (S2A2), that integrates visual features with acoustic spatial and acoustic signal information for the acoustic-aware manipulation tasks. We implemented S2A2 models that integrates policies such as ACT, Diffusion Policy, VQ-BeT, and π_0, into our framework. Simulation experiments showed that the proposed method is the most effective for tasks requiring both position and timbre. Furthermore, real-robot experiments confirm the applicability of the proposed tasks and framework to real-world manipulation.

Tri-Manual Visuomotor Imitation Learning of Robot Policies
2026-07-28 cs.RO Top author: Weiming Zhi (h=8)
James Zhao, Mingyuan Ba, Weiming Zhi
Core Contributions
  • Identifies a structural mismatch in tri-arm teleoperation: a human operator can only continuously control two arms, so naive pairwise mode-switching records sequential motions where the task actually wants concurrent ones — teaching the policy the interface's delays rather than the task's needs.
  • Solves this with Dependency-Aware Tri-Arm Scheduling (DATS), which retimes demonstrations offline — preserving local sensorimotor segments but repositioning when they occur under human-reviewed task-order and arm-usage constraints — rather than changing the recording hardware.
  • Trains a single synchronous policy for all three arms from the retimed data, and critically requires neither the dependency graph nor the scheduler at deployment.
  • Across six real-world tasks, DATS-retimed demonstrations produce more efficient coordination while task success stays comparable to non-retimed data; offline analysis shows DATS changes the cross-arm supervision itself, not just idle-time trimming.
Show abstract

Bimanual teleoperation provides an effective way to collect robot demonstrations, but it assumes that the operator and robot have matching numbers of simultaneous control channels. This assumption breaks for tri-manual systems: the robot can coordinate three arms concurrently, whereas a single operator can continuously control only two. Pairwise mode switching may therefore record otherwise independent motions sequentially, causing behaviour cloning to reproduce delays imposed by the interface rather than required by the task. We present TriManPolicy, a tri-manual imitation learning system that allows one operator to demonstrate behaviours for three arms. Its central component is Dependency-Aware Tri-Arm Scheduling (DATS). The key idea is to preserve the demonstrated arm motions while reconsidering when they occur. DATS retimes demonstrations offline by preserving local sensorimotor segments of fixed duration and repositioning them according to constraints on task order and arm usage that are reviewed by a human. The resulting data train a single synchronous policy for all three arms, while deployment requires neither the dependency graph nor the scheduler. Across six challenging tasks performed in the real world, policies trained on demonstrations retimed by DATS exhibit more efficient coordination while maintaining comparable observed task success. Offline analysis further shows that DATS changes the supervision across arms rather than merely removing idle periods.

Decompose and Reorganize: Planning with Primitives and Visuomotor Policies Learned from Demonstrations
2026-07-28 cs.RO Top author: Ben M. Chen (h=7)
Yizhou Chen, Hang Xu, Dongjie Yu, Yupu Lu, Tengye Xu
Core Contributions
  • Bridges TAMP and imitation learning by decomposing human demonstrations into atomic skills based on contact relationships, then expressing each as either a learned visuomotor policy or an object-centric primitive depending on which representation suits it.
  • Models each visuomotor policy's initiation, termination, and constraints in a TAMP-compatible form — what actually enables mixing and reordering skills learned from different demonstration sources, something pure end-to-end IL can't do.
  • Changes the scaling law of the data problem: demonstration data no longer needs to cover every possible skill sequence combinatorially, only the number of distinct skill types.
  • Demonstrated across diverse real and simulated multi-step tasks with unseen setups and physical constraints, showing the decomposed skills generalize to sequences not seen during data collection.
Show abstract

Successfully automating dexterous, long-horizon robotic manipulation requires frameworks capable of both high-level reasoning and fine-grained execution. Traditional task and motion planning (TAMP), while excellent at symbolic planning, is often brittle in contact-rich operations. Simultaneously, imitation learning (IL), while effective in manipulation tasks with visual feedback, is limited by its low capability in spatial generalization and multi-stage operation. To reconcile their complementary strengths and limitations, we propose DR-LfD (Decomposed and Reorganized Skills Learned from Demonstrations), a framework that seamlessly integrates visuomotor policies into a TAMP-gated decision-making system. Based on contact relationships, DR-LfD decomposes human demonstrations into atomic skills, which are reproduced as visuomotor policies or object-centric primitives. The initiation, termination, and constraints of the visuomotor policies are carefully modeled and implemented in a TAMP-compatible form, enabling reorganization of skills learned from different sources. DR-LfD transforms the learning problem from one requiring exponential demonstration data over possible skill sequences to one whose demonstration burden scales with the number of distinct skill types, with limited data for each skill. Through comprehensive real-world and simulation benchmarking across diverse scenarios, we demonstrate the strong performance of DR-LfD on tasks involving multiple steps, unseen setups, and physical constraints. Project website: https://dr-lfd.github.io/DR-LfD-website.

HiFi-UMI: Learning Deployable Manipulation Policies from High-Fidelity UMI Data Alone
2026-07-28 cs.RO, cs.CV, cs.LG Top author: Yushen Zuo (h=4)
Simple AI, :, Yuteng Wei, Jinming Ma, Jiawei Wang
Core Contributions
  • Asks whether raising the *fidelity* of robot-free UMI (hand-held gripper) data can eliminate the need for a real-robot 'anchor' fine-tuning stage, rather than the usual approach of shrinking the real-robot fraction while keeping UMI as pre-training only.
  • Co-designs the capture rig specifically for trajectory accuracy: head-mounted offline stereo-inertial SLAM, native (not reconstructed) relative hand pose, a shared microsecond GPIO trigger, and ~200° wide-angle coverage per hand, reaching 3mm workspace-local end-effector accuracy without external tracking.
  • Demonstrates zero-robot post-training across three different policy families (VLA and world-action-model backbones), with success-rate deltas of only -2.5 to +3.1 points versus teleoperation-only baselines, reaching 85% on a precision insertion task.
  • Open-sources HiFi-UMI-2K (2,000 hours, microsecond-synchronized) and shows 4,000 hours of pre-training cuts action error 41% on unseen tasks and lifts real-robot success by a further 18.1 points on one backbone.
Show abstract

Learning deployable manipulation policies is bottlenecked by the scarcity of data that is both high-fidelity and scalable. Real-robot teleoperation is accurate but costly to scale; robot-free UMI capture scales readily, and current practice uses the resulting data mainly for pre-training, adding a small real-robot "anchor" at post-training. We ask whether raising the fidelity of robot-free UMI data, rather than shrinking the real-robot fraction, can remove that anchor. We present HiFi-UMI, a portable UMI data-production system co-designed for trajectory accuracy, inter-gripper relative pose, synchronization, and field of view: head-mounted offline stereo-inertial SLAM, native rather than reconstructed relative pose, a shared microsecond GPIO trigger, and two wide-angle cameras per hand covering ~200 degrees. It reaches 3 mm workspace-local end-effector accuracy without external tracking infrastructure. Pre-training on 4,000 hours from the same corpus lowers action error on ten unseen tasks by 41% and, on StarVLA-QwenPI, raises real-robot success by a further 18.1 percentage points. We open-source HiFi-UMI-2K, 2,000 hours of microsecond-synchronized, ultra-wide-FoV demonstrations, each automatically reconstructed and validated through simulation replay, as a large-scale, high-fidelity resource for the robot-learning community.

Temporal-Distance JEPA: Plan-Aware Representation Learning for Latent World Model Predictive Control
2026-07-28 cs.CL, cs.RO Top author: Jiaxin Bai (h=14)
Jiaxin Bai, Jiaxuan Xiong
Core Contributions
  • Points out that prior JEPA planners inherit their planning cost from embedding-space Euclidean distance, a byproduct of representation learning rather than a progress signal mined from data, and instead mines a directed temporal cost from reward-free trajectories.
  • Uses same-trajectory step order as positive supervision, cross-trajectory pairs as heuristic negatives, and a rollout-consistency term matching the planner horizon, rather than relying on latent geometry alone.
  • The mined cost does double duty: deployed directly as the planning cost when progress is topological, and as a representation signal that improves Euclidean planning when contact geometry dominates instead.
  • Raises Two-Room success to 100.0% (vs. LeWM's 97.4%) and OGB-Cube by 14.2 points using the same encoder-predictor backbone as LeWM, isolating the gain to the mined cost rather than architecture changes.
Show abstract

Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) learn world models by predicting in representation space rather than reconstructing pixels, making them a natural backbone for latent model predictive control from offline demonstration logs. JEPA-style training optimizes short-horizon latent prediction, whereas planning requires a multi-step ranking of imagined futures by goal progress. Prior JEPA planners often inherit that ranking from embedding geometry, typically latent Euclidean distance, which arises as a byproduct of representation learning rather than as a progress cost mined from the logs. We propose temporal-distance JEPA (TD-JEPA), which retains the LeWM encoder--predictor backbone and mines a directed temporal cost from reward-free trajectories: same-trajectory step order supplies positive targets, cross-trajectory pairs act as heuristic negatives, and a rollout-consistency term matches the planner horizon. The mined supervision serves two roles: as the deployed planning cost when progress is topological, and as a representation signal that improves Euclidean planning when contact geometry dominates. Under locked evaluation, deploying the mined cost raises Two-Room success to 100.0% versus LeWM's 97.4%, while shared Euclidean planning on the same temporally trained checkpoint raises OGB-Cube by 14.2 points over LeWM and improves Push-T. Against LeWM and the concurrent RC-aux baseline under locked evaluation, TD-JEPA matches or exceeds both methods on every environment. Ablations show that the directed head, cross-trajectory negatives, and rollout consistency each contribute. TD-JEPA narrows the train--plan gap for JEPA world-model planners by discovering temporal progress structure in offline logs and co-designing cost form with plan-time deployment. Code is available at https://github.com/HKBU-KnowComp/TD-JEPA.

VisualPatchWorld: Code World Models as Latent Structured Representations for Planning
2026-07-28 cs.CL, cs.RO Top author: Jiaxin Bai (h=14)
Jiaxin Bai, Jiaxuan Xiong
Core Contributions
  • Represents world dynamics as inspectable, editable code rather than either a black-box neural predictor or a hand-built physics engine, aiming for the scalability of learning with the interpretability of simulators.
  • First selects a qualitative dynamical form via short active probes, then fits that form's free parameters from state-action traces — separating 'what kind of dynamics is this' from 'what are its exact parameters,' unlike end-to-end neural world models that conflate the two.
  • Exceeds the strongest prior code-based world model baseline by 23.5 points (69.0% mean planning success), with the largest gains appearing where choosing the correct qualitative form matters most.
  • Under the same planner, induced models approach ground-truth engine performance on navigation and grasp-rich control; a residual gap on contact-rich pushing is closed by checking a shortlist of plans directly in the physics engine.
Show abstract

Different research lines use the term world model in different ways, yet they share a common aim: to capture how the world evolves under action in a form that supports perception, simulation, and planning. Two prominent realizations are neural predictors that learn dynamics in continuous vector spaces, and hand-built physics engines that expose explicit state and physical laws. Neural predictors scale from data but leave the form of the dynamics implicit; physics engines are inspectable and editable but difficult to construct at scale. We introduce VisualPatchWorld (VPW), which represents world dynamics as code. VPW first selects a qualitative dynamical form with short active probes, then fits that form's free parameters from recorded state-action traces by minimizing multi-step prediction error. The resulting programs can be rolled forward like a simulator, inspected in source form, and used inside model-predictive control; image-derived scene graphs can supply the live state at replan time. Across comparisons with prior code-based world models, VPW attains 69.0% mean planning success and exceeds the strongest code baseline by 23.5 points. The largest gains arise when choosing the correct qualitative dynamics is essential. Under the same planner, the induced models approach ground-truth engine success on navigation and grasp-rich control; a residual gap remains for contact-rich pushing, and checking a shortlist of promising plans in the engine closes most of that gap. These results establish a practical route toward automatically constructed code world models that are useful for planning. Code is available at https://github.com/HKBU-KnowComp/VisualPatchWorld/.

P3: Probabilistic Policy Propagation for Stable VAE-Based Robot Learning
2026-07-28 cs.RO Top author: Yang Zhang (h=3)
Liyun Yan, Jianming Ma, Yang Zhang, Shengcheng Fu, Zhanxiang Cao
Core Contributions
  • Identifies a specific theoretical flaw in combining VAEs with PPO: an effective policy should marginalize over the full latent distribution, but standard implementations estimate the PPO probability ratio and KL divergence from a single latent sample, injecting variance and bias into the surrogate loss.
  • Fixes this with a moment-based (rather than single-sample) probabilistic method for stable optimization, paired with sampling-based calibration to keep policy behavior robust under latent uncertainty — addressing the bias/variance problem at its statistical root.
  • Boosts data efficiency from 64.6% to over 96% and cuts convergence steps by more than 20%, attributed to the loss-estimator change alone, not architecture.
  • Evaluated on humanoid parkour, a domain that stresses exactly the high-dimensional, noisy-observation regime where VAE-based policies are used and where single-sample bias would hurt most.
Show abstract

Variational Autoencoders are widely used to encode high-dimensional and noisy observations in robotics. However, their stochastic latent creates a mismatch with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO): an effective policy marginalizes over the latent distribution, whereas former implementations estimate its probability ratio and KL divergence using only one latent sample.
