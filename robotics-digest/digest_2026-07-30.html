Research Landscape

Today's batch is dominated by two intertwined threads: latent world models attacking increasingly specific structural blind spots, and a maturing view of VLA policies as necessary-but-insufficient components that need scaffolding rather than more scale. On the world-model side, four papers each target a different failure mode in the same lineage. ODEWorld reformulates prediction as continuous-time ODE integration in latent space to fix both the discrete-time bottleneck and the representation collapse long reported in latent world models, buying arbitrary temporal resolution and even backward prediction. TacWAM attacks a different gap in the same family — visual-only prediction gives no signal for force, deformation, or slip — adding a mechanics-aware tactile branch that supervises training without leaking into the action head, lifting contact-rich success by 37.5 points over the strongest baseline. QQWorld goes a level deeper still, diagnosing that LeWorldModel's existing Epps-Pulley regularizer loses its corrective gradient exactly on isolated tail samples — the rare states that matter most for planning — and replacing it with quantile-quantile matching. One Future, Every Robot extends the idea to a swarm setting with CS-JEPA, asking whether every robot in a fleet can predict the same shared future state from purely local, bandwidth-limited observations. Read together, these four papers make the same argument from different angles: a world model's value lives entirely in what its latent space is forced to represent, and each is chasing a specific blind spot — time, modality, tails, decentralization — rather than simply scaling up.

A second, related cluster treats pretrained VLA policies as necessary but insufficient, wrapping them with correction, transfer, or orchestration machinery instead of retraining end-to-end. Cross-Embodiment Transfer (BARX) shows that end-effector traces and other behavior-aligned representations, layered onto existing VLA training, boost sim-to-real cross-embodiment transfer by 28% — evidence that raw pixel-to-action mappings under-use structure that's cheap to compute. RedFlow and RoboBRIDGE attack the deployment gap from opposite directions: RedFlow mines a VLA's own failure rollouts for action-level corrective supervision (56.7% to 74.7% real-world success, at roughly a tenth of the data cost of on-policy RL), while RoboBRIDGE wraps a frozen VLA in a five-module orchestration layer — Monitor, Perceptor, Planner, Controller, Robot Interface — that catches failures before they cascade rather than trying to eliminate them from the policy itself. World Action Planner goes further still, using a VLM to search over imagined world-model rollouts at inference time rather than trusting a single forward pass. A related current runs through the contact-rich manipulation cluster — FA-RDP's frequency-adaptive diffusion sampling, FasTac's FPGA-accelerated tactile sensing, and SemAnCorr's training-free correspondence transfer — where the fix is again architectural or procedural rather than a bigger model. The throughline across both clusters: the field is increasingly converging on the idea that better robot autonomy means better scaffolding around a policy, not just a bigger policy.

A third theme, more diffuse but recurring across six papers, is safety and recoverability once systems leave the benchmark. PAC-MAN couples control-barrier functions with RL for a humanoid that has to dodge balls using only onboard, imperfect perception, and finds that barrier granularity (joint- vs. link-level) matters far more under realistic sensing than under privileged state. HALO addresses an adjacent but distinct problem for agentic systems: how to admit part of a heterogeneous LLM-agent response — some actions still safe, others gone stale — without either discarding everything or dispatching something unsafe, validated on cold-start PX4/Gazebo flight sessions. LabEvolver and the UAV-triage systems-engineering paper both push toward safer human-robot teaming in high-stakes settings — wet-lab safety-gate intercepts down 60%, and VLM-mediated UAV coordination that measurably reduces operator workload in a seven-participant study. Arm2Air's cross-embodiment skeleton transfer (robot arm to UAV relay placement) and the goal-oriented SemCom demo are smaller in scope but point the same direction: as much effort is going into making deployed systems robust, efficient, and safe as into raw task performance — a sign the field is shifting from benchmark chasing toward deployment engineering.

Cross-Embodiment Transfer via Behavior-Aligned Representations
2026-07-30 cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.CV, cs.LG Top author: Dorsa Sadigh (h=69)
Ajay Sridhar, Jensen Gao, Jonathan Yang, Jean Mercat, Suneel Belkhale
Core Contributions
  • Tests a specific hypothesis directly: representations invariant across embodiments but predictive of actions (bounding boxes, language motions, end-effector traces) can unify cross-embodiment data for transfer, rather than assuming scale alone fixes transfer.
  • Builds a dedicated simulation benchmark to isolate transfer quality from confounds of task/embodiment mix, enabling apples-to-apples comparison of representation choices.
  • Finds end-effector traces are the standout representation, and that representations pay off more as the prior dataset grows larger — a scaling interaction not previously isolated.
  • Shows these representations can extract value from action-free data, an important lever for extending beyond costly action-labeled demonstrations.
  • Improves sim-to-real cross-embodiment transfer, lifting task completion progress of real-robot policies pre-trained on simulation data by 28%.
Show abstract

Recent progress in large-scale imitation learning for robot manipulation has been driven by leveraging datasets across a wide range of robot embodiments. However, achieving significant cross-embodiment transfer is often still challenging. In this work, we study the role of using behavior-aligned representations (e.g., object bounding boxes, language motions, end-effector traces of robot motion) in vision-language-action (VLA) models to promote cross-embodiment transfer. We hypothesize that by possessing invariances across embodiments while being predictive of robot actions, these representations can help unify large-scale cross-embodiment data to enhance transfer. To assess our hypothesis, we develop a simulation-based benchmark designed to assess transfer with diverse cross-embodiment data to new embodiments. Using this benchmark, we compare different representations and ways of incorporating them. We identify that end-effector traces can be particularly beneficial for transfer, representations are generally more useful with larger prior datasets, and can be used to benefit from action-free data. We also demonstrate that they can enhance sim-to-real cross-embodiment transfer, improving task completion progress of real robot policies pre-trained on simulation data by 28%. We provide videos of our evaluations at our website: https://ajaysridhar.com/barx/.

RedFlow: Redirect Failure into Action-Level Corrections for Flow-matching VLA Policy
2026-07-30 cs.RO, cs.AI Top author: Zicong Hong (h=19)
Zhengyang Yan, Junhao Li, Fangqi Zhu, Zijun Wang, Quanxin Shou
Core Contributions
  • Targets a specific inefficiency in offline RL for flow-matching VLA policies: existing methods either discard failure rollouts entirely or use them only at the coarse trajectory level, wasting the most informative signal.
  • Introduces Context-Aware Corrective Matching to identify exactly which actions caused a failure and retrieve successful alternatives from similar contexts as fine-grained corrective targets.
  • Pairs this with an Adaptive Redirection Objective that simultaneously reinforces good actions, suppresses bad ones, and redirects recoverable failures — converting both successes and failures into dense action-level supervision.
  • Improves real-world manipulation success from 56.7% to 74.7% and matches strong on-policy methods (PPO, GRPO, DDPO) while needing roughly an order of magnitude fewer training samples.
  • Demonstrates that failure data, properly decomposed to the action level, is a highly sample-efficient supervision source for correcting deployed VLA policies.
Show abstract

Flow-matching Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies have shown strong potential for robotic manipulation but often suffer from compounding errors caused by distribution shifts during deployment. While offline reinforcement learning (RL) provides a practical way to improve deployed policies using rollout data, existing methods either ignore failure data or exploit it only at the trajectory level, resulting in low learning efficiency and persistent errors. We propose RedFlow, a fine-grained offline RL framework that redirects failure experiences into action-level corrective supervision for flow-matching VLA policies. RedFlow consists of two key components: (1) a Context-Aware Corrective Matching mechanism that identifies failure-inducing actions and retrieves successful alternatives from similar contexts as corrective targets, and (2) an Adaptive Redirection Objective that jointly reinforces successful actions, suppresses undesirable ones, and redirects recoverable failures toward corrective targets. Experiments on the LIBERO benchmark and three real-world manipulation tasks show that RedFlow consistently outperforms state-of-the-art offline RL baselines, improving the real-world success rate from 56.7% to 74.7%. It also matches strong on-policy methods (PPO, GRPO, and DDPO) while requiring roughly an order of magnitude fewer training samples.

2026-07-30 cs.RO, cs.AI Top author: Minjong Yoo (h=6)
Sihyung Yoon, Minjong Yoo, Sanghyun Ahn, Seojeong Choi, Honguk Woo
Core Contributions
  • Identifies specific deployment gaps left open by pretrained VLA models used as robotic agents: no failure recovery mechanism, inconsistent long-horizon execution, and limited robustness to shifts in observations, tasks, or embodiments.
  • Proposes a general orchestration layer (Monitor, Perceptor, Planner, Controller, Robot Interface) that composes robust agents from off-the-shelf components including pretrained VLAs, instead of retraining or building environment-specific modules for each gap.
  • The Monitor pairs rapid failure detection with hierarchical recovery to catch errors before they cascade, while the Planner triggers replanning and the Perceptor updates scene understanding asynchronously to avoid execution stalls.

Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have attracted growing interest as a scalable approach to robotic manipulation. While these models are effective action predictors, deploying them as robotic agents exposes critical gaps: no mechanism for failure recovery, inconsistent execution over long horizons, and limited robustness to shifts in observations, tasks, or embodiments. We present RoboBRIDGE, a modular framework that provides an orchestration layer over five coordinated modules, namely Monitor, Perceptor, Planner, Controller, and Robot Interface, to compose robust robotic agents from off-the-shelf components, including pretrained VLAs. The Monitor pairs rapid failure detection with hierarchical recovery to correct errors before they cascade. When the environment diverges from the current plan, the Planner triggers replanning while the Perceptor updates scene understanding asynchronously, avoiding execution stalls. Within the Controller, primitive skill fine-tuning factors manipulation into domain-invariant primitives with dedicated LoRA adapters, reducing sensitivity to domain shifts when a VLA is used. Across LIBERO, RoboCasa, and real-world case studies spanning multiple robot platforms and VLA backbones, RoboBRIDGE consistently outperforms both standalone policies and prior augmented VLA deployments.

ODEWorld: A Continuous Predictive Architecture via Physical-Time Flow
2026-07-30 cs.LG, cs.CV, cs.RO Top author: K. Sreenath (h=55)
Dongxiu Liu, Haoyi Niu, Peng Cheng, Yuan Gao, Xirui Kang
Core Contributions
  • Reframes world-model prediction as continuous-time ODE integration (PT-Flow) in a latent velocity field, rather than the standard discrete frame-stepping most world models use.
  • Directly targets the representation collapse failure mode documented in prior latent world-model work by enforcing ODE structure jointly on the representation space and the velocity field.
  • Continuous-time formulation enables arbitrary temporal resolution and even backward prediction — capabilities discrete-time models structurally cannot offer.
  • Demonstrates high-quality image reconstruction even after long-horizon prediction, evidence the collapse problem is substantially mitigated rather than just delayed.
  • Positions the resulting latent trajectories as directly useful for downstream policy learning, validated on both video generation and robotic control, not just visual fidelity.
Show abstract

In the physical world we inhabit, space and time are fundamentally continuous. However, existing machine learning paradigms for world modeling are largely confined to discrete-time prediction, thereby exhibiting significant inefficiency in capturing the dynamics of physical world. We introduce Physical-Time Flow (PT-Flow), a novel approach that learns a continuous latent velocity field operating in physical time. Crucially, the underlying dynamics of sequential data are parameterized by an ordinary differential equation (ODE) embedded in a well-structured representation space. Under this paradigm, the prediction of future can be recast as temporal integration via an ODE solver in the compressed latent space. Building upon PT-Flow, we construct ODEWorld, a continuous-time latent world model that is both efficient and versatile. By extracting time-variant features and enforcing ODE properties on both the dynamical representation space and the latent velocity field, ODEWorld effectively addresses the long-standing representation collapse issue in latent world model literature. This also enables high-quality image reconstruction even after long-horizon prediction. Moreover, its continuous nature allows for arbitrary temporal resolution and even backward prediction, which is impossible for most discrete-time models. Lastly, ODEWorld can provide rich planning-oriented information to facilitate downstream policy learning. Comprehensive experiments demonstrate that ODEWorld successfully reconciles planning-conducive dynamics abstraction with visual realism, excelling in both video generation and robotic control.

TacWAM: Anchor-Guided World Action Model with Mechanics-Aware Tactile Prediction
2026-07-30 cs.RO Top author: Wei Wu (h=5)
Lei Jin, Yiding Ma, Xin Zhang, Chen Gao, Wei Wu
Core Contributions
  • Notes that World Action Models predicting only visual futures give limited supervision for force, deformation, shear, and slip — exactly the signals contact-rich manipulation depends on.
  • Sets two explicit design constraints for adding tactile prediction: tactile futures must carry genuine physical information, and they must not become a privileged shortcut the action head can exploit instead of learning from vision and history.
  • A Spatially Aligned Fusion Tactile Encoder maps tactile appearance, dense force fields, and deformation flow into a shared latent space with bilateral force/torque reconstruction, while Anchor-Guided Tri-Modal Attention keeps future tactile prediction tokens separate from the tokens the action branch actually reads.
  • Achieves 75.0% average success across four real-world contact-rich tasks (fragile grasping, sustained surface contact, dynamic in-hand manipulation), exceeding the strongest baseline by 37.5 percentage points.
  • Staged ablations show performance consistently degrades when tactile history is removed or future-prediction access is relaxed, isolating that both pieces are doing real work, not just adding parameters.
Show abstract

World Action Models (WAMs) combine future-state prediction with robot action generation, but existing approaches largely rely on visual futures. Visual prediction captures scene structure and object motion, yet provides limited supervision for force, deformation, shear, and slip during contact-rich manipulation. We present TacWAM, a mechanics-aware tactile WAM that addresses this challenge in three steps. First, a Spatially Aligned Fusion (SAF) Tactile Encoder maps tactile appearance, dense force fields, and deformation flow into a shared latent prediction space, with bilateral force and torque reconstruction preserving global contact information. Second, a tactile history encoder provides temporal context so future tactile prediction reflects how force and deformation change beyond the current tactile observation. Third, Anchor-Guided Tri-Modal (AGT) Attention separates current visual and tactile anchors, future prediction tokens, and action tokens, allowing future tactile states to supervise training without being directly read by the action branch. We evaluate TacWAM on four real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks. TacWAM achieves an average success rate of 75.0%, exceeding the strongest evaluated baseline by 37.5 percentage points.

One Future, Every Robot: Label-Efficient Collective-State Prediction with Decentralized JEPA
2026-07-30 cs.RO Top author: Sergey Muravyov (h=2)
Alan-Barsag Gazzaev, Alexey Garvilov, Sergey Muravyov
Core Contributions
  • Poses a genuinely different question from single-robot world models: can every robot in a swarm predict the same shared future collective state using only local observations and bandwidth-limited messages, with no global pooling or target encoder?
  • Introduces CS-JEPA, a recurrent joint-embedding predictive architecture where each robot outputs one common future token field using only a 16-frame local history and one 64-float recurrent message per directed edge at deployment.
  • Evaluates frozen pretrained representations with ridge probes fit on as few as 6 labeled episodes, directly testing label efficiency rather than assuming abundant labeled swarm data.
  • A prospectively registered five-seed follow-up shows CS-JEPA improving prediction-error and inter-robot-agreement label-budget AUC across in-distribution, ring, mutual-kNN, and unseen-size topologies up to 108 robots, with every seed favoring CS-JEPA.
  • A separate sealed eight-seed follow-up shows CS-JEPA reduces branch-value MSE by 45.5% and improves candidate-score correlation by 0.1291, with gains holding at an unseen swarm size — evidence the representation transfers to planning-relevant value estimation.
Show abstract

Can every robot in a swarm predict the same future collective state from only local observations and bandwidth-limited messages? We formulate this as decentralized shared-state prediction and introduce Collective-State JEPA (CS-JEPA), a recurrent joint-embedding predictive architecture whose output at every robot represents one common future token field. At deployment, each robot uses a 16-frame local history and one 64-float recurrent message per directed edge; there is no global pooling, target encoder, episode clock, or recorded future action. After pretraining without downstream collective labels, frozen representations are evaluated with ridge probes fitted on 6, 12, or 24 globally labeled episodes. Against raw-future reconstruction with the same receiver anchor and deployment capacity but 9,607 additional training-only parameters, a prospectively registered five-seed follow-up improves prediction-error and inter-robot-agreement label-budget AUC on in-distribution, ring, mutual-kNN, and unseen-size families up to 108 robots. Every effect favors CS-JEPA in 5/5 outer seeds. In a separate sealed eight-seed follow-up, matched action-conditioned predictors receive each candidate four-step plan before producing receiver-local predictive representations. CS-JEPA reduces branch-value MSE by 45.5% and improves within-context candidate-score Pearson correlation by 0.1291, with both effects favorable in 8/8 seeds, including at unseen N=32.

QQWorld: Quantile-Quantile Matching for World Model Regularization
2026-07-30 cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.CV, cs.MM, cs.RO Top author: Zhoushun Yu (h=0)
Zhoushun Yu, Xiaoyu Hu, Xiangyu Xu
Core Contributions
  • Diagnoses a specific failure in LeWorldModel's existing Epps-Pulley regularizer: its corrective gradients rapidly vanish for isolated tail samples, leaving heavy-tailed latent deviations essentially uncorrected despite the objective appearing to regularize the whole distribution.
  • Replaces the EP objective with quantile-quantile matching that directly aligns projected latent samples with rank-matched Gaussian quantiles, keeping corrective gradients active specifically in the tails where EP fails.
  • Introduces cross-batch QQ, enlarging the effective ranking pool with detached samples from previous batches, and explicitly characterizes the resulting bias-variance tradeoff rather than treating it as a free lunch.
  • Improves average planning success rate across four control environments over the LeWM baseline, while also producing measurably better Gaussian alignment and thinner latent tails.
  • Shows the distributional fix translates into a downstream planning benefit, not just a prettier latent histogram — connecting representation-quality diagnostics directly to task performance.
Show abstract

Latent world models enable efficient planning by predicting future states in a compact representation space, but their performance depends critically on the quality of the learned latent distribution. LeWorldModel (LeWM) regularizes its latents toward an isotropic Gaussian using the Epps-Pulley (EP) objective. We show that the corrective gradients of EP rapidly vanish for isolated tail samples, leaving heavy-tailed deviations insufficiently controlled. To address this limitation, we propose QQWorld, which replaces EP with a quantile-quantile matching objective that directly aligns projected latent samples with rank-matched Gaussian quantiles, thereby maintaining effective corrective gradients in the tails. We further develop cross-batch QQ, which enlarges the effective ranking pool using detached samples from previous batches, and characterize its bias-variance trade-off. Across four control environments, QQWorld effectively improves the average planning success rate of LeWM, while consistently yielding better Gaussian alignment and thinner latent tails.

Static In, Dynamic Out: Counterfactual Action Augmentation for Moving Object Manipulation
2026-07-30 cs.RO Top author: Shreyas Kousik (h=13)
Woo Chul Shin, Zhenyang Chen, Alfred Cueva, Nadun Ranawaka Arachchige, Yingyan Celine Lin
Core Contributions
  • Addresses a real deployment gap: visuomotor policies trained only on static-object demos break when parts drift on conveyors or objects sway, a scenario most manipulation benchmarks don't test.
  • Factorizes moving-object manipulation into predicting where the object will be and reaching that predicted pose, rather than trying to learn dynamic behavior end-to-end from demonstrations that don't contain it.
  • Uses counterfactual action augmentation — displacing the object to a hypothesized future position and morphing the demonstrated action chunk to preserve hand-object relative pose — turning a static-only dataset into training signal for dynamic goals.
  • Requires no new dynamic demonstrations, only an object-pose predictor supplied at deployment time, making it a lightweight add-on to existing static-object policies.
  • Improves moving-object success across three simulated tasks under five motion patterns and two real-world tasks, without sacrificing performance on the original static-object case.
Show abstract

Visuomotor policies have advanced on manipulation tasks where the target object stays static during execution, but real deployments break this assumption: parts drift on conveyors and fruits sway in the wind. We introduce Static In, Dynamic Out (SIDO), a counterfactual action augmentation that enables a policy trained only on static object demonstrations to adapt to unseen object motion at test time. Our key idea is to factorize moving object manipulation into two sub-problems: predicting where the object will be, and reaching that predicted pose. SIDO displaces the object to a counterfactual future position and morphs the demonstrated action chunk to preserve the hand-object relative pose, yielding a goal-conditioned policy. At deployment an object pose predictor supplies the future position. Across three simulated tasks (Mug, Square, Stack) under five object motion patterns and two real-world tasks (Gantry, Peachtree), SIDO improves moving object success over the baselines while preserving static object performance.

FA-RDP: A Frequency-Adaptive Reactive Diffusion Policy for Contact-Rich Manipulation
2026-07-30 cs.RO Top author: Jun Lv (h=11)
Lifeng Zhuo, Wendi Chen, Han Xue, Shirun Tang, Jun Lv
Core Contributions
  • Identifies a structural tradeoff in diffusion policies for contact-rich manipulation: low-frequency multi-step sampling preserves pre-contact action multimodality but reacts too slowly to force feedback, while high-frequency sampling reacts fast but collapses distinct pre-contact modes.
  • Resolves this with a shared multi-frequency visual-force Transformer plus a learned multimodality indicator that dynamically switches between multi-step low-frequency sampling before contact and one-step high-frequency sampling once contact narrows the solution space.
  • Introduces Manifold Consistency Distillation to reparameterize the diffusion network onto the robot's action manifold while retaining DDPM-based residual supervision, rather than treating frequency and manifold constraints as separate problems.
  • Achieves the highest success rate among tested methods on three contact-rich tasks while being the only one shown to preserve diverse pre-contact trajectory modes.
  • Suggests adaptive inference frequency, not just better force sensing, is a missing piece for reactive contact-rich diffusion policies.
Show abstract

In contact-rich manipulation, action multimodality and reactivity dominate different stages of a single episode. Before contact, multiple trajectories might be equally valid, making it important to preserve diverse action modes. After contact, geometric constraints and force limits narrow the solution space, while successful execution demands rapid responses to force feedback. However, standard diffusion policies use a fixed inference frequency and sampling steps throughout the episode, forcing a fundamental compromise. To resolve this tradeoff, we present FA-RDP, a frequency-adaptive reactive diffusion policy. A shared multi-frequency visual-force Transformer predicts action chunks at both low and high frequencies, while a learned multimodality indicator dynamically selects multi-step low-frequency sampling before contact and one-step high-frequency sampling as action ambiguity decreases. We further introduce Manifold Consistency Distillation (MCD), which reparameterizes the diffusion network to predict actions on the robot action manifold while retaining DDPM-based residual supervision. Experiments on three contact-rich manipulation tasks show that FA-RDP achieves the highest success rate while preserving diverse pre-contact trajectory modes.

2026-07-30 cs.RO Top author: Zhouping Yin (h=11)
Xiaofan Lu, Kaiji Huang, Jiahui Chen, Yuankai Lin, Hua Yang
Core Contributions
  • Targets a combination current curved vision-based tactile sensors haven't achieved together: accurate 3D reconstruction, three-axis force estimation, and high-speed processing in a compact curved form factor.
  • Uses single-image-sensor multispectral photometric stereo for spatially aligned surface-normal estimation, avoiding the misalignment that multi-camera tactile designs introduce.
  • HyperForce applies position-aware dynamic convolution to explicitly model the spatially nonuniform mechanical response of curved elastomers, rather than assuming uniform elastomer behavior as flat-sensor force models typically do.
  • Deploys the full image-to-force pipeline on an FPGA, cutting processing latency from 3.26ms on GPU to 1.09ms — enabling genuinely high-speed contact sensing rather than offline analysis.

Show abstract

Curved tactile fingertips for dexterous manipulation must resolve fine contact geometry, distinguish normal and tangential loads, and capture transient signals. Existing curved vision-based tactile sensors struggle to combine accurate 3D reconstruction, three-axis force estimation, and high-speed processing in a compact form. This article presents FasTac, a curved vision-based tactile sensor integrating multispectral photometric stereo, dynamic-convolution force estimation, and hardware acceleration on a field-programmable gate array (FPGA). Single-image-sensor simultaneous multispectral imaging provides spatially aligned observations for robust surface normal estimation, followed by boundary-prior fast Poisson depth reconstruction. HyperForce uses position-aware dynamic convolution to model the spatially nonuniform mechanical response of curved elastomers and estimate three-axis forces. The complete image-to-normal-force pipeline is deployed on an FPGA. Unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) relay networks can restore connectivity after communication infrastructure is damaged. Urban relay placement is difficult because line-of-sight blockage, communication range, altitude, and three-dimensional obstacles must be considered jointly. Arm2Air transfers obstacle-avoidance skeletons from robot arms to UAV relay placement through cross-embodiment transfer. Source-domain robot-arm motions from a pretrained Neural MP model are converted into ordered skeletons that pretrain a transformer-based transfer platform, which is then adapted to the UAV domain using limited target data and Low-Rank Adaptation. On nine held-out high-clutter 3D urban maps, Arm2Air reduced median end-to-end planning runtime by 64.9 percent relative to the fastest conventional planner. On the high-obstruction group of a separate 30-map dense urban holdout, it increased bottleneck capacity by 32.6 percent, reduced capacity variance by 74.7 percent, reduced maximum hop distance by 13.2 percent, reduced hop-distance variance by 75.2 percent, and reduced relay displacement by 16.9 percent relative to IMPC-MD. With only three target-domain training maps, Arm2Air reduced relay-position root mean square error by 53.6 percent relative to training from scratch while updating 0.134 million parameters, compared with 1.383 million for Scratch and Full Fine-tuning.

Recent advances in Vision Language Models (VLMs) have created new opportunities for disaster response, where responders must interpret large volumes of sensor data under time pressure. Current VLM applications include social media monitoring for situational awareness, generation of draft action plans, and translation of technical alerts into public-facing messages. While these efforts can accelerate information flow, they remain largely limited to decision-support roles. This study explores the viability of embedding VLMs as coordination agents within the human-UAV loop. The proposed architecture integrates natural language interaction, mission-level task coordination, software-in-the-loop implementation, and communication aligned with the Incident Command System (ICS). The framework was developed using a Model-Based Systems Engineering (MBSE) approach, with use case and block definition diagrams representing system roles, internal structure, and component interactions. Three key elements, the VLM Coordinator Agent, UAV Mission Control, and Task Allocator, were implemented within an integrated simulation and control environment. A preliminary human-factors evaluation with seven participants showed reduced perceived workload across mental demand, effort, and frustration, along with high ratings for AI trust and communication clarity.
