Research Landscape

The largest cluster today sits inside vision-language-action and world-action modeling, but each paper attacks a different piece of the "make it practical" problem rather than chasing raw benchmark score. EmbodiedVAE and LiLa-WAM both target the representation a world-action model reasons over — EmbodiedVAE disentangles the robot arm's motion from the background in its VAE latent for a 2dB PSNR gain, while LiLa-WAM keeps its whole reasoning space compact enough to train end-to-end on a single 24GB GPU. PhyAI and CUDA MPC attack the adjacent systems problem of running these models fast: PhyAI unifies onboard, edge, and cloud inference into one runtime (1.40x-4.65x speedups over official pi0, pi0.5, GR00T N1.7, and MiniCPM-Robot implementations), while CUDA MPC shows a GPU-native MPC solver can beat tensor-framework baselines by up to 965x — a reminder that a lot of "the model is too slow" problems are really "the deployment stack is generic" problems. Continue-or-Replan (BCP) and Route2Step instead attack timing and progress-tracking: BCP decides per-chunk whether to keep executing or replan (lifting real-robot success from 74% to 92% on one task), and Route2Step separates semantic progress from local execution in VLN so a wrong-progress error stops masquerading as a wrong-action error (R2R-CE SR 48.1%→55.3%). ETA pushes furthest in this direction, explicitly rejecting the end-to-end observation-to-action paradigm for a Planner-Interface-World tool-calling loop with auditable memory — a bet that inspectability matters as much as raw capability.

A second thread is injecting structure — physics, symbolic knowledge, object graphs, geometry — so learned components have less to infer from scratch. Neurosymbolic InK pairs updatable symbolic planning with neural low-level control for sample efficiency; GORDON learns rewards from object-relation graphs instead of raw pixels specifically to avoid brittleness to background and robot-arm motion (+35 points over the best learned baseline on long-horizon tasks); Shooting for Contact embeds a differentiable simulator inside a trajectory-optimization program so retargeted motions are dynamically, not just kinematically, feasible before an RL policy ever imitates them; and GraspMeanFlow bakes SE(3) equivariance into a few-step flow so a 24x-plus speedup in sampling doesn't cost pose consistency. POMDPs for Autonomous Science Exploration makes the same trade in a different domain, branching planning on inferred physical hypotheses rather than raw high-dimensional sensor data to keep belief-space planning tractable while preserving the uncertainty quantification pure information-theoretic planners discard.

The remaining papers cluster around hardware and safety-adjacent concerns that rarely make VLA headlines but decide whether any of this reaches deployment: a Forbidden Region Active Constraint framework for ERCP-style surgery that handles breathing-induced tissue deformation at 43 Hz, a 20g vacuum-jammed haptic device for bilateral teleoperation, an active-stiffness controller for continuum robots, and PLS-Calib solving sensor calibration for ground robots that can't perform the free 6-DoF motion classical calibration assumes. The soft-wearable-robotics review's proposed Perception-Cognition-Actuation-Augmentation framework and the new Stanford robot-autonomy textbook both read as signs of a maturing field taking stock — one arguing wearables should be designed perception-first rather than actuator-first, the other packaging classical and learned autonomy into a single teachable curriculum.

EmbodiedVAE: Disentangled Video VAE for Efficient and Controllable Embodied Manipulation
2026-08-04 cs.RO Tianyu He (h=13)
Jiayi Luo, Hanxin Zhu, Chen Gao, Jiankun Wang, Cong Wang
Core Contributions
  • Identifies that video VAEs used inside embodied world models are borrowed from natural-scene generation and aren't optimized for manipulation, producing latents that are neither compact nor controllable for fine-grained robot action.
  • Uses a dual-encoder, single-decoder design with asymmetric spatio-temporal compression that automatically disentangles the robot arm's motion from the static background, rather than compressing both into one entangled latent.
  • Adds an optimal-transport-based consistency module specifically to preserve temporal coherence of the motion latent, targeting the flicker/inconsistency failure mode that hurts fine-grained control.
  • Reports an average 2dB PSNR improvement in reconstruction quality over state-of-the-art video VAEs at a higher compression rate, while enabling more precise downstream action control.
Show abstract

Latent diffusion models (LDMs) have recently significantly advanced embodied learning in constructing powerful embodied manipulation world models. However, despite the remarkable performance, existing LDMs predominantly rely on Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) optimized for natural scenes while failing to account for the unique characteristics of embodied manipulation scenarios, yielding latent representations that are neither compact nor controllable, thereby hindering efficient training of LDMs and precise robotic control. To solve this problem, we present EmbodiedVAE, a novel video VAE that provides compact yet controllable latent representations tailored for the robotic manipulation world models. Specifically, EmbodiedVAE adopts a dual-encoder, single-decoder architecture with an asymmetric spatio-temporal compression module, which automatically disentangles the robot arm's motion from background environment, resulting in overall compactness while providing explicit embodied latent to support fine-grained action control. To further preserve the temporal consistency of learned robotic motion latent, we introduce an optimal-transport-based consistency module that explicitly enforces motion fidelity and inter-frame coherence. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our proposed EmbodiedVAE achieves superior reconstruction quality with high compression rate, while enabling more precise action control in robotic manipulation scenarios with an average of 2dB PSNR improvement over state-of-the-art video VAEs.

PhyAI: Real-Time Physical AI at the Edge, Scalable Rollouts in the Cloud
2026-08-04 cs.AI, cs.RO Rongjie Yi (h=8)
Chenghua Wang, Daliang Xu, Dongqi Cai, Duojin Sun, Hao Zhang
Core Contributions
  • Targets a systems problem, not a modeling one: VLA/world-action-model inference is fragmented across separate programs for evaluation, cloud RL rollout, edge serving, and onboard deployment despite sharing the same checkpoint and action semantics.
  • Unifies these into one runtime with shared graph execution, kernels, memory management, and parallel services, while keeping model-specific conditioning/solver/cache logic isolated in adapters — added MiniCPM-Robot support the same day it was released.
  • Achieves 1.40x-4.65x speedups over official implementations of pi0, pi0.5, GR00T N1.7, and MiniCPM-Robot, and cuts Cosmos3-Nano-Policy-DROID latency from 2.46s to 1.18s on eight H20 GPUs (2.08x).
  • Introduces a "control-time Roofline" separating inference-bound from environment-bound control, showing pi0.5 on LIBERO is environment-bound while Cosmos3 stays inference-bound — a diagnostic for where optimization actually pays off.
Show abstract

Physical AI policies require inference throughout their lifecycle, including model evaluation, cloud reinforcement learning rollout, edge GPU serving, and onboard deployment. Although these settings share the same checkpoint and action semantics, they often rely on separate inference programs. To unify them, we build PhyAI, a Physical AI inference engine with a single runtime that keeps architecture-specific conditioning, solver, cache, and output logic in model adapters while sharing graph execution, kernels, memory management, and parallel services. The same codebase runs vision-language-action (VLA) models and world-action models (WAMs) on single or multiple GPUs across onboard, edge, and cloud deployments. We used the adapter interface to add MiniCPM-Robot on the day of its release. PhyAI achieves 1.40x-4.65x speedups over the official implementations of pi0, pi0.5, GR00T N1.7, and MiniCPM-Robot. On Cosmos3-Nano-Policy-DROID it reduces latency from 2.46 to 1.18 s on eight H20 GPUs (CFG=2, TP=4), a 2.08x speedup. Specialized runtimes remain faster in several configurations, so our goal is one runtime with competitive latency rather than the fastest result in every case. Detailed profiles reveal why different models need different execution policies: on a Hopper-series GPU at batch size one, the pi0.5 action expert accounts for 8.8% of FLOPs but 57.2% of latency; at batch size 32 its share drops to 13.5% and throughput reaches about 100 samples/s. Cosmos3 remains generation-dominated and gains only 14.3% throughput as batch size increases from 1 to 16. We further introduce the control-time Roofline, which distinguishes inference-bound from environment-bound control; the measured pi0.5 points on four LIBERO suites are environment-bound while Cosmos3 stays inference-bound.

LiLa-WAM: Lightweight Latent Reasoning World-Action Model for Robotic Manipulation
2026-08-04 cs.RO, cs.AI Fugui Fan (h=7)
Fan Yang, Yuting Su, Xiaobo Wang, Yuncheng You, Fugui Fan
Core Contributions
  • Targets training cost, not just inference cost: existing WAMs either burn capacity on visual detail irrelevant to control (pixel-space) or need multi-stage training to build a reasoning space (some latent-space methods).
  • Builds a compact latent reasoning space jointly shaped by future-state prediction and action generation, keeping the model lightweight while staying aligned with control, trainable end-to-end on a single 24GB GPU.
  • Introduces the Visual Transition Token, a language-free task representation encoding each task as a direction in visual feature space, sidestepping reliance on language conditioning for task specification.
  • Achieves 90.48% success across 50 RoboTwin 2.0 tasks with single-GPU training, plus validated results on LIBERO and real-robot tasks.
Show abstract

World-action modeling has emerged as a promising paradigm for robotic control, as it empowers models to go beyond reacting to observations and anticipate how a scene will evolve. However, existing WAMs often incur substantial computational overhead. Pixel-space methods often allocate substantial capacity to visual details that may not be directly relevant to control, while some latent-space methods require multi-stage training to construct the reasoning space. The resulting training cost can make such methods difficult to train under modest computational budgets. In this work, we propose LiLa-WAM, a lightweight world-action model that reasons about the future in a compact latent space and can be trained end-to-end on a single 24GB GPU. Its core design is a compact latent reasoning space jointly shaped by future-state prediction and action generation, which keeps the model lightweight while remaining well aligned with control. For task specification, we further propose the Visual Transition Token (VTT), a language-free task representation that encodes each task as a direction in visual feature space. Experiments on RoboTwin 2.0, LIBERO, and real-robot tasks demonstrate LiLa-WAM's effectiveness, achieving 90.48% success across 50 RoboTwin tasks with single-GPU training.

Continue or Replan? Bernoulli-Continuation Policy Learning for Adaptive Horizon Execution
2026-08-04 cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.CV, cs.LG Chengtang Yao (h=7)
Weichen Xu, Zhenhua Liu, Lin Luo, Yaobo Liang, Chengtang Yao
Core Contributions
  • Targets a specific inefficiency in chunk-based VLA execution: replanning happens on a fixed periodic schedule regardless of task progress, so a critical manipulation moment can get executed from a stale action chunk.
  • Reformulates execution-horizon selection as a sequence of continue-or-replan decisions with a prefix-sharing ordinal structure, rather than treating candidate horizons as independent classes — and keeps the base VLA frozen, adding only a lightweight continuation head.
  • Trains this head with RL from trajectory-level outcomes using a Replanning-Efficiency Reward that jointly rewards success and efficient VLA usage, specifically to stop the policy from collapsing to trivially short horizons.
  • Improves RoboTwin 2.0 success from 89.88% to 93.94% overall (+11.08% on the 13 hardest tasks), transfers to π0.5 (+1.7% LIBERO, +6.8% LIBERO-PRO), and lifts real-robot success from 74%→92% and 44%→84% on two tasks, with lower overall runtime than fixed-horizon baselines.
Show abstract

Existing chunk-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models execute a fixed number of actions (i.e., execution horizon) before replanning, turning replanning into a task-agnostic periodic schedule that is independent of task progress. As a result, when no replanning boundary falls before a critical manipulation stage, it is executed from a stale chunk rather than a freshly replanned one. To address this limitation, we propose Bernoulli-Continuation Policy (BCP), a lightweight, plug-and-play framework for adaptive horizon execution that keeps the base VLA frozen. Given a fixed-length action chunk, its continuation head decomposes execution-horizon selection into a sequence of continue-or-replan decisions, which imposes an ordinal, prefix-sharing inductive bias over candidate horizons rather than treating them as independent classes. Since the optimal horizon for each chunk is not observable, we train this head with reinforcement learning from trajectory-level outcomes and introduce a Replanning-Efficiency Reward that jointly rewards task success and efficient VLA usage. On RoboTwin 2.0 with LingBot-VLA as the base policy, BCP improves the average success rate by +11.08% on 13 low-success tasks and from 89.88% to 93.94% (+4.06%) across all 50 tasks. It generalizes to the Randomized setting (+4.06%) and transfers to π0.5, achieving a better result on LIBERO (+1.7%) and LIBERO-PRO (+6.8%). On a real robot, BCP lifts success from 74% to 92% and from 44% to 84% on two manipulation tasks, with negligible overhead.

ETA: A New Agentic Paradigm for Embodied Tasks
2026-08-04 cs.RO Hechang Chen (h=6)
Yitong Chen, Zezheng Huai, Sixian Li, Yubang Wang, Haozhe Zhang
Core Contributions
  • Explicitly departs from the dominant end-to-end observation-to-action paradigm, arguing generalization under that approach is capped by training-data coverage and long-horizon execution stays hard to control or inspect.
  • Structures the agent as a Planner that issues one Tool call at a time, an Interface that executes it, and a World that returns results plus a fresh observation — a closed loop that lets the agent verify outcomes and adapt mid-task.
  • Turns both successful and failed interactions into reusable experience, aiming at learning from deployment rather than only from pre-collected demonstrations.
  • Releases OpenETA with replaceable planners, composable tools/skills, auditable memory, and replayable trajectories, and shows it can run as a lightweight plugin exposing just observe/mark_point/move_to for coding agents like Codex.
Show abstract

When will robots have their ChatGPT moment? Such a breakthrough requires a general-purpose robot that can handle unfamiliar tasks in unfamiliar environments, remain controllable over long interactions, and learn from experience. Today's embodied systems largely follow an end-to-end observation-to-action path. Despite rapid progress, they remain far from this goal: their generalization depends heavily on the coverage of robot training data, while long task execution remains difficult to control and inspect. To realize this goal, we introduce the Embodied Task Agent (ETA), a new paradigm for extending digital agents into the physical world, and release OpenETA as its open-source implementation. ETA centers the robot around a Planner that chooses one Tool call at a time, an Interface that controls execution, and a World that returns the result and a fresh observation. This loop allows the agent to verify outcomes, adapt its plan, and turn successful and failed interactions into reusable experience. OpenETA provides replaceable Planners, composable Tools and Skills, auditable memory, replayable trajectories, and common interfaces for simulation and real robots. For Codex, OpenETA can operate as a lightweight plugin that exposes only observe, mark_point, and move_to.

How Should Vision-Language-Action Models Use Proprioceptive State?
2026-08-04 cs.RO Yandong Guo (h=5)
Yiren Zhao, Ziyang Chen, Ziyang Rao, Pengteng Li, He Zhang
Core Contributions
  • Points out that despite proprioceptive state being nearly universal in VLA models, it's wired in incompatible, largely unexamined ways — text-serialized, projected into the VLM prefix, or fed to the action expert — usually as a single current frame.
  • Runs matched ablations across five representative interfaces (discrete state prompt, VLM prefix, action prefix, state expert, feature modulation) on one fixed flow-matching VLA backbone, isolating interface choice from confounds like different backbones or training data.
  • Separately tests whether state history helps by sweeping history length from 1 to 96 frames, distinguishing genuine benefit from temporal variation versus benefit that's just added conditioning capacity.
  • Evaluates across 45 atomic tasks (three task families) plus 20 composite tasks, converting the results into testable design principles for where and how state should enter future state-aware VLA architectures.
Show abstract

Recent Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models almost universally take robot proprioceptive state as input, yet wire it in incompatible ways -- serialized into text prompts, projected into the vision-language prefix, or fed directly to the action expert -- and almost always as a single current frame. Three questions remain open: (1) whether, and on which tasks, current state actually improves closed-loop control; (2) how much state history helps, and whether its benefit reflects genuine temporal variation rather than added conditioning capacity; and (3) where state should enter the model -- the vision-language backbone or the action-generation module. We answer these questions through controlled experiments on a flow-matching VLA, fixing the backbone, training data, action representation, and evaluation protocol throughout. We implement five representative interfaces -- discrete state prompt, VLM prefix, action prefix, state expert, and feature modulation -- under matched implementation details, and evaluate them on 45 atomic tasks spanning three task families plus 20 composite tasks; we then sweep the state-history length from 1 to 96 frames to examine how historical state information affects model performance. The experiments yield systematic answers to all three questions, distilled into testable design principles for state-aware VLAs.

Track4Action: Distilling World-Centric 3D Tracker into Vision-Language-Action Policies
2026-08-04 cs.RO Bokai Lin (h=4)
Chenyi Wang, Xinkai Wang, Bokai Lin, Jialin Tian, Fucheng Zhang
Core Contributions
  • Notes that action labels tell a VLA policy what commands to imitate but not how those commands change the 3D world, and fills that missing supervisory signal from a frozen world-centric 3D tracker rather than adding another labeled dataset.
  • Distills a pooled tracker feature from clip transitions into learnable track queries that align with current VLA hidden states via a shared embedding space, gated feature-wise into a flow-matching action head — but drops both the clip and the tracker at deployment, adding no extra runtime cost.
  • Improves zero-shot LIBERO-Plus to 82.3%, +7.6 points over an alignment-free variant and +3.0 over LaMP, isolating the benefit to the tracker-alignment supervision rather than architecture changes.
  • Reaches 80.44%/81.48% on clean/randomized RoboTwin 2.0 splits and 67.5% average across four physical bimanual tasks (+25.0 points over the alignment-free variant), showing the privileged 3D-tracker supervision transfers from simulation to real hardware.
Show abstract

Action labels tell a vision-language-action (VLA) policy which robot commands to imitate, but not how those commands change the 3D world. The aligned demonstration clip contains this missing supervision because its K frame transitions record the geometry, motion, visibility, and camera change produced during the corresponding K actions. We introduce Track4Action, a framework that distills this realized transition from a frozen world-centric 3D tracker into a current-observation VLA policy. During training, Track4World encodes the clip into a pooled tracker feature. Learnable track queries infer this feature from current VLA hidden states, match it in a shared space, and condition a flow-matching action head through a feature-wise gate. The tracker feature only defines the alignment target, so neither the clip nor the tracker is used at deployment. Track4Action reaches 82.3% on zero-shot LIBERO-Plus, improving the alignment-free variant by 7.6 points and LaMP by 3.0 points. It obtains 80.44% and 81.48% on the clean and randomized RoboTwin 2.0 splits, and 67.5% average success across four physical bimanual tasks, 25.0 points above the alignment-free variant. The gains support action-aligned 3D tracker features as privileged supervision for tracker-free VLA deployment.

Shooting for Contact: Contact-Implicit Multiple Shooting for Dynamic Motion Retargeting
2026-08-04 cs.RO, eess.SY Vince Kurtz (h=14)
Sergio A. Esteban, Jason H. K. Siu, Derrick Mach, Junheng Li, Vince Kurtz
Core Contributions
  • Addresses a gap between motion retargeting (kinematic similarity) and what RL policies actually need to imitate (dynamically feasible, contact-consistent trajectories under real actuation limits) — most retargeting pipelines optimize the wrong objective for downstream RL.
  • Embeds a differentiable simulator directly inside a nonlinear program (contact-implicit multiple shooting) so contact, friction, impacts, self-collision, and joint limits are resolved internally, without prescribing a contact schedule or hand-designed constraints.
  • Accelerates motion-imitation RL training and produces policies with higher success and lower tracking error compared to existing retargeting methods.
  • Demonstrates zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on the Unitree G1 for genuinely contact-rich, dynamic behaviors — command-conditioned crawling and a 180-degree jump-turn — not just simple locomotion.
Show abstract

Motion retargeting approaches often prioritize kinematic similarity over whole-body dynamics, contact consistency, and actuation limits, yielding references that are difficult for reinforcement learning (RL) policies to reproduce, particularly for contact-rich behaviors. We present a contact-implicit, direct simulation-based multiple shooting (DSMS) framework that transforms kinematically feasible references into dynamically feasible whole-body trajectories. By embedding a differentiable simulator within a nonlinear program, DSMS resolves contact, friction, impacts, self-collision, and joint limits internally while enforcing tracking, actuation, and task constraints without prescribing a contact schedule or introducing explicit contact constraints. Compared with existing retargeting methods, DSMS accelerates motion-imitation RL training and yields policies with high success rates and low tracking error. We further demonstrate zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on the Unitree G1 through command-conditioned contact-rich crawling and a highly dynamic 180-degree jump-turn.

Bridging Online and Offline Handwriting via Differentiable Physical Rendering
2026-08-04 cs.CV, cs.RO Hae-Gon Jeon (h=5)
Seonmi Park, Seunghyun Shin, Vihaan Misra, Dongmin Shin, Ukcheol Shin
Core Contributions
  • Unifies two historically separate handwriting paradigms — online trajectory estimation and offline image synthesis — that each capture only half of what's needed (structure/timing vs. fine texture), rather than picking one.
  • Introduces a compact physical brush model as the missing link between stroke kinematics and pixel-level appearance, paired with a differentiable renderer so the whole pipeline is end-to-end trainable despite the gap between motion and pixel domains.
  • Solves the paired-data problem — no dataset has aligned trajectory/image pairs — by rendering images from predicted strokes rather than requiring such pairs to already exist, then refining with a zero-shot diffusion-based image refiner.
  • Validates on real-world robotic calligraphy, showing the framework transfers from digital handwriting synthesis to physical brush-stroke execution, achieving both structural and visual fidelity.
Show abstract

Realistic handwritten text generation plays an important role in numerous applications, such as font design, biometric authentication, and robotic calligraphy. Existing methods are typically divided into two independent paradigms: online approaches that estimate handwriting trajectories and offline approaches that synthesize realistic handwriting images. While online models capture structural and temporal dynamics, they often lack fine-grained textures, whereas offline models reproduce realistic appearance but discard stroke order. However, unifying online and offline models remains challenging due to the lack of an explicit physical model linking stroke kinematics to pixel-level appearance and the absence of paired trajectory-image datasets. To address these challenges, we propose a compact physical brush model that bridges stroke dynamics and visual appearance, together with a differentiable rendering module that converts stroke trajectories into stylized images. The proposed framework consists of four core modules: 1) a text-to-stroke generator that predicts the target stroke conditioned on the given text and style image, 2) a brush parameter observer that extracts brush model parameters from style references, 3) a differentiable brush renderer that maps a stroke sequence and physical brush parameters into a handwritten image, and 4) a zero-shot image refiner that refines rendered images via diffusion models. Extensive experiments and real-world robotic calligraphy demonstrations validate our approach, achieving both structural and visual fidelity.

GraspMeanFlow: SE(3)-Equivariant MeanFlow for Few-Step 6-DoF Grasp Generation
2026-08-04 cs.RO Amirhossein Mollaali (h=4)
Jiyong Kwon, Yikun Bai, Amirhossein Mollaali, Guang Lin
Core Contributions
  • Targets the inference-speed bottleneck of SE(3)-equivariant flow-based grasp generation, which needs tens of iterative integration steps per grasp — too slow for real-time manipulation despite good pose-consistency properties.
  • Learns the average velocity over a finite time interval via a time-ordered exponential so it reproduces exactly the rigid-body displacement accumulated across that interval, rather than approximating it through many small integration steps.
  • Proves equivariance is retained under few-step sampling by conditioning the field on a pair of times lifted to equivariant vectors, so the speed gain doesn't cost the pose-consistency guarantee that motivated using equivariant flows in the first place.
  • Reaches with a single function evaluation the EMD that an iterative SE(3) flow model needs five steps to approach, and a second variant of the same framework improves grasp success by up to 24.3 points in the few-step regime on ACRONYM.
Show abstract

Recent data-driven methods for synthesizing 6-DoF grasp poses use generative models to learn complex grasp pose distributions and generate diverse candidate poses. In particular, SE(3)-equivariant flow-based models generate grasp poses that transform consistently with object rotations and translations. However, these methods sample by iterative numerical integration, requiring tens of function evaluations per grasp and limiting their use in real-time manipulation. We propose GraspMeanFlow, an SE(3)-equivariant MeanFlow framework for few-step 6-DoF grasp generation. Our method learns the average velocity over a finite time interval, defined through the time-ordered exponential so that it reproduces exactly the rigid-body displacement accumulated over that interval. We prove that a point-cloud-conditioned distribution transported by an equivariant average-velocity flow map remains invariant, so equivariance is retained under few-step sampling. For stable training, we pair a flow-matching boundary term with either of two consistency terms. Experiments on ACRONYM show that a single function evaluation of GraspMeanFlow reaches the EMD that an iterative SE(3) flow model needs five steps to approach, that a second instantiation of the same framework improves grasp success by up to 24.3 points in the few-step regime, and that both generate grasp distributions transforming exactly with the object.

Accelerating Human-Aware Robot Trajectory Generation via Diffusion and Consistency Distillation
2026-08-04 cs.RO Byeong-Il Ham (h=4)
Byeong-Il Ham, Hyun-Bin Kim, Kyung-Soo Kim
Core Contributions
  • Targets non-redundant manipulators with a fully specified end-effector pose, where collision and self-collision avoidance can't be handled as simple null-space secondary tasks — a structurally harder setting than the redundant-arm case most planners assume.
  • Builds a training set of collision- and self-collision-aware trajectories via RRT/RRT*, then trains a diffusion model to generate constraint-satisfying trajectories through guided sampling, rather than solving the constrained optimization online each time.
  • Applies consistency distillation specifically to cut iterative diffusion sampling cost, plus a joint-weighted jerk regularization term in the loss to penalize abrupt joint-acceleration changes for smoother motion.
  • The distilled consistency model generates 150 trajectory candidates in under 100ms while maintaining a high episode success rate, and substantially reduces joint/end-effector jerk when regularization is applied.
Show abstract

This research proposes a constrained motion planning framework for robot manipulators in human-robot interaction (HRI). For a non-redundant manipulator with a fully specified end-effector pose, additional requirements such as collision avoidance and self-collision avoidance are difficult to handle as simple null-space secondary tasks. This limitation makes it challenging to generate feasible joint-space trajectories in HRI environments where safety and kinematic constraints must be considered simultaneously. To address this limitation, collision- and self-collision-aware trajectories are generated using Rapidly-exploring Random Tree (RRT) and RRT* algorithms, and the resulting dataset is used to train a diffusion model that generates constraint-satisfying trajectories through guided sampling. To reduce the inference time required for iterative diffusion sampling, consistency distillation is applied, and a joint-weighted jerk regularization term is incorporated into the loss function to promote smoother trajectories by penalizing abrupt changes in joint acceleration. Simulation results show that the consistency model generates 150 trajectory candidates in less than 100 ms, maintains a high episode success rate, and substantially reduces joint and end-effector jerk when jerk regularization is applied.

Sign language recognition (SLR) enhances communication between hearing and hearing-impaired individuals. Although deep learning (DL) has achieved promising performance in SLR, its high computational cost limits deployment on edge devices. To address this challenge, we propose a lightweight reservoir computing (RC)-based approach for SLR. In the proposed method, MediaPipe extracts body and hand keypoints to capture the spatial and temporal dynamics of gestures. These keypoints are then processed by a hybrid reservoir computing (HRC) architecture that combines deep reservoir computing (DRC) and bidirectional reservoir computing (BRC), transforming the input into a high-dimensional dynamic representation. A ridge regression model maps the final HRC state to class labels. This HRC-based SLR method achieved Top-1, Top-5, and Top-10 accuracies of 61.12%, 86.05%, and 92.56%, respectively, on the Word-Level American Sign Language 100 (WLASL100) video dataset, demonstrating competitive performance compared to deep learning-based approaches.
