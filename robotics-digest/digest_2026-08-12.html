Research Landscape

Today's 17 papers split cleanly into two clusters that echo each other. The first is a set of pointed questions about VLA architecture rather than new end-to-end policies: G0.5 unifies reasoning and action into one autoregressive stream instead of pairing a VLM with a separately-trained flow-matching action expert, arguing the split itself throws away instructability. RIFT asks a narrower version of the same question about world-action models — does action generation need the full iterative video rollout, or just the future representation it produces? — and finds that for two of four tested models, a single cached future suffices, motivating a rollout-free replacement that cuts latency by up to 89% with no accuracy loss. StellaVLA attacks the problem from the opposite direction, skipping retraining altogether in favor of in-context structured demonstrations at test time. And Policy-Induced Hand Priors is a useful check on all of this optimism: it shows that a dual-arm VLA's aggregate success rate can hide a strong, policy-specific bias toward using the wrong hand depending on initial pose — a reminder that architectural gains don't automatically fix representational blind spots underneath.

The second cluster is infrastructure rather than policy: four of today's papers are literally about generating simulation content or training data rather than new algorithms for acting. D3D-GEN generates domain-grounded interactive 3D worlds via retrieval-augmented generation instead of relying on fixed asset libraries; RoadWeaver generates complete lane-level HD maps from scratch rather than reconstructing them from real-world data; Video2Track converts ordinary driving video into steerable, adversarial closed-track test scenarios; and HandEdit turns the human-to-robot data gap into an image-editing benchmark spanning 26 robot URDFs and 200M+ editing instances. Read together, these suggest the field is treating data and simulation scarcity as the actual bottleneck for scaling embodied AI right now, more so than model capacity — a view reinforced by RoboSaGA's saliency-guided augmentation, which squeezes more robustness out of existing behavior-cloning data rather than collecting more of it.

A smaller but consistent thread is model-based rigor holding its ground alongside the learning-heavy majority: ContactIPM benchmarks a new interior-point solver head-to-head against two existing contact-implicit solvers with mixed, honestly-reported results rather than claiming a clean win; Scalable Multi-Agent Maze Traversal proves formal completeness and asymptotic-optimal makespan for a communication-limited multi-agent coordination protocol tested up to 625 agents; and the SMPC-to-sparse-RL loco-manipulation paper uses classical Sample-based MPC purely as an automated data generator to sidestep reward engineering, with the resulting policy reportedly surpassing its own MPC teacher. Even the driving-behavior planner in this batch pairs a learned scene interpreter with a classical constraint-checking supervisor rather than trusting a learned planner alone — a pattern that shows up across navigation, control, and perception papers today (including the maritime IoT and wind-aware UAV routing papers) whenever the deployment stakes involve safety margins under uncertainty.

IoT-Enabled Autonomous Maritime Navigation in Smart Ports: A Curriculum-Guided Shared Policy Learning Framework
2026-08-12 cs.RO, cs.LG Kum Fai Yuen (h=61)
Yuqing Lin, Rangya Zhang, Kum Fai Yuen
Core Contributions
  • Targets a specific deployment constraint: onboard IoT-edge devices in ports must decide navigation actions locally under partial observability, so training is centralized and offline while every action is executed fully onboard, rather than assuming continuous ship-to-shore links.
  • Uses a curriculum that ramps up traffic density during training instead of training directly on the hardest congested-waterway scenarios, aimed at training stability as much as final accuracy.
  • A single recurrent policy is shared across the fleet to keep memory and compute costs low enough for edge hardware while still tracking temporal state under partial observability.
  • Validated on generalization to previously unseen high-density traffic scenarios not present in the curriculum, not just interpolation within the training distribution.
Show abstract

As smart port infrastructures increasingly rely on autonomous maritime devices enabled by the Internet of Things (IoT), ensuring reliable onboard navigation intelligence has become a critical challenge for safe and scalable operations in congested waterways. This paper investigates onboard autonomous navigation for such IoT devices under partial observability and dense traffic conditions. A curriculum-guided reinforcement learning framework with a shared recurrent policy is developed to enhance temporal reasoning, deployment scalability, and robustness of edge-level decision-making. Centralized training is adopted as an offline design-time strategy, while all navigation actions are executed fully onboard, consistent with IoT edge intelligence paradigms. Extensive simulations in multiple realistic port environments demonstrate that the proposed approach improves navigation reliability, collision avoidance, and training stability compared with standard baseline methods, and generalizes effectively to previously unseen high-density scenarios. The results indicate that curriculum-guided shared learning provides a practical solution for scalable deployment of IoT-enabled autonomous maritime devices in smart port operations.

Scalable Multi-Agent Maze Traversal with Local Communication
2026-08-12 cs.RO, cs.MA Genki Miyauchi (h=3)
Julian Rau, Jahir Argote-Gerald, Grace McFassel, Genki Miyauchi, Paul Trodden
Core Contributions
  • Targets communication-limited, cyclic, unknown graph environments such as cave or pipe networks, where agents can't share global maps — coordination happens through local leader-follower relationships with leader switching, not centralized planning.
  • Only one agent explores at a time, running a single-agent maze solver while others wait or follow — a deliberate tradeoff against parallel exploration to keep the coordination protocol provably correct.
  • Provides formal guarantees: proven completeness and a makespan asymptotically matching an optimal full-knowledge strategy as agent count grows, plus derived time and space complexity, not just empirical results.
  • Scales to simulations with up to 625 agents, showing average sum-of-fuels decreasing as more agents join — more agents help rather than congest — and beating a naive independent-exploration baseline.
Show abstract

Cave networks, pipe systems, and similar maze-like environments pose significant challenges for multi-agent navigation in unknown settings with limited communication. We propose a distributed algorithm that enables agents to collectively traverse an unknown, possibly cyclic graph. Agents enter sequentially at a designated start node and are tasked to localize and reach an undisclosed goal while avoiding collisions. They coordinate via local communication using leader-follower relationships and leader switching. At any moment in time, exploration is performed by only one of the agents, which runs a single-agent maze solver. We prove that the algorithm is complete, that its makespan is asymptotically equivalent (in the number of agents) to that of an optimal full-knowledge strategy, and derive its time and space complexity. Simulations with up to $625$ agents show a decreasing average sum-of-fuels as the number of agents increases and demonstrate that the proposed approach outperforms a naïve baseline in which all agents independently execute the single-agent solver.

Energy-Aware Wind-Resilient Routing for Truck-Assisted Multi-UAV Delivery under Wind Uncertainty
2026-08-12 eess.SY, cs.RO Tianshun Li (h=0)
Tianshun Li, Yanggang Sheng, Hongliang Lu, Zhongzhen Wang, Haoang Li
Core Contributions
  • Points out that existing truck-UAV delivery routing uses static or deterministic energy models that can underestimate wind-driven risk (headwind, crosswind, battery voltage, return feasibility); EWR instead treats wind cost as online and only partially observable.
  • Represents the delivery environment as a time-dependent directed energy graph whose edge costs update from delayed, noisy wind estimates plus conservative uncertainty margins — a risk-sensitive formulation rather than expected-cost optimization.
  • Tested with synthetic delivery graphs replayed against real wind logs from a public truck-UAV dataset, grounding the routing risk model in actual wind variability rather than a synthetic distribution.
  • Reports improved mission success and fewer wind-induced return failures versus baseline routing, addressing a safety failure mode — UAVs unable to return — rather than just delivery efficiency.
Show abstract

Energy feasibility under wind uncertainty is a critical safety issue for low-altitude air-ground delivery. In truck-UAV systems, UAVs complete assigned deliveries and safely return to a mobile truck or depot, while wind-induced propulsion costs vary online and are only partially observable. Existing routing methods often rely on static or deterministic energy models, which may underestimate headwind, crosswind, battery-voltage, and return-feasibility risks. This paper proposes Energy-Aware Wind-Resilient Routing (EWR), an online risk-sensitive planning framework for wind-aware and energy-safe UAV routing. The delivery environment is represented as a time-dependent directed energy graph whose edge costs are updated using delayed noisy wind estimates, payload states, and conservative uncertainty margins. Experiments using synthetic delivery graphs with replayed wind logs from a public truck-UAV delivery dataset show that EWR improves mission success rates and reduces wind-induced return failures.

RoadWeaver: Large-Scale Lane-Level HD Map Generation from Scratch for Autonomous Driving Simulation
2026-08-12 cs.RO, cs.AI Hanyang Zhuang (h=11)
Yueyuan Li, Zexi Chen, Weijie Xi, Mingyang Jiang, Songan Zhang
Core Contributions
  • Existing map methods either reconstruct real-world maps (not scalable) or generate only local road fragments (not complete HD maps); RoadWeaver instead generates full lane-level HD maps from scratch, coarse-to-fine — global layout, then connected network, then lane geometry.
  • Explicitly optimizes topological consistency of lane connectivity, not just visual plausibility, reflected in core metrics like a 99.8% reachability rate and 10.7% dead-end ratio rather than pixel-level similarity scores.

Show abstract

Autonomous driving simulation requires diverse and scalable lane-level HD maps to support long-horizon evaluation across complex road networks. Existing approaches either rely on handcrafted or reconstructed real-world maps, which limits scalability, or generate only local road structures rather than complete HD maps. We present RoadWeaver, a coarse-to-fine framework for from-scratch generation of diverse, large-scale HD maps. RoadWeaver first synthesizes a global road layout, expands it into a connected road network, and then constructs lane-level geometry with topologically consistent lane connectivity. Experimental results show that RoadWeaver achieves a 99.8\% reachability, a 10.7\% dead-end ratio, and an endpoint alignment error of 0.24 m. Video2Track: From Real-World Interaction Videos to Steerable Adversarial Closed-Track Testing for Automated Driving Systems
2026-08-12 cs.RO Peizhi Zhang (h=9)
Mengjie Tian, Xinrui Zhang, Tianyu Li, Peizhi Zhang, Guirong Zhou
Core Contributions
  • Converts real-world driving video into closed-track adversarial test scenarios, addressing the gap where closed-track ADS testing typically relies on scripted, predefined trajectories that miss the complexity of real traffic interactions.
  • Uses a vision-language model to extract structured scenario semantics from video and grounds them via retrieval-augmented generation onto a library of real closed-track topologies, matching scenarios to physically testable venues rather than generating them in the abstract.
  • Regulates how aggressive generated adversarial agents are via a Stackelberg game with a parameterized objective, giving testers a dial on interaction intensity and risk level instead of a fixed difficulty.
  • Demonstrated to faithfully reproduce real interactive scenarios while producing controllable, executable variants — reproducible yet realistic testing, a combination scripted approaches don't offer.
Show abstract

Closed-track testing plays a fundamental role in the verification and validation of automated driving systems (ADS), particularly for safety-critical scenarios, by enabling reproducible evaluation under controlled conditions. However, most existing approaches still rely on standardized protocols or predefined trajectories, leading to overly scripted interactions and limited ability to reproduce the natural complexity of public-road traffic. To address this limitation, we propose Video2Track, a framework that transfers real-world interactive driving scenarios from videos into steerable adversarial closed-track testing. The framework consists of two tightly coupled modules. The first is a scenario semantic mapping module, which extracts structured semantics from driving videos using a vision-language model and grounds them onto a closed-track topology library via retrieval-augmented generation, thereby identifying compatible map segments and interaction anchors. The second is a dynamic interactive testing module, which conditions on the grounded topology and anchors to generate diverse multi-agent trajectories through a conditional diffusion model, while regulating interaction intensity via a Stackelberg game with a parameterized adversarial objective. Closed-track experiments demonstrate that the proposed framework can faithfully reproduce representative real-world interaction scenarios and generate executable scenario variants with controllable risk levels and interaction styles, providing a scalable approach for realistic and steerable ADS validation.

Enhancing Visual Domain Robustness in Behaviour Cloning via Saliency-Guided Augmentation
2026-08-12 cs.RO Ville Kyrki (h=39)
Zheyu Zhuang, Ruiyu Wang, Nils Ingelhag, Ville Kyrki, Danica Kragic
Core Contributions
  • Argues that both standard augmentations (random crop, color jitter) and generic superimposition-based augmentation from computer vision are risky for behavior cloning because they can corrupt task-critical spatiotemporal and agent-target relationships that classification tasks don't depend on.
  • Uses policy-driven saliency to set augmentation strength per pixel — aggressive blending in task-irrelevant background, minimal disturbance where the policy is attending — instead of one fixed strength across the whole image.
  • Drops into existing BC architectures with no new losses or structural changes, unlike methods that need auxiliary objectives to keep task semantics intact under augmentation.
  • Shown to hold in-domain performance steady while improving robustness to distractors, background changes, and lighting or shadow shifts in both simulated and real-world tests — the tradeoff other augmentation methods often fail to avoid.
Show abstract

In vision-based behavior cloning (BC), conventional image augmentations such as Random Crop and Color Jitter often fall short under substantial visual domain shifts, including changes in shadows, distractors, and backgrounds. Superimposition-based augmentations, which blend in-domain and out-of-domain images, have shown promise for improving generalization in computer vision, but their suitability for BC remains uncertain because task-critical semantics, spatiotemporal relationships, and agent-target interactions must be preserved. To address this, we introduce RoboSaGA, a Saliency-Guided Augmentation method within the superimposition family tailored for vision-based BC. RoboSaGA dynamically adjusts augmentation intensity at the pixel level using policy-driven saliency, enabling aggressive augmentation in task-irrelevant regions while preserving task-critical information. It integrates seamlessly into existing architectures without requiring structural modifications or additional learning objectives. Experiments in both simulated and real-world settings show that RoboSaGA preserves in-domain performance while substantially improving robustness to visual domain shifts, including distractor and background changes, as well as lighting and shadow variations. Code is available at https://github.com/Zheyu-Zhuang/RoboSaGA.

Learning Loco-Manipulation From SMPC Demonstrations With Sparse Offline-to-Online RL
2026-08-12 cs.RO, cs.AI Maks Sorokin (h=7)
Martin Schuck, Maks Sorokin, Simone Manni, Duy Ta, Angela P. Schoellig
Core Contributions
  • Sidesteps the manual reward-shaping bottleneck in RL for combined locomotion-and-manipulation by using Sample-based MPC purely in simulation as an automated expert that generates large offline datasets, instead of hand-tuning dense rewards.
  • Because the offline SMPC data already resolves exploration, the downstream RL agent trains with sparse task rewards only, cutting the engineering overhead standard dense-reward pipelines require.
  • The learned high-level agent is paired with a separate low-level stability controller, and the combination is reported to exceed the SMPC teacher itself — the student surpassing its own offline-generated teacher, not just matching it.

Show abstract

Integrating locomotion and manipulation is essential for robot autonomy, but scaling standard Reinforcement Learning (RL) to complex tasks is severely bottlenecked by the slow, manual process of dense reward shaping. To bypass this limitation, we leverage Sample-based Model Predictive Control (SMPC) entirely in simulation as an automated, rapidly tunable expert to generate massive offline datasets. Because this data solves the fundamental exploration problem, we can train an off-policy RL agent using purely sparse task rewards, drastically reducing the time required to learn new skills and eliminating the need for manual tuning. Integrating this high-level agent with a low-level dynamic stability controller yields more optimal behaviors that strictly align with true task objectives, ultimately allowing the learned policies to surpass the original optimal control teacher. G0.5: One Autoregressive Stream for Robot Reasoning and Action
2026-08-12 cs.RO, cs.AI Ji Cui (h=15)
Yicheng Liu, Zibin Dong, Baijun Ye, Tianyuan Yuan, Tao Jiang
Core Contributions
  • Breaks from the dominant VLA recipe of a pretrained VLM feeding a separately-trained flow-matching action expert — a single autoregressive transformer emits reasoning and action tokens under one objective, making the VLM a decision-maker rather than a context encoder.
  • Introduces a learnable cross-embodiment action tokenizer that maps heterogeneous robot action spaces into a shared vocabulary, so one weight set can pretrain across robots instead of needing per-embodiment action heads.
  • Because language and action share weights, prompts directly steer action granularity, task horizon, and out-of-distribution scene handling at inference with no further training — an instructability decoupled VLM-plus-action-expert designs don't get for free.
  • Reports gains across 7 regimes: real-robot fine-tuning (76.7% vs. 53.3% for π0.5, 24.4% for GR00T-N1.7), the 2025 BEHAVIOR Challenge (31.4% vs. 26.3%/26.1%), zero-shot DROID transfer (82.5%), and strong LIBERO/RoboTwin/SimplerEnv-Bridge scores — breadth rather than one favorable benchmark.
Show abstract

The prevailing recipe for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models couples a pretrained VLM with a separately trained flow-matching action expert. This makes the VLM a context encoder rather than a decision-maker. We introduce G0.5, a pretrained autoregressive VLA in which a single transformer decoder emits reasoning and action tokens under a single objective. Three components make this tractable at foundation-model scale: a learnable cross-embodiment action tokenizer that maps heterogeneous robot actions into a shared vocabulary; a native chain-of-thought stream interleaving task decomposition, object grounding, and action hints with action tokens; and a visual memory module that injects multi-second history through the vision encoder. Because reasoning and action share a single set of weights, the pretrained VLM's capabilities carry over to physical behavior: the model follows instructions closely, and prompts directly steer action granularity, task horizon, and out-of-distribution scene handling without further training. Pretrained on a large collection of robot datasets together with VQA samples, G0.5 surpasses state-of-the-art models across 7 independent regimes: real-world fine-tuning on R1lite and R1pro robots (76.7\% vs.\ 53.3\% for $π_{0.5}$ and 24.4\% for GR00T-N1.7), the 2025 BEHAVIOR Challenge on 50 long-horizon household mobile manipulation tasks using a generalist policy (31.4\% vs.\ 26.3\% for $π_{0.5}$ and 26.1\% for the challenge winner), DROID post-training followed by zero-shot transfer to an unseen environment and objects (82.5\%), a language-following Pick-and-Place benchmark, LIBERO (98.9\%), RoboTwin 2.0 (93.3\%), and SimplerEnv-Bridge (87.3\%).

StellaVLA: In-Context Structured Demonstration for Generalizable Vision-Language-Action Models
2026-08-12 cs.RO Daochang Liu (h=11)
Siyu Xu, Yunke Wang, Zijian Wang, Dihao Zhu, Chenghao Xia
Core Contributions
  • Reframes test-time adaptation: instead of imitating a retrieved demo's raw trajectory pixel-for-pixel, an offline pipeline converts trajectories into structured demonstrations (task plan, sub-goals, verbalized 3D motion) so the policy reasons about why a demo worked rather than copying what it did.
  • Structured demonstrations are embodiment-agnostic by construction, so a single in-context demo from a real robot, human hand, or XR capture can guide adaptation — unlike raw-trajectory retrieval, which is tied to one embodiment.
  • A dual-training design internalizes the reasoning during training via a joint action-and-language objective, but inference uses only the action expert, so there's no added latency for the reasoning capability at deployment.
  • Tops the VLA-Arena leaderboard (0.63 vs. 0.44 for π0.5 and 0.22 for LingBot-VLA) and leads LIBERO (98.8%) and LIBERO-Plus (85.1%), with real-robot experiments confirming the human/XR-demo transfer rather than simulation results alone.
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models can follow instructions and manipulate objects, but their performance often collapses out of distribution (OOD), when the scene, viewpoint, or object differs from training. Adapting to each new situation typically requires collecting more data and fine-tuning. We present StellaVLA, a framework that instead adapts at test time by conditioning on a single retrieved demonstration.
