Research Landscape

Today's 30 papers cluster most heavily around making inference-time computation, not just training-time scale, do more work. τ0-VLA turns high-level subtask selection into a compute-scalable search guided by a world model; Zetta keeps its base policy frozen entirely and instead evolves runtime critics and recovery skills online across three timescale-separated loops, reaching 90.8%/93.6% on LIBERO-Pro/RoboCasa with an 11.1× inference speedup; and BATON gets a long-horizon manipulation win (+11.6% success on RoboMemArena) with zero parameter updates, purely by restructuring exploration and memory around subtasks and their transitions. UCA-Flow and NebulaVLA push the same instinct into the low-level controller, unifying conditioning and action generation into one pass or splitting reasoning and control into asynchronous frequencies to buy 2.7–45× speedups without giving up accuracy. Together these papers suggest the VLA research agenda is shifting from "bigger backbone" toward "smarter allocation of the compute you already have," whether that's search, critique, or architectural decoupling.

A second, newer thread treats embodied and LLM-driven agents as attack surfaces in their own right rather than purely as capability targets. The same author group contributes both a broad trust-boundary-centric survey (58 attacks, 61 defenses catalogued through mid-August) and a focused paper on state-semantic injection — corrupting the scene/object state an agent uses for grounding rather than its prompt. Their quantitative finding, that attack research concentrates on perception and action interfaces while context/memory, middleware, and multi-agent trust stay comparatively unexamined, is itself a research agenda for the field: expect more papers probing those gaps. This sits alongside a quieter but related theme of formalizing what used to be hand-tuned or assumed reliable — the Embodied Perceptron reframes body design as literally a neural network, RoboStriker proves a latent self-play game is approximate-Nash equivalent to the full action-space game, and the soft-manipulator design-optimization paper derives a closed-form feasibility check that used to require building a prototype first.

A third cluster is navigation and perception papers explicitly chasing the long tail and edge cases rather than average-case benchmark scores: DPNet targets dead ends specifically (a distinct UAV failure mode from generic obstacles), the urban-navigation paper builds tooling to characterize and diagnose long-tail failure structure rather than just reporting aggregate success, and Cyclops translates LiDAR into synthetic RGB specifically to recover camera-based perception in near-dark conditions where cameras fail outright. X2Localizer's biggest gains (+4.7 R@1, +11.5 R@10) show up precisely in its hardest single-frame setting rather than the easy full-video case — a pattern repeated across several of today's papers, where the interesting result is disproportionate improvement in the conditions baselines already struggle with, not incremental gains everywhere.

τ0-VLA: a Hierarchical Robot Foundation Model with World-Model-Guided Test-Time Computation
2026-08-17 cs.RO Xinlin Ren (h=6)
Xiaowei Cai, Yunuo Cai, Bingao Chen, Jingxiao Chen, Zhi Chen
Core Contributions
  • Departs from the single-forward-pass norm in hierarchical VLA models by treating high-level subtask selection as a compute-scalable search problem: the policy can spend more test-time computation on difficult or consequential decisions rather than treating every step equally.
  • Uses a world model to guide this test-time search, evaluating candidate subtasks against imagined outcomes before committing — a mechanism largely absent from prior hierarchical VLA architectures that commit to a subtask immediately.
  • Trains on a notably large heterogeneous corpus (40,115 hours) with multimodal co-training and a low-level executor spanning multiple robot embodiments, aiming for broad transfer rather than single-robot specialization.
  • Shows extra test-time compute reliably improves next-subtask prediction accuracy and that these gains carry through to higher closed-loop success on long-horizon tasks, both in-domain and under distribution shift — evidence that LLM-style test-time scaling transfers to embodied hierarchical control.
Show abstract

Long-horizon robot manipulation requires a robot to both execute individual skills reliably and sequence them coherently over extended tasks. Most hierarchical vision-language-action (VLA) models make each such decision with a single forward pass, leaving no mechanism to allocate additional computation to difficult or consequential choices. We introduce τ0-VLA, a hierarchical robot foundation model that formulates high-level subtask generation as a compute-scalable inference problem through world-model-guided test-time computation. At each inference step, the high-level policy uses execution memory to generate a subtask and, when needed, searches over alternatives before committing to its output. A low-level policy then executes the generated subtask across multiple robot embodiments. The policy is trained on 40,115 hours of heterogeneous real-world data with multimodal co-training. Across in-domain and distribution-shifted settings, allocating additional test-time computation substantially improves next-subtask prediction accuracy, and these gains translate into higher closed-loop success on long-horizon robot manipulation tasks.

Zetta ζ: An Efficient Closed-Loop Embodied Harness for Self-Evolving Physical Intelligence
2026-08-17 cs.RO Ting Cao (h=4)
Xin Ding, Liang Mi, Mingzhe Huang, Zixuan Wang, Chao Zhang
Core Contributions
  • Targets a structural weakness of current LLM-agent robot harnesses: they only reflect after an episode ends, too slow to govern execution as physical states change. Zetta instead runs three timescale-separated loops — action-frequency governance, rollout-level critic/recovery, validation-gated skill updates — concurrently with execution.
  • Keeps the base policy frozen and evolves code-based runtime critics and recovery skills online instead — a lighter-weight alternative to retraining the policy itself, paired with Z-Infra, infrastructure that decouples agent logic from execution resources for fast rollout.
  • Reports state-of-the-art results on LIBERO-Pro and RoboCasa (90.8% and 93.6% success) with an 11.1× inference speedup, plus zero-shot skill transfer and continued improvement as self-exploration experience accumulates.
  • The reported emergence of distinct "Aha Moment"-style behavior during self-evolution is offered as qualitative evidence that closed-loop critique, not just more data, is driving the capability gains.
Show abstract

Embodied agents are increasingly used to close the gap left by end-to-end policy models. Yet the agentic path has not realized closed-loop learning in physical execution: existing harnesses remain largely open-loop, following fixed skills during rollout and reflecting only after an episode completes. Such post-hoc reflection cannot govern execution as it unfolds, because physical interaction requires decisions to track rapidly changing robot-environment states at a frequency beyond today's large agentic models. We present Zetta, a closed-loop embodied harness that evolves code-based runtime critics and recovery skills online while keeping the base policy frozen. Through three timescale-separated loops, Zetta provides action-frequency governance, rollout-level critic-recovery proposal, and validation-gated skill updates. Together with Z-Infra, a rollout infrastructure decoupling agent logic from heterogeneous execution resources, Zetta achieves state-of-the-art success on LIBERO-Pro and RoboCasa under our current rollout budget, reaching 90.8% and 93.6%, with an 11.1x inference speedup; success continues to scale with self-exploration experience; learned skills transfer zero-shot, and clear robotic "Aha Moments" emerge. These results show that closed-loop harness self-evolution opens a scaling path for reliable physical intelligence.

Unified Condition-Action Modeling for Accurate One-Step Action Generation
2026-08-17 cs.RO Boyu Ma (h=4)
Xinyu Zhou, Zikun Cai, Kuangji Zuo, Gen Li, Boyu Ma
Core Contributions
  • Identifies a specific architectural weakness in diffusion/flow action policies: they typically bolt conditioning signals onto the action-generation process as static auxiliary inputs rather than letting them co-evolve with the trajectory being denoised.
  • Fixes this by putting observation, timestep, interval, and action tokens into one shared token sequence processed by a single transformer, so condition representations get dynamically reconstructed as generation proceeds and can emphasize whatever's relevant at each denoising stage.
  • Adds a dual-pass supervision scheme over both the u and v terms of the flow objective for tighter optimization — a training-side contribution independent of the architecture change.
  • Delivers a 9.3-point average success-rate gain over the strongest baseline while being dramatically faster: 45.6× over DP3, 33.4× over Simple DP3, and still 2.3–4.3× faster than other one-step methods (FlowPolicy, MP1) — accuracy and speed improving together rather than trading off.
Show abstract

Robot manipulation requires policies that are both accurate and efficient, as robot control must respond to changing observations under tight latency constraints. Recent diffusion and flow policies are promising, but they often treat conditions as auxiliary signals rather than jointly evolving them with action trajectories. We find that this limitation can be effectively mitigated by a simple yet effective unified condition-action modeling design that represents conditions and actions in a shared token space, allowing a compact model to achieve high performance while improving both inference speed and accuracy. Therefore, we propose UCA-Flow, a unified condition-action modeling framework for accurate one-step action generation. Our method unifies observation conditions, timestep conditions, interval conditions, and action tokens into a single sequence, and processes them with a Unified Condition-Action Transformer for joint condition-action representation learning. As a result, condition representations are dynamically reconstructed according to the current generation stage, highlighting information most relevant for action refinement. Furthermore, we introduce an improved dual-pass supervision scheme over u and v for stronger optimization of unified condition-action modeling. UCA-Flow improves the average success rate by 9.3 percentage points over the strongest baseline, while achieving 45.6x and 33.4x speedups over DP3 and Simple DP3, and remaining 4.3x and 2.3x faster than one-step FlowPolicy and MP1, respectively.

HAF: Adapting Generalist VLAs to Humanoid Whole-Body Loco-manipulation via Hierarchical Action Flow and Spectral Latent RL
2026-08-17 cs.RO, cs.AI Langzhe Gu (h=3)
Langzhe Gu, Chengkai Hou, Meng Li, Xinhua Wang, Jiaming Liu
Core Contributions
  • Addresses why generalist VLA foundation models don't transfer cleanly to humanoids: single-stage architectures struggle to coordinate the high-dimensional, interdependent demands of locomotion, waist posture, and dual-arm manipulation simultaneously.
  • HAF-VLA splits action denoising into three sequential stages with cross-stage KV caches that preserve kinematic dependencies between stages — avoiding the incoherent whole-body motion that one-shot generation produces.
  • HAF-Steer applies offline-to-online RL in a compact latent noise subspace (via flow-matching invertibility and DCT-based dimensionality reduction) rather than fine-tuning the large VLA backbone directly — a cheaper, safer way to close the sim-to-real behavior-cloning gap.
  • Validated on seven real-world humanoid loco-manipulation tasks, beating vanilla single-stage VLA baselines on whole-body coordination and task performance — real hardware, not just simulation.
Show abstract

Humanoid robots hold great promise as general-purpose agents in human-centered environments, yet generalist vision-language-action (VLA) foundation models are not readily applicable to humanoid whole-body loco-manipulation. The high dimensionality and interdependence of humanoid motions make it challenging for conventional single-stage VLA architectures to coordinate locomotion, waist posture, and dual-arm manipulation effectively. Moreover, policies trained through offline behavior cloning can remain suboptimal during real-world deployment. Although online reinforcement learning can refine policies through real-world interaction, directly tuning large VLA backbones demands excessive computation and may introduce safety risks during real-robot exploration. To address these bottlenecks, we introduce HAF (Humanoid Adaptation Framework), a two-part framework consisting of HAF-VLA and HAF-Steer that transfers off-the-shelf generalist VLA foundation models to humanoid whole-body loco-manipulation. HAF-VLA is a hierarchical action-flow generator built on a pretrained flow-matching VLA. It splits full-body action denoising into three sequential stages with stage embeddings and cross-stage KV caches that retain kinematic dependencies, avoiding incoherent whole-body actions from one-shot generation. On top of the frozen HAF-VLA, HAF-Steer is a latent offline-to-online RL pipeline that leverages flow-matching invertibility and DCT-based dimensionality reduction to restrict RL optimization to a compact noise subspace and train a regularized SAC policy. This avoids updating the large VLA backbone and enables efficient real-world policy refinement. Evaluated on seven real-world humanoid loco-manipulation tasks, HAF surpasses vanilla single-stage VLA baselines and improves whole-body coordination and task performance. Project website: https://grange007.github.io/HAF .

NebulaVLA: A Dual-Frequency Vision-Language-Action Model With Guide Action for Robotic Manipulation
2026-08-17 cs.RO, cs.AI Bosch Tang (h=1)
Cong Zhao, Shuai Tian, Xu Zhang, Baocheng Ni, Xinguo Song
Core Contributions
  • Splits high-level semantic reasoning from low-level action control into asynchronous dual-frequency modules rather than running one synchronous network end-to-end, aiming to spend expensive reasoning compute less often than cheap control compute needs to run.
  • Introduces GESTURE-7, a unified language-grounded action representation meant to bridge action spaces across heterogeneous robot embodiments rather than requiring embodiment-specific action heads.
  • Uses mask-based smoothness constraints (the "Guide Action" algorithm) specifically to prevent the jerkiness that asynchronous high/low-frequency control can introduce at the boundary between reasoning updates.
  • Reports 85.5% average success on LIBERO-Plus alongside a ~2.7× speedup in action generation over synchronous baselines — evidence the decoupling buys real inference-time savings without giving up task performance.
Show abstract

Real-world deployment of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models is often bottlenecked by efficiency-performance trade-offs, cross-embodiment generalization, and execution smoothness. We present NebulaVLA, an asynchronous dual-frequency architecture that decouples high-level semantic reasoning from low-level action control, optimizing computational resources and modularity. To bridge semantic gaps across heterogeneous robots, we introduce GESTURE-7, a unified language-grounded action representation. Furthermore, our Guide Action algorithm enforces kinematic continuity via mask-based smoothness constraints. Comprehensive evaluations demonstrate that NebulaVLA significantly outperforms synchronous baselines, achieving an 85.5% average success rate on LIBERO-Plus and accelerating action generation by ~2.7x. This asynchronous design enables highly efficient and responsive control for practical robotics.

MatchingPolicy: Correspondence-Aware Policy Enables Cross-Object In-Context Learning
2026-08-17 cs.RO Qijin She (h=0)
Qijin She, Hanyang Yu, Zeming Li, Ping Tan
Core Contributions
  • Targets a specific weakness in in-context imitation learning: existing approaches conflate matching a demonstration to the current scene with learning the action itself, which hurts generalization when test-time objects differ from the demonstration.
  • Explicitly decouples the two by conditioning a diffusion policy on dense semantic correspondences produced by a separate two-stage matching algorithm built on vision foundation models, rather than letting the policy network implicitly learn correspondence.
  • Frames this separation as resolving a fundamental conflict — correspondence identification and action adaptation pull a jointly-trained network in different directions, and decoupling avoids that tension.
  • Shows superior few-shot performance on RLBench and real-world manipulation, specifically generalizing to unseen object instances and semantic categories the demonstrations never covered.
Show abstract

In-context imitation learning enables few-shot policy generalization but struggles to maintain performance on unseen objects and novel scenarios. To address this, we introduce MatchingPolicy, a correspondence-driven framework that explicitly decouples demonstration-to-scene matching from policy learning. Central to our method is a correspondence-aware diffusion policy that conditions robotic actions directly on dense semantic correspondences. This architectural separation resolves the inherent conflict between correspondence identification and action adaptation, enabling robust out-of-distribution transfer. Our framework integrates vision foundation models with a novel two-stage matching algorithm to dynamically establish reliable correspondences. Extensive evaluations on RLBench and real-world manipulation tasks confirm that MatchingPolicy achieves superior few-shot performance, generalizing reliably across unseen object instances and semantic categories.

Arm-Aware Guided Dexterous Grasp Generation with Arm-Agnostic Grasp Models
2026-08-17 cs.RO Kangchen Lv (h=8)
Yongyi Jia, Yongpeng Jiang, Kangchen Lv, Yi Ren, Mingrui Yu
Core Contributions
  • Reframes arm-aware grasping as inference-time guided diffusion rather than retraining: derives closed-form gradients for arm/environment constraints and proves gradient-based optimization is mathematically equivalent to guided diffusion sampling, letting a pretrained arm-agnostic grasp model be steered toward feasible grasps without touching its weights.
  • Sidesteps the sample-efficiency bottleneck of rejection sampling common in prior arm-aware pipelines — instead of discarding infeasible hand poses generated in isolation, it nudges near-feasible samples into the feasible region during generation itself.
  • Decouples the grasp model from the robot: the same pretrained hand-centric diffusion model generalizes across different arms and environments because arm/collision constraints are injected only at inference, not baked in via arm-specific training data.
  • Validated at scale — 10k objects across 6 scenarios — with significantly higher feasible-grasp rates in highly constrained settings (workspace boundaries, collision-prone poses, consecutive grasps) than baseline approaches.
Show abstract

Dexterous grasp generation that considers arm-related constraints is crucial in real-world scenarios involving arm environment collision avoidance, workspace boundary grasps, and consecutive grasping. Existing hand-centric grasp models, which primarily focus on the floating hand's pose, are insufficient for such cases. Conventional arm-aware methods either rely on rejection sampling to discard infeasible samples or require retraining on arm-specific data, leading to low sample efficiency under adverse conditions or limited generalization across different robots and environments. To overcome these limitations, this letter presents an arm-aware dexterous grasp generation framework that leverages pretrained arm-agnostic grasp models while integrating arm and environmental information only at inference time. Specifically, we formulate arm-aware constrained grasp generation as a joint optimization of hand pose and arm configuration, and derive closed-form gradients for arm-related constraints. Assuming the hand pose distribution is represented by a diffusion model, we prove that gradient-based optimization is equivalent to guided diffusion sampling, steering near-feasible samples toward the feasible region. Through comprehensive evaluation involving 10k objects across 6 scenarios, we demonstrate that the proposed framework generates feasible grasps in highly constrained settings with significantly higher probability, highlighting its advantages in real-world applications. Supplementary materials and appendix are available at https://arm-aware-dexgrasp.github.io/.

ViHaTeleop: A Low-Cost, Lightweight Visual-Haptic Teleoperation System for Dexterous Manipulation Learning
2026-08-17 cs.RO Fucai Zhu (h=1)
Fucai Zhu, Yanhou Lai, Paul Maestre, Koichi Hashimoto
Core Contributions
  • Targets the demonstration-collection bottleneck for contact-critical dexterous tasks: cheap teleoperation rigs typically give no haptic feedback, making it hard for operators to judge contact quality during data collection.
  • Delivers haptic feedback in a genuinely low-cost, lightweight package (0.7 kg, $550) using SLAM-based wrist tracking, camera-based hand tracking, and finger-wise vibrotactile actuators — a fraction of the cost of typical force-feedback teleop hardware.
  • Includes a simulated tactile proxy (depth-camera based) in Isaac Sim, so the same pipeline can generate demonstrations in simulation as well as reality and train visual-tactile policies from either source.
  • A user study (n=9, six contact-critical tasks) shows haptic feedback improves success rate on every task (+2.2 to +15.6 points), and downstream policies trained with tactile cues gain +17 points on peg-in-hole over vision-only — concrete evidence that better teleop hardware translates into better learned policies, not just a better teleop experience.
Show abstract

Learning from demonstration is a promising approach for dexterous manipulation, but collecting high-quality contact-critical demonstrations remains difficult with low-cost teleoperation hardware. We present ViHaTeleop, a lightweight (0.7 kg), low-cost ($550) visual-haptic teleoperation system with SLAM-based wrist tracking, camera-based hand tracking, and finger-wise vibrotactile feedback through Linear Resonant Actuators (LRA). The system includes several design choices (LED illumination, fisheye hand camera, and tactile-aware retargeting constraints) and is deployed on Franka + LEAP Hand + 9DTact in both real and simulated environments. Under matched with/without-haptic conditions with nine participants across six contact-critical tasks, haptics improved success rates across all tasks (+2.2 to +15.6 percentage points), while completion-time effects were task-dependent. Subjective ratings showed significant gains in contact clarity and grasp confidence in both simulation and real-world settings (Wilcoxon signed-rank, p<0.05). We also integrate a lightweight depth-camera-based tactile proxy in Isaac Sim, enabling a full pipeline from multi-modal demonstration collection to visual-tactile policy training. Preliminary downstream validation by training visual-tactile policies from collected demonstrations shows tactile cues benefit contact-critical subtasks (peg-in-hole: +17 percentage points over vision-only).

Don't Drop the BATON: Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation via Agentic Subtask Exploration and Transition-aware Memory
2026-08-17 cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.CV Bingxin Xu (h=0)
Bingxin Xu, Yuzhang Shang, Emilio Ferrara
Core Contributions
  • Diagnoses two specific failure modes in the "frozen VLA + LLM planner" recipe for long-horizon manipulation: whole-task exploration cost scales multiplicatively with the number of stages (T^K), and there's no representation of what state a subtask hands off to the next one.
  • Fixes the cost problem by making the subtask, not the whole task, the unit of exploration — each subtask is explored cheaply in isolation and its solution stored in memory, turning cost from multiplicative (T^K) to additive (T×K) and letting failures be attributed to a specific stage.
  • Fixes the handoff problem with transition-aware memory: a verifier agent gates VLA invocation until the wrist camera confirms readiness, a handoff transition restores entry state disturbed by the prior subtask, and a lookahead transition picks strategies whose outcomes the next subtask can actually use.
  • All of this happens with no parameter updates — pure inference-time orchestration — yet improves task success by 11.6% and cumulative success by 14.9% over the prior state of the art on the RoboMemArena long-horizon benchmark.
Show abstract

Long-horizon robot manipulation chains many contact-rich skills into one multi-stage task. Vision-language-action (VLA) models increasingly master the individual skills, yet the chain still fails: errors compound beyond the policy's ability to correct, and one subtask silently constrains the next. A promising recipe freezes the VLA and puts an LLM agent in charge: it plans in language, moves in free space with analytic primitives, invokes the VLA only for contact-rich segments, and writes adaptation into language memory. Applied to long horizons, it breaks twice. (1) Competence comes from whole-task exploration at test time, whose cost is multiplicative in stages: if one stage needs T episodes, a K-stage task needs about T^K, and a failure does not reveal which stage caused it. (2) It has no representation of transitions: the VLA primitive carries an exit but no entry condition, so a subtask can succeed in a form its successor cannot use. We present BATON. Against (1), BATON makes the subtask the unit of exploration: each is explored in the cheap short-horizon regime and its solution stored in memory; a long-horizon trajectory is then composed from these solutions rather than discovered whole. Cost becomes additive (T*K) and every failure is attributed to a single stage. Against (2), BATON equips exploration with a transition-aware memory. Within a subtask, a verifier agent governs the invocation transition: the VLA is called only after the wrist view confirms the scene is ready. Across subtasks, a handoff transition restores an entry state disturbed by the predecessor's residue, and a lookahead transition selects the strategy whose outcome the successor can inherit. No parameters are updated. On the long-horizon benchmark RoboMemArena, BATON improves task success by 11.6% and cumulative success by 14.9% over the SoTA.

Neurosymbolic Embodied Agents
2026-08-17 cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.CL Mohammad Albinhassan (h=0)
Mohammad Albinhassan, Yuming Feng, Alessandra Russo, Pranava Madhyastha
Core Contributions
  • Targets a core reliability gap in LLM/VLM-based planning: outputs are plausible but not guaranteed executable, since nothing stops them from violating environment dynamics or acting on wrongly grounded objects.
  • Splits the problem into two phases — vision-language exploration that acquires grounded predicates and instance bindings into a symbolic initial state, then PDDL-constrained decoding plus Monte Carlo tree search that only ever considers actions valid under a transition model — making plans executable by construction rather than by post-hoc filtering.
  • On VirtualHome and ALFWorld, models as small as 4B parameters exceed 90% success, and the smallest configuration substantially beats a 27B direct visual policy — suggesting the constraint-and-search structure buys more than raw model scale does here.
  • Ablation shows the two ingredients are complementary, not redundant: on ALFWorld, constraints alone or search alone each solve under a third of tasks, but combined they solve over 95% — plus large efficiency gains (far fewer tokens than extended thinking, far fewer images than direct interaction).
Show abstract

Language and vision-language models generate plausible embodied plans but do not guarantee executability, as their outputs can violate environment dynamics or act on incorrectly grounded entities. We present a neurosymbolic agent that factors long-horizon household tasks into task-directed visual exploration and constrained symbolic planning. In the first phase, a vision-language model and exploration harness acquire goal-relevant predicates and instance bindings from egocentric observations and grounded interactions, producing a symbolic initial state. In the second, a PDDL transition model restricts decoding to tokens that extend applicable actions. Monte Carlo tree search then evaluates executable continuations using a domain-independent planning heuristic. The resulting plans are executable by construction under the transition model, with transfer to the environment conditioned on correct visual grounding. On VirtualHome and ALFWorld, open 4B-27B models exceed 90% success in both environments, and our smallest agent substantially outperforms a 27B direct visual policy in each. Constraints and search prove complementary rather than interchangeable: in ALFWorld either alone solves under a third of tasks, whereas their combination solves over 95%. The method also uses several times fewer generated tokens than extended thinking and far fewer model-visible images than direct interaction, and residual failures localize to state acquisition rather than plan generation without any specialized training.

Semantic- and Density-Aware Planning for Accessibility-Preserving Multi-Object Placement
2026-08-17 cs.RO Benno Wingender (h=0)
Benno Wingender, Nils Dengler, Nicolas Busch, Sicong Pan, Maren Bennewitz
Core Contributions
  • Addresses a gap in shelf-stocking/placement planning: prior work optimizes semantic organization or space density or reachability in isolation, but a robot placing groceries online (future arrivals unknown) needs all three simultaneously to avoid painting itself into a corner.
  • Scores candidate poses with a combined semantic-density metric (inter-object similarity plus spatial proximity) rather than optimizing pure density or pure semantic grouping separately.
  • The Accessibility Map explicitly penalizes placements that would reduce future reachable workspace, addressing the failure mode where a dense, semantically tidy placement early on blocks manipulator access for later items.
  • Achieves both the best semantic placement quality and the highest average shelf density versus baselines in simulation, with the Accessibility Map substantially cutting the time to find feasible poses; a qualitative real-world demo confirms the pipeline runs outside simulation.
Show abstract

Long-term manipulation planning requires robots to reason not only about immediate task success but also about how current decisions affect future interactions with the environment. In this context, household service robots may need to organize groceries in partially occupied shelves while using limited storage space efficiently and preserving access for subsequent placements. In this paper, we consider an online multi-object shelf-placement setting in which future objects arrivals are unknown. Existing approaches do not jointly address semantic organization, dense space utilization, and manipulator accessibility during sequential shelf filling.
