Research Landscape

Today's batch is dominated by a single quiet argument playing out across a third of the papers: scaling data is not the only, or even the best, lever for generalization in robot learning. Physics Filtering Favors the Generalization of Robot Learning states this most directly, showing a lightweight physics-filtered feedback module lets quadrupeds, drones, and aerial manipulators generalize under dynamics uncertainty with limited training data. The seven VLA papers in the VLA & Foundation Models group make the same bet from different angles rather than pushing more demonstrations through bigger models: Act with Intent (INDI) distills behavior-level intent from a teacher VLM instead of only cloning motor commands (lifting GR00T-N1.7 from 64.3% to 84.7% on SimplerEnv-Bridge); UniMem unifies memory and control under one backbone rather than bolting on a second VLM; and Pointing-VLA and TOWN-VLA both attack brittle prompt/coordinate interfaces — the latter's discovery that meaningless text appends collapse success from 92.47% to 3.00% just as badly as meaningful ones is a striking demonstration that VLA failure often lives in interface design, not semantics.

A second thread is the move from raw pixel or text signals toward structured, geometry- or certification-aware intermediate representations. GeoWAM makes this case explicitly for driving world models, arguing point-cloud geometry is a more natural state space than pixels because it aligns directly with the space where actions execute; Pointing-VLA makes the analogous argument for manipulation, replacing text-serialized coordinates with typed geometric heads; and CSymPlan pushes structure furthest of all, offering formally certified reach-avoid policies for high-DOF manipulators with zero safety violations in both simulation and real Franka FR3 trials. This connects to a cluster of papers solving reliability under partial observability without dedicated sensing: RAFT adapts to thruster faults using a privileged critic that never needs a fault sensor at deployment, the Reward-Free Continual Adaptation paper adapts space robots to hardware degradation without ever observing a reward, and RACO treats UAV navigation's coarse goal as a correctable runtime hypothesis rather than ground truth — three different domains converging on the same design pattern of training-time privilege or structure that's discarded or hidden at deployment.

Elsewhere, the batch reveals a field increasingly willing to challenge its own assumptions: the Shaping the Evolutionary Dynamics of Robot Morphology paper argues the well-known morphological Baldwin effect is largely an artifact of premature fitness evaluation rather than genuine evolutionary tendency, and the Spinning Quadrotor paper inverts the standard assumption that yaw must always be suppressed in hover, turning it into a source of aerodynamic lift instead (22% thrust reduction in preliminary tests). Meanwhile RoboRacer Arena and MIVIFI both tackle the perennial data/asset bottleneck in simulation and generative modeling — one by generating racing environments directly from occupancy maps and natural language, the other by bridging fisheye and perspective camera domains to compensate for scarce fisheye training data — underscoring that infrastructure for scale, not just algorithms, remains a first-class research problem in robotics.

ROS2SmolVLA: Enabling Small Vision-Language-Action Models for Integration into Industrial-Grade Lightweight Robots

2026-08-24cs.ROL. Mikelsons (h=12)
Nils Mandischer, Noah Böckmann, Ludwig Holl, Lars Mikelsons
Core Contributions
  • Targets the deployment gap left open by big VLA models: rather than scaling further, adapts Hugging Face's SmolVLA to run entirely on-premise on a Universal Robots UR10e, sidestepping the compliance/security concerns of cloud-hosted large VLAs in factory settings.
  • Ships as an open-source ROS 2 bridge (ROS2SmolVLA) rather than a new model architecture — the contribution is integration engineering that makes a small VLA usable on industrial-grade hardware instead of lab-only arms.
  • Validated on a real pick-and-place task on production-grade hardware, a step most VLA papers skip in favor of simulation or lab manipulators.
  • Positions small, locally-computable VLAs as the practical answer for short-batch, high-mix manufacturing lines where large cloud models are a non-starter.
Show abstract

Industrial demand changes the paradigms of production. Due to smaller batch sizes and more variations in products, companies face a growing challenge to adopt more adaptive production systems. In particular, robot-based automation is usually static and fails to respond to constantly changing processes. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models are a promising opportunity to mitigate this challenge by generating robot actions based on the observed system state. However, current research either focuses on large models that cannot be computed on premise, creating compliance and security challenges, or use lab-grade robot hardware that obscures exploitation in real industrial settings. In this work, we adapt Hugging Face's SmolVLA for Universal Robots lightweight robots. Further, we release the open-source repository ROS2SmolVLA that implements an interface for ROS 2 to SmolVLA, and makes it applicable for industrial-grade hardware. By this, we allow a lenient adoption into lab and industrial environments. We validate the functionality of SmolVLA for a Universal Robots UR10e using a pick-and-place task and give implementation guidelines. Our findings support that SmolVLA is a well-suited option for small-sized tasks that need to be computed on premise.

Triplet2Track: A Hierarchical System with Object-Centric Representations for Reliable Long-Horizon Manipulation

2026-08-24cs.RO cs.AIChuan Wen (h=5)
Jianxiang Liu, Gaojing Zhang, Chuan Wen, Qipeng Liu, Yuxuan Zhao
Core Contributions
  • Splits the difference between end-to-end VLA and classical hierarchical planning: represents subgoals as instance-grounded triplets that get translated into continuous track priors, keeping the interpretability of a pipeline while staying closed-loop.
  • Learns from human videos rather than robot teleop data, cutting the dependency on expensive robot-collected demonstrations that plague end-to-end VLA training.
  • Adds online progress monitoring from observations to trigger replanning — directly targeting the open-loop hallucination failure mode that hierarchical pipelines are typically criticized for.
  • Reports 74.8% average success on diverse long-horizon real-world tasks with both object-level and compositional generalization, a regime where flat VLA policies typically degrade sharply.
Show abstract

Ensuring reliability in uncertain environments remains difficult for long-horizon robotic manipulation. End-to-end VLA models are data-heavy and opaque, making diagnosis and verification difficult. Hierarchical pipelines are more interpretable, but their plans are often weakly grounded in observations, weakly aligned with low-level actions, and computed without online feedback, leading to open-loop behavior and hallucinations. To address these issues, we introduce the Triplet-to-Track System (TTS), a closed-loop long-horizon imitation learning system that uses human videos to reduce reliance on robot-collected data. TTS represents high-level subgoals as instance-grounded triplets, translates them into continuous track priors for execution, and monitors task progress from observations for online replanning. Across diverse real-world long-horizon tasks, TTS achieves a 74.8% average success rate and supports object-level and compositional generalization.

Act with Intent: Distilling Behavior Intent for Vision-Language-Action Models

2026-08-24cs.RO cs.AI cs.CVWook-Shin Han (h=3)
Sangoh Lee, Sangwoo Mo, Wook-Shin Han
Core Contributions
  • Reframes what action decoders should learn from: instead of only cloning the demonstrated motor command, distills the intent behind a behavior segment from a frozen teacher VLM watching the execution video — closer to 'why' than 'what.'
  • Differs from prior future-prediction supervision (frames, trajectories, motion) by targeting the shared semantic objective rather than one particular realization of what might happen next, making the signal less brittle to nuisance variation.
  • Lifts GR00T-N1.7 from 64.3% to 84.7% on SimplerEnv-Bridge and from 64.1% to 70.3% on RoboCasa Kitchen, with consistent gains transferring to π0.5 on both benchmarks — a large jump for what is essentially an auxiliary training signal.
  • On real hardware, improves average success from 62.0% to 68.7%, with the biggest gains (up to 12 points) concentrated on the longest-horizon tasks, suggesting intent modeling pays off most exactly where behavior cloning struggles most.
Show abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models can turn multimodal context into robot actions, but their action decoders are still trained largely by behavior cloning. This supervises which motor command was demonstrated while leaving implicit the local objective served by the behavior under the instruction. Future-based supervision enriches action learning with frames, latent observations, trajectories, or motion representations, but these signals capture particular realizations of what may happen rather than the shared semantic objective of the forthcoming behavior. We propose Intention Distillation (INDI), which distills behavior-level intent into the action decoder. During training, a frozen teacher VLM interprets a demonstrated segment from the current observation, instruction, coarse action summary, and corresponding execution video. From its standard inputs, the deployed VLA recovers the resulting multimodal intent representation at an intermediate decoder layer and uses it to organize action prediction together with representations of how the behavior unfolds and what it achieves. On SimplerEnv-Bridge, INDI improves GR00T-N1.7 from 64.3% to 84.7%, and on RoboCasa Kitchen it improves the controlled GR00T-N1.7 baseline from 64.1% to 70.3%, with consistent gains on pi-0.5 across both benchmarks. In real-world tasks, INDI improves average success from 62.0% to 68.7%, with gains of up to 12.0 pp on longer-horizon tasks. Further analyses show that the recovered latent is used by the decoder, captures behavior objective and execution progress, and organizes downstream predictions in an objective-dependent manner. These results show that action decoders benefit from explicitly modeling the semantic objective of the behavior they generate.

InstructMove: A Text-Indispensable Benchmark for Instruction-Following Manipulation

2026-08-24cs.ROZhizhong Su (h=3)
Mengao Zhao, Ziang Li, Chaodong Huang, Mengchen Ma, Haoyi Jiang
Core Contributions
  • Diagnoses a hidden flaw in existing manipulation benchmarks: because the correct object/destination is often visually obvious or the only feasible choice, policies can score well while ignoring the language instruction entirely.
  • Introduces a 'text-indispensable' design principle — scenes are built with semantic distractors so multiple actions are physically plausible but only one matches the instruction, forcing genuine language grounding to be measured rather than assumed.
  • Decomposes instruction-following into category identification, attribute discrimination, spatial reasoning, and compositional pick-and-place, giving a diagnostic breakdown rather than a single pass/fail number.
  • Shows that training on InstructMove's simulation data transfers to improve real-world instruction-following, indicating the benchmark also functions as a data source, not just an eval.
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have made general-purpose robot manipulation increasingly plausible by conditioning robot actions on natural-language instructions. A key test of such generality is whether policies actually follow language instructions. Yet many manipulation benchmarks leave this ability underdetermined: the intended object or destination is often visually salient or uniquely feasible, allowing policies to succeed without grounding the instruction. We argue that instruction-following evaluation should be text-indispensable: multiple actions should be visually and physically plausible, while only one should be consistent with the language instruction. We introduce InstructMove, a text-indispensable benchmark for instruction-following manipulation. InstructMove instantiates this principle in pick-and-place scenes with semantic distractors, decomposing instruction following into category identification, attribute discrimination, spatial reasoning, and compositional pick-and-place. InstructMove supports a train-eval protocol with InstructMove training data and held-out evaluation tasks, with additional diagnostics for language dependence. Experiments with representative VLA policies show that InstructMove provides a controlled testbed for diagnosing visual shortcuts and that InstructMove simulation data can improve real-world instruction-following manipulation performance. Code: https://github.com/HorizonRobotics/RoboOrchardSim

UniMem: Unifying Multimodal Memory and Control for Vision-Language-Action Models

2026-08-24cs.RO cs.CVLars W. Osterberg (h=2)
Lars Osterberg, Maggie Wang, Mac Schwager
Core Contributions
  • Attacks VLA memory with a single backbone instead of the common pattern of bolting a separate long-term-memory VLM onto the policy, removing the memory bottleneck and the fractured two-model training pipeline that approach creates.
  • Uses an event classifier to decide when to update memory and a keyframe encoder/caching scheme to keep dense spatial memory affordable at rollout time, rather than conditioning on frames sampled at arbitrary fixed intervals.
  • Outperforms fixed-interval frame sampling baselines by a wide margin (93.4% vs. 68.2% in simulation) and hierarchical memory baselines by an even larger one (80.0% vs. 43.5% on hardware), while running faster at inference.
  • Evaluated across nine tasks spanning both simulation and four hardware setups targeting sequential and spatial memory specifically, rather than folding memory evaluation into generic long-horizon benchmarks.
Show abstract

While Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have leveraged internet-scale pretraining and task-focused finetuning to achieve strong performance on long-horizon tasks, they often struggle with non-Markovian tasks that require memory. Existing approaches to memory typically involve additional Vision-Language-Models (VLMs) for long-term memory management, introducing a memory bottleneck and a fractured training pipeline. Conditioning on multiple historical frames can provide the VLA with access to more descriptive features of past scenes, but can degrade performance if frames are chosen at arbitrary, fixed intervals. To address these limitations, we present UniMem, a framework that unifies high-level, multimodal memory and low-level control under one backbone. UniMem employs an event classifier for memory updates, a keyframe encoder for dense spatial memory, and a keyframe caching technique to minimize overhead during policy rollouts. We evaluate UniMem across five simulation and four hardware tasks targeting sequential and spatial memory, demonstrating that our unified, single-model system outperforms fixed-interval image sampling baselines (93.4% vs. 68.2%) in simulation and hierarchical baselines (80.0% vs. 43.5%) in hardware, while offering faster inference and a simple training pipeline for easy adoption. Project website: https://losterberg3.github.io/unimem-vla/

Think Only When Needed: Prompt-Authority Control for Selective Slow-Path Intervention in Vision-Language-Action Manipulation

2026-08-24cs.RO cs.AI cs.CVZhiruo Zhou (h=0)
Zhiruo Zhou, Zelin Li, Xiwen Chen, Jiazhuo Li, Chenwei Wang
Core Contributions
  • Surfaces a sharp failure mode in retrieval-augmented VLA: appending retrieved text to the executed prompt collapses success from 92.47% to 3.00%, and critically, semantically meaningless appends of matched length fail just as badly — the form of the prompt, not its content, is what breaks the policy.
  • Names this 'prompt-form collapse' and responds with a prompt-authority interface that only allows a canonical, pre-authorized compact instruction to alter the policy input; anything else is rejected and the original base prompt is restored exactly.
  • Runs a large-scale audit (900 routes) to verify the contract holds: 525 routes correctly fall back to the base prompt with matching hashes, and all 375 authorized prompts preserve the task signature — a rare example of a VLA paper auditing its own safety mechanism at this granularity.
  • Improves LIBERO-Plus success from 69.5% to 73.1% and, on a physical PiPER arm with a frozen π0.5 checkpoint, raises success from 52.7% to 78.7% over 150 trials (p = 3.16×10⁻⁶) — without retraining the underlying policy at all.
Show abstract

Retrieval can efficiently and effectively augment a frozen vision--language--action (VLA) policy without retraining, yet retrieved text becomes a control intervention once it enters the executed prompt. In a matched audit, raw appended text reduces mean success from 92.47% to 3.00%, while meaningful and length-matched meaningless appends both fail on all 500 states. This result identifies prompt-form collapse: changing the instruction form, rather than adding useful semantics, can dominate execution. We introduce TOWN-VLA (Think Only When Needed), a prompt-authority interface that separates candidate generation from permission to alter the policy input. A fixed compatibility rule authorizes a canonical compact instruction; otherwise, the interface restores the original Base prompt exactly. Across 900 audited routes, every route follows this contract: 525 routes recover Base with matching hashes, and all 375 authorized prompts preserve the task signature. On a matched 4x7 LIBERO-Plus evaluation with 10,030 episodes per method, success rises from 69.5% to 73.1% (+362 episodes; 95% CI 1.89--5.45 points), improving on six perturbation axes and all four suites. On a physical PiPER arm with a frozen pi-0.5 checkpoint, success rises from 52.7% to 78.7% over 150 trials per method (p=3.16x10^-6). Prompt authority is enforceable for a frozen controller; oracle-free admission calibration is the next deployment target.

Pointing-VLA: Typed Spatial Grounding Interfaces for Vision-Language-Action Manipulation

2026-08-24cs.RO cs.AI cs.CVXiwen Chen (h=0)
Xiwen Chen, Zelin Li, Zhiruo Zhou, Huiming Chen, Chenwei Wang
Core Contributions
  • Replaces the usual VLA spatial-grounding interfaces — autoregressive text coordinates or opaque action tokens — with typed hidden-state heads that predict normalized points, object-functional-grounding heatmaps, and trajectories directly, avoiding the brittleness of serializing geometry as text.
  • Assigns an explicit execution contract per manipulation stage (source-conditioned grounding for PICK, point prediction for PLACE) rather than using one generic spatial output for the whole task.
  • Achieves SOTA on Bridge/WidowX (72.9% average across four tasks) without dataset-specific finetuning, and the typed heads run 6.68–6.90× faster than Embodied-R1's text-decoding approach on a shared benchmark.
  • As a plug-in spatial guidance module for a π0.5 policy, raises real-robot autonomous success from 52.7% to 80.7% across three visual contexts, and separately cuts controller latency by over 20× when transferred to NORA-1.5.
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models often expose spatial grounding through autoregressive text coordinates or opaque action tokens, creating brittle interfaces between multimodal reasoning and robot execution. We present Pointing-VLA, a typed hidden-state spatial readout built on Embodied-R1. Geometry-specific heads predict normalized points, object-functional grounding (OFG) heatmaps, and visual trajectories without serializing geometry as text. For the evaluated Bridge/WidowX and physical pick-place deployments, an explicit execution contract assigns PICK to source-conditioned OFG and PLACE to Pointing, providing direct stage-aligned spatial targets. Pointing-VLA achieves SOTA performance on Bridge/WidowX, averaging 72.9% across the evaluated four-task set without Bridge-specific finetuning under collision-enabled CuRobo execution. Pointing and OFG show complementary strengths across native and cross-dataset evaluations. The OFG/contact readout transfers to NORA-1.5, preserving or improving success while reducing recorded controller time by more than 20x; typed heads are also 6.68--6.90x faster than Embodied-R1 text decoding on a shared external suite. When integrated as spatial guidance for a pi-0.5 action policy, Pointing-VLA raises autonomous real-robot success from 52.7% to 80.7% across three visual contexts. These results establish typed spatial readouts as an efficient, inspectable interface between embodied reasoning and robot execution.

VikPath: A Vision Kansformer Framework for Effective Obstacle Avoidance in Self-Supervised Pathfinding

2026-08-24cs.ROM. A. Faruque (h=40)
Junyao Wang, Yulin Xu, Mohammad Abdullah Al Faruque
Core Contributions
  • Moves pathfinding away from supervised imitation of conventional planners: its Vision Kansformer module learns obstacle-distribution representations self-supervised, so it isn't capped by the quality of hand-crafted heuristic labels the way prior learned planners are.
  • Explicitly optimizes for obstacle clearance and trajectory smoothness together, not just path length — addressing a gap where prior learned pathfinders produce technically short but hard-to-execute routes.
  • Adds a sharp-turn penalty specifically to discourage jagged, difficult-to-track paths, a concrete mechanism rather than a generic smoothness loss term.
  • Reports 3.28% greater obstacle clearance and, more strikingly, 87.07% lower inference latency than SOTA baselines — a latency reduction large enough to matter for real-time replanning.
Show abstract

Pathfinding is a fundamental problem in artificial intelligence and autonomous systems. Traditional heuristic-based algorithms, such as A*, rely on predefined heuristic functions to guide the search process. Although effective in structured environments, their search efficiency can degrade substantially in complex, obstacle-rich scenarios, where handcrafted heuristics may provide limited guidance. Recent studies have explored learning-based approaches to improve pathfinding efficiency; however, most existing methods rely on supervised learning and require labels generated by conventional planners or obtained through manual annotation. As a result, their performance is inherently influenced by the quality of the underlying supervision and may degrade when the labeling heuristics fail to capture complex environmental structures. Moreover, existing methods primarily optimize for path length while paying limited attention to obstacle clearance and trajectory smoothness, which can lead to paths that are difficult or unsafe to execute in real-world environments. To address these limitations, we propose $\Design$, a self-supervised pathfinding framework that jointly considers obstacle proximity and path smoothness. At its core, our novel Vision Kansformer module learns representations of obstacle distributions without relying on labeled trajectories, enabling the model to better adapt to complex environments. We further introduce a sharp-turn penalty to encourage smoother and more practically executable paths. Extensive experiments demonstrate that, compared with state-of-the-art (SOTA) approaches, achieves an average of 3.28% greater obstacle clearance and 87.07% lower inference latency while maintaining smooth path generation.

Contextrast++: Robust Multi-Scale Contextual Contrastive Learning for Semantic Segmentation

2026-08-24cs.CV cs.ROHyungtae Lim (h=20)
Changki Sung, Hyungtae Lim, Wanhee Kim, Youngwoo Seo, Hyun Myung
Core Contributions
  • Extends contrastive learning for semantic segmentation to tackle two separate problems at once — multi-scale local/global context fusion and long-tailed class imbalance — rather than the single-axis focus of most contrastive segmentation work.
  • Adds an adaptive fusion module that dynamically balances local and global features per-pixel, feeding both a pixel-to-anchor loss and an anchor-to-anchor loss that draws on a class-balanced memory bank to specifically counter rare-class underrepresentation.
  • Introduces boundary-aware negative (BANE) sampling that mines hard negatives specifically from misclassified boundary regions, sharpening fine-grained segment edges rather than relying on random negative sampling.
  • Improves over prior contrastive-learning segmentation SOTA with zero additional inference-time compute, since all the added machinery (fusion, losses, sampling) is training-only.
Show abstract

Semantic segmentation has rapidly advanced with deep learning; however, challenges remain in effectively capturing local and global contexts as well as addressing the long-tailed distribution problem. To tackle these issues, we present Contextrast++, a robust contrastive learning method for semantic segmentation that improves multi-scale feature integration and mitigates class imbalance issues. Our method consists of two key components: 1) contextual contrastive learning (CCL) and 2) boundary-aware negative (BANE) sampling. CCL includes three subcomponents: adaptive fusion module, pixel-to-anchor (PA) loss, and anchor-to-anchor (AA) loss. The adaptive fusion module dynamically balances local and global feature integration, resulting in a more context-aware representation. While the PA loss leverages the fused multi-scale features to improve feature representation learning, the AA loss focuses on addressing the long-tailed distribution problem by utilizing a memory bank that stores a fixed number of class-balanced representative anchors. Meanwhile, BANE sampling enhances segmentation precision by selecting hard negatives from misclassified boundary regions, which refines fine-grained details during contrastive learning. As verified in extensive experiments using public datasets, we demonstrate that Contextrast++ substantially improves semantic segmentation performance over existing contrastive learning-based state-of-the-art approaches, while introducing no additional computational overhead during inference.

OptiSight: Bridging Semantic Reasoning and Geometric Control for Embodied Navigation

2026-08-24cs.RO cs.CVJordi Sanchez-Riera (h=10)
Alperen Avan, Jordi Sanchez-Riera
Core Contributions
  • Splits the navigation problem cleanly along its two hard parts: uses a VLM only for high-level semantic reasoning at sparse decision points, and hands continuous control to deterministic visual servoing via camera-projection geometry — avoiding the cost of querying a VLM every step.
  • Uses Grounded-SAM for open-vocabulary target localization, meaning the system isn't restricted to a fixed object vocabulary the way earlier geometric navigation stacks were.
  • Skips dense mapping entirely by converting visual observations straight into navigation commands, cutting the memory and compute overhead of full SLAM-based approaches.
  • Runs zero-shot across diverse indoor scenarios, including obstacle avoidance and semantic ambiguity, within an 8 GB VRAM budget — a constraint that matters for onboard deployment rather than workstation-class GPUs.
Show abstract

Autonomous indoor navigation requires both semantic understanding and precise geometric control. We propose OptiSight, a hybrid framework that combines Vision-Language Model reasoning with deterministic visual servoing through a finite-state Chain-of-Thought architecture. Grounded-SAM localizes open-vocabulary targets, while camera projection geometry converts visual observations into navigation commands without requiring dense mapping. The VLM is queried only at key decision points, reducing computational overhead while geometric control handles continuous navigation. Experiments in AI Habitat demonstrate reliable zero-shot navigation across diverse indoor scenarios, including obstacle avoidance and semantic ambiguity, while operating within an 8 GB VRAM budget. The source code is available at https://github.com/avanalperen/OptiSight-Python-Multimodal-CoT-for-Visual-Reasoning.

SuperMap: A Spatio-Temporal SLAM System for Visual-Language Navigation

2026-08-24cs.ROShibo Zhao (h=7)
Shibo Zhao, Guofei Chen, Honghao Zhu, Zhiheng Li, Changwei Yao
Core Contributions
  • Targets the specific failure of naively bolting foundation-model perception onto SLAM: open-vocabulary detections are intermittent and view-dependent, which causes identity drift and stale semantics if just dumped into a map over time.
  • Introduces a consistency-driven mapping engine with 3D-aware instance association/re-activation plus confidence-weighted existence-and-label updates, explicitly designed to prune outdated content as scenes change — a temporal-consistency problem largely unaddressed by prior open-vocabulary mapping work.
  • Produces a queryable 4D scene graph (not just a static point cloud with labels), enabling compositional VLM queries over objects and relations rather than simple nearest-neighbor semantic lookup.
  • Validated on both benchmarks and real robots in dynamic scenes with object appearance, disappearance, and relocation, and released fully open-source as a deployable baseline.
Show abstract

Robotic navigation in human environments requires a spatio-temporal semantic representation that can reconcile open-vocabulary perception with long-term environmental changes. While foundation models provide strong zero-shot recognition, their predictions are intermittent and view-dependent, and naively integrating them into mapping pipelines leads to identity drift and stale semantics over time. We present SuperMap, a 4D spatio-temporal mapping framework for language-guided navigation that integrates high-frequency geometric SLAM with asynchronous open-vocabulary perception. Our core contribution is a consistency-driven mapping engine that combines 3D-aware instance association/re-activation with a principled existence-and-label confidence update to maintain stable object identities and prune outdated map content under occlusions and scene changes. SuperMap produces a queryable 4D scene-graph representation that interfaces naturally with Vision-Language Models by supporting compositional queries over object semantics, relations. We demonstrate SuperMap on benchmarks and real robots, including dynamic scenes with appearance/disappearance and relocation, and provide ablations and runtime analysis. We release the full system as open-source to provide the community with a deployable baseline for open-vocabulary spatio-temporal mapping. Project website: superodometry.com/supermap.

Macro-Action Topological Navigation under Noisy Localization using Reinforcement Learning

2026-08-24cs.LG cs.ROSimon Hakenes (h=3)
Simon Hakenes, Tobias Glasmachers
Core Contributions
  • Challenges the assumption that plain RL can't handle photorealistic pixel-based indoor navigation, by replacing the earlier version's reliance on ground-truth simulator pose with an onboard, object-centric pose estimate built from ORB features and a minimal EKF.
