Research Landscape

The largest cluster in today's batch — seven papers — is squarely about pushing vision-language-action and world-action models past naive behavior cloning by grafting on extra structure. Temporal Forcing diagnoses observation aliasing in long-horizon tasks and fixes it by aligning a history-aware latent to a pretrained 4D foundation model, lifting a physical hidden-placement task from 20.0% to 43.3% success. PAVE attacks a related pair of gaps — representations not modeling multi-horizon scene evolution, and mixed-quality trajectories being reused indiscriminately — with multi-horizon JEPA alignment plus a value-guided flow-matching actor. Zeva goes further still, proposing in-context causal learning so a frozen policy improves online from its own deployment experience without any gradient updates, while Motus2 unifies policy, simulator, and evaluator into one set of shared weights to close the decision-and-learning loop end to end. SUN/Kuafu and CometVLA both push structure earlier in the pipeline — SUN by compiling task semantics once into aligned MPC costs and RL rewards so learned behavior can't drift from what a controller verified, CometVLA by testing directly whether better physical-commonsense pretraining actually improves downstream manipulation (it finds a real correlation). Behavior-Skill closes the loop from the evaluation side, showing across pi0.5 and GR00T that failures in these long-horizon pipelines cluster overwhelmingly in contact-rich manipulation skills — a concrete signal for where the next round of "extra structure" should be aimed.

A similarly large navigation and perception cluster (seven papers) shares a notable throughline of benchmark self-scrutiny rather than pure capability gains. Driving on Memory and Failure or Drift? are both explicitly critical papers: the former shows that discarding a driving model's camera feed entirely and substituting location memory nearly matches NAVSIM leaderboard scores, implying the benchmark rewards location memorization as much as genuine reactivity; the latter shows that a common synthetic corruption proxy (illumination) can reorder SLAM systems differently than the real degraded condition it's meant to approximate, while structured rain/fog proxies preserve the real ranking. Against that backdrop, capability papers like LightNav-0 (eliciting a pretrained VLM's own spatial intelligence for a unified navigation interface across embodiments) and Scaffolding Foundation Models / NavMCP (coupling a VLM's evidence-seeking reasoning with a navigation foundation model's closed-loop execution via a persistent memory channel) report strong numbers — NavMCP's margin over baselines grows from 10 to 45 points as task horizon increases on a real Unitree Go2 — that the field would do well to stress-test with the same skepticism the critique papers apply to driving benchmarks. CanonNav contributes a clean architectural insight in the same space: disentangling navigation behavior from platform-specific camera geometry via canonicalization turns out to matter more than adding RGB-D sensing.

A third, smaller but coherent theme is sensing miniaturization paired with data-efficient learning under real-world constraints. SpectraTac (camera-free optical tactile sensing under $5 and 19mm across) and Proximity3D (shape reconstruction from a curved capacitive textile treated as a non-planar sensing manifold) both push tactile-adjacent sensing toward cheaper, lower-dimensional hardware rather than more cameras and compute. GAFT tackles the flip side of the same problem — learning reliably from very little data — by geometrically anchoring LoRA fine-tuning to fix off-road hazard detection despite having almost no labeled failure examples, roughly sextupling its benchmark F2 score. Rounding out the day, more traditional mechatronics and controls work — the shared-gas-spring humanoid ankle actuator from Tsagarakis's group (today's highest author h-index at 32), a hybrid physics-plus-Gaussian-Process quadcopter identification framework, decentralized contingency MPC under limited sensing, and a semi-passive velocity-field controller that relaxes strict passivity guarantees — shows the field's more established control-theory core continuing to make steady, less flashy progress alongside the foundation-model-driven majority of today's papers.

SUN: Persistent Programs For Language-Grounded Control-to-Learning-to-Real Policies

2026-08-31cs.ROcs.AI
Weiqi Wang, Zhi Li, Yudong Lei, David Martinez, Xiaofeng Gao
Core Contributions
  • Core idea is compiling task semantics once into a typed SUN Program that simultaneously produces MPC costs, RL rewards, satisfaction predicates, and diagnostics — instead of hand-crafting rewards separately from the control spec, so a learned policy can't silently drift from what the controller already verified as feasible.
  • Kuafu uses large VLMs to auto-generate these programs from language and scene semantics, then screens candidates for MPC feasibility before ever running RL — filtering out infeasible task specs before burning simulation budget on them.
  • Reports 82.03% macro-success across nine tasks versus 35.67% for sparse-reward RL and 24.75% for Stage-BC, and generates 10.57x the successful-trajectory time per hour of human teleoperation at 8192-way parallel scale.
  • Sim-trained DP3 policies reach only 34.7% success on physical Franka/Kinova arms versus 46.0% in simulation — a reminder that strong simulation-screening numbers don't automatically close the harder sim-to-real half of the pipeline.
Show abstract

Bridging model-based control and learned policies in long-horizon manipulation has harbored a silent disagreement: control executes specified objectives, learning amortizes that behavior into a reactive policy, yet existing protocols discard task semantics, leaving rewards hand-crafted and behavior drifting from what control verified. We introduce Semantically UNified (SUN) Programs, typed executables where geometric and contact relations are defined once and compiled into aligned Model Predictive Control (MPC) costs, satisfaction predicates, RL rewards, transition guards, and diagnostics. Our system, Kuafu, driven by large vision language systems, automatically synthesizes SUN Programs from language and scene semantics, screens feasibility via MPC, and retains semantics while training stage-conditioned policies. Across nine tasks, Kuafu achieves 82.03% macro-success, outperforming sparse-reward (35.67%) and Stage-BC (24.75%) baselines. At 8192-way scale, it generates 10.57x the successful trajectory time per hour of human teleoperation. With 500 trajectories per task, Kuafu data trains DP3 policies to 46.0% simulation success (vs. 22.4% for alternatives) and 34.7% on physical Franka and Kinova robots. These results establish that simulation-screened task semantics can effectively amortize control into robust policies, without demonstrations or manual dense rewards, unifying symbolic planning and data-driven execution.

Zeva: In-Context Causal Learning for Generalizable Embodied Manipulation

2026-08-31cs.RO
Fu Chen, Xin Ding, Bingjia Huang, Xiangyu Li, Mingju Wang
Core Contributions
  • Proposes in-context learning for manipulation with zero gradient updates: a frozen policy accumulates a 'causal memory' of past action-to-state-change pairs during deployment and retrieves relevant entries as context for future actions.
  • The Causal Interaction Extractor compresses each interaction into a compact causal signal rather than storing raw trajectories, keeping the dual-timescale memory tractable as deployment experience accumulates over time.
  • Reports a success rate that keeps climbing purely from accumulated deployment experience — the frozen policy improves online without retraining, unlike standard frozen VLAs or world-action models that plateau at their pretrained level.
  • Claims the learned causal-interaction knowledge transfers across tasks, suggesting the 'what happened when I did X' signal captures something more general than a single task's skill.
Show abstract

Generalizable embodied manipulation remains difficult to achieve through pretraining alone, due to unseen physical conditions in the real world. We argue that robots need to learn from their own physical interactions on the fly during real-world deployment and use this knowledge to inform subsequent actions. We present Zeva, the first framework that enables in-context learning from a robot's own physical interaction experience while keeping the policy model frozen. Zeva employs a Causal Interaction Extractor to encode an executed action and its induced state change into a causal interaction signal, which is stored in a dual-timescale causal memory. For subsequent actions, relevant causal interaction signals are retrieved from memory and injected into the frozen policy model as context. Experiments in simulation and real-world manipulation demonstrate that Zeva achieves the best performance among the compared frontier VLAs and WAMs and, more importantly, enables self-evolution during deployment without gradient updates. Its success rate continues to improve as the robot accumulates interaction experience. Furthermore, the acquired interaction experience can generalize across tasks.

Temporal Forcing: 4D Representation Alignment for Vision-Language-Action Models

2026-08-31cs.RO
Xingyu Ding, Yuzhong Zhao, Chunhai Zhao, Yinghuan Shi, Chaoyang Zhao
Core Contributions
  • Diagnoses a specific VLA weakness: aligning representations to 3D geometry — a popular recent trick — only captures the current scene, so models still confuse visually similar states that differ only by history, a classic observation-aliasing problem in long-horizon tasks.
  • Fix is a history pathway that summarizes past observations into a temporally-aware latent, which is then aligned not to static 3D features but to a pretrained 4D foundation model's temporally-consistent geometric representations.
  • Reports 98.8% on LIBERO (+2.2 points over its base model) — a modest but real gain on an already near-saturated benchmark — versus a much larger real-world gain: full-task success on a physical hidden-placement task rises from 20.0% to 43.3%.
  • The gap between the small simulation gain and the large real-world gain suggests the temporal/4D alignment specifically helps with the long-horizon, partially-hidden-state scenarios that simulation benchmarks under-represent.
Show abstract

Recent vision-language-action (VLA) methods improve manipulation performance by aligning their representations with 3D scene geometry. However, these methods often struggle with long-horizon manipulation and observation aliasing between visually similar states due to a lack of temporal information: the 3D scene geometry captures only the current state, rather than how it has evolved over time. To resolve this, we present Temporal Forcing, a 4D representation alignment method for VLA models. Specifically, we first introduce a history pathway that enables a vanilla VLA model to summarize observation history into temporally aware latent representations. Then, the latent representations are aligned with the geometric features extracted by a pretrained 4D foundation model, which captures the evolving 3D world through temporally consistent geometric representations, enabling a deeper understanding of dynamic environments. Temporal Forcing reaches 98.8% on LIBERO, outperforming its base model by 2.2 points. On a physical hidden-placement task, it raises full-task success from 20.0% to 43.3%. Code will be publicly available.

Behavior-Skill: A Fine-Grained Benchmark for Evaluating Vision-Language-Action Policies in Long-Horizon Tasks

2026-08-31cs.RO
Chunyun Ma, Lun Luo, Xingjian Luo, Xiexing Feng, Hang Zhang
Core Contributions
  • Reacts to a measurement gap: existing long-horizon manipulation benchmarks score only full-task rollouts, so when a policy fails partway through a 10-step task it's opaque which constituent skill actually broke.
  • Restructures evaluation around 235,492 individually-executable skill instances from 10,000 demonstrations across 50 household tasks and 34 skill categories, each with a restorable intermediate state so skills can be tested independently of what came before them.
  • Testing pi0.5 and GR00T across the full 50-task suite shows failures are highly non-uniform — contact-rich manipulation skills are the persistent bottleneck, not perception or navigation sub-skills — a concrete target for where the field should focus next.
  • Its trajectory-level and skill-level metrics let researchers separate 'policy fails immediately' from 'policy degrades late in a long chain,' a distinction that aggregate task-success numbers erase entirely.
Show abstract

Reliable execution of long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks remains challenging because overall task success depends on the successful completion of multiple constituent skills. Existing benchmarks, however, still rely primarily on full-task rollouts and aggregate task-level metrics, making intermediate failures difficult to observe and analyze. We present Behavior-Skill, a benchmark that reformulates the learning and evaluation of long-horizon tasks around executable constituent skills. It contains 235,492 skill instances from 10,000 demonstrations across 50 household tasks and 34 semantic skill categories. Each instance pairs a skill instruction with an aligned observation-action segment, and is further associated with a restorable intermediate state and a skill success condition to enable independent evaluation under valid preconditions. We further introduce trajectory-level and skill-level metrics to characterize policy capability beyond aggregate task success. Extensive experiments across representative VLA policies including pi0.5 and GR00T on the complete 50-task benchmark show that failures are highly non-uniform across skills, with contact-rich manipulation skills forming persistent bottlenecks. These results demonstrate that Behavior-Skill complements full-task evaluation by exposing intermediate capability profiles for analyzing and improving long-horizon VLA policies. Behavior-Skill is publicly available at https://github.com/nubot-nudt/Behavior-Skill.

PAVE: Predictive Alignment and Value-Guided Evolution for World-Action Policies

2026-08-31cs.ROcs.AI
Botong Zhao, Fang Yu, Tim, Senhua Zhu, Xinyuan Chen
Core Contributions
  • Targets two behavior-cloning weaknesses at once: representations aren't required to model how the scene evolves over multiple horizons, and mixed-quality deployment trajectories get reused without distinguishing good dynamics from bad behavior.
  • Adds trajectory-relative multi-horizon transition alignment — predicting scene evolution at 25/50/75/100% of the remaining episode — as a training-only JEPA-style objective, so the representation captures longer-range task progress without an explicit future-token decoder at inference.
  • Separately trains a distributional value critic on cumulative deployment data, converts action-chunk advantages into positive/negative/null text conditions, and feeds those into a flow-matching actor — so every trajectory, even suboptimal ones, teaches the dynamics model while the actor trains only toward relatively-better actions.
  • Removes the predictor and critic at inference, so the richer training-time supervision costs nothing at deployment — a useful pattern for keeping direct-policy runtime cost low while still benefiting from offline signal.
Show abstract

Direct vision-language-action policies generate continuous robot actions efficiently, but standard behavior cloning leaves two complementary gaps: their representations are not explicitly required to describe how the scene evolves over multiple time scales, and deployment trajectories of unequal quality are often reused without separating useful dynamics from undesirable behavior. We introduce \method, a direct world-action policy that combines outcome-agnostic predictive learning with outcome-aware policy improvement. \method first retains a local fixed-offset JEPA objective and adds trajectory-relative multi-horizon transition alignment at 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100% of the remaining episode. These training-only targets require the current policy representation to preserve both local physical changes and longer-range task progress, without supplying explicit future tokens to the action head. \method then trains an independent distributional value critic on cumulative deployment trajectories, computes action-chunk-aligned $N$-step advantages, and converts them into positive, negative, or null text conditions for a flow-matching actor. Thus, every valid trajectory can teach what physically happened, while the actor is deployed only under the condition associated with relatively better actions. The multi-horizon predictor and critic are removed from online execution, preserving direct action generation from the current observation, language instruction, and proprioception. Across the three simulation benchmarks, the method achieves the strongest overall performance while preserving the direct actor's online execution path.

CometVLA: Co-Training on an Embodied Data Pyramid towards Physical Understanding

2026-08-31cs.RO
Hanwen Wan, Dafeng Chi, Linbo Zhai, Tianao Shen, Yuzheng Zhuang
Core Contributions
  • Tests a specific hypothesis directly rather than assuming it: does giving a VLM better physical commonsense — not just better vision-language alignment — actually translate into better downstream action generation? Most physical-VQA work stops at the VLM level without checking this link.
  • Builds CometData/CometBench, a physical VQA corpus explicitly aligned to the robot's own action space and embodiment, addressing the common mismatch where physical-reasoning datasets are disembodied and don't match what the action head actually needs.
  • Introduces Global Action Prior (GAP) tokens as a compact bottleneck isolating task-agnostic motion regularities, letting the action head use physical-commonsense signal without corrupting the pretrained VLM backbone's existing representations.
  • Reports a direct correlation between CometBench VLM performance and downstream VLA success rate — this correlation, more than the raw benchmark numbers, is the real evidence that physical-understanding pretraining genuinely helps manipulation rather than being assumed to.
Show abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models remain brittle in manipulation tasks that require physical commonsense. Current physical VQA data is typically disembodied and misaligned with robot action domains. Egocentric videos are used only as auxiliary pre-training. It remains unclear whether improved VLM physical understanding actually benefits downstream action generation. Therefore, we present CometVLA to close this gap. We construct CometData and CometBench, an embodied physical VQA corpus and benchmark strictly aligned with the robot's action data and embodiment. We introduce Global Action Prior (GAP) tokens, a compact learnable bottleneck that isolates task-agnostic motion regularities and lets the action head consume physical commonsense without corrupting the pre-trained VLM backbone. We co-train CometVLA across the embodied data pyramid, spanning teleoperation, simulation, egocentric trajectories, and VQA layers. On real-world manipulation tasks and RoboTwin simulation, CometVLA consistently outperforms strong VLA baselines. Correlation analysis shows that stronger VLM performance on CometBench indicates higher VLA success rates. Results demonstrate that physical understanding pre-training genuinely benefits downstream manipulation.

Motus2: A Self-Evolving General World Model for Dexterous Manipulation

2026-08-31cs.ROcs.AIcs.CVcs.LG
Hongzhe Bi, Zihao Zhou, Yihang Tang, Jingrui Pang, Shuhe Huang
Core Contributions
  • Pursues a genuinely unified world model: a single set of shared weights exposes three interfaces — a policy (proposes actions), a simulator (predicts visual consequences), and an evaluator (scores predicted outcomes) — closing a decision-and-learning loop that most 'world model plus action head' systems leave open.
  • Uses failed and suboptimal interactions productively, not just curated expert demos: expert data trains the policy, but any trajectory — good or bad — still teaches the dynamics model and value critic.
  • Data scaling is deliberately staged rather than one big mixed dataset: large-scale monocular egocentric video first, then synchronized stereo egocentric data, then robot-domain adaptation with real robot trajectories and human-robot alignment data.
  • Instantiated on a fully biomimetic platform (stereo vision, dual arms, dual dexterous hands, tactile sensing) with tactile feedback folded into contact-aware control, making it one of the more hardware-ambitious world-model papers in this batch.
Show abstract

General embodied agents should perceive, predict, act, evaluate, and improve within a unified system. World models have shown great promise in building such agents, yet existing models typically append an action output head to a world simulator, without coupling them into a closed decision-and-learning loop for policy improvement. We present Motus2, a self-evolving general world model for dexterous manipulation. Motus2 advances world modeling through model scaling and data scaling. For model scaling, a single model with shared weights exposes three control interfaces: a policy (world-action model), a simulator (action-conditioned world model), and an evaluator (value model). The policy proposes candidate action chunks, the simulator predicts their visual consequences, and the evaluator assesses the predicted outcomes. Their coupling forms a closed decision-and-learning loop for policy improvement. This formulation uses curated expert demonstrations for action learning, while failed and suboptimal interactions provide valuable evidence for dynamics modeling and value learning. For data scaling, Motus2 progresses from large-scale monocular egocentric data to synchronized stereo egocentric data, followed by robot-domain adaptation with robot trajectories and supplementary human-robot alignment data. Motus2 further studies global-autoregressive and hybrid-memory extensions of its sliding-window context, adds tactile feedback for contact-aware control, and is instantiated on a fully biomimetic platform with stereo vision, dual arms, dual dexterous hands, and tactile sensing. Together, egocentric data scaling and closed-loop general world model scaling provide a general path toward self-evolving dexterous manipulation.

Driving on Memory

2026-08-31cs.CVcs.LGcs.RO
Christian Löwens, Thorben Funke, Alexandru Paul Condurache
Core Contributions
  • Provocative negative result: replacing the camera feed entirely with memories from prior drives at the same location — no view of current traffic at all — nearly matches or beats leading end-to-end planners on NAVSIM.
  • Directly challenges how the field reads benchmark leaderboards: a high NAVSIM score is shown to be largely achievable from static location priors (road layout, typical maneuvers there) rather than genuine perception-to-action reactivity.
  • The effect is benchmark-dependent — memory substitution causes far larger performance drops on Bench2Drive and RealEngine, suggesting those benchmarks better isolate real reactive-driving competence than NAVSIM does.
  • Practical takeaway for benchmark users: pair a route-memorization ablation with headline metrics, or risk over-crediting a driving model for memorizing locations rather than perceiving traffic.
Show abstract

End-to-end autonomous driving models plan future trajectories from raw sensor input. While earlier driving benchmarks often measured deviation from the human trajectory, current benchmarks such as NAVSIM and Bench2Drive evaluate models with richer simulation-based metrics intended to capture safe and compliant driving. A high benchmark score should reflect that a model can understand the scene in front of it and act accordingly. But how much of that score specifically comes from reacting to the dynamic part of that scene? To probe this, we remove a model's camera input and replace it with memories from prior drives at the same location. The retrieved memories can provide persistent scene information, including road layout and location-conditioned regularities, but not the current traffic state. Surprisingly, memory is nearly sufficient on NAVSIM, reaching or even exceeding the performance of leading end-to-end methods without actually observing the evaluated scene. Our results suggest that a high NAVSIM score does not require a planner to react to the current traffic scene and should be treated with caution. This effect is benchmark-dependent: driving from memory causes substantially larger performance drops on Bench2Drive and RealEngine. We provide our code at https://github.com/boschresearch/MemoryDrivoR .

DARP: A Calibrated Dual-Arm RGB-D-IR Dataset for Multi-View Robotic Perception

2026-08-31cs.ROcs.CV
Manish Kansana, Mohammed Yusuf Mujawar, Sudip Mittal, Shahram Rahimi, Noorbakhsh Amiri Golilarz
Core Contributions
  • Fills a specific perception gap — single-viewpoint self-occlusion — with a calibrated dual-arm, eye-in-hand RGB-D-IR rig where two independently moving arms observe the same tabletop object from opposing angles at once.
  • Automates the tedious parts of multi-view dataset construction: localization, cross-arm confirmation, and adaptive viewpoint generation happen without manual pose annotation or fiducial markers.
  • Establishes a credible geometric ground truth using a deterministic (non-learned) fusion baseline: 2.13 mm median point-to-mesh distance and 96.56% of 1.56M query points within 10 mm of the reference mesh across 224 held-out keyframes.
  • Positioned as infrastructure rather than a new algorithm — its value is enabling downstream active-perception and collaborative multi-arm reconstruction research, not a novel method of its own.
Show abstract

Robotic perception from a single viewpoint is often limited by self-occlusion and incomplete surface visibility. This paper presents DARP(Dual-Arm Robotic Perception) https://doi.org/10.21227/rmv3-be47, a calibrated dual-arm RGB-D-IR dataset for object-centered robotic perception using two independently moving eye-in-hand manipulators positioned on opposite sides of a shared tabletop workspace. Each arm carries an Intel RealSense sensor that continuously records RGB, depth, and stereo infrared data while synchronized robot joint states are logged for pose recovery. Objects are placed without fixed poses or marked locations, and the acquisition procedure performs automatic localization, cross-arm confirmation, adaptive viewpoint generation, and continuous multimodal recording. DARP contains ten unique tabletop objects and preserves the original sensor recordings, robot-state logs, object-level metadata, and calibration information required to reconstruct camera trajectories in a shared metric frame. To evaluate the geometric consistency of the acquisition, we implement a deterministic multi-view fusion pipeline that converts calibrated RGB-D observations into complementary partial point clouds and measured surface meshes without using learned or generative completion methods. Evaluation on 224 held-out RGB-D keyframes comprising 1,563,466 three-dimensional query points yields a median point-to-mesh distance of 2.13~mm and an RMSE of 4.04~mm, with 96.56\% of points within 10~mm of the measured-surface mesh. DARP is intended as a reusable resource for multi-view reconstruction, collaborative robotic perception, multimodal fusion, active perception, and future learning-based reasoning over partial object observations.

LightNav-0: Eliciting VLM Spatial Intelligence for Generalist Embodied Navigation

2026-08-31cs.ROcs.AI
Shaoan Wang, Aocheng Luo, Fei Huang, Jingyi Xu, Xiaoyang Wang
Core Contributions
  • Elicits navigation behavior directly from a pretrained VLM's existing spatial-grounding and pointing abilities rather than bolting on task-specific prediction heads — one compact generalist model instead of a fragmented, per-task navigation stack.
  • Key mechanism: dual-channel pointing expresses task/scene/embodiment-agnostic spatial intent, and a residual vector-quantized action tokenizer converts that intent into embodiment-specific trajectories — a single interface driving many robot bodies.
  • Trained on 2K+ scenes and 4K+ hours of embodied navigation data through staged mid-training, SFT, and RL; the intermediate LightNav-ER checkpoint alone tops 8 embodied-reasoning benchmarks before navigation-specific training even begins.
  • Claims state-of-the-art monocular success across all 10 public navigation simulation settings plus zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer in real-world tests — a strong claim worth watching for independent replication since no head-to-head baseline numbers appear in the abstract.
Show abstract

Embodied navigation requires agents to translate heterogeneous goals and visual observations into actions across tasks, environments, and robot embodiments. Modern vision-language models (VLMs) already encode spatial priors for visual grounding, spatial reasoning, and pointing, but these capabilities are rarely elicited directly for robot control. Existing navigation systems instead rely on task- or embodiment-specific components, fragmenting perception, reasoning, and action while offering limited generalization. Here we present LightNav-0, a compact generalist embodied navigation model that elicits the spatial intelligence of a pretrained VLM and aligns it with navigation, without task-specific prediction heads. LightNav-0 represents diverse navigation tasks through a unified token interface: dual-channel pointing expresses task-, scene-, and embodiment-agnostic spatial intent, while a residual vector-quantized action tokenizer maps this intent to precise, embodiment-specific trajectories. Together with temporally aware visual history compression, ER mid-training, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning, this formulation supports instruction following, open-vocabulary object navigation, and visual tracking within a single model. The navigation training corpus spans 2K+ scenes and 4K+ hours of embodied navigation data. LightNav-ER, the embodied-reasoning checkpoint used to initialize LightNav-0, attains the highest complete-set average across 8 embodied-reasoning benchmarks, while LightNav-0 achieves state-of-the-art monocular success rates across all 10 public navigation simulation settings. Real-world evaluations further demonstrate zero-shot generalization across robot embodiments, diverse scenes, and static and dynamic targets. These results establish compact VLMs as a unified and transferable backbone for generalist embodied navigation.

Failure or Drift? Evaluating Monocular SLAM under Synthetic and Real-World Corruptions

2026-08-31cs.CVcs.RO
Abhay Skaria Thomas, Shashank Agnihotri, Margret Keuper
Core Contributions
  • Makes a methodological point that's easy to get wrong: a synthetic corruption benchmark for SLAM is only useful if failing on it predicts the same engineering conclusion as the real degraded condition it's meant to proxy — and the paper finds this doesn't always hold.
  • Separates two failure modes a single trajectory-error number conflates: explicit tracking loss versus quiet accumulated drift from a tracker that never declares failure — learned trackers mostly convert the former into the latter, arguably worse since there's no failure signal for downstream safety logic.
  • Finds structured corruption proxies (rain, fog) preserve the same relative ranking of learned trackers as real adverse conditions from 4Seasons, but a naive illumination-corruption proxy does not — some common synthetic stress tests can lead engineers to the wrong model choice.
  • Practical implication: benchmark designers should validate that a synthetic corruption reproduces the ranking of systems, not just degrade the headline metric, before using it to pick a production SLAM system.
Show abstract

Visual SLAM is commonly evaluated on clean trajectories, although deployment failures are often caused by adverse weather, illumination, blur, and sensor artifacts. Controlled corruptions are attractive because they isolate such factors, but a synthetic stress test is useful only when it leads to the same engineering conclusion as the condition it is intended to approximate. This work examines that question for monocular SLAM. Reliable three-dimensional (3D) navigation of magnetically actuated untethered microrobots remains a major barrier to clinical translation. X-ray fluoroscopy is the standard real-time imaging modality for endovascular procedures, but single-plane fluoroscopy provides only a two-dimensional (2D) projection, eliminating depth information and complicating autonomous navigation. Recovering this information through biplane imaging or repeated contrast-enhanced angiography increases procedural complexity, radiation exposure, or contrast burden. Here, we introduce VISTA (Virtual Integration for Spatial Tracking and Autonomy), a digital twin framework enabling contrast-free autonomous navigation under single-plane fluoroscopy.
