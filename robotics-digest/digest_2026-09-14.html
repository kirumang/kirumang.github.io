Research Landscape

Contact-rich, tactile dexterous manipulation is the largest cluster in this batch. Touch2Trace and SlipSense, both built on the same custom TacV5 piezoresistive sensor, tackle complementary problems—fine-motor cable tracing and low-latency slip detection—and both report the kind of precisely quantified latency and accuracy numbers (93% success at 60 Hz; 76% of slips caught within 23.1 ms) that the field has historically lacked. Bench2Dex responds to the same underlying problem from a different angle: tactile hardware for dexterous hands still hasn't converged on a common design, so it benchmarks 12 hand morphologies under one shared simulated tactile interface rather than waiting for hardware to standardize. On the control side, two papers from Küçüktabak et al. show sampling-based MPC used as scaffolding rather than as the end product—bootstrapping real-world dexterous RL in one paper, biasing MPC's own sampling with learned motion primitives in the other—suggesting the field is moving past an MPC-versus-learning framing toward hybrid pipelines where each compensates for the other's weakness. CMDIR extends impedance retargeting toward continuous supervision for imitation learning, rounding out a cluster that is converging on structured, hybrid classical-learned representations for contact rather than either pure end-to-end RL or pure model-based control.

A second theme is foundation and world models for action generation, and each entry names a specific failure mode it is fixing rather than proposing a generic scale-up. DIDO distills a multi-step video diffusion World Action Model into a single denoising step while explicitly preserving interaction-centric dynamics, because it finds that naive truncation keeps scene structure but silently loses the gripper/object dynamics that manipulation actually depends on. PhysBrain 1.5 unifies understanding, action generation, and future-state prediction in one autoregressive vision-language model pretrained on human video, reporting parity with proprietary systems across 28 embodied benchmarks. GRAVA does the analogous thing for driving, tying reasoning to a trajectory-anchored typed graph so intermediate reasoning stays grounded in physical evidence instead of drifting into loosely-connected chain-of-thought. X-WBC extends the same foundation-model logic to humanoid whole-body control, training one policy jointly across nine simulated bodies and four real robots and showing joint training actually improves tracking rather than merely enabling transfer. And P-POSEMEM identifies a parallel grounding failure at the SLAM layer: semantic memory built on pose graphs silently mis-names objects when loop closures rewrite the map underneath it, which is conceptually the same class of problem—keeping a symbolic or semantic layer consistent with an evolving lower-level representation—that DIDO and GRAVA are solving for action and reasoning respectively.

A third, smaller but notable thread is treating safety and legality as separate, certifiable objectives rather than folding everything into one reward function. ResSafe's residual-policy safety filter for humanoids, the exact CBF feasibility certificate for multi-robot systems, and the Defeasible Deontic Logic legal-planning stack all decouple "what to optimize for" from "what must never happen," and all go further than a pass/fail result: the CBF paper identifies exactly which agent interaction breaks feasibility 94% of the time it's tested, and the legal-reasoning paper explicitly measures where perception and planning translation introduce compliance gaps. Rounding out the batch, two papers from the same aerial-robotics group push spring-legged hopping quadcopters toward large, hardware-validated energy savings (30–83% depending on baseline) by encoding physical energy manifolds directly into the RL reward rather than hand-coding hopping state machines, while DuctAM tackles a related but distinct problem of adding sustained horizontal force to aerial manipulators without destabilizing flight.

ResSafe: Learning Safety Filtering with Residual Reinforcement Learning for Humanoids
2026-09-14 cs.RO K. Sreenath (h=55)
Gechen Qu, Tong Zhang, Bike Zhang, Yen-Jen Wang, Koushil Sreenath
Core Contributions
  • Splits humanoid RL control into two decoupled policies instead of one monolithic reward-balanced policy: a nominal task policy plus a residual policy that corrects unsafe actions.
  • Why it matters: prior approaches cram performance, safety, and robustness into a single reward function, forcing brittle manual tuning across competing terms; decoupling sidesteps that tuning problem entirely.
  • The residual policy functions as an implicit, learned safety filter rather than a hand-derived analytic filter (e.g., a CBF-QP), letting it adapt to contact-rich, high-dimensional humanoid dynamics that are hard to model explicitly.
  • Positions residual RL as a general recipe for retrofitting safety onto an existing locomotion or tracking policy without retraining it from scratch.
Show abstract

Safe control of humanoid robots remains challenging due to their high-dimensional dynamics, contact-rich interactions, and sensitivity to disturbances. Although reinforcement learning has enabled effective locomotion and motion tracking, learned policies can still generate unsafe actions that lead to instability or falls. In this work, we propose residual reinforcement learning as an implicit safety-filtering mechanism for safe humanoid control. Instead of relying on a single nominal policy to simultaneously balance performance, safety, and robustness, we decouple performance and safety. The nominal policy focuses solely on task performance, while a residual policy learns safety corrections. This decoupling leads to a better performance--safety Pareto trade-off and avoids the need for careful tuning of multiple competing reward terms within a single policy training. We show that the residual policy can act as an implicit safety filter.

X-WBC: A Cross-Embodiment Foundation Model for Humanoid Whole-Body Control
2026-09-14 cs.RO Chun Gu (h=11)
Juntong Zhang, Chun Gu, Li Zhang
Core Contributions
  • Trains a single cross-embodiment whole-body control policy across nine simulated humanoids and four real robots, instead of the standard practice of training one policy per robot body that leaves motion knowledge siloed.
  • Separates shared human-motion semantics from embodiment-specific execution using human-centered command tokens that align human motion, robot reference motion, and sparse VR observations, with a causal Transformer learning reusable temporal structure and lightweight robot-specific adapters handling each body's action space.
  • Shows joint training across many robot bodies actually improves tracking, not just enables transfer, and that the aligned representation supports consistent control regardless of which command source drives it.
  • Demonstrates the policy remains competitive on motions outside the training corpus, supporting the case for heterogeneous humanoid data as a genuinely useful joint training resource.
Show abstract

Scaling humanoid whole-body control toward general-purpose deployment requires large human motion corpora and training experience shared across robot bodies. Existing methods usually train one policy per robot, leaving motion experience isolated across embodiments. We introduce X-WBC, a cross-embodiment foundation framework that separates relatively shared human motion semantics from embodiment-specific physical execution. Human-centered command tokens align full human motion, robot reference motion, and sparse VR observations. A causal Transformer learns reusable temporal structure from mixed multi-robot rollouts, while lightweight robot-specific modules map the shared representation to each robot's proprioception and action space. Across nine simulated embodiments, external motions, and four real robots, experiments show that joint training improves tracking, the aligned representation supports consistent control across command sources, and the learned policy remains competitive beyond the training corpus. These results support heterogeneous humanoids as joint data sources and establish cross-embodiment joint training as a practical route toward whole-body control foundation models.

Legislating World-Model-Based Planning with Legal Reasoning
2026-09-14 cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.LO Guido Governatori (h=54)
Dylan Waldner, Yiannis Kantaros, Guido Governatori, Risto Miikkulainen, Amir Banifatemi
Core Contributions
  • Introduces ‘ex ante’ legal governance: a Defeasible Deontic Logic reasoner intervenes before an illegal action executes, rather than penalizing bad behavior after the fact through reward shaping.
  • Formalizes two failure modes new to robot-law literature: a grounding isomorphism gap (perception error feeds false facts to the legal reasoner) and an ontological isomorphism gap (one legal rule maps to many different numeric planning constraints).
  • Shows that explicitly modeling perception uncertainty, rather than treating percepts as ground truth, further increases legal abidance on a simulated cube-pushing task.
  • Demonstrates legal verdicts remain auditable and efficient to compute at runtime, and that the stack adapts to both exogenous signal changes and endogenous rule changes—a step toward regulatable, explainable planners rather than opaque end-to-end policies.
Show abstract

As robotic systems grow more general, legal norms are needed to integrate them into society. This paper extends the isomorphism problem of aligning legal source texts with their encodings, and measures two key challenges to robot normative control: (1) the \textit{grounding isomorphism gap}, where perception error grounds false atoms for legal reasoning, and (2) the \textit{ontological isomorphism gap}, where one legal conclusion admits many faithful translations into planning constraints. The paper introduces a legal planning stack that employs Defeasible Deontic Logic (DDL) to constrain a motion planner. The stack leverages learned world models to plan and to provide legal context, enabling \textit{ex ante} governance that intervenes before an illegal action is executed. It was deployed on a simulated robot arm pushing a cube across a $3\times3$ grid. The findings were (1) the legislated agent abided substantially more often than the non-legislated one, and modeling perception uncertainty lifted abidance even further, (2) the legal reasoning ran efficiently at runtime and its verdicts were auditable, and (3) the stack adapted to exogenous signals and endogenous rule changes. Both gaps were measured: (4) world model and probe error corrupted the factual input for the DDL reasoner, and (5) a single law admitted several faithful metric interpretations yielding drastically different abidance. Thus, \textit{ex ante} legislation functions as intended, and closing these gaps with a standardized mapping from the law to runtime constraints and improved fact grounding from perception will yield robust laws that align robot behavior with society's norms.

An Information-Space Perspective to Scene Graph Sufficiency for Robotic Task Planning
2026-09-14 cs.RO Francesco Verdoja (h=15)
Başak Sakçak, Francesco Verdoja
Core Contributions
  • Provides a first formal, task-centric definition of what makes a scene graph ‘sufficient’ for planning, rather than relying on ad hoc pruning or hierarchical heuristics as prior work does.
  • Frames the problem via information-space transition systems, defining derived scene graphs through information mappings that merge or prune nodes and induce quotient transition systems augmented with motion primitives for higher-level actions.
  • Specifies two precise sufficiency conditions—deterministic quotient and task well-posedness over derived traces—that guarantee a plan found on the reduced graph is feasible on the full system, closing a correctness gap heuristic pruning can't guarantee.
  • Illustrates both sufficient and insufficient reduced graphs on an example task, giving practitioners a concrete diagnostic rather than only abstract theory.
Show abstract

Planning in complex environments requires task specifications grounded in representations that capture objects, relations, and affordances; scene graphs meet this need, but their size in large environments hinders efficient planning. While task-aware pruning and hierarchical abstractions have been explored, a general, task-centric formalization of what constitutes a sufficient scene graph for planning remains open. This paper provides such a formalization by modeling planning over scene graphs within an information-spaces framework through the definition of scene graph transition systems and relevant action semantics for navigation and manipulation. We then introduce derived scene graphs via information mappings that merge and prune nodes and induce quotient transition systems augmented with motion primitives to capture higher-level actions over merged graph nodes. Sufficiency is characterized by two conditions: (i) the information mapping yields a deterministic quotient, and (ii) the task is well-posed over derived traces, ensuring plans found on the derived model are feasible on the maximal system. We illustrate the framework using a task over an example environment, showing both sufficient and insufficient reduced scene graphs.

HydroMap: Probabilistic Water Surface Elevation Mapping for Semantic Scene Representation in Inland Waterways
2026-09-14 cs.RO H. Bruyninckx (h=46)
Zhongbi Luo, Yunjia Wang, Herman Bruyninckx, Peter Slaets
Core Contributions
  • Targets a blind spot in LiDAR SLAM: water surfaces return sparse or no LiDAR echoes, leaving autonomous surface vehicles with no persistent representation of the water they float on.
  • Reconstructs water elevation from stereo vision instead, fusing per-frame noisy observations (with propagated stereo and pose uncertainty) into a persistent probabilistic elevation map that is decoupled from the vehicle's odometry pipeline.
  • Combines the elevation map with structural LiDAR geometry into one 2.5D semantic map spanning water, shorelines, structures, and overhead clearance—a representation pure structural maps can't offer.
  • Achieves elevation RMSE below 5 cm against LiDAR ground truth on two real canal datasets, publishing at 1–2 Hz, showing the method is practical for real-time use rather than offline reconstruction only.
Show abstract

Autonomous surface vehicles operating in inland waterways require a persistent representation of both surrounding structures and the water surface. LiDAR-based simultaneous localization and mapping often produces sparse or missing water returns, leaving this operational surface absent from the reconstructed scene. We propose HydroMap, an odometry-decoupled framework that reconstructs water surface elevation from stereo observations and integrates it with the structural map. Per-frame water points form joint cell observations with propagated stereo and pose uncertainty, and successive observations are fused into a persistent probabilistic elevation map. Semantic map conversion then combines the elevation map with structural geometry in a unified 2.5D representation of water, boundaries, structures, and overhead regions. On the Pohang Canal and Leuven Vaart datasets, the elevation RMSE remains below 5 cm relative to LiDAR references expressed in the same map frame. The elevation and semantic maps are published at 2 Hz and 1 Hz, respectively. HydroMap thereby complements LiDAR maps with a persistent representation of the water surface for downstream navigation in inland waterways.

P-POSEMEM: Projective Semantic Memory for Consistent Language Grounding under Pose-Graph Rewrites
2026-09-14 cs.RO Xian-Jia Yu (h=13)
Ha Sier, Ali Salmasi, Mengya Xu, Haizhou Zhang, Jie Lu
Core Contributions
  • Solves a subtle but important SLAM problem: semantic memory built on top of pose graphs breaks when loop closures or marginalization move or remove the anchors objects were originally measured from, causing the same physical object to get renamed.
  • Stores every observation as an immutable event tied to its birth keyframe and retains the Bayes-tree elimination conditional of marginalized keyframes, integrating semantic likelihood over the full reconstructed joint posterior rather than committing detections to a single world coordinate.
  • Introduces Dproj, a total-variation defect metric between language-goal distributions of full vs. marginalized graphs, to directly measure grounding consistency rather than relying only on downstream task success.
  • Across 40 HM3DSem scenes, 112,000 queries, and an 8-run campaign with 761 closures rewriting the map by up to 47 m, keeps Dproj below 10^-13 with zero goal flips when elimination follows closures, while every ablation and a naive frozen-coordinate baseline does flip—and even a live bounded solver still degrades to 23/288 flips versus 53/288 for the frozen baseline.
Show abstract

A robot following language instructions needs its semantic memory to keep naming the same physical object while the SLAM pose graph underneath is optimized, loop-closed and compressed. Maps committing each detection to a world coordinate cannot: a closure moves the anchor it was measured from, or the solver marginalizes that anchor, and the query then selects a different object although both graphs represent the same posterior. P-POSEMEM stores each observation as an immutable event at its birth keyframe, retains the Bayes-tree elimination conditional of every marginalized keyframe, and integrates the semantic likelihood over the reconstructed joint posterior of poses, anchors and identities. Dproj, the total-variation defect between the language-goal distributions of inference-equivalent full and marginalized graphs, measures this directly. Over 40 HM3DSem scenes and 112,000 queries, P-POSEMEM reproduces the full-graph oracle (Dproj = 0) and reduces goal flips against every memory-reducing baseline. On an eight-run campaign whose 761 closures rewrote the map by up to 47 m, Dproj stays below 10^-13 with 0/288 goal flips when elimination follows the closures, where every ablation and a coordinate committed at insertion flip goals it does not; under a live bounded solver the same memory flips 23/288 against 53 for that frozen coordinate. A pre-registered negative control is detected by Dproj while leaving calibration error and navigation success unchanged, indicating that these measures capture distinct failure modes. Retrieval is held fixed by a shared frozen detector, isolating the gain to memory consistency. Code and data: https://anonymous.4open.science/r/posemem-2328/.

Comparing Trajectories from Positions Alone: Curvature-Based Time Alignment and Drift Error Metric
2026-09-14 cs.RO C. Dune (h=12)
Effie Daum, Daniele De Martini, Claire Dune, François Pomerleau
Core Contributions
  • Points out that the field's default SLAM/localization metrics (ATE, RPE) rest on rarely-stated assumptions about temporal alignment and evaluation parameters, which can silently make published comparisons misleading.
  • Proposes a curvature-based temporal alignment method that doesn't require synchronized timestamps between estimate and reference trajectories, addressing the practical difficulty of acquiring independent, more-accurate reference trajectories at scale.
  • Normalizes its drift error metric by traveled distance and explicitly separates temporal synchronization, sampling alignment, and extrinsic calibration effects via sensitivity analysis, rather than bundling them into one opaque number.
  • Aims at standardizing evaluation protocol rather than proposing a new SLAM algorithm, targeting reproducibility across the field.
Show abstract

In field robotics, acquiring independent large-scale reference trajectories more accurate than the evaluated estimates remains an open challenge. The domain is widely reliant on Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) and Relative Pose Error (RPE), computed with automated tools, that rest on assumptions and evaluation parameters rarely made explicit. When unreported, the errors can be misleading and hinder fair comparisons. This paper introduces a trajectory-evaluation protocol for standardized and reliable accuracy assessment in state estimation, localization, and Simultaneous Localization And Mapping (SLAM). The approach combines a novel temporal alignment method based on curvature signals with an error metric normalized by travelled distance. We explicitly account for temporal synchronization, sampling alignment, and extrinsic calibration, quantifying their influence through a sensitivity analysis. The proposed protocol contributes to more rigorous, reproducible, and standardized trajectory evaluation.

2026-09-14 cs.RO Yuzhe Yang (h=6)

Core Contributions
  • Wraps a training-free MLLM-based navigation agent in an ‘Agent Harness’ that validates the MLLM's proposed actions against spatial evidence, geometric feasibility, and subgoal consistency—addressing that prior training-free MLLM navigation methods often propose actions without reconciling them against what the robot has actually observed.

Embodied navigation requires agents to interpret visual observations, accumulate spatial knowledge, and execute actions to follow instructions or locate objects. Training-based methods face generalization challenges, while training-free methods exploit multimodal large language models (MLLMs) but often lack mechanisms to reconcile proposed actions with spatial evidence, task progress, and execution failures. We present HarnessVLN, a zero-shot, training-free framework whose Agent Harness coordinates perception, retrieval, grounding, navigation, recovery, and termination through a unified tool interface. Learning to Exploit Passive Dynamics for Energy-Efficient Target Hopping of a Spring-Legged Quadcopter
2026-09-14 cs.RO Y. Or (h=21)
Ruigang Chen, Qi Zhang, Zhicheng Zhong, Zhuorui Yun, Yizhar Or
Core Contributions
  • Replaces a heuristic hopping state machine plus PID attitude control with a single direct-to-motor PPO policy, removing the manual staging logic conventional monopedal hopping controllers rely on.
  • Combines an Energy-Manifold Shaping reward (mass-normalized vertical-energy tracking plus apex-state anchoring) with an Efficiency Shaping term that penalizes power draw via a history-aware estimator—explicitly rewarding coordination between thrust and spring dynamics rather than just tracking height.
  • On real hardware, cuts cycle-averaged electrical power by 30.7% and mean thrust by 49.8% versus a tuned PID baseline, while keeping repeatable hop heights and tighter landings.
  • A rare case of RL beating classical control on both efficiency and precision on physical hardware rather than in simulation only.
Show abstract

Combining aerial thrust with spring-loaded hopping makes monopedal quadcopters promising for locomotion over complex terrain, but heuristic proportional-integral-derivative (PID) tuning limits coordination between active thrust and passive contact dynamics. We present a direct estimated-state-to-motor Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) policy that commands four motors without an explicit hopping state machine or low-level attitude PID. Its reward combines Energy-Manifold Shaping for mass-normalized vertical-energy tracking and apex-state anchoring with Efficiency Shaping, which uses a history-aware power estimator to penalize general power use, impose an additional airborne-power cost, and penalize airborne near-stationarity. In representative hardware runs, the PPO-based control stack reduced cycle-averaged measured electrical power by 30.7% and mean total normalized thrust by 49.8% relative to the tuned PID-based control stack, while retaining repeatable commanded-height hopping and more concentrated landings. These observations are consistent with improved use of passive dynamics and reduced measured electrical demand.

Dynamics-Informed Reinforcement Learning for Agile and Energy-Efficient Locomotion of a Monopedal Hopping Quadcopter
2026-09-14 cs.RO Y. Or (h=21)
Ruigang Chen, Qi Zhang, Zhicheng Zhong, Zhuorui Yun, Yizhar Or
Core Contributions
  • From the same group as the companion spring-legged hopping paper: targets ‘reward hacking’ in RL for hybrid hopping dynamics by constraining optimization to a physically viable Specific-Energy manifold, instead of letting an unconstrained reward invent exploits.
  • Adds phase-consistent stance-phase impulse shaping so the policy learns bio-inspired push-off timing implicitly, again avoiding a hand-coded state machine.
  • Demonstrates forward velocity tracking up to 2.0 m/s in MuJoCo despite severe attitude-contact coupling—a harder regime than pure vertical hopping.
  • Reports 82% and 73% energy reductions versus hovering and an inefficiency baseline respectively, a larger simulated efficiency gain than the companion paper's hardware numbers, suggesting the reward-shaping approach scales well even before hardware transfer.
Show abstract

Although aerial-legged robots offer combined agility and efficiency, controlling high-speed hopping under complex hybrid dynamics is challenging. Reinforcement Learning (RL) is promising but prone to energy-inefficient "reward hacking". We propose a Dynamics-Informed RL framework for a monopedal hopping quadcopter. By embedding a target Specific Energy into the reward, we constrain the optimization to a physically viable energy manifold, ensuring stable hopping behaviour. By rewarding the phase-consistent behavior, it can encourage bio-inspired stance-phase impulse. Furthermore, penalizing the electro-mechanical power waste induces the motors generate an efficient impulse. This enables the policy to inject energy strictly during spring restitution without heuristic state machines. MuJoCo simulations validate robust height regulation and forward velocity tracking up to 2.0 m/s despite severe attitude-contact coupling. Ultimately, our approach yields a highly agile hopping gait, reducing energy consumption by 82% and 73% compared to hovering baselines and inefficiency baseline, respectively.
