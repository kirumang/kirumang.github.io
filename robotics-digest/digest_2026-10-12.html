🔭 Research Landscape

The most provocative result in today's batch is #6: a frozen frontier VLM (GPT-6-Astra) used directly as the policy scores 92.1% on the memory-dependent RoboMME benchmark, beating the reported human-with-oracle-skills score (90.5%) and a hierarchical system that pairs the same model with a trained low-level policy (79.1%). It sits alongside a cluster of papers that push general-purpose language models deeper into the stack without fine-tuning them: D-World (#19) keeps the LLM frozen and instead makes the world model explicit and self-correcting as an executable digital twin; #21 shows an LLM coding agent can be steered from imperative defaults to declarative reactive behaviors with expert-written guidelines; ProAgent (#27) asks the model to decide when to act unprompted. The counterweight comes from the evaluation papers: the Habitat long-horizon benchmark (#8) finds simple ReAct beats VLM-TAMP and Tru-POMDP yet tops out at 45% success, and #30 finds that asking LLM agents to predict a partner's next action lowers success in 15 of 16 configurations. Language models are increasingly capable controllers, but explicit reasoning about other agents or uncertainty is still shallow.

A second theme is skepticism toward cheap proxies in imitation learning. The audit in #3 is a model of careful science: published smoothness metrics for demonstration curation (SAL, TED) mostly encode episode length, and curating by TED actually cuts downstream success by a third (0.240 vs 0.360). Two other papers locate the value of supervision in subtle places: #10 shows that human interventions arrive late and proposes learning a cost from where and when experts intervene, while FOMO-Dex (#18) shows that raw teleop commands are worse imitation targets than force-corrected ones. VAQ (#14) makes a related point about representation: action quantization should preserve state reachability (and therefore value), not reconstruction error. Together they argue that what you train on matters more than the policy class, a useful corrective to scale-only narratives.

Third, formal guarantees are being reshaped to fit real robot objectives. Four control papers extend safety filters beyond forward invariance: recurrence/liveness via backup reachability CBFs (#7), infinite-horizon reach-avoid certificates for stochastic hybrid contact systems (#9, collisions 68→0 in ball dribbling), joint-feasibility margins that anticipate when multiple HOCBF constraints will exhaust bounded acceleration (#25), and anytime-valid conformal target retention for multi-agent interception (#23). Meanwhile, generative policies are being steered at inference time rather than retrained: MOSAIC (#24) composes frozen diffusion priors under new constraints, and PAF (#11) adds local anchors to SE(3)-equivariant policies. The emerging direction is a stack where pretrained priors (diffusion models, VLMs) supply behavior and lightweight, verifiable layers supply constraint satisfaction.

Can a Frontier VLM Serve as a Robot Policy for Memory-Dependent Manipulation?

📅 2026-10-12cs.RO👤 Jack B. Jedlicki (h=n/a)
Jack B. Jedlicki, Amish Sethi, Heng Yang
Core Contributions
  • A frozen GPT-6-Astra, with no RoboMME training, outputs every end-effector waypoint and gripper command while keeping the full episode in context.
  • Reaches 92.1% on 800 test episodes, above the human-with-oracle-skills score (90.5%), the best leaderboard entry (60.8%), and a hierarchical variant using the same model plus a trained policy (79.1%).
  • The gap versus the hierarchical system suggests that handing control to a separately trained low-level policy can hurt when memory and timing matter.
  • Remaining 63 failures cluster in two tasks (premature completion, mistimed button presses), giving a concrete map of where frontier models still slip.
Show abstract
We use a frozen frontier vision-language model, GPT-6-Astra, directly as the robot policy on RoboMME, a benchmark of 16 memory-dependent manipulation tasks spanning temporal, spatial, object, and procedural memory. The model keeps the whole episode in context and outputs every end-effector waypoint and gripper command, with no RoboMME-specific training. On the 800 test episodes it succeeds in 92.1%, above the previously reported human score of 90.5%, which was obtained with an oracle skill interface, and far ahead of the best leaderboard entry (60.8%) and of a hierarchical system that pairs the same model with a RoboMME-trained policy (79.1%). It matches or exceeds the human score on 13 of the 16 tasks. Its 63 failures concentrate in two tasks and are mostly premature completions and mistimed or unintended button presses. These results blur the usual division between long-horizon planning, memory and end-effector control, and raise the question of how much of this stack a single general-purpose model can take over in embodied systems. Project website: https://embodied-astra.github.io. Code, prompts and all episode logs: https://github.com/jackbjed/embodied-astra-robomme.

D-World: An Explicit World-Language-Action Model with Recursive Self-Improvement under Dual Control

📅 2026-10-12cs.ROcs.AI👤 Xuan Wen (h=n/a)
Xuan Wen, Baichao Wang
Core Contributions
  • Makes the robot's world model explicit: an LLM perception agent writes the scene as an executable digital twin that can be inspected and corrected.
  • After each action chunk, named physical parameters are revised against measurements and only accepted if replay residuals do not grow, a verifiable form of 'recursive self-improvement' with a frozen LLM.
  • Frames probing actions as dual control and shows that cheap local revision lowers the threshold at which exploration pays off.
  • On a dual-arm robot, a single revision cut a bread-depth residual from 22.4 mm to 1.5 mm, avoiding full scene reconstruction.
Show abstract
Frontier large language models (LLMs) now reason about space well enough to program robots, yet the world models behind robot policies are implicit, held in network weights or predicted video, and cannot be inspected or corrected directly. We present D-World, an explicit world-language-action model built on the Sense, Plan and Act decomposition. A perception agent writes the scene as an executable digital twin using generated vision and geometry code. A planning agent searches for actions in simulation and, after each executed action chunk, revises the twin's object states and named physical parameters against what the robot measured. A parameter revision is accepted only if replaying the latest chunk does not increase any measured residual beyond its tolerance, and every chunk is checked in the updated twin before it runs. Structural mismatches return to perception for reconstruction. We call this measurement-grounded cycle of correction and revision recursive self-improvement (RSI), in which the LLM stays frozen while the world it acts in improves. We model the choice of actions that both advance the task and expose model error as a dual control problem and show that the cost of incorporating evidence into the twin sets the threshold at which probing pays, so cheap local revision counteracts the turn-off of cautious controllers. Each episode runs a real-to-sim-to-real loop, and its recorded twins can be refitted and re-rendered offline into higher-fidelity environments for post-training. Because perception and planning communicate only through the twin and language, either can be replaced by a learned model, and since inference dominates the loop, the loop speeds up with faster models. On a dual-arm robot, towel and toaster tasks show local revisions, such as one reducing the bread's rendered-depth residual from 22.4 to 1.5 mm, that let execution continue without reconstruction.

Declarative Reactive Robot Behaviors from Imperative Language Models

📅 2026-10-12cs.RO👤 Vincent Berenz (h=n/a)
Vincent Berenz, Tasha Kim, Bernhard Schölkopf
Core Contributions
  • Targets open-ended behaviors (patrolling, greeting) rather than terminating tasks, which suit declarative reactive programs that LLMs rarely see in training.
  • Shows that a reusable set of expert-written guidelines redirects an LLM coding agent from imperative habits to correct reactive Playful scripts.
  • The agent co-designs Python sensing/acting modules and the declarative behavior logic starting from a generic robot API.
  • Guidelines generalize across robots and behaviors, supporting both one-shot generation and conversational refinement.
Show abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) are predominantly trained on imperative programming languages, and existing work on LLM-based robot programming reflects this: it focuses on goal-directed tasks expressed as plans. However, many robotic applications require not tasks but behaviors--open-ended, continuous patterns of sensory-motor engagement with no termination condition (e.g., greeting visitors, patrolling an area). Such behaviors are better served by declarative and reactive programming, on which LLMs have virtually no training. We investigate whether this gap can be bridged. Using Playful, a reactive scripting language, we show that an LLM-based coding agent can generate correct declarative and reactive programs for robotic behaviors. These programs are produced from under-specified natural language descriptions, given suitable context and a set of reusable programming guidelines. The guidelines encode design principles that redirect the agent from imperative defaults toward proper reactive patterns. Developed once by an expert, they generalize across robots and behaviors. Starting from only the generic robot operating system API, the agent jointly designs both the imperative component (Python modules for sensing, acting, and evaluating) and the declarative component (the reactive script that composes them into behavior logic). The declarative nature of reactive programming supports both incremental refinement through conversational turns and one-shot generation of complete behaviors.

Proactive Physical AI through Continuous Observation and Timely Intervention

📅 2026-10-12cs.CVcs.AIcs.RO👤 Zhenhua Yang (h=n/a)
Zhenhua Yang, Jiafeng Liang, Xingyu Zhou, Yi Jiang, Xiayu Cao
Core Contributions
  • Defines Proactive Physical AI: the system must notice, without instruction, when a situation warrants action and act at the right moment.
  • Separates a streaming timing model (whether/when to intervene) from a response model (what to say or which device capability to invoke).
  • An automated pipeline converts ordinary videos into supervision for intervention timing, enabling scale.
  • Achieves more accurate timing with fewer false triggers than the backbone baseline, and works on a real robot.
Show abstract
Physical AI is commonly formulated around an explicitly specified task: given a goal or instruction, a system perceives, reasons, and acts. In many real-world settings, however, important tasks emerge from continuously changing world states before a person explicitly formulates them. We study this complementary problem as Proactive Physical AI, where an AI system continuously observes the physical world, identifies situations that warrant intervention, and initiates appropriate actions at the right time. We develop ProAgent, an end-to-end system built around two components: a Jev-style proactive timing model (dubbed StreamingJev) that determines whether and when intervention is needed, and a Response Model that generates responses for people or invokes available device capabilities for physical execution. To support learning at scale, we develop an automated data pipeline that converts videos into supervision for proactive timing. Experiments demonstrate strong proactive performance under both explicit objectives and standing roles, combining more accurate intervention timing with fewer false triggers than the source-backbone baseline. Real-robot evaluations further demonstrate ProAgent's ability to identify emerging needs and translate them into appropriate physical actions.

Generative Flow on Symbolic Abstractions for Embodiment Capability Discovery

📅 2026-10-12cs.ROcs.AIcs.LGcs.SC👤 Youwei Yu (h=n/a)
Youwei Yu, Lantao Liu
Core Contributions
  • Generates training tasks grounded in what a specific robot can actually do, instead of LLM-sampled tasks that are only semantically plausible.
  • A GFlowNet builds symbolic task skeletons; each is compiled into a simulator and rewarded by low-level learning success, steering generation to the capability frontier.
  • Discovers diverse, executable tasks across heterogeneous robots, including non-obvious learnable behaviors.
  • Distilling discovered tasks into one language-conditioned policy improves unseen long-horizon tasks, offering an automatic-curriculum alternative to hand design.
Show abstract
Achieving complex, long-horizon robot behavior remains challenging because successful execution depends on skills grounded in a robot's physical affordances. Existing training-task distributions are often handcrafted or sampled from large language models (LLMs), relying on semantic plausibility rather than embodiment-specific feasibility. This mismatch can produce curricula that are too difficult, redundant, or blind to non-obvious but learnable behaviors. We propose Generative Flow on Symbolic Abstractions (FOSA), a framework for discovering embodiment-specific capabilities by learning a grounded task generator. FOSA parameterizes an abstract policy as a Generative Flow Network that constructs symbolic task skeletons. Each skeleton is compiled into a simulation environment, where low-level policy-learning performance provides the reward signal for the abstract policy. Across heterogeneous robot platforms, FOSA discovers diverse and executable tasks near the robot's empirical capability frontier. After distilling the discovered task policies into a single language-conditioned policy, FOSA improves performance on unseen complex and long-horizon tasks. The code and demonstration videos are available at https://www.youwei-yu.com/fosa.

Evaluating Long-Horizon Robot Planning under Uncertainty

📅 2026-10-12cs.RO👤 Harsh Muriki (h=n/a)
Harsh Muriki, Gayathri Aravindan, Yuchen Zheng, Sonia Chernova
Core Contributions
  • Provides a controlled benchmark (7 household tasks, 90 task-environment-memory combinations, 1,350 episodes in Habitat) that varies how wrong the robot's initial memory is.
  • The simplest planner, ReAct, wins (45.3% success, 71.7% completion) over VLM-TAMP and Tru-POMDP, questioning the payoff of heavier structured planners.
  • Counter-intuitively, accurate initial memory does not always produce the best results, indicating planners use prior knowledge inconsistently.
  • Rule-based trace analysis surfaces recurring placement, precondition, and redundant-action errors that aggregate success rates hide.
Show abstract
Household robots must successfully navigate im-perfect circumstances, both in their internal models and the external world. We introduce a benchmark of seven long-horizon household tasks in Habitat that vary initial robot memory and environment configurations. We evaluate adaptations of ReAct, VLM-TAMP, and Tru-POMDP within a shared execution framework across 90 tasks-environment-memory combinations, totaling 1,350 episodes. Our evaluation measures task success, partial completion, and action efficiency, along with rule-based analysis of execution traces. ReAct achieves the highest overall task success (45.3%) and completion (71.7%), while having accurate initial memory does not consistently yield the highest performance for every planner. We identify recurring placement errors, precondition errors, and redundant actions through trace analysis. These findings highlight differences in how the evaluated planners use initial knowledge and respond to uncertainty.

Interactive Navigation Beyond Reachability: Benchmark and Agentic Framework

📅 2026-10-12cs.ROcs.AI👤 Delun Li (h=n/a)
Delun Li, Xueyao Wei, Rui Chen, Yifei Wang, Junzhi Yu
Core Contributions
  • Extends ObjectGoal navigation to cases where the agent must change the world (open doors, open containers) to reach or reveal the target.
  • MultINav-Bench (3K episodes) annotates whether interaction is required or unnecessary, and scores navigation success, interaction effectiveness, and cost separately.
  • MultINav-Agent maintains a state-conditioned interaction graph and uses a VLM to choose interactions by expected change in connectivity and visibility.
  • Consistently outperforms adapted baselines across channel, container, and mixed tasks.
Show abstract
Existing embodied navigation tasks commonly assume that traversability and object states remain fixed during an episode. Yet reaching a goal may require opening a door to access a room and then opening a container to reveal a hidden target. We formulate interactive navigation beyond reachability, unifying channel-induced changes in spatial connectivity, container-induced changes in target visibility, and their sequential dependencies. We introduce MultINav-Bench, a 3K-episode ObjectGoal benchmark in MolmoSpaces spanning channel, container, and mixed tasks, with interaction-necessity annotations covering required and unnecessary episodes. Its data generation pipeline automates task construction across multiple task types. The evaluation protocol separately measures navigation success, interaction effectiveness, and cost. We further propose MultINav-Agent, a three-stage agentic framework built on a persistent, state-conditioned interaction graph. Semantic interactive perception grounds entities and interaction states; a vision-language model selects task-relevant actions based on expected changes in connectivity and target observability; and post-interaction verification updates the graph to guide subsequent navigation, exploration, or interaction. Experiments on MultINav-Bench show that MultINav-Agent consistently outperforms adapted baselines across channel, container, and mixed tasks.

Observe, Share, or Predict? Disentangling Partner Information in Embodied Multi-Agent Collaboration

📅 2026-10-12cs.AIcs.RO👤 Adrián Szlatincsán (h=n/a)
Adrián Szlatincsán, Filippo Ziliotto, Bruno Lepri, Luciano Serafini, Massimiliano Luca
Core Contributions
  • Isolates three forms of partner information for LLM agents without dialogue: observing the partner's state (OAS), sharing object knowledge (KE), and predicting the partner's next action (OANA).
  • OAS improves task quality and reduces wasted effort; KE mainly improves efficiency (up to 25% fewer steps).
  • Explicitly predicting the partner's next action lowers success in 15 of 16 settings, suggesting current LLM 'theory of mind' is shallow.
  • Coordination gains come from reacting to shared context rather than anticipation, with clear asymmetries between robot and simulated human roles.
Show abstract
LLMs are used as high-level controllers for embodied agents, but it remains unclear whether they support collaborative reasoning, such as anticipating a collaborator's actions in real-time teamwork without dialogue. We ask if observing the partner's state, exchanging object knowledge, and predicting the partner's next action, shape task outcomes and coordination when agents can share percepts but never intentions, plans, or task-level messages, and whether the two agents occupy symmetric roles. Using the Habitat-based PARTNR benchmark, we introduce a Decoupled Partial Observability regime that enforces strictly individual perception, and we augment decentralized zero-shot ReAct planners with Other Agent's State (OAS) observation, object Knowledge Exchange (KE) between co-located agents, and Other Agent's Next Action (OANA) prediction, evaluated on the full 2 x 2 x 2 grid with four planners on 1,000 validation tasks. OAS observation improves task quality, reducing extraneous effort for every planner, whereas KE primarily improves execution efficiency, cutting simulation steps by up to 25% with little change in success. The two are largely redundant for strong planners but complementary for the weakest, for which KE without the OAS is the worst configuration. OANA prediction lowers the success rate in 15 of 16 model x context pairs while raising explicit partner mentions to 55-100%, and its accuracy gains lie mainly in the coarse action type, partly because agents recognize the action the partner is already performing. The robot retrieves objects sooner and completes its tasks earlier than the simulated human. Shared context reduces total task time by shortening the remaining duration that the human works alone. Also, coordination benefits arise from responding to shared context, especially the OAS, rather than explicit anticipation, yielding shallow, planner-dependent partner modeling.

UniLAP: A Unified Latent Action Policy for Cross-Embodiment Manipulation

📅 2026-10-12cs.ROcs.CV👤 Lei Yao (h=n/a)
Lei Yao, Qiao Feng, Mutian Tong, Qihao Yun, Lap-Pui Chau
Core Contributions
  • A continuous latent-action policy that avoids the granularity bottleneck of discrete latents and does not need predicted future frames at inference.
  • Distills a latent-action-model teacher with a flow-matching objective, keeping multimodality while producing directly executable latents.
  • Label-free motion targets decouple latents from appearance changes, yielding an embodiment-agnostic manifold that adapts quickly to new platforms.
  • At 1.1B parameters, beats latent-action and VLA baselines on hand-pose forecasting, single-arm, humanoid, and real-world tasks.
Show abstract
Latent Action Models (LAMs) can capture manipulation priors from action-free videos, but discrete latents usually impose a granularity bottleneck, limiting their ability to represent fine-grained action. Recent continuous alternatives mitigate this, but they are typically used as proxies for world modeling and require a further planning step to produce executable actions. We thus introduce UniLAP, a unified continuous latent-action policy that models the latent space via flow matching and bypasses reliance on future frames during inference. By distilling a LAM teacher with a flow-adaptive objective, UniLAP inherits the teacher's action representation while retaining the multimodal distribution of feasible behaviors. To decouple latent representations from task-irrelevant appearance, we extract a label-free motion target to isolate relevant kinematic cues from spurious visual dynamics. With the established embodiment-agnostic latent manifold, UniLAP can be easily adapted to novel platforms. Pretrained on multi-source videos at a compact 1.1B parameter scale, UniLAP outperforms state-of-the-art latent-action and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) baselines across hand pose forecasting, single-arm and humanoid manipulation, and real-world deployment.

MOSAIC: Mode- and Support-Aware Inference-Time Control of Robot Diffusion Models

📅 2026-10-12cs.RO👤 Sabrina Bodmer (h=n/a)
Sabrina Bodmer, Rahel Rickenbach, René Zurbrügg, Melanie N. Zeilinger, Colin Jones
Core Contributions
  • Gradient-free inference-time steering of pretrained diffusion policies that avoids both pushing samples off the learned support and collapsing modes.
  • Treats initial noise and every reverse-step perturbation as a joint input, adapting its distribution with importance weights computed on fully denoised outputs.
  • Mode-wise updates prevent averaging between modes into infeasible actions; adaptive penalties balance feasibility against staying in-distribution.
  • Can coordinate multiple independently trained diffusion models through shared costs, e.g., collaborative box manipulation without a joint model.
Show abstract
Generative policies such as diffusion models provide expressive data-driven priors for robot behavior, yet the situations a robot faces at execution time rarely match its training data and impose constraints and objectives the policy never learned. Existing inference-time guidance methods either rely on gradients that can push samples away from the learned prior, or on sampling procedures that can be inefficient, collapse modes, or average them into out-of-distribution predictions. We introduce MOSAIC, a mode- and support-aware framework for gradient-free inference-time control of robotic diffusion models. MOSAIC treats the initial noise and all stochastic reverse-step perturbations as a joint input to the pretrained denoising process and adapts a distribution over this input using importance weights computed from fully denoised outputs. Physical feasibility and consistency with the learned support are balanced using adaptive penalties, while mode-wise importance-weighted updates preserve modes and avoid destructive averaging. The same formulation can jointly steer multiple independently trained diffusion models through shared coupling costs, enabling coordinated behavior without requiring a joint model. We evaluate MOSAIC on navigation, dexterous grasping, motion planning with unseen geometry, and collaborative box manipulation with independently trained robot priors. Across these settings, MOSAIC adapts and composes frozen diffusion priors under new deployment constraints without retraining.

Value-Preserving Action Quantization

📅 2026-10-12cs.ROcs.LG👤 Max Muchen Sun (h=n/a)
Max Muchen Sun, Jueun Kwon, Benjamin Riviere, Todd Murphey
Core Contributions
  • Proves that the value error from action quantization is tightly bounded by the reconstruction error of state reachability, not of the actions themselves.
  • VAQ therefore quantizes actions to preserve their effects on future states, making discrete search (MCTS) valid for continuous robot control.
  • VAQ-MCTS matches strong BC baselines and is markedly more data-efficient and robust to heterogeneous demonstrations, with zero-shot adaptation to new environments.
  • Solves all 30 real-world pushing trials using only 10 minutes of demonstrations.
Show abstract
Search-based policy learning, such as Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS), offers the flexibility to trade runtime computation for data efficiency and adaptation to novel environments. For robot learning, however, applying search requires discretizing the continuous action space and associated dynamics, typically through action quantization. While reconstruction-based quantization is sufficient for representing the continuous action space alone, policy learning additionally requires the quantized actions to preserve their effects on future states and values. We show that such requirement is not necessarily captured by action reconstruction error. Instead, we prove that the error in the value function resulting from action quantization is tightly bounded by the reconstruction error of state reachability under the quantized actions. Based on these results, we propose value-preserving action quantization (VAQ), which quantizes actions by preserving state reachability. We further combine VAQ with MCTS (VAQ-MCTS) as a value-preserving, search-based policy learning framework for behavioral cloning. Using a wide range of object pushing tasks as a controlled benchmark, we evaluate VAQ-MCTS against state-of-the-art behavioral cloning methods. VAQ-MCTS matches or exceeds their performance under standard settings, while substantially improving data efficiency and robustness to heterogeneous demonstrations, while enabling zero-shot adaptation to novel environments. Hardware experiments further demonstrate the effectiveness of VAQ-MCTS, completing all 30 real-world object pushing trials with only 10 minutes of demonstration data.

Phase-Conditioned Affordance Fields for SE(3)-Equivariant Visuomotor Policies

📅 2026-10-12cs.ROcs.AI👤 Xinzhe Chen (h=n/a)
Xinzhe Chen, Liqi Huang, Sihua Ren, Haowen Sun, Mingyang Li
Core Contributions
  • Breaks the 'global rigid transform' assumption of SE(3)-equivariant policies, which fails when the task-relevant object changes across task phases.
  • Learns a phase-conditioned per-point affordance score; its weighted centroid becomes a moving canonical anchor replacing the fixed gripper frame.
  • Adds local inductive bias while keeping global equivariance, consistently improving MimicGen success under varied spatial randomization.
  • Maintains its lead on real multi-object tasks under lighting changes.
Show abstract
SE(3)-equivariance provides a useful geometric inductive bias for visuomotor policies, encoding that actions covary with rigid transformations of the observation. However, existing SE(3)-equivariant visuomotor policies build this bias on a global equivariance assumption that treats the entire observation as governed by a single rigid transformation, which breaks down in real-world manipulation tasks where the task-relevant region changes across different objects. We address this by learning a phase-conditioned affordance field (PAF) over the observed point cloud, a per-point score that the SE(3)-equivariant policy uses in two complementary ways. Its score-weighted centroid serves as a learned, phase-conditioned anchor that canonicalizes the observation and the action around the active region, replacing the fixed gripper-frame reference, and the score itself is appended as a per-point feature. This introduces a local geometric inductive bias, while preserving the policy's global SE(3) equivariance. We validate on multiple MimicGen tasks under varying spatial randomization, and PAF consistently improves the success rate of the underlying policy. On multi-object real-robot tasks, PAF outperforms baselines and keeps its lead under lighting variations.

MIRROR: Reflecting Hidden Symmetries from Offline Robot Interaction Data

📅 2026-10-12cs.RO👤 Shiqi Liu (h=n/a)
Shiqi Liu, Yihang Yao, Peide Huang, Mengdi Xu, Ding Zhao
Core Contributions
  • Learns how a known symmetry group acts jointly on pixel observations and actions from offline data, removing the need for hand-specified, viewpoint-dependent image transforms.
  • Adversarial tuple matching over transition tuples plus group-axiom regularization yields a 61% lower compatibility error than an adapted LaLiGAN.
  • Using the learned transforms for frame-averaged BC lifts mean Robosuite success from 80.5% to 85.1% (up to +9.7 pts per task).
  • Policies trained in simulation on heightmaps/segmentation masks transfer zero-shot to a real Kinova GEN3.
Show abstract
Symmetry-aware robot policy learning requires coupled observation-action transformations. For pixel observations, even simple physical symmetries can induce complex, viewpoint-dependent image transformations that are difficult to specify by hand. Existing symmetry-aware methods often rely on representations with predefined transformations, requiring preprocessing tailored to the sensing setup and task. We introduce MIRROR, a framework that learns how a known symmetry group operates jointly on observations and actions in partially observable Markov decision processes (POMDPs) from offline interaction data. MIRROR couples these transformations through adversarial tuple matching over one-step transition tuples, while explicit group-consistency regularization penalizes violations of the group-operation axioms. On four Robosuite tabletop manipulation tasks, MIRROR achieves the lowest aggregate error on three of four symmetry-quality metrics, including a 61% reduction in compatibility error (which measures consistency under transformation composition) relative to an adapted LaLiGAN baseline. Using the learned transformations in frame-averaged behavior cloning (BC) yields higher mean success rates than vanilla BC on all four tasks, increasing average success from 80.48% to 85.12%, with per-task gains up to 9.66 percentage points. We also repeat symmetry discovery and policy training in simulation with heightmap and segmentation-mask observations, then deploy the resulting policy zero-shot on a real Kinova GEN3 arm. Project page: https://pomdp-mirror.github.io.

Act4R: Action-conditioned 4D reconstruction for kinematics-free control

📅 2026-10-12cs.RO👤 Zhaoliang Zhang (h=n/a)
Zhaoliang Zhang, Chenhao Yu, Zhimin Shao, Xijun Liu, Cheng Peng

Understanding how many aircraft, and which traffic configurations, can be safely and efficiently managed by air traffic controllers within a given airspace is fundamental to air traffic management. Air traffic controllers continuously modify aircraft trajectories through interventions such as speed advisories and radar vectoring, and the patterns of these interventions are difficult to capture effectively using existing air traffic simulators. To address this limitation, we propose Diffusion Transformers for Traffic Scene Generation (DiTSG), which learns to generate air traffic scenes from recorded multi-aircraft trajectories. DiTSG employs stacked multi-agent inverted Transformer layers as the denoising backbone of a diffusion transformer, jointly generates the trajectories of multiple aircraft within a traffic scene, and conditions the generation on agent-specific required times of arrival. The resulting trajectories generated by DiTSG preserve realistic inter-aircraft interactions observed in actual air traffic operations. Experiments show that the DiTSG generates diverse traffic scenes that reproduce the statistical properties of recorded traffic while preserving realistic inter-aircraft interactions.
