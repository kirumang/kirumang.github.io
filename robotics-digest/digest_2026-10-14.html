🔭 Research Landscape

The sharpest thread in today's batch is a re-examination of what a VLA's action space should look like. Sreenath and Qu's group (#2) argues that naive uniform-bin discrete actions match or beat continuous ones, and that the real weakness of discrete policies is factorization error — not precision — which discrete flows and classifier-free guidance can repair. Coordi-π (#29) attacks a related structural problem from the other side: instead of concatenating both arm–hand vectors, it interleaves per-side tokens with a hand-identity embedding and factorized RoPE, lifting π0.5 from 31.9% to 51.3% on DexJoCo. RAIN (#30) asks how much generative iteration is actually needed per state, and #27 systematically disentangles four ways of bolting world models onto VLAs, finding 2D semantic futures and a decoupled MoT expert the most robust. Read together, these papers suggest the community is moving from "scale the backbone" to careful engineering of the action interface and its inductive biases.

A second, strongly convergent theme is improving policies after they ship, without new human demonstrations. AdaRSI (#12) uses a coding agent to decide whether a failure is a harness problem or a missing motor skill, fine-tuning GR00T only on the latter at 28% of the GPU cost. Back on Track (#14) monitors a black-box π0.5 with a liveness value and plans corrective actions in a latent world model; TEMPO (#26) adapts locomotion actors online through a frozen critic; WEDGE (#28) turns diagnosed navigation failures into procedurally generated training worlds; Robota-Embed's Embed2Evolve (#5) grows task-local memory around a frozen GPT-6 Astra policy. Two surveys (#21 on agentic robots, #4 on multimodal agent memory) try to put structure on this fast-moving space — and #25 provides a sobering counterpoint: its log audit shows a GRPO loop granting full reward to a 0.95 m miss because the verdict came from the program's own output, a warning that self-improvement loops are only as good as their independent success checks.

Third, data infrastructure for contact remains a bottleneck being attacked at scale. TacClaw-Infinity (#7) claims 10,000 hours / 1B frames of synchronized vision-tactile-action data from a UMI-style handheld device, FOMI (#8) adds sensorless force feedback so operators can actually feel contact during robot-free collection, and the tactile survey (#18) frames the shift from touch-as-feedback to touch-as-foundation-modality. Elsewhere, a quieter efficiency theme connects classical work: VIGOR-MPC (#6) cuts MPC computation by >90% by learning cost parameters from vision, tiered mapping (#9) shrinks maps 13–15× by letting the planner tell the mapper where resolution matters, and RAIN (#30) cuts policy latency 5.7× — all three spending compute only where the decision actually depends on it. Finally, #1's finding that a kinematic prior appears in first-layer humanoid weights under both RL and IL hints that embodiment structure may be learnable as a reusable object, a natural complement to PolyMorph's (#13) explicit URDF-conditioned cross-embodiment policy.

When Do Discrete Actions Make Good Continuous Controllers?

2026-10-14 cs.RO 👤 K. Sreenath (h=59)
Zeji Yi, Soham Dasgupta, Xinyang Zhang, Koushil Sreenath, Guannan Qu
Core Contributions
  • Directly challenges the consensus that robot foundation models need continuous actions: across single-task and multi-task VLA training, sim and real, even naive uniform-bin discrete actions match or beat continuous parameterizations.
  • Pinpoints the actual failure mode of discrete policies as factorization error from product distributions across time and action dimensions — not quantization precision, which is the usual objection.
  • Shows that discrete flows (iterative computation) or classifier-free-guidance sharpening fix this, and even make discrete policies more responsive to mid-chunk action changes than continuous ones.
  • A useful negative result: injecting continuous geometry into discrete corruption processes does not help consistently — implying robot actions may not need specialized machinery distinct from LLM-style token prediction.
Show abstract
Though modern AI models like LLMs predict discrete tokens, today's robotic foundation models represent actions as continuous vectors. In this paper, we challenge this common consensus by asking if continuous action parametrizations are superior to their discrete counterparts. We conduct an extensive comparison of single-step regression and multi-step flow variants of both continuous and discrete policies in both from-scratch single-task and multi-task VLA training, in both simulation and on real hardware. In all regimes, we find that discrete action prediction, even the most naive, uniform-bin quantization, can match or even outperform continuous-action policies in task success rate. Moreover, we find that the challenge to discrete action policies is \emph{not} action precision, but rather factorization error introduced by product-distribution predictions across time and action dimensions. We show that, when paired with the correct interventions---iterative computation through discrete flows, or distribution sharpening through classifier-free guidance---discrete flow policies can turn this weakness into a strength, enabling more responsive action changes during a predicted action chunk. Lastly, we show that attempts to explicitly inject geometric information into discrete policies, e.g. through corruption processes that emulate continuous flow matching, do \emph{not} yield consistent improvements. Taken together, our findings question existing assumptions that robotic action prediction requires distinct design decisions compared to other AI domains, and motivates the possibility of discrete action prediction as a compelling alternative to today's continuous action parametrizations. Robota-Embed: Unified VLA Embeddings for Robot In-Context Learning and Evolution

2026-10-14 cs.ROcs.CV 👤 Yu-Ke Zhu (h=46)
Yunze Liu, Jing Wang, Shenrui Wu, Wenkun He, Guanqi Zhan
Core Contributions
  • First embedding model to jointly encode vision, language and action for both current-state queries and recorded future continuations, so retrieval returns 'what should happen next', not just similar-looking scenes.
  • Trained on ~1.7M curated anchors; on its 4,000-query benchmark reaches 81.77% episode-level R@1 — 33.5 points above action-free Gemini Embedding 2 — and ablations show dropping action hurts more than dropping vision.
  • Embed2ICL plugs retrieved experience into policies: DROID-Sim success 47.0%→56.3%, LIBERO-difficult 86.4%→92.8%, with gains carrying to real robots.
  • Embed2Evolve builds task-local memory around a frozen GPT-6 Astra policy: 68% vs. 24% success on five ManiSkill tasks, with ~79.5% fewer policy tokens than an Omni-Embed-based harness.
Show abstract
Retrieving useful context for a robot requires matching its task, execution stage, and the continuation that should follow. We introduce Robota-Embed, to our knowledge the first vision-language-action (VLA) embedding model to jointly encode all three modalities in both current-state queries and recorded future continuations. Predictive retrieval training aligns these endpoints, enabling historical continuations to serve as context at inference time. We construct a multi-source corpus of 1,695,684 cleaned training anchors and introduce Robota-Embed-Bench, with 4,000 queries and a shared 8,000-entry gallery. Robota-Embed achieves 47.33% clip-level and 81.77% episode-level R@1, exceeding action-free Gemini Embedding 2 by 22.09 and 33.47 percentage points. Controlled retrieval studies show that removing native action degrades retrieval more than removing vision. We study two complementary applications. Embed2ICL uses retrieved experience for training-free and training-based policy conditioning, increasing success from 47.0% to 56.3% in DROID-Sim and from 86.4% to 92.8% on the LIBERO difficult subset, while improving task progress on real robots. Embed2Evolve uses execution feedback to grow task-local memory and refine context use around a frozen GPT-6 Astra policy. Across five ManiSkill tasks, it achieves 68% success, compared with 24% for GPT-6 Astra Direct and 33% for a harness independently evolved with Omni-Embed-Nemotron-3B under the same protocol. Relative to the latter, it uses approximately 79.5% fewer policy tokens per test rollout, counting both successes and failures. These results show that a unified, action-aware retrieval space can support robot in-context learning and task-local context evolution.

One-shot Human Video-Guided Adaptation for Vision-Language-Action Models

2026-10-14 cs.RO 👤 Yu-Wei Wu (h=21)
Qi Chen, Yuwei Wu, Quan Chen, Kunpeng Gao, Jin Chen
Core Contributions
  • CapPro adapts a pretrained VLA to an unseen manipulation task from a single human video, with no target-task robot demos and no target-task fine-tuning.
  • A phase-aware video module selects a compact set of visual tokens and learns task queries supervised by phase captions — so the video conveys what to do, not an embodiment-matched trajectory to copy.
  • These representations are injected into the VLA's action-conditioning context while largely preserving the backbone, improving unseen target groups without degrading source-task performance.
Show abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) models have recently achieved remarkable progress by leveraging large-scale robot datasets and foundation models for general-purpose robot manipulation. However, their ability to rapidly adapt to novel manipulation tasks beyond the training distribution remains limited. Existing approaches often require task-specific robot demonstrations and additional training, which are labor- and compute-intensive, limiting the scalability of VLA adaptation to open-world scenarios. To reduce this dependence on target-task robot data, we propose CapPro, a framework that enables a VLA policy to adapt to previously unseen manipulation tasks using only a single human demonstration video. CapPro consists of two components. First, a phase-aware human video representation module constructs a compact video representation through learned visual token selection and learns task-relevant query representations under phase-caption supervision. Second, a video-conditioned adaptation module jointly incorporates the compact video and query-derived representations into the action-conditioning context of the pretrained VLA policy. % This design introduces task-specific guidance while largely preserving the original VLA action-generation backbone. In this way, the human demonstration provides task-specific execution guidance rather than an embodiment-aligned trajectory to imitate, without requiring target-task robot demonstrations or target-task fine-tuning. Experiments across source and unseen target tasks show that CapPro improves average performance across unseen target groups while maintaining comparable performance on the source tasks. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of human-video guidance for cross-task VLA adaptation.

What Matters for World Modeling in Vision-Language-Action Models?

2026-10-14 cs.ROcs.CV 👤 Jian-Hua Yang (h=16)
Qisen Ma, Yixiang Chen, Yuan Xu, Peiyan Li, Jiabing Yang
Core Contributions
  • Disentangles a confusing literature by grouping world-model-in-VLA methods into four paradigms (implicit future alignment, explicit future query, MoT future expert, external predictor) and re-implementing them in one framework.
  • Finds 2D semantic future representations give robust gains across paradigms, while other representations are paradigm-dependent.
  • No paradigm wins everywhere, but the decoupled MoT future expert is strongest overall and for OOD robustness, and decoupled designs integrate better with already-pretrained VLAs.
  • World modeling helps most on long-horizon and dynamic tasks — concrete guidance on where the extra complexity is worth it.
Show abstract
Integrating world models into Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) has emerged as a promising direction, enabling policies to anticipate future dynamics beyond reactive observation-to-action mapping. However, existing approaches vary simultaneously in their world representations, integration paradigms, and policy backbones, making it difficult to determine what truly matters for effective world model integration. To disentangle these factors, we categorize existing efforts into four representative paradigms, namely Implicit Future Alignment, Explicit Future Query, MoT Future Expert, and External World Predictor. We instantiate each within a unified framework and systematically evaluate these paradigms with diverse world representations and policy backbones across in-domain, out-of-domain, long-horizon, dynamic, few-shot, and real-world settings. Our study yields four main findings: 1) world representation effectiveness is paradigm-dependent, whereas 2D semantic representations offer robust, cross-paradigm gains; 2) no single paradigm is universally optimal, yet the decoupled MoT Future Expert offers competitive overall performance, particularly in OOD robustness; 3) world modeling consistently benefits policies built on standard VLMs, while decoupled designs exhibit greater compatibility with pretrained VLAs; and 4) world modeling improves performance over action-only baselines across multiple settings, with notable gains on long-horizon and dynamic tasks. Project page and video demonstrations are available at https://dylanmqs.github.io/WM-in-VLAs/.

Coordi-pi: Coordination-Aware Dual-Hand Action Representation for Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation

2026-10-14 cs.RO 👤 Ping Luo (h=15)
Yiming Meng, Yanming Shao, Weijie Shen, Kunyang Lin, Jiayu Sun
Core Contributions
  • Bimanual VLAs usually concatenate both arm–hand actions into one vector, leaving coordination implicit. Coordi-π instead emits per-side action tokens in an interleaved sequence.
  • A learnable hand-identity embedding plus a factorized RoPE (sequence position vs. control timestep) lets same-timestep actions share time while staying distinguishable.
  • On DexJoCo, average success rises over π0.5 from 31.9% to 51.3% (rand-obj) and 18.3% to 32.3% (rand-full), and it outperforms π0.5 on four real-world bimanual dexterous tasks.
Show abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) models have shown strong potential for general-purpose robotic manipulation, but extending them to bimanual dexterous manipulation introduces a high-dimensional and structured action space. Existing bimanual VLA policies commonly concatenate right- and left-side arm--hand actions at each control timestep, leaving cross-hand interactions implicit within a single action vector. We introduce Coordi-$π$, a structured action representation for bimanual dexterous manipulation. Coordi-$π$ represents the right- and left-side arm--hand actions as individual action tokens and organizes them into an interleaved sequence, explicitly exposing the two sides within the action sequence. To explicitly encode hand identity and temporal synchronization in this interleaved representation, a learnable hand-identity embedding distinguishes the two sides, while sequence--control-timestep factorized rotary positional encoding (RoPE) separates sequence position from control timestep, allowing actions at the same control timestep to share a temporal coordinate while retaining distinct sequence positions. Across all five bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks in DexJoCo, Coordi-$π$ achieves state-of-the-art performance under both \textit{rand-obj} and \textit{rand-full}, improving the average success rate over $π_{0.5}$ from 31.9\% to 51.3\% and from 18.3\% to 32.3\%, respectively. Coordi-$π$ also consistently outperforms $π_{0.5}$ across four real-world bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks.

Refine Actions If Needed: Adaptive Action Chunk Refinement for Robot Manipulation

2026-10-14 cs.RO 👤 Yu-Feng Yue (h=14)
Hanlin Liu, Zichen Zhou, Jiacheng Zhang, Liuyuan Na, Jin Jin
Core Contributions
  • Combines fast, lightweight policies with expensive iterative refinement by predicting — before refining — whether and how much each action proposal needs refining.
  • An ensemble learns refinement-level errors from offline demonstrations without explicit labels, calibrated to trade off uncertainty, compute and temporal consistency.
  • In simulation reaches 66.6% success, near the best diffusion policy, with 5.71× lower latency; on a real robot raises ACT from 55.0% to 72.5%.
  • Dynamic routing matches the best fixed-refinement level with 26.6% fewer refiner calls, showing per-state compute allocation pays off.
Show abstract
Computationally lightweight manipulation policies predict executable actions with low inference latency, but prediction errors can limit task success. Iterative generative policies can achieve higher task success rates, but repeated network evaluations increase inference latency. Refining action proposals from computationally lightweight policies can combine these strengths, but the amount of refinement needed varies across states. We introduce RAIN (Refine Actions If Needed), a framework that predicts action errors at different refinement levels to decide whether and how much to refine each executable proposal, before performing any refinement. We train an ensemble to predict these errors by comparing action proposals and their refined versions with expert actions from offline demonstrations, without requiring explicit labels of refinement needs. Calibration balances uncertainty, computation, and temporal consistency. We evaluate RAIN on eight ManiSkill tasks and four real-robot tasks. In simulation, our policy achieves 66.6% success, approaching the best evaluated diffusion-policy success rate with 5.71 times lower policy latency. On the real robot, success increases from 55.0% with Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) to 72.5%, with comparable average successful completion time. Matched controls show that dynamic routing maintains the strongest observed fixed-refinement success level with 26.6% fewer refiner evaluations. Project page: https://rain-policy.github.io/.

Seeing, Maintaining, and Learning: The Evolution of Multimodal Agent Memory

2026-10-14 cs.AIcs.CLcs.RO 👤 Yu-Gang Jiang (h=50)
Minghao Guo, Qingyue Jiao, Xiujin Liu, Jianhong Pang, Ruimeng Yang
Core Contributions
  • Proposes a two-axis taxonomy for multimodal agent memory: how memory is formed/represented (embeddings, structured records, parametric) and how it is managed over time.
  • Introduces a three-tier management scale — static, dynamic (fixed update policy), and self-evolving (update policy itself learns) — which gives a vocabulary for comparing lifelong embodied agents.
  • Distills design principles relevant to robots: trading source fidelity against abstraction, tracking temporal validity and provenance, and grounding retrieved memories in recoverable evidence.
Show abstract
Multimodal models and agents have emerged to enable AI systems to learn from and interact with the real world more like humans by integrating information across multiple modalities. However, enabling multimodal agents to continually acquire useful information from complex, heterogeneous multimodal data remains a significant challenge as they move toward long-horizon tasks and lifelong settings. Addressing this challenge requires effective memory mechanisms that can maintain continuity across sessions, adapt to changing users and environments, and refine memory-management policies through accumulated experience. We introduce a two-axis analytical framework for multimodal agent memory structured around two key questions: how multimodal information is formed and represented (Axis I, Formation and Representation), and how its management adapts with accumulated experience (Axis II, Memory Management). Axis I, Formation and Representation, characterizes what information is retained and how it is encoded (e.g., modality-specific embeddings, textual and structured records, latent and parametric representations) to support subsequent reasoning and action. Axis II, Memory Management, distinguishes three tiers: T1 Static Memory reuses fixed memory; T2 Dynamic Memory updates memory through a stable policy; and T3 Self-Evolving Memory updates memory through an evolving policy. Based on this framework, we synthesize recurring design patterns and derive principles for balancing source fidelity with abstraction, maintaining temporal validity and provenance, grounding memory use in recoverable evidence, and evolving memory policies over time. Furthermore, we discuss the challenges and potential future directions for the memory mechanism of multimodal agents.

AdaRSI: On-Demand Recursive Self-Improvement for Embodied Agents

2026-10-14 cs.CLcs.RO 👤 Da-Ling Wang (h=27)
Peidong Wang, Ying Chang, Zhiming Ma, Xiaocui Yang, Shi Feng
Core Contributions
  • Asks a question most self-improving embodied systems skip: is a failure due to how the executor is invoked (harness) or what it can physically do (weights)? AdaRSI retries failing sub-steps from saved states to tell them apart.
  • A coding agent first improves skills and harness, then fine-tunes the VLA only on sub-steps that remain unreliable, using self-collected demonstrations and experience from earlier tasks — no human demos.
  • On all 50 RoboCasa365 target tasks with GR00T N1.5, raises unseen composite-task success from 4.6% to 13.8%.
  • Matches or beats fine-tuning every sub-step with only 28% of the GPU time and 22% of the demonstrations; fine-tuning without a harness trains more yet gains far less.
Show abstract
Pretrained vision-language-action models (VLAs) often fail midway through unseen long-horizon tasks. Recursive self-improvement (RSI) offers a remedy, but embodied RSI methods either refine the harness around a frozen executor and so cannot add a missing motion, or train the executor on persistent failures without asking whether a failure lies in how the executor is called or in what it can do. We ask when a self-improving embodied system should change the weights of its executor, and propose AdaRSI, an on-demand RSI framework for embodied agents. A coding agent first improves its skills and harness, then attributes persistent failures by retrying failing sub-steps from saved states, and fine-tunes only the sub-steps that remain unreliable, with demonstrations it collects itself and experience carried over from earlier tasks. On all 50 target tasks of RoboCasa365, with GR00T N1.5 as the executor and no human demonstrations, AdaRSI improves the VLA on all three suites and raises its success on unseen composite tasks from 4.6% to 13.8%. Compared with fine-tuning every sub-step, it reaches slightly higher success while using only 28% of the GPU time and 22% of the self-collected demonstrations, and fine-tuning without a harness trains more yet gains far less.

Back on Track: Deployment-time Policy Improvement via World Model Planning

2026-10-14 cs.RO 👤 Somil Bansal (h=25)
Colton Crosby, Hao Wang, Mac Schwager, Somil Bansal
Core Contributions
  • Improves a deployed visuomotor policy with only black-box query access — no retraining, no architecture changes — via an offline-trained monitor-and-intervene layer.
  • A policy-conditioned 'liveness' value learned from a few labeled rollouts detects loss of progress; a compact latent world model (trained with rollouts plus play data) is then searched for corrective actions using that same value as objective.
  • Control returns to the nominal policy after recovery, so the intervention is short and real-time; demonstrated with π0.5 and a diffusion policy on LIBERO-Goal and a Franka Panda with significant success-rate gains.
Show abstract
State-of-the-art visuomotor policies are increasingly capable of executing complex tasks, yet can fail during deployment in ways they cannot predict or recover from on their own. Improving their performance and robustness typically requires collecting more data, retraining, or modifying the policy architecture, all of which are time- and resource-intensive in practice. We instead propose an offline-trained monitoring-and-intervention framework that improves a policy during deployment using only black-box query access. From a limited set of labeled on-policy rollouts, we learn a policy-conditioned liveness value that measures the nominal policy's progress toward task completion; we additionally train a compact latent world model using these rollouts together with play data. At deployment, the liveness value continuously monitors execution. When it detects a loss of progress, a short-horizon planner searches the world model for corrective actions using the same value as its planning objective, after which control returns to the nominal policy. We evaluate our method on the LIBERO-Goal task suite and on a Franka Panda manipulator, using the vision-language-action model $π_{0.5}$ and a diffusion policy as nominal policies, and demonstrate significant improvements in task success rates, while supporting real-time control. Additional results and videos are available on the project website (https://policy-improvement-wm.pages.dev/).

Towards Agentic Robots: Model, Data, Environment, and Harness

2026-10-14 cs.RO 👤 Da-Hua Lin (h=19)
Chonghan Qin, Dongming Wu, Kaituo Feng, Jiaxu Wang, Xinlei Yu
Core Contributions
  • Defines 'agentic robots' — systems where foundation-model agents orchestrate data collection, training, planning and execution, not just predict actions — and traces the lineage from VLAs to world-action models.
  • Organizes the literature into 17 categories across four domains (Model, Data, Environment, Harness), giving a map for papers like AdaRSI and WEDGE that otherwise look unrelated.
  • Highlights open problems in reliability and composition of agentic functions into complete robot systems.
Show abstract
Foundation models are driving a paradigm shift in robotic intelligence, moving beyond low-level action prediction to goal-directed orchestration of data collection, model training, task planning, and real-world execution. We characterize this emerging paradigm as an agentic robots, in which agents reason over human instructions, interact with robotic environments, rectify execution errors autonomously, and dynamically adjust operational strategies. Although this field develops fast, current studies are not well connected. Researchers have no consistent view of how to combine different agentic functions into a complete robot system. This paper provides a unified overview of existing research on agentic robots. We first trace the development from robot policies to agentic systems, covering vision-language-action models, world models, and world-action models. We then clarify the definition and scope of agentic robots and organize existing research into seventeen categories across four functional domains: Model, Data, Environment, and Harness. Across these domains, we examine how agents shape model learning and robot decisions, collect and curate robot data, construct worlds, tasks, and tests for learning and evaluation, and organize capabilities for robot use. Finally, we discuss open challenges and outline directions for future research. Looking forward, we hope this work contributes to a clearer understanding of agentic robots and supports new approaches to making them more capable, reliable, and adaptive.

Instruction-Conditioned Motion Control for Robotic Arms: Language-to-Code Decomposition and Reinforcement Learning

2026-10-14 cs.ROcs.AI 👤 Ji-Sun An (h=18)
Fan Huang, Dezhong Tong, Changyu Chen, Haewoon Kwak, Jisun An
Core Contributions
  • Separates parameter specification, instruction generation and code synthesis (ICMC) so execution outcomes can be traced back to specific instructions and programs.
  • Trains an instruction policy with GRPO through a frozen code generator; logged positive-reward rate rose only modestly, from 31.6% to 35.3%.
  • The key contribution is an audit: positive rewards were given alongside geometric-failure messages (one case: reward 1.0 for a 0.95 m miss vs. 0.05 m tolerance) because success was read from the program's own output.
  • Argues for independent evaluation against fixed specifications — a directly relevant warning for the many LLM-in-the-loop robot learning papers that use self-reported success.
Show abstract
Instruction-Conditioned Motion Control (ICMC) separates parameter specification, instruction generation, and code synthesis for language-driven robot control, making execution outcomes traceable to instructions and programs. We compare one-shot generation with ICMC under o1 and o4-mini on cube pick-and-place and letter-reshaping tasks for a UR5e arm in MuJoCo. We then train an open-weight instruction policy by GRPO through a frozen code generator on cube placement onto two- and five-cube letter layouts, averaging the rewards of five programs per instruction. The logged positive-reward rate rose from 31.6% to 35.3% (253/800 to 282/800) between the first and last five training steps. A log audit identifies positive binary labels associated with geometric-failure messages: at least 19 in that run, 18 in the dense-reward run, and 5 in the run with one program per instruction. In one case, all five rewards were 1.0 while the log reports a 0.9515 m miss against a 0.05 m tolerance; the verdict came from the program's own output stream. We distinguish logged reward rates from independently verified task success and report descriptive results for each configuration's single training trajectory. We introduce an independent evaluation protocol that checks task completion against fixed specifications, separating robot control from success assessment to support reproducible evaluation.

Efficient Test-Time Adaptation For Robot Policies

2026-10-14 cs.ROcs.LG 👤 Christos Louizos (h=17)
Motasem Alfarra, Pietro Mazzaglia, Markus Peschl, Daniel Dijkman, Christos Louizos
Core Contributions
  • Studies reward-free test-time adaptation for locomotion: when terrain, payload or sensing shift, the actor is updated online using a frozen pretrained critic as a performance surrogate.
  • TEMPO stabilizes this with an entropy regularizer on the critic's predictive distribution (to avoid overconfident extrapolation) and a prioritized memory that focuses updates on low-value 'hard' observations.

Collaborative robots and humanoids operating in shared workspaces must adapt their behavior to the physical capabilities of their human partners. Achieving this requires robots to autonomously estimate task-relevant human mobility during interaction, without relying on external clinical assessments. In this work, we formulate the estimation of upper-limb functional mobility as an active perception problem. We model task-available upper-limb functional ranges of motion through impairment coefficients that parameterize reductions of healthy joint limits, and integrate this representation into a human model used by the robot during interaction. Based on this formulation, we propose an online active perception pipeline in which the robot actively probes the user through repeated handover requests while progressively updating a Bayesian belief over the impairment coefficients. This enables the robot to continuously refine its internal model of the user's joint-level capabilities through interaction rather than passive observation. Reliable recognition of accessibility-critical objects (e.g., audible pedestrian signals, door-activation buttons, and handrails) is essential for assistive navigation technologies that support safe, independent mobility for blind and low-vision (BLV) users. Yet these categories are underrepresented in existing large-scale vision datasets, and the few datasets that include them suffer from limited class coverage, inconsistent annotations, and poor diversity. We introduce NavAble, a large-scale dataset spanning 11 accessibility-critical object classes, combining 41K curated open-source images with 8K newly collected, densely annotated real-world images. To extend scale and diversity, we develop a synthetic data generation pipeline that renders high-fidelity 3D assets across 37 environments under diverse styles and viewpoints, yielding 452K frames with rich ground-truth annotations from 565 assets. The pipeline minimizes manual effort by combining automated web image crawling, VLM-based filtering, SAM 3D-based asset generation, and convenient camera trajectory configuration, enabling large-scale data generation at a diversity unattainable through manual collection. Experiments show that fine-tuning existing segmentation models on the NavAble Dataset achieves reliable accessibility-object segmentation (>80% mIoU on our held-out test set). We also demonstrate the effectiveness of synthetic data augmentation, which yields up to +3.5 mIoU over real-world-only fine-tuning. Beyond benchmark evaluation, validation on egocentric data from two mobility-assistive robots demonstrates improved perception reliability in real-world navigation scenarios. Together, NavAble establishes a scalable foundation for BLV navigation perception, directly addressing the data scarcity and embodiment gaps. The dataset and pipeline are publicly available at https://huggingface.co/datasets/NavAble/NeurIPS_2026_BLV.

Reconstruction of 3D structures using under-ice AUV video footage has been inaccurate, although it is not certain whether this is due to shortcomings in the methods or an inherent deficiency of the capture system. We test four different models for reconstruction on the same Antarctic under-ice AUV video (Icefin, Kamb Ice Stream): classical feature matching SfM, differentiable photometric splatting, volumetric depth fusion, and a pose-free feed-forward foundation model. All four fail, each in a distinct way: near-total registration yielding almost no structure, geometrically broken output despite healthy photometric metrics, a persistent coverage artifact, and unstable cross-chunk scale. We show that all four are consistent with the same capture regime, a small camera-baseline-to-scene-depth ratio. Following our analysis, we propose a detection system that flags this type of failure in minutes, rather than hours of wasted compute.
