🔭 Research Landscape

The dominant theme today is memory and test-time structure for generalist policies. Three papers attack the same question — how a VLA should carry state across a long horizon — with different answers: Mem4Act (#2) learns a fixed-size latent memory with multi-granularity aggregation and a memory-first training stage, LTM (#10) stores compact latent transitions inside π0.5's KV cache, and Value as Memory (#21) uses recent value predictions as a task-progress memory. Even the proactive-assistant paper PAM (#18) is a memory story, with an editable finite-state machine updated by a reflection VLM rather than by gradients. Their results are notably comparable (58.5% on RoboMME for LTM versus 49.9% for Mem4Act), but LTM's tenfold data-scaling jump from 57.7% to 87.5% suggests data, not memory format, may be the larger lever.

A second thread is making VLAs cheaper and more reliable at inference time without retraining. Fast-VLA (#28) runs a 4B VLM once per episode and reuses its KV task memory (10.9–17.1× faster amortized inference), while FastVLA (#27) takes the opposite, training-free route of pruning visual tokens adaptively (35–59% fewer FLOPs). VeriVLA (#23) spends extra compute only on critical states, ranking sampled action chunks with a JEPA latent world model, and CarryOn (#14) wraps a frozen VLA with a safety shield that cuts violations from 52.6% to 6.1%. Against this engineering optimism, #25 delivers a sobering diagnosis: current VLAs do not actually follow how-level instructions, and fixing responsiveness does not fix success — a reminder that benchmarks like Bench2Drive (#15) and Evo-DexBench (#29, best method just 38.5%) often measure something other than what we think they do.

Finally, the dexterity and world-model papers show a shift toward self-generated data and structure-aware learning. DSP (#1) bootstraps a world model from minutes of human play and matches RL with 490× less interaction; CoDA (#9) co-evolves a simulator and policy through VLM diagnoses; DemoStream (#5) makes TAMP a demonstration engine at 66 demos/hour; T2-z (#19) infers hidden physics through test-time latent optimization. On the body side, Whole-Body Dexterity (#3) and Compliant GMT (#12) both show that the whole humanoid — not just the hands — is becoming the manipulation interface, and both lean on sim-to-real distillation of specialists into a single policy. The field is clearly moving from 'collect more demonstrations' toward 'let the robot generate, diagnose and adapt on its own'.

Mem4Act: Learning Effective Latent Memory for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation

📅 2026-10-15cs.RO⭐ Jia-Qi Ma (h=44)
Yun Zhang, Zhiyu Huang, Tianhui Cai, Chen Tang, Wei Wang
Core Contributions
  • Reframes memory as a bounded interface: instead of feeding ever-longer histories to the policy, a fixed-size latent is learned specifically for control.
  • Combines three design choices — multi-granularity temporal aggregation, QA supervision plus key-frame DINO reconstruction (semantic and perceptual content), and memory-first two-stage training that forces the policy to actually use memory.
  • The last point targets a known failure mode in which policies ignore memory and shortcut from the current frame.
  • Reaches 49.88% on RoboMME, 61.0% on RMBench and 81.5% across four real tasks.
Show abstract
Long-horizon robotic manipulation fundamentally requires memory: agents must retain task-relevant information from past observations and interactions and use it to guide future actions. Existing approaches typically preserve raw historical observations, compressed visual tokens, or language summaries, but these representations either expose the action policy to increasingly long histories or risk discarding task-relevant temporal, semantic, and perceptual information. We introduce Mem4Act, a framework for learning effective latent memory as a fixed-size representation explicitly optimized for long-horizon robotic control. Mem4Act addresses three coupled challenges: how to efficiently cover long histories, what information memory should retain, and how to ensure the policy actually relies on memory. To this end, it organizes history via multi-granularity temporal aggregation, preserves complementary semantic and perceptual information through question-answering supervision and key-frame DINO feature reconstruction, and employs memory-first two-stage training to encourage greater use of memory by the action policy. The resulting memory captures both high-level task state and fine-grained perceptual evidence while providing a bounded interface to the action policy. Across RoboMME, RMBench, and four real-world manipulation tasks, Mem4Act achieves state-of-the-art performance on memory-dependent control, with success rates of 49.88%, 61.0%, and 81.5%, respectively. Extensive ablations further demonstrate the importance of temporal aggregation, complementary memory supervision, and memory-first training.

LTM: Latent Transition Based In-Context Memory for Vision-Language-Action Models

📅 2026-10-15cs.RO, cs.AI⭐ Peng-Wei Wang (h=21)
Qixin Sun, WenLong Gao, Xiansheng Chen, Zhongyuan Wang, Si Liu
Core Contributions
  • Stores history not as frames or text but as compact latent transitions between consecutive observations, so memory encodes what changed.
  • Built on π0.5 with a frozen latent action encoder, lightweight adapter and causal KV caching — no memory-specific annotations or external reasoning models.
  • 58.54% on RoboMME and 80.5% on LIBERO-Mem; scaling demos tenfold lifts RoboMME from 57.67% to 87.50%.
  • Remains at 84.00% under a 200-token memory budget, and real-robot counting jumps from 11.25% to 82.50%.
Show abstract
Robotic manipulation often requires remembering interactions that are no longer visible. We introduce Latent Transition Memory (LTM), which stores history as compact latent transitions between consecutive observations. Instantiated on $π_{0.5}$ as LTM-$π$, our method uses a frozen latent action encoder, a lightweight adapter, and causal KV caching for incremental inference without memory-specific semantic annotations or external reasoning-model calls. With DreamDojo transitions, LTM-$π$ achieves 58.54% success on RoboMME and 80.5% on LIBERO-Mem, performing best among baselines without external reasoning models or memory-specific annotations. Ablations support the contribution of transition content beyond temporal cues and the efficiency of delayed memory admission. Scaling RoboMME demonstrations tenfold improves success from 57.67% to 87.50%, while a Cup Swap diagnostic highlights the role of process supervision in temporal composition. Under a 200-token memory budget, success remains 84.00%, versus 87.50% without compression. Finally, real-robot counting success improves from 11.25% to 82.50%. Together, these results demonstrate the effectiveness, scalability, and efficiency of latent transition memory, highlighting its capacity for complex temporal information, bounded-context operation, and real-world robotic control.

VeriVLA: Adaptive Test-Time Verification for Flow-Based VLAs with Latent World Models

📅 2026-10-15cs.RO⭐ Heng-Shuang Zhao (h=13)
Xueli Sun, Yuxiang Lu, Yantong Liu, Xianzhe Fan, Xi Chen
Core Contributions
  • Exploits the stochasticity of flow-based VLAs: different noise seeds give different action-chunk quality, yet policies commit to one sample blindly.
  • A training-free two-axis trigger (within-chunk variation and cross-sample variance) detects critical states so verification is spent only when needed.
  • A JEPA latent world model ranks candidate chunks by predicted future latents and task progress, avoiding pixel reconstruction.
  • Raises real-world completion from 50.8% to 65.5% for +326 ms on an RTX 4090, and RoboTwin 2.0 clean success from 66.4% to 73.6%.
Show abstract
Flow-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies are inherently stochastic: different noise seeds can produce action chunks of varying quality from the same observation. At test time, however, the policy typically commits to a single sample without an explicit estimate of its quality. In fine-grained manipulation, executing a suboptimal action can irreversibly disrupt task progress and lead to failure. To exploit policy stochasticity, we introduce a training-free two-axis trigger that uses two initial samples to measure within-chunk temporal variation and cross-sample variance, detecting the critical states. Once triggered, a JEPA-based latent world model ranks candidate action chunks by predicting action-conditioned future latent states and the corresponding changes in task progress. By operating in latent space, the verifier avoids pixel reconstruction and enables efficient and more accurate ranking. Across three real-world tasks, $\textit{i.e.,}$ pen insertion, pipetting, and apple placement), our method raises the average completion score from 50.8% to 65.5%, while adding 326 ms for sampling and verification per triggered decision on a single RTX 4090. Consistent gains are also observed in simulation, where the average success rate rises from 66.4% to 73.6% in the clean setting of RoboTwin 2.0, from 62.8% to 69.6% in its randomized setting, and from 96.9% to 97.6% on LIBERO.

FastVLA: Task-Conditioned and Temporally Adaptive Visual Token Compression for Efficient Vision-Language-Action Models

📅 2026-10-15cs.RO⭐ Zheng-Zhong Tu (h=12)
Jiuyi Xu, Chengyuan Qian, Zhengzhong Tu, Yang Sui, Yangming Shi
Core Contributions
  • Argues that generic saliency pruning is wrong for control: decision-critical tokens depend on the task, and the right token budget varies across timesteps.
  • Scores tokens by task-to-vision attention and sets compression adaptively from the inverse-Simpson uniformity of importance, with online sliding-window calibration — training-free.
  • On OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT and CogACT, cuts LLM FLOPs 35–59% and action latency 9–32% with success maintained.
  • On a Franka Research 3 it reduces end-to-end latency 20% (1.25× speedup) at comparable success.
Show abstract
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models present a promising paradigm for general-purpose robot manipulation, but closed-loop deployment is often limited by inference efficiency: at each timestep, high-resolution, often multi-view images are encoded into hundreds of visual tokens that dominate transformer computation and latency. Token compression via attention is a natural solution but is challenging for VLA control because (1) decision-critical tokens are determined by the task, not generic visual saliency, and (2) the token budget varies substantially across steps, so static metrics and budgets waste computation or discard control-critical tokens. We introduce FastVLA, a training-free, task-aware, and temporally adaptive visual token compression framework. At each step, FastVLA scores token importance via task-to-vision attention and adaptively sets the compression rate from the importance-distribution uniformity, measured by an inverse-Simpson metric with online sliding-window calibration. We evaluate FastVLA on OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, and CogACT across LIBERO and SIMPLER, and on a physical Franka Research 3 robot. In simulation, it reduces LLM-backbone FLOPs by 35-59\% and action latency by 9-32\% (a 1.09$\times$-1.46$\times$ speedup) while maintaining task success, achieving a comparable or better efficiency-performance trade-off than existing plug-and-play token compression/pruning/reuse baselines. On the real robot, it cuts end-to-end latency by 20\% (1.25$\times$ speedup) at comparable success. Code is available at https://github.com/jiuyixu25/FastVLA.

Fast-VLA: You Only Think Once for Robot Manipulation

📅 2026-10-15cs.RO⭐ Michael Bi Mi (h=12)
Kehong Gong, Guanli Hou, Xiaoxu Zheng, Qi Wang, Mingxi Xu
Core Contributions
  • Notes that task understanding changes far more slowly than robot state, yet most VLAs rerun a multi-billion-parameter model at each action chunk.
  • Runs a frozen 4B VLM once per episode to create multi-layer KV task memory; a lightweight actor queries it with time- and arm-indexed queries plus live feedback.
  • 92.46% / 92.40% on RoboTwin 2.0 clean/randomized and 95.95% / 79.11% on LIBERO / LIBERO-Plus.
  • Cuts amortized inference time 10.9–17.1× versus continually conditioned baselines with 1.22 GiB online memory; memory-swap tests confirm the actor uses episode-matched context.
Show abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) policies commonly place a multi-billion-parameter model inside the feedback loop, repeatedly recomputing task context whenever they predict a new action chunk. Yet task understanding changes far more slowly than the robot state. We introduce Fast-VLA, which invokes a frozen 4B VLM once per episode to contextualize the instruction and initial views as fixed, multi-layer key-value task memory. During execution, a lightweight online actor fuses learned time- and arm-indexed queries with current visual and proprioceptive feedback to form action queries. These queries retrieve task context from the fixed memory and predict action chunks without re-running the foundation model. Fast-VLA achieves 92.46% and 92.40% success on RoboTwin 2.0 Clean and Randomized, and 95.95% and 79.11% on LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus evaluation. Against three continually conditioned baselines on the same hardware, it reduces RoboTwin amortized policy inference time by 10.9-17.1x and requires 1.22 GiB of post-prefill online resident memory. Task-memory swaps further show that the actor relies on episode-matched task memory. These results show that task context can be established once while closed-loop control remains responsive throughout execution. Project page: https://garfield-kh.github.io/FastVLA

Do VLAs Really Follow Instructions? Diagnosing How-Level Instruction Following in Vision-Language-Action Models

📅 2026-10-15cs.CV, cs.RO⭐ Chanyoung Park (h=13)
Sangwu Park, Wonjoong Kim, Yeonjun In, Chanyoung Park
Core Contributions
  • Distinguishes What-level instructions (which task) from How-level ones (how to do it) and shows current VLAs do not benefit from the latter — success even drops.
  • Hypothesizes single-instruction-per-episode training is the cause; segment-level enriched instructions make actions respond but do not consistently raise success.
  • Introduces SIMPLER-IN, where success requires conditions stated only in the instruction.
  • Finds the gained responsiveness fails on demanding or out-of-distribution How-level instructions, so the problem remains open.
Show abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) models are expected to follow instructions like their language backbones, yet prior work has focused on What-level instructions that specify the task rather than How-level instructions that specify how to carry it out. Through our diagnoses, we find that How-level instructions do not help current VLAs and lower task success. We hypothesize that pairing each training episode with a single instruction limits How-level instruction following, and show that training with segment-level enriched instructions makes actions respond to such instructions without consistently raising task success. To measure How-level instruction following beyond standard benchmarks, we introduce SIMPLER-IN, where success requires conditions given only in the instruction. It shows that the gained responsiveness does not extend to demanding or out-of-distribution How-level instructions. How-level instruction following thus remains an open challenge.

CarryOn: A Dual-Loop Harness for Personalized and Reliable Robot Assistance

📅 2026-10-15cs.RO, cs.CV⭐ Peng-Guang Chen (h=17)
Zixuan Wang, Yuqi Liu, Yuxin Chen, Sengqiao Yang, Jinhui Ye
Core Contributions
  • Wraps a frozen VLA in a dual-loop harness, adding personalization and safety without retraining the policy.
  • The planning loop maintains persistent user- and scene-level context grounded in new observations, so 'make me a sandwich' follows each user's preferences.
  • The execution loop shields actions from predicted hazardous contact and invokes reusable control-based recovery skills refined through reflection.
  • Cuts violation rate of a safety-finetuned VLA from 52.6% to 6.1% and lifts recovery success from 13% to 80% with refined skills.
Show abstract
While generalist vision-language-action (VLA) models have enabled diverse open-world manipulation, real-world robotic assistance requires two critical capabilities they currently lack: personalization and reliable execution. First, executing an instruction like "make me a sandwich" requires grounding subtask decomposition in persistent user preferences rather than relying on a single generic solution. Second, reliable deployment demands explainable safety assurance that proactively prevents hazards and gracefully recovers from occasional physical failures. To address these gaps, we present CarryOn, a dual-loop harness designed to augment a frozen VLA policy. The planning loop maintains persistent user-level and scene-level context that is continually grounded against new observations and revised through interaction, enabling personalized task selection and execution grounding across conversations and changing scenes. The execution loop actively supervises policy rollouts, shielding actions from predicted hazardous contact. When execution fails, the system invokes reusable control-based recovery skills that restore task progress and are refined through reflection on earlier failures. On a real robot and in simulation, CarryOn dispatches preference-consistent subtasks across users and scenes, reduces the violation rate of an already safety-finetuned VLA from 52.6% to 6.1%, and increases recovery success from 13% to 80% when refined skills are reused.

Value as Memory: Online Value-Guided Post-training for General Robot Policies

📅 2026-10-15cs.RO⭐ Jun Lv (h=13)
Wenye Yu, Yijin Chen, Kaiyi Xiong, Wenbo Han, Wendi Chen
Core Contributions
  • Tackles two issues in real-world post-training of generalists: ambiguity from partial observability and delayed feedback from offline methods.
  • Value caching feeds recent value predictions to the policy as a task-progress memory — a neat use of a critic as state.
  • Online RL mixes offline demonstrations, off-policy interventions and on-policy rollouts, moving from offline warmup to autonomous learning with sparse rewards.
  • Mean success gain of 29.4% from value caching and 32.8% from online learning on deformable and contact-rich tasks, with 8× more policy iterations per gradient-step budget.
Show abstract
Real-world post-training of generalist robot policies faces two challenges: partial observability makes action selection ambiguous, while offline post-training methods delay feedback from deployment experience. We present Value as Memory (VaM), a framework that addresses these challenges through value caching and online real-world reinforcement learning (RL). Value caching conditions actions on recent value predictions as task-progress memory, while online updates enable timely policy improvement by combining offline demonstrations, off-policy interventions, and on-policy rollouts for sample-efficient adaptation. As a result, VaM transitions from offline RL warmup to online autonomous learning with sparse rewards. Across long-horizon tasks involving deformable objects and contact-rich manipulation, VaM yields a mean absolute success-rate gain of $29.4\%$ with value caching, while achieving a $32.8\%$ gain with online autonomous learning over matched offline training. The evaluated online schedule performs $8\times$ as many policy iterations under the same gradient-step budget, with more than $2\times$ lower recorded time per gradient step with concurrent data collection and training.

Dexterity from Supervised Play

📅 2026-10-15cs.RO, cs.AI⭐ Y. Aloimonos (h=53)
Botao He, Yiming Dou, Yiannis Aloimonos, Jitendra Malik, Akash Sharma
Core Contributions
  • Replaces task-specific demonstrations with play: a behavior-cloned play policy trained on only minutes of human play is turned loose to gather hours of contact-rich data for a world model.
  • The same play policy does double duty at inference — it proposes candidate action sequences for MPC in the learned world model, avoiding the huge random-sampling cost of planning over dexterous hands.
  • A self-play distillation step compresses this slow planner into a closed-loop goal-conditioned policy, making deployment fast.
  • Beats hindsight behavior cloning by 69.6% in real-world success and matches RL while using 490× less interaction data, with no sim-to-real transfer.
Show abstract
How can robots autonomously collect the contact-rich experience needed to learn dexterous manipulation? We present DSP: Dexterity from supervised play, a framework in which a robot learns a play policy from minutes of human play through behavior cloning, then deploys it autonomously to collect hours of interaction data to train a world model. During inference, the same play policy then guides model-predictive control (MPC) by proposing candidate action sequences for evaluation under the learned world model. To accelerate inference, we also introduce a method for distilling a closed-loop, goal-conditioned policy through self-play within the learned world model. We evaluate this framework on underactuated pushing and any-to-any in-hand pose reorientation with a multifingered hand, in simulation and real world. In real world experiment, our method outperforms hindsight behavior cloning by 69.6% in success rate and achieves comparable success to reinforcement learning with 490 times less interaction data. These results demonstrate that supervised play can autonomously collect interaction data, guide model-based planning, and enable policy learning without any task-specific demonstrations or sim-to-real transfer.

Whole-Body Dexterity

📅 2026-10-15cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.LG, eess.SY⭐ Mustafa Mukadam (h=33)
Conor Mc Gartoll, An Dang, Pranay Thangeda, Arjun Gupta, Carolina Higuera
Core Contributions
  • Treats the whole body — arms, torso, bracing — as contact surface, going beyond end-effector grasps that limit current humanoid manipulation.
  • Pipeline: generate a dataset of feasible whole-body grasps, train RL experts on any-to-any grasp repositioning, then distill them into one policy using only proprioception and object geometry.
  • Transfers zero-shot to a Unitree G1 for basketballs and boxes, including a long-horizon stow–carry–place task steered by coarse teleoperator references.
  • Succeeds in 90% of real trials versus 18% for direct retargeting and 4% for a generalist whole-body controller.
Show abstract
Current humanoid dexterous manipulation remains largely confined to end-effectors and simple grasps, falling short of humans' ability to engage the entire body by bracing and sliding bulky objects against the arms and torso. We introduce Whole-Body Dexterity (WBD), a general framework that uses the robot's body and appendages as contact surfaces to grasp and manipulate objects. Our sim-to-real pipeline first generates a dataset of feasible whole-body grasps, then trains reinforcement-learning experts on a general any-to-any repositioning objective by sampling initial and goal grasps from the dataset, and distills these experts into a single policy that uses proprioception and object geometry. We show that the distilled policy transfers zero-shot to a real Unitree G1, where it repositions a basketball and a cardboard box to regions around the torso and completes a long-horizon task that stows a box under one arm, carries it from a shelf to a table, and places it. The controller operates interactively, steered by coarse motion references from a human teleoperator. Across real-world whole-body manipulation tasks, the distilled controller completes 90% of trials, compared with 18% for direct retargeting and 4% for a generalist whole-body controller.

Uni-PNP: Unified Prehensile and Non-prehensile Policy Learning for Generalizable Object Pose Reaching

📅 2026-10-15cs.RO⭐ Zhi-Zheng Zhang (h=15)
Wenxuan Zhu, Yuze Wang, Jiangran Lyu, Jiayi Chen, Yixin Zheng
Core Contributions
  • Unifies grasping and non-prehensile actions (pushing, tipping) in a single policy instead of sequencing separate primitives through hand-set intermediate goals.
  • Pretrains a geometry encoder to predict contact-induced object motion from a simulated contact-dynamics dataset across diverse objects and grippers.
  • A two-stage RL curriculum moves from graspable to ungraspable objects, prompting discovery of behaviors such as tip-then-grasp and table-assisted reorientation.
  • 76.39% success on seen objects and 57.91% on unseen ones, beating the strongest baseline by 20.52 and 17.79 points, with real-world transfer.
Show abstract
Object manipulation often requires combining non-prehensile actions with grasping. However, existing approaches typically develop these capabilities separately and integrate them by sequencing distinct primitives through prescribed intermediate objectives, limiting the coordination of contact motions and grasping. Their integration also remains sensitive to object and gripper geometry and depends on particular environmental supports, complicating real-world deployment. We present Uni-PNP, a unified policy that jointly learns prehensile and non-prehensile manipulation for object pose reaching across diverse objects and grippers. To achieve this, we first pretrain a geometry encoder to predict contact-induced object motion using a contact-dynamics dataset constructed from simulated interactions between diverse objects and grippers. We then train the policy in a two-stage reinforcement learning curriculum from graspable objects to ungraspable peers, encouraging the discovery of non-prehensile interactions and their integration with grasping. Experiments show 76.39% success on hundreds of seen objects with grippers, and 57.91% on unseen peers, outperforming the strongest pose-reaching baseline by 20.52 and 17.79 percentage points, respectively. We also analyze emergent behaviors, including nonprehensile and prehensile repositioning, grasp acquisition that requires grasping after tipping, and table-assisted reorientation while grasping, and transfer to the real-world scenario. Project page available at lgswdn.github.io/Uni-PNP.

Evo-DexBench: Benchmarking Generalization across Related Tasks for Dexterous Manipulation

📅 2026-10-15cs.RO⭐ Jun-Chi Yan (h=12)
Bing Cheng, Yiran Mao, Zewei Ye, Yifan Jin, Xinyi Chen
Core Contributions
  • Builds a benchmark that separates true knowledge transfer from task memorization via the Core-Gen principle: related source tasks plus a novel target with few demonstrations.
  • Contains 30 tasks in six functional families and 1,248 VR-teleoperated demonstrations with Shadow Hands carrying 123 taxels.
  • Even the best method averages only 38.5% success, showing transfer of functional interaction knowledge is far from solved.
  • Joint Core+Gen training beats Core-then-fine-tune by 12% and 38% on two tasks, and functionally matched Core data helps more than mismatched data.
Show abstract
General-purpose dexterous manipulation requires policies to transfer knowledge from related tasks to new tasks with only a few target demonstrations. Existing benchmarks often provide too many target demonstrations. They also leave the differences between source and target tasks uncontrolled. As a result, they cannot clearly distinguish knowledge transfer from task memorization. Our key insight is the Core-Gen principle. Core tasks are related tasks that share a functional interaction and provide source knowledge, while a Gen task is a novel target within the same functional family that has only a few demonstrations. This setup tests whether a policy can reuse functional interaction knowledge. Based on this principle, we propose Evo-DexBench, which controls both source-task relatedness and target-task exposure. It comprises 30 tasks in six functional families and a VR teleoperation pipeline. The resulting dataset covers three everyday scenes per task. It contains 1,248 multimodal demonstrations collected with simulated Shadow Hands equipped with 123 taxels. Experiments show that even the best-performing method achieves an average success rate of only 38.5%, highlighting the challenges existing methods face in transferring functional interaction knowledge. Further analysis shows that, across the two tasks, joint Core+Gen training improves performance by 12% and 38%, respectively, over training on Core followed by fine-tuning on Gen. This indicates that joint training makes better use of Core knowledge and that functionally matched Core data yields greater gains in success rate than mismatched data. We release the Evo-DexBench dataset and benchmark to support the evaluation of dexterous manipulation policies' generalization across related tasks.

Perceive, Predict, and Plan: a Unified World Action Model for Egocentric Dexterous Manipulation

📅 2026-10-15cs.CV, cs.RO⭐ Chen Guo (h=11)
Zirui Wu, Chen Guo, Tianjian Jiang, Zeren Jiang, Zeming Li
Core Contributions
  • Unifies perception, prediction and planning for egocentric dexterous manipulation in one world action model with Perceive, Predict and Plan modes.
  • Inserts a compact structured 3D action representation into a pretrained video diffusion transformer via a mixture-of-transformers with bidirectional visual-action attention.
  • Randomized modality visibility lets video and 3D actions act as either context or target, so one model serves all three tasks.
  • Unified training improves every mode over single-mode training and beats specialists on 3D hand reconstruction and action-conditioned video prediction across four datasets.
Show abstract
We introduce P$^{3}$-WAM, a unified world action model for egocentric dexterous manipulation that learns 3D perception, action-conditioned prediction, and goal-directed planning through three complementary modes: Perceive, which reconstructs articulated hand motion and camera trajectories from video; Predict, which generates visual futures under prescribed actions; and Plan, which jointly generates action trajectories and their anticipated visual consequences conditioned on an initial image, a language instruction, and an optional visual goal. Our model integrates a compact, structured 3D action representation into a pretrained video diffusion transformer through a mixture-of-transformers architecture with shared base weights, modality-specific adaptation, and bidirectional visual-action attention. Randomized modality visibility lets video and 3D actions serve as either conditioning context or prediction targets across the three modes. Experiments show that unified training improves all three capabilities over single-mode training, including gains in planning quality under a vision-language model evaluation. Through unified training, our model outperforms specialized baselines on 3D hand reconstruction and action-conditioned video prediction across four datasets, while producing goal-directed manipulation videos with stronger instruction adherence and action progression than video-generation baselines. Project Page: https://p3-wam.pages.dev/

CoDA: Co-Evolution of Data Engines and Action Policies

📅 2026-10-15cs.RO, cs.AI⭐ Ismini Lourentzou (h=21)
Tianjiao Yu, Zhilin Ou, Yifan Shen, Xinzhuo Li, Yuanzhe Liu
Core Contributions

Growing interest in in-space servicing, manufacturing, and assembly has renewed attention on reliable metal joining for modular construction and on-orbit repair. Robotic welding is promising, but reduced-gravity and low-pressure operation remains poorly characterised and flight-like tests are limited. The UK Space Agency-supported ISPARK project is developing a physics-grounded digital twin for structured process de-risking. This paper evaluates a model-based prototype combining a finite-volume thermal weld-pool model, Gaussian-process (GP) and neural-network surrogates, and a simulated six-axis manipulator. The reduced model includes solid-liquid phase change, effective thermocapillary transport, and a local-pressure parameter. Gravity is assessed separately through a hydrostatic-to-capillary pressure ratio and does not enter the thermal energy equation. Across repeated five-fold cross-validation, the GP has lower normalised mean absolute error than the neural network for all eight outputs. In 10 held-out boundary cases, 74 of 80 solver responses fall within nominal 90% GP predictive intervals. A three-output GP query takes 249 microseconds, including scaling and predictive variance. In a simulated UR5 L-shaped-seam study, a proportional controller adjusts the synergic power-source setting as travel speed falls near the corner. Finite-volume re-evaluation finds all 12 closed-loop operating points and 9 of 12 open-loop points within an illustrative 1.0-3.0 mm root-side-width band. These simulation results support using the framework to screen welding conditions and control strategies before experimental tests. Thermal-vacuum, targeted reduced-gravity, and robotic hardware-in-the-loop studies are needed to assess the model and controller.
