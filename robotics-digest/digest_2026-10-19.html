🔭 Research Landscape

The dominant theme today is what a VLA should predict before it acts, and the answers are converging on compact, structured intermediates rather than raw futures. VLDA (#13) argues that full future-state prediction wastes capacity on static appearance and instead learns a compact "Delta" of task-relevant change; TrajWAM (#19) makes a related bet for dynamic scenes by grounding imagination in forecast object-mask trajectories and distilling to one-step inference; PhysWord (#17) anchors latent dynamics to atomic action verbs; and LECDrive (#24) injects dense DINOv3/SAM3/DepthAnything3 knowledge through latent tokens. Ropedia W0 (#8) pushes the same idea to scale, with 10k hours of egocentric human data and 3k hours of robot data in a shared kinematic representation. The common move is to put a structured, low-bandwidth interface between perception and action.

A second thread is the efficiency and embodiment cost of deploying those models. SPAR (#28) shows that 96% of a pi0.5 language backbone can be pruned and recovered with RL, PaceVLA (#29) decides per step how coarsely to quantize actions in time, and HAF-VLA (#6) adapts a flow-matching VLA to humanoids by denoising locomotion, waist and arms in successive stages. Meanwhile the policy-learning papers attack data quality instead of model size: PGPG (#16) and ALIGN (#25) extract signal from imperfect rollouts without human labels or noisy thresholds, DISCO (#23) distills MCTS into a deployable policy, and Policy Search in Code Space (#5) lets coding agents write interpretable multi-agent pathfinding algorithms that beat competition entries by 35-59%. PhysRSI-eval (#27) is the measurement counterpart, asking how quickly and how far VLM agents improve from physical feedback.

Third, a cluster of papers is a corrective on evaluation and assumptions. ForagerSim (#2) finds learned policies lag humans once maps and topology get large, ReInsVLN (#18) shows that re-reading history recovers failures a frozen navigator already had the information to avoid, VeSTA (#20) shows that permissive simulator contact hides a physical executability gap in VLN, and HandsOn (#3) reports that the best trained multi-task dexterous policy reaches only 29.1% on Core-50 while GPT-6 prompting manages 5.2%. In estimation, Self-Supervised Inertial Odometry (#1) and the wheel-uncertainty LIO (#15) both replace hand-set noise or ground truth with learned structure, within 0.07 m/s of a supervised baseline in the first case. Taken together, the batch suggests the field is moving from "can the model act" toward "does the interface, the benchmark and the hardware assumption actually hold".

HAF-VLA: Hierarchical Action Flow Framework for Humanoid Vision-Language-Action Control

2026-10-19 cs.RO 👤 Zhengping Che (h=29)
Langzhe Gu, Chengkai Hou, Meng Li, Xinhua Wang, Xinyuan Lv
Core Contributions
  • Addresses why generalist VLAs struggle on humanoids: base and waist errors trigger aggressive upper-body compensation in a high-dimensional whole-body action space.
  • Instead of one-shot chunk generation, a pretrained flow-matching VLA denoises a shared action space in stages: locomotion and head, then waist, then full-body manipulation.
  • Learnable stage embeddings plus cross-stage KV-cache conditioning let later stages refine what earlier stages committed to, keeping structural dependencies.
  • Reports significant gains over existing models on six real-world humanoid loco-manipulation tasks, a recipe for adapting mobile-manipulation VLAs to legged bodies.
Show abstract
Humanoid robots hold great promise as general-purpose agents in human-centered environments, yet adapting generalist vision-language-action (VLA) policies to real-world humanoid loco-manipulation remains challenging. Unlike fixed-base or wheeled manipulators, humanoids must coordinate locomotion, posture, gaze, and dual-arm manipulation over a high-dimensional whole-body action space, where inaccurate base or waist motions can easily induce unstable and aggressive upper-body compensation. We propose HAF-VLA, a hierarchical action-flow framework for humanoid VLA control. Built upon a pretrained flow-matching VLA, HAF-VLA inherits the vision-language prefix encoder and action expert weights, and adapts them to humanoid whole-body loco-manipulation with learnable stage embeddings and cross-stage KV-cache conditioning. Instead of generating the full action chunk in a single pass, HAF-VLA progressively denoises a shared whole-body action space: first locomotion and head commands, then waist refinement, and finally full-body manipulation. To preserve structural dependencies across stages, we introduce a cross-stage KV cache conditioning mechanism, enabling subsequent action generation stages to refine and augment their outputs based on the action representations from preceding stages. Experimental results across six real-world tasks demonstrate that our framework significantly enhances the performance of existing models in humanoid loco-manipulation tasks, demonstrating an effective pathway for adapting strong mobile-manipulation VLAs to humanoid whole-body loco-manipulation.

Ropedia W0: Scaling Loco-Manipulation World Models across Embodiments with Human Experience

2026-10-19 cs.CVcs.RO 👤 Zhao-Xi Chen (h=24)
Tianqi Liu, Hanfei Zhao, Qifeng Liang, Hui En Pang, Jiarui Xu
Core Contributions
  • Builds a world foundation model that jointly covers locomotion and manipulation, across humans and robots, from egocentric views.
  • Backs it with Xp-Data (10k hours of human and 3k hours of robot data with unified annotations) and a kinematic representation encoding locomotion via camera pose and manipulation via projected skeletons.
  • Reports state of the art on five cross-embodiment benchmarks and held-out human video, with performance improving as human pre-training data scales for both human and robot evaluation.
  • After distillation it supports real-time, minute-long rollouts for live VR interaction, and is applied to policy evaluation, planning and world-action modeling.
Show abstract
World models enable embodied agents to anticipate action consequences, yet jointly modeling locomotion and manipulation across diverse embodiments remains challenging due to heterogeneous action spaces and limited multimodal supervision. We present Ropedia W0, the first egocentric loco-manipulation world foundation model spanning humans and robots with diverse embodiments. We curate Xp-Data, comprising 10k hours of egocentric human data and 3k hours of robot data with unified multimodal annotations and systematic quality control. A unified kinematic representation encodes locomotion through camera poses and manipulation through projected skeletons, with robot skeletons combining embodiment-specific forward kinematics with extensive manual keypoint annotation and correction. We also introduce Xp-Teleop, a long-horizon loco-manipulation benchmark based on high-quality dexterous humanoid teleoperation data. Evaluations demonstrate state-of-the-art performance across five cross-embodiment benchmarks (DreamDojo, $Ψ_0$, $ω$-HOME, Xp-Teleop, and AgiBot) and on held-out human egocentric video, from zero-shot inference to few-shot and full-data post-training. Scaling human pre-training data improves performance on both human and robot evaluations. After distillation, our model supports real-time, minute-long rollouts, enabling live VR interaction. Applications in policy evaluation, model-based planning, and world-action modeling further demonstrate the model's utility for embodied decision-making.

Vision-Language-Delta-Action: Learning Compact Deltas as Actionable Transition Interfaces for Robotic Manipulation

2026-10-19 cs.ROcs.CV 👤 Rui Shao (h=18)
Lingsen Zhang, Wei Li, Jie He, Henghua Zhang, Kai Mo
Core Contributions
  • Argues that manipulation is realizing a state change, so full future prediction wastes capacity on static appearance; VLDA predicts a compact latent Delta as the interface between language-vision understanding and action.
  • Delta is trained with a Local Transition Filter (task-relevant changes, suppressing static reconstruction bias) and a Global Transition Anchor (coarse direction of change).
  • Delta-Driven Action Refinement then uses Delta as a structured seed for action denoising, grounding it in the action manifold.
  • Claims improved long-horizon consistency, robustness and generalization, implemented in the open-source LoongForge framework.
Show abstract
Robotic manipulation is not merely observation-to-action generation, but the realization of task-specified state changes in the physical world. Existing vision-language-action (VLA) methods directly learn observation-to-action mappings, imposing a substantial learning burden due to the gap between semantic understanding and action execution. Although world-action models (WAMs) improve control through future prediction, modeling complete future states wastes capacity on static appearance and action-irrelevant changes. We propose Vision-Language-Delta-Action (VLDA), a paradigm that reformulates full future prediction as compact actionable transition modeling. Its core is a compact Delta variable that captures latent world-state changes, serving as an interface between vision-language understanding and action generation. VLDA learns Delta through a unified objective composed of the Local Transition Filter (LT-Filter) and Global Transition Anchor (GT-Anchor). LT-Filter captures task-relevant manipulation-centric changes while suppressing static reconstruction biases. GT-Anchor provides coarse future-level regularization to preserve the overall direction of state changes. Delta-Driven Action Refinement (DDAR) further grounds Delta in the action manifold and injects it as a structured seed to guide action denoising. We implement VLDA within LoongForge, an open-source training framework for large-scale foundation and embodied models, using its modular and distributed infrastructure for unified two-stage optimization. Experiments show that VLDA improves long-horizon consistency, robustness, and generalization, validating actionable Delta as an alternative to direct action prediction and full future-state modeling.

Physical Words: A Vocabulary for Structuring Latent Reasoning in Robot Manipulation

2026-10-19 cs.RO 👤 Soha Pouya (h=15)
Jiaming Liu, Xinghao Zhu, Yiming Zhang, Shanghang Zhang, Yinxi Wang
Core Contributions
  • Introduces Physical Words: atomic action verbs used as semantic anchors for continuous latent representations of physical dynamics, between language reasoning (too coarse) and unstructured latent prediction.
  • PhysWord predicts upcoming action words and aligns their hidden states with latent targets from future observations, building a temporally structured space with visual and optional tactile variants.
  • Reports 90.3% on visuotactile UniVTAC and 99.6% on LIBERO-Long, and wins on real bimanual-gripper and dexterous-hand tasks.
  • Gives reasoning-before-acting models reusable primitives for long-horizon manipulation.
Show abstract
Multimodal reasoning provides robotic foundation models with predictive context needed for reliable action generation. However, language-based reasoning captures task semantics but struggles to express fine-grained physical dynamics, while latent reasoning models future state evolution but offers limited structure for organizing these dynamics into reusable reasoning primitives. To bridge this gap, we introduce PhysWord, a reasoning-before-acting foundation model built on Physical Words, which use atomic action verbs as shared semantic anchors for continuous representations of physical dynamics. Specifically, PhysWord predicts upcoming atomic action words and directly aligns their contextual hidden states with latent targets derived from the corresponding future observations. Building on these primitives, we construct a temporally structured PhysWord space with visual and optional tactile variants of each action word. Autoregressively predicting Physical Words across future timesteps captures transitions between execution stages, providing structured multimodal context for action generation. PhysWord achieves state-of-the-art success rates of 90.3\% on visuotactile UniVTAC and 99.6\% on vision-based LIBERO-Long, and outperforms competing methods on real-world tasks using bimanual grippers and dexterous hands. Further evaluations demonstrate that our structured latent reasoning framework generalizes to unseen scenarios and enables long-horizon manipulation.

TrajWAM: An Efficient Trajectory-Guided World Action Model for Dynamic Robotic Manipulation

2026-10-19 cs.RO 👤 Jian-Min Ji (h=14)
Haohan Geng, Sha Zhang, Weishuo Sun, Guanting Ye, Wenhao Yu
Core Contributions
  • Targets dynamic object manipulation where scene-level video prediction underrepresents the target and iterative denoising is too slow.
  • A history-conditioned predictor forecasts future object masks; a lightweight Trajectory ControlNet steers a pretrained video model to trajectory-consistent futures that condition the action expert.
  • Decoupled distillation (distribution matching for video, ControlNet realignment, action rollout distillation) enables one-step video-action inference while keeping the trajectory pathway.
  • Claims state-of-the-art dynamic manipulation in simulation and real world with far lower latency than iterative world-action models.
Show abstract
Dynamic object manipulation requires robots to anticipate target motion while replanning fast enough to avoid executing outdated actions. Existing approaches either rely on fast reactive control without an explicit future target state or encode anticipated motion only implicitly. World Action Models (WAMs) offer a promising alternative by coupling future visual prediction with action generation, but scene-level video objectives may underrepresent target dynamics that are critical for manipulation, while iterative video-action denoising introduces substantial latency. We present TrajWAM, a trajectory-guided world action model for efficient dynamic robotic manipulation. Our key insight is to explicitly ground future visual imagination in predicted target-object motion and use the resulting future representation to condition action generation, while preserving this anticipation pathway after acceleration. Given a short history of object masks, a history-conditioned motion predictor forecasts future masks encoding the target object's trajectory. A lightweight Trajectory ControlNet then steers a pretrained video model toward trajectory-consistent futures. The resulting future representation conditions an action expert to predict executable action chunks. To enable one-step inference without breaking this interface, we introduce a decoupled distillation scheme that combines distribution matching for the video backbone, Trajectory ControlNet realignment, and action rollout distillation to support accurate one-step action prediction conditioned on the compressed future representation. Comprehensive evaluations in simulation and the real world show that TrajWAM achieves state-of-the-art performance on dynamic object manipulation tasks while enabling efficient one-step video-action inference. Project page: https://kevingeng3.github.io/TrajWAM/.

Think Densely, Act Sparsely: Latent Expert Cognitive Chains for Vision-Language-Action Autonomous Driving

2026-10-19 cs.CVcs.RO 👤 Hang-Jun Ye (h=9)
Jie Wang, Guang Li, Zhijian Huang, Jinlong Li, Chenxu Dang
Core Contributions
  • Argues driving VLAs fail not for lack of capacity but because sparse supervision (language, trajectories) never forces a dense world representation; LECDrive adds task-specific latent tokens that absorb expert knowledge.
  • Tokens internalize DINOv3 visual features, SAM3 semantics and DepthAnything3 geometry through a curriculum from representation to semantics to geometry, keeping inference efficient.
  • Consistently improves base VLMs across driving benchmarks for perception, state prediction and trajectory planning.
  • Distills heavyweight vision foundation models into the VLA without running them at test time.
Show abstract
Autonomous driving fundamentally demands dense understanding of visual, semantic, and geometric information, yet existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are typically trained with sparse supervision such as language instructions or trajectory signals. This direct mapping from dense visual observations to sparse representations forces the model to compress or even discard critical scene information essential for safe driving. We argue that this limitation does not stem from insufficient model capacity, but from the lack of explicit mechanisms that encourage the formation of dense world representations in the latent space. In this spirit, we propose a novel paradigm for driving intelligence, Think Densely, Act Sparsely, and instantiate it with an efficient and interpretable framework, LECDrive (Latent Expert Cognitive Chains for Vision-Language-Action autonomous Driving). The core idea is to mimic the hierarchical perception process of humans by embedding a progressive chain of latent expert cognition within the VLA model. Concretely, we inject a compact set of task-specific latent tokens as carriers, enabling the model to internalize dense knowledge from foundational vision models, including visual representations (DINOv3), semantic structures (SAM3), and spatial geometry (DepthAnything3), while maintaining efficient inference. This interdependent cognitive chain follows a curriculum learning principle, where supervision is progressively structured from representation to semantics and geometry. Extensive experiments demonstrate that LECDrive consistently improves the performance of base VLMs across multiple autonomous driving benchmarks, covering key tasks such as object perception, state prediction, and trajectory planning. These findings suggest that constructing dense world models through structured latent expert chains is a promising direction for VLA-based autonomous driving.

SPAR: Structural Pruning and Action Recovery for Vision-Language-Action Models

2026-10-19 cs.ROcs.AI 👤 Yuan Du (h=7)
Shenli Zheng, Zhou Fang, Yuan Du, Yijiang Liu, Li Du
Core Contributions
  • Prunes the hidden and feed-forward dimensions of the LLM backbone with pruning-aware tuning, then uses RL to recover manipulation performance, which supervised fine-tuning cannot fully do.
  • On pi0.5, the language backbone shrinks from 2.34B to 0.09B (about 96%) and the full model from 3.37B to 1.12B (about 67%).
  • RL raises average LIBERO success from 84.55% to 93.45%, an 8.90-point gain, with 1.98x faster full-policy inference.
  • Indicates large redundancy in VLA intermediate dimensions and a route to onboard deployment.
Show abstract
The limited onboard computing resources of robots make the inference demands of vision-language-action (VLA) models costly to accommodate. Reducing these demands through aggressive compression, however, can degrade manipulation performance beyond what supervised fine-tuning alone recovers. We present SPAR (Structural Pruning and Action Recovery), which simultaneously prunes the hidden and feed-forward intermediate dimensions of the large language model (LLM) backbone through pruning-aware tuning, then applies reinforcement learning (RL) to recover policy performance. Applied to $π_{0.5}$, SPAR reduces the language backbone from 2.34B to 0.09B parameters and the full model from 3.37B to 1.12B, removing approximately 96% and 67% of their parameters, respectively. At this compression level, RL raises average success across the four LIBERO suites from 84.55% to 93.45%, an 8.90-percentage-point gain. The compressed policy achieves a $1.98\times$ full-policy inference speedup over the unpruned model. These results identify substantial intermediate-dimension redundancy and show that post-pruning RL can recover manipulation performance in compact policies.

PaceVLA: Adaptive Temporal Action Quantization in Vision-Language-Action Models

2026-10-19 cs.ROcs.AI 👤 Joonwoo Ahn (h=7)
Hojin Jeon, Jimin Lee, Taekwan Kim, Nayoung Oh, Taeyoung Kim
Core Contributions
  • Targets slow execution from fine-grained teleoperation actions; naive temporal action quantization trades speed for accuracy in precision-critical phases.
  • PaceVLA uses mode-specific action decoders on a shared action expert, supporting several temporal granularities and prediction horizons.
  • A quantizability-guided scheme and a context-conditioned router choose coarse or fine execution at each inference step, speeding up easy phases and slowing for delicate ones.
  • Improves efficiency while maintaining success across three simulation benchmarks and six real tasks, including long-horizon and dexterous ones.
Show abstract
Fast and reliable manipulation is crucial for practical robot deployment. Although Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have recently shown remarkable progress as generalist robot policies, their execution often remains temporally inefficient. A key source of this inefficiency is that policies learn fine-grained temporal patterns from teleoperated demonstrations, requiring many low-level action steps. Temporal action quantization offers a simple way to accelerate execution by aggregating consecutive low-level actions into temporally coarser actions. However, applying action quantization without considering when fine-grained control is required introduces a speed-accuracy trade-off: accelerated actions may efficiently reduce redundant steps in some phases, while discarding critical motion details in precision-sensitive interactions. To address this, we propose PaceVLA, a unified framework for adaptive temporal execution in VLAs that integrates two coupled components: (1) mode-specific action decoders built on a shared action expert, supporting multiple temporal granularities and prediction horizons, and (2) a quantizability-guided mode selection scheme which decides whether coarse execution is appropriate, and a context-conditioned router that selects an execution mode at each inference step. Experiments across three multi-task simulation benchmarks and six real-world tasks show that PaceVLA improves execution efficiency while maintaining task success rate across multiple embodiments, including long-horizon and dexterous manipulation.

Policy Search in Code Space for Multi-Agent Pathfinding

2026-10-19 cs.ROcs.AI 👤 V. Farias (h=32)
Matthias Eder, Pantelis Emmanouil, Vivek Farias, Fabian Fischer, Adam Jozefiak
Core Contributions
  • Proposes searching directly in code space: coding agents propose, implement, evaluate, diagnose and refine multi-agent coordination algorithms under human direction, with explicit correctness verification.
  • On a hard 2024 League of Robot Runners map, a two-day search yielded a policy beating the stronger locally reproduced entry by 35.0% on the densest instance and generalizing to unseen maps.
  • In simulated warehouses with irregular lanes and asynchronous motion, a week-long search found a new class of MAPF algorithm with 58.9% higher throughput than a calibrated proprietary solution.
  • Unlike RL, the output is readable code, and the authors prove under stated assumptions that it is collision-free and completes every accepted goal in finite time.
Show abstract
Developing coordination policies for complex multi-agent systems relies on either labor-intensive algorithm design or opaque task-specific reinforcement learning. With recent advances in autonomous coding agents, we show that policy search directly in code space offers a compelling third path that combines automated optimization with explicit interpretability. We investigate a human-directed workflow in which coding agents iteratively propose, implement, evaluate, diagnose, and eventually refine algorithms guided by simulation feedback and explicit correctness verification. We conducted two case studies and found that this workflow robustly outperformed any manually engineered solutions. On a challenging map in the 2024 League of Robot Runners competition, a two-day search discovered a policy that exceeds the stronger locally reproduced competition entry by 35.0% on the densest instance and generalizes to unseen map instances. In simulated real-world warehouses with irregular lanes, continuous robot geometry, and asynchronous motion, a week-long search discovered an entirely new class of multi-agent pathfinding algorithm that outperforms a calibrated proprietary solution in throughput by 58.9%. Under stated assumptions, we further prove that the new algorithm is collision-free and completes, in finite time, every accepted goal that is not withdrawn or replaced. Together, the studies show that policy search in code space powered by state-of-the-art coding agents offers a highly competitive path to building real engineering solutions that are both effective and analytically tractable.

Simulation Priors for Rapid Adaptation in Model-Based RL for Autonomous Racing

2026-10-19 cs.LGcs.ROeess.SY 👤 Nicklas Hansen (h=21)
Adrian Remonda, Jiajun Xi, Nicklas Hansen, Marcus Greiff, Thomas Lew
Core Contributions
  • Treats simulators as biased priors rather than transfer targets: PIA pretrains a world model on mixed low- and high-fidelity simulated trajectories, then corrects the mismatch online with uncertainty-regularized planning.
  • In simulation it adapts faster than strong baselines across track and surface shifts, with lower lap times and fewer failures.
  • On a real full-size autonomous race car, it adapts few-shot with under one hour of on-vehicle interaction, which matters where data is costly and failures are unsafe.
  • Shows cheap, imperfect simulation can be useful without system identification of an accurate simulator.
Show abstract
Reinforcement learning enables learning high-performance control policies, but its reliance on extensive interaction limits deployment on physical systems where data collection is costly, and failures are unsafe. We introduce Prior-Informed Adaptation (PIA), an offline-to-online model-based RL framework that uses structured simulation priors for rapid adaptation. PIA pretrains a world model on heterogeneous low- and high-fidelity simulated trajectories, then adapts it with limited target-domain interaction using uncertainty-regularized planning. Rather than requiring an accurate simulator for policy transfer, PIA treats simulated trajectories as biased priors whose residual mismatch is corrected during online adaptation. We evaluate PIA in autonomous racing using high-fidelity simulation and on a real full-size autonomous race car. In simulation, PIA adapts faster across track and surface shifts than strong baselines, achieving lower lap times and fewer failures. On hardware, PIA achieves reliable few-shot adaptation with less than one hour of on-vehicle interaction.

Robot Learning with Vision-Language-Action Models for Agricultural Manipulation

2026-10-19 cs.RO 👤 J. M. Velni (h=17)
Hamid Hafezi, Javad Mohammadpour Velni, Mohammadreza Davoodi
Core Contributions
  • Builds a sim-to-real pipeline for tomato-plant reaching and grasping: a MuJoCo environment with varied plant structures, joystick-teleoperated demonstrations, then real Franka fine-tuning.
  • Compares two pretrained generalists, Octo and pi0.5, under in-distribution, viewpoint/spatial-shift and out-of-distribution conditions.
  • pi0.5 scores higher and degrades less under distribution shift; the paper also studies how much proprioception contributes.
  • Practical evidence that simulated demonstrations can cheaply seed generalist policies for agriculture, where real data is expensive.
Show abstract
Robotic manipulation in agricultural environments remains challenging due to variations in plant geometry, occlusion, and camera viewpoints. Pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) and generalist robot policies offer a promising approach to addressing these challenges by learning manipulation behaviors from multimodal observations and language instructions. However, adapting these policies to agricultural tasks requires domain-specific demonstrations, which are costly and time-consuming to collect on physical robots. In this work, we develop a simulation-to-real robot-learning framework for agricultural reaching and grasping that integrates task-specific demonstration collection and pretrained policy adaptation. A MuJoCo-based agricultural environment is developed with diverse tomato plant structures to support demonstration collection and controlled evaluation. Expert demonstrations are collected through joystick teleoperation, with a low-level controller used to execute robot actions. Two pretrained generalist robot policies, Octo and $π_{0.5}$, are adapted using simulated demonstrations, followed by further fine-tuning on a physical Franka Research 3 manipulator using real-robot demonstrations. The influence of proprioceptive information on policy performance is also investigated. Results show that $π_{0.5}$ achieves higher task-performance scores than Octo and exhibits less degradation under distribution shifts. These findings demonstrate the potential of integrating pretrained generalist policies with task-specific demonstration learning for agricultural manipulation.

Progress-Guided Preference Generation for Aligning Generative Control Policies

2026-10-19 cs.LGcs.AIcs.RO 👤 V. Ngo (h=17)
Tung M. Luu, Tri Ton, Duc M. Nguyen, Xuan Thanh Nguyen, Nguyen Gia Binh
Core Contributions
  • Replaces human preference labels with automatic ones: ordered coverage alignment measures how much of the expert demonstrations' sequential progress each rollout achieves.
  • Ranks autonomously collected rollouts by progress to produce pseudo-preferences, with enhancements for label reliability, then fine-tunes pretrained generative policies.
  • Outperforms supervised fine-tuning on long-horizon manipulation and works with both diffusion and flow-matching policies.
  • Removes both the human annotation and the hand-designed reward typically needed for preference alignment.
Show abstract
Generative policies have achieved strong performance in imitation learning tasks, but their success is often constrained by the limited availability of expert demonstrations. Although preference-based fine-tuning can leverage suboptimal rollouts to improve performance, existing approaches heavily rely on expensive human annotations. In this work, we introduce Progress-Guided Preference Generation (PGPG), an unsupervised preference alignment framework that eliminates the need for human-in-the-loop supervision. Our method leverages ordered coverage alignment to measure sequential progress relative to a small set of expert demonstrations, enabling the automatic extraction of pseudo-preference labels from autonomously collected rollouts. Furthermore, we introduce key enhancements to improve the reliability of these progress-guided preferences, enabling effective fine-tuning of pretrained generative policies without requiring manually designed reward functions. Extensive experiments across a suite of challenging long-horizon manipulation tasks demonstrate that PGPG outperforms standard supervised fine-tuning baselines. Moreover, our conceptually simple framework is broadly applicable across multiple classes of generative policies, demonstrating strong versatility when integrated with both diffusion and flow-matching policies.

Long-Horizon Multi-Object Rearrangement via Distilled Tree Search over Continuous Actions

2026-10-19 cs.RO 👤 Maxim Likhachev (h=9)
Kallol Saha, Yining Hong, Amber Li, Yejin Choi, Maxim Likhachev
Core Contributions
  • Asks how to transfer long-horizon search into policies in continuous action spaces: progressive-widening MCTS in GPU-parallel physics simulation, with a learned policy proposing actions and a learned value function scoring progress.
  • Periodically distills the MCTS plans back into the policy-value model, so later searches start from better proposals and values (an AlphaZero-style loop for continuous manipulation).
  • At deployment the policy-value model runs without simulator search.

We introduce DexOAK, a learning-based object-aware kinematic retargeting framework for generating contact-consistent robot hand references from human motion-capture data. DexOAK first minimizes an interaction-graph Laplacian objective over a joint hand-object graph, so the robot reproduces the demonstrated object-relative interaction geometry rather than the human hand pose alone. These per-frame solutions form seed retargeted references, from which a feed-forward network conditioned on human keypoints and object point clouds learns the retargeting function.
