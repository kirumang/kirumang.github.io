🔭 Research Landscape

The strongest signal today is a push to make learning cheaper by moving it into a model. SuperMimic (#1) replaces cluster-scale model-free RL for humanoid motion tracking with one differentiable world model and analytic gradients, matching a 7-day, 128-GPU baseline in 3 hours on one GPU. The same logic runs through the world-action papers: TaskWAM (#14) adds Mode and State tokens so imagined futures stay on the right task stage, PhysCanvas (#15) turns a visuo-tactile video model into an evaluator and RL environment for pi0.5, and UniWALM (#16) co-denoises video, action and language so generated text serves as memory. The shared bet is that a good learned simulator or predictive representation is worth more than additional real rollouts. Multi-View Latent Action Pretraining (#25) and the predictive-geometry work (#28) attack the same bottleneck from the data side, extracting action signal from action-free video.

A second theme is a sober round of diagnosis. Perceives but Doesn't Brake (#11) shows driving VLAs encode hazards yet let ego-speed history dominate the plan, then fixes it at sampling time without retraining. RoboCasa-Shift (#19) shows that identical end-to-end drops hide different stage-level failures, CAPSafe (#17) finds models recognize unsafe actions at over 90% but plan safely far less often, and JARVIS (#24) finds assembly performance collapsing with horizon. The two GPT-6 Astra papers (#18, #30) are a useful contrast: one finds only 11.1% zero-shot success on geometry-constrained tasks, the other reaches 93.8% on tactile tasks when expert demonstrations are summarized into guidance. Together they suggest general models are strong where the task can be expressed as reasoning plus feedback, and weak where precise motion is the bottleneck.

Third, there is steady progress on structure and guarantees for classical problems. SPAR (#4) learns which pushes to try from search outcomes, #5 and #13 give learned safety and reach-avoid sets either verification-guided or probabilistic rather than worst-case, PUN (#7) uses neural operators for noise-aware planning, and RILC (#8) couples RL with ILC while keeping a stability proof. On the data side, LocoPilot (#9) and the PointHand study (#22) both argue the interface shapes the demonstration, and SceneRig (#10) turns single images into physics-stable simulation scenes. Taken together, the batch points to a field where scale-oriented learning and formal methods increasingly borrow each other's tools.

SuperMimic: Scaling Humanoid Whole-Body Control with One Differentiable World Model

2026-10-20 cs.RO 👤 K. Sreenath (h=58)
Qiayuan Liao, Yiyang Shao, Junfeng Long, Kevin Zakka, Roberto Horowitz
Core Contributions
  • Fits one differentiable world model to logged transitions of a standard rigid-body simulator and trains the tracking policy through it by backprop over short imagined rollouts, replacing high-variance model-free RL with analytic gradients.
  • Cost drops sharply: 3 hours on a single GPU beats the return of a baseline trained for 7 days on 128 GPUs (over 1000x fewer GPU-hours), with 25 failures on 129,514 motions versus 179.
  • Scaling holds up: world-model error stays stable from 1 to 264 hours of motion and zero failures are reached after 9.8 hours on two GPUs, which makes motion-tracking scaling laws measurable.
  • Transfers to a physical Unitree G1 on acrobatic motions, so the differentiable model is not just a simulator artifact.
Show abstract
Tracking large human-motion datasets has become the dominant route to general, expressive humanoid whole-body control, but scaling across many motions is expensive. Current pipelines use model-free reinforcement learning, either merging per-motion specialists through lossy distillation, or brute-forcing a single policy with cluster-scale compute. SuperMimic amortizes learning across all motions with one differentiable world model of their shared dynamics, fit from logged transitions of a standard rigid-body simulator. The world model and the tracking policy train together in one loop, and backpropagation-through-time over short imagined rollouts gives the policy analytic gradients. Fast imagined rollouts and analytic gradients make policy learning efficient. In 3 hours on a single GPU, SuperMimic surpasses the return of a state-of-the-art motion tracker baseline trained for 7 days on 128 GPUs. It reduces GPU-hours by more than $1000\times$ and fails on only 25 of 129,514 benchmark motions, versus 179 failures for the baseline. After 9.8 hours on two GPUs, SuperMimic achieves zero failures. From 1 to 264 hours of motion, the world model's prediction error stays stable, and GPU-hours to a target return grow slowly. Our policy transfers to a physical Unitree G1 across diverse and acrobatic motions. SuperMimic removes compute as the bottleneck to scaling humanoid whole-body control, making it practical to measure motion-tracking scaling laws.

Learning Adaptable Dexterous Skills via Hybrid Dexterous Mimicking

2026-10-20 cs.ROcs.CVcs.GRcs.LG 👤 Shubham Tulsiani (h=48)
Yuxuan Kuang, Jiashun Wang, Yixuan Sun, Yufei Ye, Sungjae Park
Core Contributions
  • Identifies the trade-off in dexterity learning: goal-reaching RL generalizes but yields unnatural fingers, while motion tracking looks human but cannot deviate from its clip.
  • Hybrid Dexterous Mimicking trains one shared policy on two environment populations at once, dense-tracking environments that inject the human prior and noised-goal environments with sparse object-success reward that force generalization.
  • Needs only a few seconds of hand-object trajectory per skill, from mocap or reconstructed from in-the-wild video, and yields grasping, in-hand reorientation with finger gaiting and throw-and-catch.
  • Shows transfer across simulators and to a real hand, which matters because naive RL dexterity policies often break at that step.
Show abstract
Dexterous multi-finger hands hold promise to unlock diverse skills like functional grasps, in-hand reorientation, and dynamic handover that the human-designed world assumes, yet existing learning recipes force a trade-off. Goal-reaching reinforcement learning generalizes to arbitrary object poses but, lacking any human prior, converges to unnatural and unreliable finger strategies; motion tracking reproduces human-like behavior from a single clip but cannot deviate from it. We present AdaDex, a goal-conditioned RL framework that turns a single hand-object trajectory of a few seconds---from mocap data or HOI reconstruction from in-the-wild RGB(D) video---into an adaptable dexterous skill. At the core of AdaDex is Hybrid Dexterous Mimicking: a shared policy is trained on two populations of massively parallel environments: motion tracking environments trained with dense motion tracking objective, which instill the human prior from human motion; and generalization environments with noised initial states and goals and a sparse object-success reward, which force the skill to work far off the reference. From seconds of human motion per skill, the resulting policies can learn diverse adaptable dexterous skills, such as functional grasping, object reposing, in-hand reorientation with finger gaiting, and dynamic behaviors like throw-and-catch. Our method outperforms baselines in terms of generalization and functionality, and we demonstrate transfer across simulators and to the real world. Project page: https://yuxuank.com/AdaDex/.

VLM-Guided Contact Retargeting and Generative Partner Modeling for Humanoid-Human Interaction

2026-10-20 cs.ROcs.AIcs.CV 👤 Kashu Yamazaki (h=15)
Yuto Shibata, Kotaro Amaya, Kashu Yamazaki, Lalit Jayanti, Yoshimitsu Aoki
Core Contributions
  • Targets humanoid-human contact where the partner is occluded or out of view, which breaks pose estimation and corrupts mocap from heavy occlusion.
  • Uses a VLM to find semantically meaningful contacts and retarget them to humanoid-human geometry, then trains privileged policies with contact rewards and distills them into egocentric depth policies.
  • Adds generative partner-state modeling to predict the partner's motion from occluded views, improving contact recall on four of five interactions.
  • Runs all five interactions on a real Unitree G1 using onboard sensing only, without external tracking.
Show abstract
Humanoid-human interaction requires robots to operate under severe partial observability: during close physical contact, the human partner is occluded, truncated, or outside the robot's field of view, causing pose estimation to fail and often necessitating external tracking or privileged human state. At the same time, heavy mutual occlusions introduce errors into the human-human motion-capture data used for learning. We present REACT, a framework for learning deployable humanoid-human interaction policies from human-human demonstrations. REACT treats semantic contact structure as the key supervisory signal connecting demonstration processing, interaction learning, and onboard control. First, a VLM-guided contact modeling and retargeting pipeline identifies semantically meaningful contacts and transfers them onto humanoid-human geometry while preserving interaction structure. Using these annotations, privileged interaction policies learn physically grounded behaviors through interaction-aware contact rewards and are then distilled into deployable egocentric depth policies trained entirely from onboard sensing using photorealistic human depth rendering. To address severe partial observability, we introduce generative partner-state modeling objectives that predict partner motion from occluded egocentric views. We evaluate REACT in simulation and on a real Unitree G1 humanoid across five contact-rich interactions. Semantic contact modeling substantially improves preservation of demonstrated interactions, while generative partner-state modeling improves contact recall on four of five interactions, most under heavy occlusion. On real hardware, REACT performs all five interactions using onboard sensing alone.

Plausible Future, Wrong Choice: Steering World-Action Models toward Intended Future

2026-10-20 cs.ROcs.AI 👤 Jia-Wei He (h=23)
Zhuohao Li, Jiawei He, Yuxuan Yan, Lang Zhou, Yinghao Li
Core Contributions
  • Identifies Stage Confusion: in long-horizon tasks where similar observation-action segments recur, world-action models generate futures that are locally plausible but belong to the wrong task stage.
  • TaskWAM adds Mode Tokens for fine-grained task requirements and State Tokens for execution progress, forming a structured task state that steers generation in closed loop.
  • Ships StageBench, a controlled multi-stage benchmark with parameterized stage variations and automated scoring, so the failure can be measured.
  • Plugs into different WAM architectures and embodiments (single-arm, dual-arm, dexterous hands, humanoids), suggesting it fixes a general weakness.
Show abstract
World-Action Models (WAMs) have emerged as a promising paradigm for embodied generalist policies by jointly modeling future world evolution and robot actions across diverse manipulation tasks. However, representing diverse futures is only the first step: a capable WAM must also generate futures that match the requirements of the current task and execution progress. In long-horizon manipulation where similar observation-action segments occur across different tasks and stages, existing approaches may generate locally plausible yet misdirected rollouts to drift toward wrong task stage, i.e., a phenomenon we term Stage Confusion. To address this problem, we propose TaskWAM, a closed-loop, stage-aware framework that represents fine-grained task requirements with Mode Tokens and tracks execution progress with State Tokens, forming a structured task state to steer world--action generation toward intended future. We further introduce StageBench, a controlled multi-stage manipulation benchmark with parameterized stage variations and automated evaluation.Extensive experiments on both simulation benchmarks and real-world tasks demonstrate that TaskWAM effectively reduces confusion and achieves competitive performance with state-of-the-art methods. Moreover, TaskWAM provides strong plug-and-play transfer across heterogeneous WAM architectures and robot embodiments, including single-arm robots, dual-arm grippers, dexterous hands, and humanoid robots.

Physical Canvas: A Vision Tactile World Model For Robot Policy Evaluation And Learning

2026-10-20 cs.RO 👤 Yu-Peng Zheng (h=20)
Zebin Xing, Yuhang Zheng, Yupeng Zheng, Yichen Zhang, Zichen Li
Core Contributions
  • Argues that visually plausible world-model rollouts can misrepresent contact dynamics, making them unreliable for policy evaluation and learning.
  • Maps RGB, tactile images, marker displacement, task state and actions into one visual space with a shared video VAE and backbone, so touch reuses visual and temporal priors, with forward-kinematics motion images for action conditioning.
  • Post-trains with scene, modality and state reward models; the state reward raises the mean Pearson correlation between world-model and real success rates from 0.602 to 0.885.
  • Using the world model for GRPO updates lifts pi0.5 from 59.1% (SFT) to 70.8% real-world success.
Show abstract
Evaluating and post-training robot policies through physical interaction is costly and difficult to scale, motivating action-conditioned world models as interactive simulators. However, visually plausible predictions can misrepresent contact dynamics, providing unreliable feedback for policy evaluation and learning. We introduce PhysCanvas, a state-consistent visuo-tactile world model for contact-rich robot manipulation. PhysCanvas unifies multi-view RGB, tactile images, marker displacement, task state, and actions within a unified vision space. All modalities share a video VAE and pretrained generative backbone, allowing optical touch to reuse visual and temporal priors. Motion images derived through forward kinematics and camera projection provide spatially aligned action conditioning, while an initial RGB anchor and updated reference observations guide sequential generation. Our consistency-aware post-training uses the frozen Scene Reward Model, Modality Reward Model, and State Reward Model to supervise scene preservation, visual-tactile compatibility, and agreement with real task outcomes. The resulting world model supports closed-loop policy rollouts for evaluation and provides imagined interactions and rewards for GRPO policy updates. Across four real-world manipulation tasks, when policies are rolled out in the world model, State Reward Model supervision increases the arithmetic mean of task-wise Pearson correlations between world-model and real-world success rates from 0.602 to 0.885. Policy post-training in the reward-guided world model further increases $π_{0.5}$'s mean real-world success rate from a 59.1% SFT baseline to 70.8%. These results demonstrate the value of unified multimodal modeling and consistency-aware post-training for robot policy evaluation and learning. Project page: https://zebinx.github.io/PhysCanvas_HomePage/.

UniWALM: Unified World Action Language Model for Embodied Robot Control

2026-10-20 cs.ROcs.AIcs.CV 👤 J. van Baar (h=19)
Rahul Sajnani, Diego Romeres, Mingyuan Zhang, Kai Gao, Carl Qi
Core Contributions
  • Puts video, action and language into one mixture-of-transformers with per-modality diffusion heads that co-denoise, rather than attaching a VLM planner as a separate stage.
  • Generated language is fed back at the next step, so subtask and textual memory work as a loop without an external planner or memory bank.
  • Adds Flow Matching with Correction, which teaches recovery from perturbed action trajectories at no inference-time cost.
  • Reaches 34.5% on memory-dependent RMBench versus 28.5% for a keyframe-equipped baseline, and on a real Find-the-Item task textual memory lifts success from 11.1% to 61.1% while VLAs stay at or below 33.3%.
Show abstract
Motivated by the human ability to integrate experience across language, perception, and action, we introduce UniWALM, a unified world-action-language model that learns video, action, and language in a single shared representation, where each modality is both a conditioning signal and a supervision target. We ask: can jointly generating language enrich this shared representation, improving manipulation and supporting memory-dependent behavior? Unlike prior approaches that attach a reasoning or vision-language model to a video-action backbone and run them as distinct stages, UniWALM unifies all three modalities inside a single mixture-of-transformers backbone whose per-modality diffusion heads co-denoise synchronously, decoding subtask and textual memory in the same pass as action with no separate planning stage; the generated language is re-consumed on the next step, closing a language-as-memory loop without an external planner or memory bank. To keep the action stream reliable under this richer objective, we further introduce Flow Matching with Correction (FMC), a training objective that teaches recovery from perturbed action trajectories at no inference-time cost. UniWALM consistently improves over same-backbone baselines (e.g., FastWAM) on compositional pick-and-place (MolmoSpaces) and across the memory-dependent subset of RMBench, where it reaches 34.5% without any pretrained language or reasoning model and exceeds Mem-0's keyframe-equipped setup (28.5%) using only a single anchor frame. On a real-world Find-the-Item task, generating textual memory lifts success from 11.1% to 61.1%, while state-of-the-art VLAs stay at or below chance (<=33.3%).

Multi-View Latent Action Pretraining for Cross-Embodiment Robot Learning from Videos

2026-10-20 cs.RO 👤 Angela P. Schoellig (h=13)
Oliver Hausdörfer, Felix Minzenmay, Mani Majd, Maximilian P. Christof, Shiyuan Zhang
Core Contributions
  • Argues single-view latent action models resolve 6D robot actions ambiguously, missing out-of-plane motion and occlusions, and fixes this with a multi-camera LAM using cross-attention.
  • Dual-view pretraining improves decodability of ground-truth 6D actions by 17% over single-view, and scaling single-view data by two orders of magnitude does not recover the gap.
  • Downstream success rises by 13.5% versus 6.2% for single-view; adding a second viewpoint beats adding up to four more embodiments.
  • On hardware with human-hand video demonstrations it improves success by 20% over action-only policies across 850 rollouts, while noting video benefits are task-dependent.
Show abstract
Action-free videos are an appealing substitute for costly teleoperated data. Latent action models (LAMs) turn videos into a supervision signal by inferring abstract actions between frames. Existing LAMs observe a single camera view, which resolves 6D robot actions only ambiguously missing out-of-plane movements and potential occlusions. We propose a LAM that ingests synchronized multi-camera views using cross-attention to resolve depth and occlusions before compressing them into latent actions. The latent actions are fed into a policy in which a latent expert and an action expert are coupled by sequential flow matching around a frozen VLM backbone. We further evaluate in strict cross-embodiment settings where task-specific demonstrations come from other embodiments. We show that dual-view LAM pretraining improves the decodability of ground-truth 6D actions by 17% compared to single-view, while scaling single-view data by two orders of magnitude is not sufficient to fully recover out-of-plane motions. The downstream policy improves success rate by +13.5% compared to +6.2% for single-view LAM. A single cross-embodiment is sufficient for skill transfer, and adding a second viewpoint outperforms adding up to four further embodiments. The results transfer to hardware where demonstrations come from a human hand improving success rate by +20% over action-only policies in 850 rollouts. We show that video and robot data are complementary, and that benefits of learning from video are task-dependent. Website: https://learnsyslab.github.io/multiview_latent_action_pretraining/

From Predictive Geometry to Action: Learning Complementary Predictive Representations for Embodied Manipulation

2026-10-20 cs.RO 👤 Hao Li (h=11)
Xiaoxu Xu, Hao Li, Hongtao Zhang, Yili Tang, Jiafei Cao
Core Contributions
  • Asks how predictive geometry should be represented for action generation, noting prior work either adds current geometry or uses future prediction only as auxiliary supervision.
  • Pretrains on multi-view video, compressing frozen 3D-foundation-model features into predictive geometry, then applies action supervision to geometric transitions to get a complementary motor representation.
  • Future observations are privileged supervision only, so inference needs no future input; gains include +10.4 absolute points on WidowX evaluation and improvements on LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, SimplerEnv and real tasks.
  • Transfers across several VLA and WAM architectures.
Show abstract
Effective manipulation requires a policy to capture not only about the current scene geometry, but also about how that geometry will evolve under interaction. Existing approaches either augment policies with geometric information from current observations or introduce future prediction as auxiliary supervision, leaving open how predictive geometry should be represented for action generation. Therefore, we propose a two-stage framework, which learns complementary predictive representations from multi-view video clips. During pretraining, a frozen 3D foundation model provides dense geometric features that are compressed into predictive geometric representations and optimized to preserve scene structure and its temporal evolution. Action supervision is then applied to geometric transitions to extract a complementary motor representation that emphasizes action-relevant transitions. During downstream post-training, predictive geometry is transferred through geometry queries, while the motor representation guides intermediate features along the action-generation pathway. Future observations are used only as privileged supervision during training and are not required at inference time. Across LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, SimplerEnv, and real-world bimanual and single-arm tasks, our approach consistently improves downstream policies, including an absolute gain of 10.4 percentage points on the WidowX evaluation. We further demonstrate transfer across multiple VLA architectures and WAMs, with improvements in both future-world and action prediction. The code will be available at https://github.com/xuxiaoxxxx/CPR.

Perceives but Doesn't Brake: A Perception-Action Binding Gap in Reasoning Driving VLAs

2026-10-20 cs.ROcs.AI 👤 Marco Pavone (h=27)
Matteo Cercola, Katie Z Luo, Simone Formentin, Marco Pavone
Core Contributions
  • Names a failure mode: reasoning driving VLAs encode in-path hazards in their visual features but longitudinal plans are dominated by ego-speed history and barely react to hazard proximity or the model's own reasoning.
  • The trajectory generator can brake, so the fault is in coupling rather than capability, which makes a retraining-free fix plausible.
  • Injects a hazard-conditioned energy into the frozen flow-matching sampler of Alpamayo-1.5, cutting footprint intrusion from 58.1% to 14.5% with exact obstacle geometry and removing collisions in the braking-relevant closed-loop tests.
  • Shows the plan flips from tracking ego speed to responding to obstacle range, supporting the diagnosis.
Show abstract
Reasoning-augmented vision-language-action (VLA) driving models can perceive and reason about safety-critical hazards yet fail to translate that evidence into safe actions. We characterize this failure as a perception-action binding gap: hazard information is available inside the model but exerts insufficient control over trajectory generation. Using matched simulated counterfactuals and logged driving replay, we show that in-path hazards are actually encoded in the visual representation, but longitudinal plans are dominated by ego-speed history and respond only weakly to hazard proximity or the model's own reasoning trace. Further analysis shows that the trajectory generator is capable of braking but sometimes fails to do so due to the perception-action binding gap. Without retraining or modifying any weights, we inject a hazard-conditioned energy into the frozen flow-matching sampler of Alpamayo-1.5, restoring the missing coupling at generation time. With exact obstacle geometry, guidance cuts footprint intrusion from 58.1% to 14.5% and recovers safe braking whenever sufficient stopping distance remains, eliminating collisions entirely in our braking-relevant closed-loop battery. It also reverses the diagnosed dependence of the plan, from tracking ego speed to responding to obstacle range. These results separate hazard perception from hazard control in driving VLAs, show that unsafe behavior can arise even when the model has both the evidence and the ability to act on it, and propose a simple fix for bridging this binding gap.

CAPSafe: A Benchmark for Contextual Action and Plan Safety Reasoning in Embodied Agents

2026-10-20 cs.ROcs.CL 👤 Svetlana Stoyanchev (h=19)
Youmna Farag, Svetlana Stoyanchev, Simon Keizer, Mohan Li, Rama Doddipatla
Core Contributions
  • Separates action-level safety (is this step safe in context) from plan-level safety (is a long-horizon plan safe), with situational hazards, co-occurring hazards and severity labels.
  • Evaluating nine LLMs shows recognition and planning diverge: best models exceed 90% on unsafe-action recognition, yet the top safe-plan success is 78.19% (Gemini-3-pro) and others fall below 65%.
  • The result implies that safety filters validated on single actions overstate how safe an embodied agent is over a full task.
Show abstract
Reasoning about safety is essential for embodied agents powered by large language models (LLMs). We introduce the Contextual Action and Plan Safety benchmark (CAPSafe), designed to evaluate safety reasoning at two levels: (1) an action-level dataset that tests whether a single action is safe given its environmental context, and (2) a plan-level dataset that evaluates whether agents can generate safe long-horizon plans for complex hazardous household tasks. CAPSafe models safety conditions where hazards are situational (inferred from object properties), multiple hazards can co-occur within the same environment, and each is annotated with a relative severity level for fine-grained analysis. Evaluating nine closed and open-source LLMs reveals a substantial gap between hazard recognition and safe long-horizon planning. Several models achieve high accuracy in recognizing unsafe actions (exceeding 90% in the best cases), yet safe long-horizon planning remains substantially harder. Gemini-3-pro achieves the highest safe success rate (78.19%), while other models remain below 65%. These results highlight that robust safety reasoning over long action sequences remains an open challenge.

Large Multimodal Models for Direct Robot Control: GPT-6 as a Beginning

2026-10-20 cs.RO 👤 Shang-Hang Zhang (h=19)
Youwei Zhou, Bohan Hou, Yankai Fu, Yaoxu Lyu, Yifan Ye
Core Contributions
  • Tests GPT-6 Astra as a direct closed-loop manipulation controller, first probing depth perception, embodied in-context learning and tactile correction, then five benchmarks across six capability axes.
  • Results are mixed by design: 76.0% on RoboTwin 2.0 and 26.92% on RoboDojo, but a clear gap between semantic reasoning and physical execution.
  • Introduces LIBERO-GeoMotion for geometry-constrained tasks needing coordinated translation and rotation, where zero-shot success is only 11.1%.
  • Real-robot tests confirm deficits in motion feasibility, precision and safety, and inference latency is a further barrier to deployment.
Show abstract
Recent advances in visuospatial understanding and agentic reasoning enable direct robotic manipulation with general-purpose multimodal models, but their embodied capabilities remain poorly understood. This paper systematically evaluate GPT-6 Astra for closed-loop robotic manipulation using a lightweight agent execution framework. We first probe three fundamental embodied capabilities-depth perception, embodied in-context learning, and tactile understanding-and demonstrate that the model can exploit depth information, acquire simple manipulation strategies from contextual demonstrations, and use tactile feedback to correct its actions. We then evaluate GPT-6 Astra across five established manipulation benchmarks along six capability dimensions. Despite competitive performance on several benchmarks-26.92\% success on RoboDojo and 76.0\% on RoboTwin 2.0-our analysis reveals a pronounced gap between semantic reasoning and physical execution, particularly in consequence-aware action planning, precision, and complex motion coordination. To further examine these limitations, we introduce LIBERO-GeoMotion, a new benchmark of geometry-constrained tasks that require precise pose alignment, coordinated translation and rotation, and anticipation of physical action consequences. GPT-6 Astra achieves only 11.1\% zero-shot success, exposing the difficulty of translating spatial understanding into physically feasible motion. Real-world experiments corroborate these findings: zero-shot execution is feasible for simple tasks, yet persistent deficits remain in motion feasibility, precise control, and physical safety, while substantial inference latency further hinders practical deployment. Overall, our results characterize the emerging embodied capabilities of general-purpose multimodal models and delineate the key challenges toward safe, efficient, and reliable robotic manipulation.

RoboCasa-Shift: Stage-Aware Robustness Evaluation of Vision-Language-Action Policies in Long-Horizon Household Manipulation

2026-10-20 cs.RO 👤 Ben Fei (h=19)
Haocheng Wang, Youquan Liu, Weiyu Guo, Aokai Zhang, Donghang Wu
Core Contributions
  • Challenges aggregate success as a robustness metric by pairing clean and perturbed rollouts that differ in exactly one shifted factor, with stage-level diagnostics.
  • Evaluates GR00T N1.5 on four long-horizon tasks with four perturbation factors and three severities over 1,040 rollouts.
  • Equal drops can hide different failures: camera L2 and Gaussian blur L1 both give a 0.188 success-rate drop, but first-stage failure rates are 19.0% and 36.4%.
  • Severity is not always monotonic, and one camera shift shows zero net change while 8 of 80 matched episodes flip outcome, so averages can hide real sensitivity.
Show abstract
