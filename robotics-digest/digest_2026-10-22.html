🔭 Research Landscape

The strongest thread in today's batch is the search for better structure to put inside a robot's learned model, and the cheapest ways to check that structure is real. UniFlowWAM (#12) promotes scene-level 3D flow to a first-class modality beside vision and action in a diffusion transformer, lifting real-robot success to 87.0% against 68.5% for the best baseline. Echo (#13) puts task status (stop and failure) inside the action chunk and denoises it faster than the actions, so the policy conditions on a clean completion estimate. At the data end, Learning Robot Behavior Priors from Non-Robotic Synthetic Data (#28) and the Soft-Object World Model (#29) both lean on procedurally or simulator-generated data plus in-context learning, while Co^2Gen (#16) shows planner-generated demonstrations can match human teleoperation for multi-robot VLA fine-tuning. Sensorimotor Alignment (#7) closes the loop by offering a training-free offline score that correlates with downstream success, which is exactly what you would want for choosing among such recipes without running rollouts.

A second theme is contact and uncertainty as interfaces. Humanoid Mobility via Attention-based Contacts (#1) makes predicted body-environment contact the bridge between motion priors and RL, OpenTac (#2) and GelSlim 5.0 (#9) widen what touch hardware can deliver (3D shear-plus-normal skins with kilohertz partial readout; vision and touch from one camera), and ProTrav (#8) and TRACE (#4) carry perceptual uncertainty or VLM confidence straight into MPPI and NMPC costs for off-road and forest navigation. The common move is to keep the controller classical and let a learned module expose a well-typed signal, whether that is contact, a risk bound or a soft barrier. The same instinct appears in the planning papers: GlobMP (#24) and the root-locus method (#6) aim for provable guarantees that can also correct learned proposals.

Third, the evaluation papers are unusually candid about gaps. AstroBench (#20) reports a frozen generalist checkpoint capturing only 2 of 200 static and 4 of 200 moving targets, PhysTrace (#19) shows video models violate physics in time-localizable ways, and the diffusion-planner study (#17) finds that no test-time expert-routing scheme beats a static default while fine-tuning on just 20 target scenes cuts offset from 12.4 m to 6.9 m. LocoManiLab (#23) gives humanoids a 234-task testbed and shows rankings in simulation track the real world. Read together with the medical cluster (#11, #18, #22), where careful phantom and simulation protocols reveal that imitation error and task success rank policies differently, the message is that offline metrics and imitation losses are not enough, and adaptation or task-grounded testing is where the headroom lies.

UniFlowWAM: A Unified World-Action Model with Visual Semantics and 4D Geometry

2026-10-22 cs.RO 👤 Lixin Yang (h=17)
Jialin Tian, Zhuofan Li, Xinkai Wang, Bokai Lin, Chenyi Wang
Core Contributions
  • Prior work keeps 3D scene flow outside the policy, as a separate module, inference prior or extra head; UniFlowWAM makes it a third modality with its own supervision beside vision and action.
  • A diffusion transformer with one expert per modality shares information by joint attention, and a ten-objective role grid (each modality is target, condition or absent) lets one model act as policy, forward and inverse dynamics, and feature or flow predictor.
  • On RoboCasa-GR1 success rises from 52.1% to 56.4% over the same base without flow, and LIBERO-Plus zero-shot reaches 87.7%.
  • On four real bimanual tasks it averages 87.0% versus 68.5% for the strongest baseline, a large real-world margin for adding geometry.
Show abstract
Robot demonstrations record what the robot saw and what it did. They do not specify how the 3D world changed in between. Scene-level 3D flow describes it by tracking 3D scene points, providing the world representation with 4D geometry. Prior work has used flow to improve robot policies. Flow served as an intermediate prediction that a separate model converts into actions, as a prior injected at inference time, or as an extra output learned together with the action. In all of these designs, flow stays outside the policy, as a separate module or as an extra prediction head. We introduce UniFlowWAM, a Unified World-Action Model that makes 3D scene flow a third modality on par with vision and action. Its world representation therefore covers both visual semantics, through future visual features, and 4D geometry, through 3D flow, and flow receives its own supervision. UniFlowWAM is a diffusion transformer with one expert per modality, and the three experts exchange information through joint attention. We train UniFlowWAM under a ten-objective role grid, where each objective assigns every modality exactly one of three roles, namely target, condition, or absent. The same model thus serves as a policy, as forward and inverse dynamics, and as a predictor of future visual features or 3D flow. We evaluate UniFlowWAM on RoboCasa-GR1, LIBERO, and LIBERO-Plus in simulation, and on four real-world bimanual manipulation tasks. On RoboCasa-GR1, UniFlowWAM raises the success rate of its base model without flow from 52.1% to 56.4%. It preserves in-distribution success on LIBERO at 98.1% and reaches 87.7% zero-shot on LIBERO-Plus. On the real robot, it achieves 87.0% average success, compared with 68.5% for the strongest baseline.
#13 h=15

Echo: Coupled Action and Task Status Signal Denoising for Vision Language Action Models

2026-10-22 cs.RO 👤 Ying-Dong Hu (h=15)
Yufeng Liu, Bohao Fan, Juntu Zhao, Xinyi Wang, Hang Yu
Core Contributions
  • Adds stop and failure signals as two extra dimensions of every action chunk and denoises them with the actions, so status prediction needs no external monitor or special architecture.
  • Learns the signals without frame-level progress labels by constructing successful, failed and recovery trajectories with complementary supervision.
  • Denoises the status signals faster than the actions so later action steps condition on a cleaner completion estimate, giving status a direct role in action generation.
  • Real-world experiments show accurate status prediction and consistently better policy performance.
Show abstract
Recognizing task completion and failure enables robots to stop upon success and initiate recovery or replanning after failure. Existing methods augment Vision Language Action (VLA) policies with task status signal prediction to support these execution decisions. However, learning and integrating these signals can require fine-grained supervision, external monitoring models, or complex architectural designs. Moreover, the potential of task status signals to directly guide action generation remains underexplored. We present Echo, a framework that combines task status signal learning without frame-level progress annotations with a compact design for status prediction and action generation within a single VLA. Specifically, Echo represents task status with stop and failure signals, added as two dimensions of each action chunk and denoised together with robot actions. Their combination distinguishes ongoing execution, successful completion, and failure. To learn these signals without fine-grained progress annotations, Echo constructs successful, failed, and recovery trajectories with complementary supervision properties. To enable status signal-guided action generation, Echo controls interactions between the signals and actions and denoises the signals faster than the actions. These allow later action denoising steps to condition on cleaner status estimates while preserving accurate signal prediction. Extensive real-world manipulation experiments show that Echo accurately predicts task status and consistently improves policy performance.

Co^2Gen: Collaboratively Generating Task Plans for Decentralized Multi-Robot and Human Collaboration

2026-10-22 cs.RO 👤 Malika Meghjani (h=15)
Yi Loo, Siddhant Shrivastava, Minh Long Pham, Malika Meghjani
Core Contributions
  • Automates multi-robot demonstration collection: a decentralized collaborative planner writes executable plans, which are run to produce data without human teleoperation.
  • Fine-tunes VLAs in a decentralized fashion, so each robot's policy learns to collaborate with others and with humans, an area generalist VLAs have barely covered.
  • VLAs fine-tuned on this generated data match the task performance of those trained on human-teleoperated collaborative data, removing a major scaling bottleneck.
Show abstract
Visual Language Action (VLA) models are designed to be generalist robot models that can be implemented across embodiments, tasks and environments. However, their ability to collaborate with other robots and humans remains underexplored. In this work, we propose a framework to automate the generation of multi-robot collaborative data for decentralized VLA training without requiring human teleoperation. Our framework first utilizes a decentralized collaborative planning approach to generate multi-robot collaborative robot plans in the form of an executable function. We then utilize the collaborative plan to produce collaborative demonstration data to finetune VLA models in a decentralized manner. We show that our approach produces demonstration data that when used to finetune VLA models on decentralized collaboration tasks matches the task performance of VLA models finetuned on human-teleoperated collaborative data.

Learning Robot Behavior Priors from Non-Robotic Synthetic Data

2026-10-22 cs.ROcs.LG 👤 Amanda Prorok (h=10)
Mateusz Odrowaz-Sypniewski, Michael Amir, Jasmine Bayrooti, Ajay Shankar, Amanda Prorok
Core Contributions
  • Tests whether useful robot priors can be learned with no robot data: three procedural pipelines modeling mathematical abstractions of control pretrain a Transformer on action prediction.
  • The model shows non-trivial zero-shot action prediction on unseen physical robot tasks via in-context learning, and control-specific priors beat generic causal priors.
  • A compute-matched model trained on real robot data wins only on datasets it saw, suggesting synthetic priors complement rather than replace embodied data.
Show abstract
Real robot data, rich across tasks and morphologies, is often treated as the bottleneck for training robot learning models, including embodied foundation models that aim for zero-shot physical transfer. In this paper, we investigate the degree to which such models can acquire necessary structure using data from systems without embodiment. To this end, we test if inexpensive, procedurally generated synthetic data can yield useful priors before a model observes any real-world behavior. We study this through robot action prediction, a natural pretraining objective and a direct test of whether structure learned from arbitrary synthetic tasks transfers to embodied settings. We introduce three data generation pipelines that model mathematical abstractions of robotic control, and use them as pretraining priors for a Transformer. We demonstrate that a model trained purely on this non-robotic data attains non-trivial zero-shot prediction performance on unseen physical robot tasks via an in-context learning mechanism, and that our specialized control priors notably improve upon generic causal priors. We also compare against a compute-matched baseline trained on real robot data, and find that it outperforms our model but only on the datasets it was trained on. These findings suggest that procedurally generated, mathematical abstractions can yield useful robotic priors, offering a potential complement to expensive embodied data collection.

In-Context Learning World Models for Robot Dual-Arm Manipulation of Soft Garment with Partial Observations

2026-10-22 cs.RO 👤 Darwin G. Caldwell (h=10)
Hengyi Sim, Quentin Rouxel, Dennis Hong, Darwin Caldwell, Fei Chen
Core Contributions
  • A transformer world model trained on large-scale simulated soft-object interactions performs generative full-state estimation and action-conditioned dynamics prediction from partial point-cloud histories.
  • Infers latent shape and material cues by in-context learning over the history, generalizing across embodiments, garment types and materials without weight updates, with sim-to-real mitigations.
  • Coupled with a goal-conditioned planner for bimanual garment folding, it improves reconstruction, prediction and task success in simulation and on a real robot.
Show abstract
Robust manipulation of soft objects, such as garments, is a critical skill for service and household robots to adopt, but remains challenging due to its high-dimensional state space, and material-dependent deformations. Humans address this challenge by using mental world models that "imagine" the shape and material properties from partial observations, which enables them to predict how actions affect future states. As such, embedding such world models in robots allows for better generalization in manipulating a wide variety of garments in the real world. However, learned world models typically require large amounts of real world data that is strenuous to collect, and a mechanism to infer latent properties that govern dynamics. We present a Soft-Object World Model that performs generative full-state estimation and action-conditioned dynamics prediction using a transformer backbone trained on large-scale simulated soft object interactions, with measures to mitigate the sim-to-real gap. By learning to infer latent shape and material cues through in-context learning over a variable-length partial point-cloud history, the model generalizes across robot embodiments, material parameters, garment types and shapes, and manipulation coordination modes without weight updates. The world model is then integrated with a Goal-Conditioned action planner for motion synthesis. We validate the approach in simulation and real world for bimanual garment folding, reporting improvements in reconstruction fidelity, prediction accuracy, and task success.

To Route or to Adapt? Multi-Expert Diffusion Planners in Unseen Regions

2026-10-22 cs.RO 👤 Ehsan Javanmardi (h=14)
Yun Li, Ehsan Javanmardi, Simon Thompson, Jiachen Gong, Qunli Zhang
Core Contributions
  • Asks whether a diffusion planner with per-domain expert adapters should route among experts or adapt in unseen regions; across three held-out maps none of eight routing schemes beats the static default on fleet-mean lateral offset.
  • Even an oracle expert selector improves mean offset by only 0.9 m, so the headroom in selection is small.
  • Reward-based fine-tuning on just 20 target-region scenes with joint replay gets 6.9 m versus 12.4 m for the static default, while replay limits forgetting.
  • Caveats worth noting: on nuPlan closed-loop performance worsens, and two test-time baselines achieve low offsets by emitting degenerate trajectories.
Show abstract
Diffusion trajectory planners with domain-specific experts face a practical question in unseen regions: can they rely on selecting among existing experts, or must the planner itself adapt? We study this question using a planner with a shared adapter and per-domain expert adapters. Across three real-world held-out maps, none of eight test-time routing schemes improves the fleet-mean lateral offset over the static default, which uses the same expert for every scene. Even an oracle that selects the best expert for each scene reduces the mean offset by only 0.9 m. In contrast, reward-based fine-tuning on just 20 target-region scenes yields substantially larger gains. On matched evaluation slices, joint replay achieves a fleet-mean offset of 6.9 m, compared with 12.4 m for the static default. Comparisons under the same target-region training budget show that target-region training drives the improvement, while replaying training scenes limits forgetting on the original ODDs. On nuPlan, fine-tuning across training cities also improves open-loop offsets, although closed-loop performance deteriorates. Further diagnostics reveal that two test-time adaptation baselines achieve low offsets by producing degenerate trajectories. These results show the limited headroom for selecting among existing experts and the greater open-loop gains available from adapting the planner.

Humanoid Mobility via Attention-based Contacts

2026-10-22 cs.RO 👤 V. Koltun (h=116)
Junzhe He, Kehan Wen, Chong Zhang, Clemens Schwarke, Zewei Zhang
Core Contributions
  • Treats motion capture data strictly as a training prior: a self-supervised attention-based planner predicts future body-environment contacts, and a contact-conditioned tracker turns those plans into whole-body motion, so the policy learns the structure of locomotion rather than copying kinematics.
  • Bridges the usual split between reward-only RL (generalizes but moves unnaturally) and reference tracking (natural but cannot leave its trajectories) by making contact the interface between perception and control.
  • A fine-tuning stage then explores terrain variations beyond the original motion data, and deployment needs only onboard observations with no reference input.
  • Notable for having the field's most-cited author on the list (h=116); a clean recipe for combining data priors with RL on hard terrain.
Show abstract
Humanoid mobility over complex terrain requires accurate foot placement and coordinated whole-body motion capable of robust adaptation. Reinforcement learning (RL) policies trained without motion references generalize well across terrains, yet eliciting effective whole-body coordination through reward design and exploration alone remains challenging. In contrast, reference-based methods yield natural motions but struggle to adapt beyond the predefined trajectories in their training data. We introduce a learning framework that bridges these two approaches by using motion data strictly as a training prior. An explicit, self-supervised, attention-based contact planner predicts future body-environment contacts, while a contact-conditioned tracker translates these plans into coordinated whole-body motion. Together, they establish contact as an interface between sensory observations and control actions. This abstraction allows the policy to internalize the contact-aware structure of locomotion rather than directly replicating kinematics. Consequently, a single policy can exploit data-driven motion priors for natural whole-body coordination while retaining the flexibility to discover environment-dependent behaviors through reinforcement learning. Building on the learned contact planner and tracker, a subsequent fine-tuning stage bootstraps their acquired capabilities to explore environment variations beyond those covered by the original motion data. Operating entirely from onboard observations without any reference inputs at deployment, this framework enables versatile humanoid mobility in challenging environments.

OpenTac: An Open Ecosystem for Customizable, Scalable, and Transferable 3D Tactile Sensing

2026-10-22 cs.ROeess.SPeess.SY 👤 Gregory Hager (h=80)
Ariel Slepyan, Geeta Chandra Raju Bethala, Jiafeng Gu, Anagha Rajendra Dangle, Yinzhe Zhou
Core Contributions
  • A resistive tactile skin with a structured deformable overlay converts tangential load into pressure, so differential taxel responses give local shear and the common-mode response gives normal force: 3D force from a cheap, scalable array.
  • Goes beyond a sensor with an ecosystem: a CAD tool that generates fabrication files from 2D outlines or 3D surfaces (even from text such as 'a size 9 shoe insole'), a simulator, cross-sensor transfer and adaptive acquisition.
  • Over 30 sensors were fabricated across robot embodiments, and adaptive readout reaches kilohertz rates, 19.2x faster than raster scanning a 1024-taxel skin at 16 sampled taxels per update.
  • Fully open hardware and software lowers the barrier to putting skin on arbitrary robot surfaces, which has been the main practical obstacle for tactile learning.
Show abstract
We present OpenTac, a novel sensor and a full-stack ecosystem for tactile skins that are highly customizable, scalable, and readily deployed in downstream robot control and learning applications. Our core contribution is twofold. First, we develop a scalable resistive tactile skin with a structured deformable overlay that converts tangential loading into spatially varying pressure. Differential responses across neighboring "taxels" support decoding of local shear vectors, and the common-mode response encodes normal force. Second, we provide a highly customizable sensor design tool that is able to automatically generate fabrication files for these tactile skins from 2D outlines and developable 3D surfaces, including from abstract descriptions such as text (e.g., "design a sensor for a size 9 shoe insole"). We develop an ecosystem around the OpenTac sensor, including the CAD design tool, a sensor simulator, cross-sensor transfer, and fast/adaptive data acquisition. We demonstrate the broad applicability of the OpenTac-CAD sensor design tool by designing and fabricating more than 30 sensors across a variety of robot embodiments. We characterize the three-dimensional tactile fields produced by OpenTac with a commercial, high-precision force/torque sensor. We demonstrate the broad applicability of 3D tactile sensing across contact-rich feedback control and manipulation policy learning on four real-world robot embodiments. Additionally, adaptive acquisition enables kilohertz-rate partial readouts, exceeding full-array raster scanning on the same sensor by 19.2X at 16 sampled taxels per update for a 1024-taxel skin. We provide all hardware, software, and result videos are at opentac.github.io

GelSlim 5.0: Unifying Vision and Touch with a Single Fingertip Camera

2026-10-22 cs.RO 👤 Nima Fazeli (h=21)
Xiangdong Wang, I-Hsuan Huang, Xiaohao Xu, Xili Yi, Nima Fazeli
Core Contributions
  • One fixed camera sees both through the membrane (vision) and the contact-sensitive UV layer (touch) by switching illumination, which removes inter-camera extrinsic calibration.
  • VisTacFusion uses asymmetric bottleneck fusion so visual context aids pose while tactile spatial features are preserved for depth and surface normals; modality dropout allows either input alone.
  • On 12 known objects, paired inputs cut depth MSE, angular error and translation L1 by 61.2%, 72.3% and 61.3% versus tactile-only.
  • Relative to vision-only, the large gain is in depth (93.7%) while angle and translation improve modestly (6.1%, 8.6%), showing touch's complementary role.
Show abstract
Vision and touch provide complementary cues for object pose and local contact geometry, but external cameras are often occluded at the contact site. We present GelSlim 5.0, a fingertip sensor that acquires visual and tactile observations with one fixed camera and a continuous UV-responsive contact layer. By switching illumination, the camera sequentially acquires through-membrane visual images and contact-sensitive tactile images from a shared viewpoint, avoiding inter-camera extrinsic calibration. To combine these observations, we introduce VisTacFusion, a multimodal transformer with separate prediction paths for planar object pose and contact geometry. Asymmetric bottleneck fusion incorporates visual context while preserving tactile spatial features for depth and surface-normal estimation. Modality dropout enables inference with paired inputs or either modality alone. On real validation data from 12 known objects, paired inputs reduce depth MSE, angular error, and translation $L_1$ by 61.2%, 72.3%, and 61.3%, respectively, relative to tactile-only inference with the same model. Relative to visual-only inference, the corresponding reductions are 93.7%, 6.1%, and 8.6%.

Extending Harmonic Function Methods via Root Locus for Coordinated Path Generation

2026-10-22 cs.ROeess.SY 👤 L. Pallottino (h=26)
Matteo Frascarelli, Lucia Pallottino, Alberto Landi
Core Contributions
  • Maps N starts and N goals to the poles and zeros of a rational function after a conformal map, so paths are streamlines of the logarithmic potential, which gives disjoint paths and a goal assignment as a byproduct instead of a separate matching step.
  • Uses the gain as a clock so all robots arrive together at a prescribed time, and handles polygonal obstacles through Milne-Thomson reflections with a finite safety test.
  • Compared on 100 random instances against independent harmonic planning and artificial potentials, plus a ten-robot, ten-obstacle scene: a rare analytic alternative to sampling-based multi-robot planners.
Show abstract
We consider the offline generation of coordinated paths for N interchangeable robots and N goals in planar environments with polygonal obstacles. After a conformal map to a circular domain, starts and goals become the poles and zeros of a rational function R, and the paths are the branches of its Generalized Root Locus (GRL), i.e. the streamlines of the logarithmic potential -log R. We show that, for every phase outside a finite set of critical phases and a set of boundary phases, the induced gradient motion drives each robot to a distinct goal along pairwise disjoint paths that never reach the obstacles; the robot-goal assignment is produced by the construction, and with the gain used as a clock all robots move as the closed-loop roots of the GRL and arrive together at a prescribed time. Obstacles become streamlines through successive Milne-Thomson reflections, and for a finite number of reflections a finite test certifies safety for the potential that is actually computed. Reciprocal root-locus planning is recovered as a special case. The method is illustrated on two polygonal workspaces, 100 random instances compared with independent harmonic planning and artificial potentials, and a scene with ten robots and ten obstacles.

Continuous-Time Motion Planning and Steering with Global Convergence

2026-10-22 cs.RO 👤 Sima Didari (h=11)
Mohsen Amiri, Sima Didari, Davoud Ataee Tarzanagh, Hankyu Moon, Yeongdae Kwon
Core Contributions
  • Poses planning and steering of learned proposals as one continuous-time program with constraints enforced at every instant, not only at sampled times.
  • GlobMP runs gradient projection over piecewise-polynomial curves, where each constraint becomes a polynomial that must stay nonnegative per piece, alternating cost reduction with feasibility restoration.
  • Under mild assumptions, cost approaches the global optimum as pieces increase while constraints hold to tolerance, a guarantee the authors say no earlier algorithm gives with smoothness cost.
  • Demonstrated with up to 200 robots, Robotarium, maze navigation and steering tactile-reactive policies on a bimanual humanoid.
Show abstract
Motion planning is central to robotics, and it is increasingly used to correct the motions proposed by learned planners and policies. In both uses, constraints must hold at every instant of the motion, not only at sampled times. To our knowledge, no algorithm provably converges to a globally optimal motion once a smoothness cost is combined with such constraints. We cast planning and steering as a single continuous-time program: robots avoid one another and static or moving obstacles, and a learned proposal is steered by keeping the motion close to it. The program is elementary to state, yet nonconvex. We propose GlobMP (Global Motion Planning), a gradient projection method over piecewise-polynomial curves, on which each constraint is one polynomial per piece that must stay nonnegative over the whole motion; it alternates cost reduction with feasibility restoration. Under mild assumptions, we show that, from a fine grid start, its cost approaches the global optimum as the number of polynomial pieces grows, with every constraint met to any chosen tolerance, or exactly on stable instances. We demonstrate its superior performance over strong baselines in real and synthetic experiments, including teams of up to $200$ robots, multi-robot planning on the Robotarium testbed and an extended benchmark, maze navigation, and steering tactile-reactive policies on a bimanual humanoid.

Consistent Distributed Invariant EKF SLAM with Point and Object Features

2026-10-22 cs.RO 👤 Shou-Dong Huang (h=43)
Mengya Xu, Xianjia Yu, Ha Sier, Shoudong Huang, Tomi Westerlund
Core Contributions
  • Identifies why covariance intersection breaks in right-invariant EKF SLAM: point features' invariant errors depend on robot rotation, so a neighbor's map is in its own error coordinates and correlated with its pose.
  • Joint-CI builds a joint estimate of the receiver's pose and common features in the receiver's error coordinates, then applies partial-state CI, keeping robot-feature and feature-feature correlations.
  • Proves that fusion preserves the six unobservable directions (global rotation and translation) and first-order covariance consistency, which is what prevents overconfident distributed maps.
  • Works for point, object and mixed maps, with simulation and real-dataset experiments showing better accuracy and mildly conservative uncertainty.
Show abstract
Multi-robot SLAM allows robots to improve their estimates by exchanging map information, but repeated exchanges create unknown correlations between local estimators. Covariance intersection (CI) can fuse estimates without tracking these correlations. In right-invariant extended Kalman filter (RI-EKF) SLAM, however, point features obtain their invariant errors through the robot rotation, so a neighbor's map estimate is expressed in its own error coordinates and is correlated with its pose, and cannot be fused by CI directly. This paper presents Joint-CI, a unified fusion method for distributed RI-EKF SLAM with point, object, and mixed point--object maps. Using a neighboring robot's local posterior and an inter-robot observation, a joint estimate of the receiving robot's pose and the common features is constructed in the receiving robot's error coordinates, retaining the robot--feature and feature--feature correlations. Partial-state CI then fuses this estimate with the receiving robot's local posterior. We show that the unified formulation and the fusion preserve the six unobservable directions associated with global rotation and translation, and that communication and fusion preserve first-order covariance consistency given consistent local posteriors. Monte Carlo simulations and a real-world dataset-derived experiment show that Joint-CI improves estimation accuracy while maintaining consistent, mildly conservative uncertainty.

TRACE: Traversability-aware Reactive Navigation via VLM-driven Adaptive Constraints Estimation

2026-10-22 cs.RO 👤 G. Nikolakopoulos (h=44)
Mario A. V. Saucedo, Midhun Edatharam Kunnath, Elias Small, Akash Patel, Shruti Kotpalliwar
Core Contributions

  • Extracts left and right path boundaries by percentile binning and spline or regularized polynomial fitting, and feeds them to an NMPC as soft barrier constraints: language-grounded perception with a classical safety-aware controller.

Vehicle-to-everything (V2X) communication enables connected vehicles to make safety-critical decisions, but delayed, missing, or incorrect information can compromise driving safety. Systematically testing such failures is challenging because traffic interactions, communication conditions, and application responses must be jointly configured to produce meaningful scenarios. This paper presents LAST-V2X, a language-driven, adaptive framework for automated scenario synthesis and safety testing of V2X applications. LAST-V2X leverages large language models (LLMs) to translate natural-language test descriptions into executable scenarios grounded in roadway geometry, V2X communication semantics, and vehicle behavior. The framework integrates physical traffic simulation with spatially aware communication modeling and message-level fault injection to evaluate how information degradation affects vehicle decisions. To establish whether an observed safety failure is caused by communication rather than the underlying traffic conditions, LAST-V2X employs matched executions under degraded and ground-truth information. Furthermore, an execution-guided refinement mechanism combines parameter search and LLM self-reflection to identify ineffective test configurations, improve scenario effectiveness, and produce minimal reproducible failure cases. Experiments across diverse CARLA environments and V2X safety applications demonstrate the framework's ability to generate reliable scenarios, distinguish communication-induced failures from unrelated safety violations, and substantially improve failure discovery through adaptive refinement. These results establish LAST-V2X as a systematic approach to language-driven, causally verified, and adaptive safety testing for connected vehicle systems.
