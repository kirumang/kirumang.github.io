🔭 Research Landscape

The dominant theme today is data without real robots. Zero-HLM (#1) turns smartphone captures into 4,800 validated simulated demonstrations and reaches 66.7% mean success on three Unitree G1 tasks with GR00T N1.7, against 46.7% when the same model trains on real data. ZAP (#18) pushes further by fine-tuning a 27B VLM with SFT and GRPO on simple simulated tasks only, hitting 96.7% on real tasks and matching a much larger frontier model at 3x the speed and 17x lower cost. The Gaussian-splat pipeline (#28) is the same bet at smaller scale: 1,500 rendered reaching trajectories beat 150 teleoperated demonstrations (80.0% vs 58.7%). Three different routes, all concluding that the bottleneck has moved from collecting demonstrations to making simulated or reconstructed ones physically and visually faithful.

A second thread is the agent harness as the unit of study. EBench's profiling of a frontier MLLM (#10) finds direct control strong on short horizons and generalization but weak on long-horizon and contact-rich work, with a harness that can invoke a VLA or WAM recovering the best of both. RoboDoctor (#13) and VISTA (#7) then ask how such harnesses improve without weight updates, one through persistent diagnosis records and the other through active exploration instead of passive reflection, while EmbodiedLab (#5) and RobotAgentBench (#6) show how far agents still are: even the strongest agent solves under half of act-to-know experiments. Meanwhile VLA-HWBench (#12) reminds everyone that deployment cost is dominated by action-generation strategy (up to 157x latency spread), which OSWAM (#17) attacks directly with one-step action generation at 40 ms.

Underneath both, the classical-control papers keep supplying guarantees that learned stacks lack. FLORE (#14) shows that force-aware policies reach 80% success where position-only policies reach 26.7%, the differentiable contact paper (#15) trains a deployable trotting policy in under two minutes, and the quadruped planner (#4), uncertainty-aware swarm CBFs (#8) and bristlebot flocking (#30) all certify safety through barrier functions or Bernstein convexity. Read together, the field is converging on learned high-level modules wrapped in verifiable low-level structure, and on benchmarks (#20, #24) that finally score how a run succeeded rather than only whether it did.

Zero-HLM: Generalizable Humanoid Loco-Manipulation without Real Robot Data

2026-10-26 cs.RO 👤 Si-Yuan Huang (h=16)
Dianyi Yang, Mengya Liu, Zhumei Wang, Yu Liu, Xiaochen Zhang
Core Contributions
  • Closes the real-to-sim-to-real loop from nothing more than smartphone captures: Gaussian-guided agentic reconstruction aligns visual and physical scenes, so scenes are both photorealistic and simulation-ready.
  • Motion-environment co-adaptation uses rollout feedback to repair whole-body references and interaction geometry, rather than replaying source demonstrations that no longer fit the new scene.
  • Sim-only training of GR00T N1.7 on 4,800 validated demonstrations beats real-data training (66.7% vs 46.7%), and on generalized configurations lifts pooled success from 23% to 69% with no extra real demos.
Show abstract
Learning generalizable humanoid loco-manipulation remains bottlenecked by costly real-world demonstrations. Simulation provides a scalable alternative, but requires visually faithful, physics-ready scenes and executable whole-body motions. We present Zero-HLM, a real-to-sim-to-real framework that transforms smartphone captures and simulated source demonstrations into scalable training data for humanoid loco-manipulation. Gaussian-guided agentic real-to-sim reconstruction aligns visual and physical scenes; motion-environment co-adaptation uses rollout feedback to correct whole-body references and interaction geometry. We generate 4,800 physically validated demonstrations for zero-shot transfer across three Unitree G1 tasks. Simulation-trained GR00T N1.7 achieves 66.7% mean full-task success, versus 46.7% with real-data training. On generalized configurations, expanded simulation training raises pooled success from 23% for base-trained real-data policies to 69%, without additional real demonstrations. Project page: https://young-bit.github.io/zero-hlm/

Pretraining Knows What, Simulation Teaches How: Compositional Generalization in a Sim-Trained Robot Agent

2026-10-26 cs.ROcs.AIcs.CVcs.LG 👤 Benjamin Pikus (h=0)
Benjamin Pikus, Daniel Saunders, Mircea Mironenco, Salman Mohammadi, Mathew Mathew
Core Contributions
  • Splits the problem into 'what' and 'how': a pretrained 27B VLM supplies world understanding and reasoning, and SFT plus GRPO on simple simulated tasks teaches movement through a tool harness.
  • With no real-world robot training data, it reaches 96.7% on real tasks, matching GPT-6 Astra in the same harness and beating the MolmoAct2 VLA (33.3%), at 3x the speed and 17x lower cost.
  • Generalizes to new objects, goals and language (test tube seating, mid-air handoff, tic-tac-toe, chess) and to an unseen Franka embodiment, reaching 55.9% on RoboLab-120, 4.9 points above the best VLA/WAM baseline.
Show abstract
A robot policy needs to understand what a task is asking for in a scene, and how to move the robot to accomplish that task. To achieve these two skills, we introduce Zero-real Agent Policy (ZAP), a recipe that teaches a small off-the-shelf vision-language model to control a robot via a simple tool harness. We use a pretrained 27B parameter model to bootstrap world understanding and reasoning, followed by supervised fine-tuning and GRPO on simple simulated tasks to teach it how to move the robot. Without any real-world robot data during training, ZAP reaches 96.7% success on real-world tasks, matching GPT-6 Astra in our harness and outperforming the MolmoAct2 VLA (33.3%), while running 3x faster and 17x cheaper than GPT-6 Astra. We demonstrate that ZAP also generalizes to completely new objects, goals, strategies, and language by seating a test tube in a rack (and choosing a different hole in the rack when prompted), handing a test tube between arms mid-air, drawing an X on a tic-tac-toe board, and playing chess. These goals and types of manipulation don't appear in our training data. Furthermore, ZAP also transfers to an unseen embodiment. On a single-arm Franka, ZAP reaches 55.9% on RoboLab-120, 4.9 percentage points ahead of the leading VLA/WAM baseline.

Robot-Aligned 3D Gaussian Splatting for Precise Reaching with Wrist-Camera VLA Policies

2026-10-26 cs.RO 👤 Zuo-Xu Wang (h=0)
Zuoxu Wang, Xiao Liang
Core Contributions
  • Targets pre-contact reaching in e-waste disassembly, where thin, reflective, densely packed parts make full digital twins impractical.
  • Reconstructs the workspace with 3D Gaussian Splatting and renders wrist-camera views from robot-aligned poses paired with smooth structured trajectories, plus an instruction-conditioned reach verifier for reliable termination.
  • With pi0.5, 1,500 generated trajectories reach 80.0% precise-reaching success versus 58.7% for 150 teleoperated demos, with under 10 minutes of human capture and no external camera.
Show abstract
Efficient robotic data collection remains a major bottleneck for vision-language-action (VLA) policy learning, especially for tasks requiring accurate end-effector positioning and camera-action alignment. This challenge is particularly severe in electronic-waste disassembly, where robots operate in cluttered assemblies of densely packed, visually similar components. Full digital-twin simulation is difficult because many parts are thin, reflective, or mechanically complex, making accurate object-level meshes and contact models difficult to obtain. In this work, we focus on pre-contact reaching and propose a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)-based data generation pipeline for wrist-camera VLA adaptation. Our method reconstructs the real workspace with 3DGS and renders diverse wrist-camera observations from robot-aligned poses. These observations are paired with smooth, structured reaching trajectories to provide robot-aligned visual-action supervision for VLA fine-tuning. We further augment the VLA policy with an instruction-conditioned reach verifier that estimates target proximity and provides reliable termination before physical interaction. Experiments in motherboard disassembly demonstrate precise VLA-based reaching using wrist-camera observations alone, without an external side-view camera. With $π$0.5, 1,500 generated trajectories achieve 80.0\% precise-reaching success across three targets, compared with 58.7\% using 150 teleoperated demonstrations, while requiring less than 10 minutes of active human capture. Project website: https://001-wang.github.io/PrecisionReach-GS/

Video World Models Need Predictable Latents

2026-10-26 cs.LGcs.AIcs.CVcs.RO 👤 Sherry Yang (h=7)
Mattia Scardecchia, Sherry Yang
Core Contributions
  • Argues that the autoencoder latent used by video world models is optimized for reconstruction, not for being easy to predict, which is why long rollouts and out-of-distribution inputs degrade.
  • Adds temporal-predictability objectives to autoencoder training, paired with a past-conditioned decoder, so one latent serves both pixel-accurate prediction and simple dynamics.
  • Reports up to 5x cheaper inference, over 7x faster training and 15-24% lower pixel and perceptual error out of distribution and over long horizons, with closer agreement to real-world policy evaluation.
Show abstract
Video world models trained on large-scale robot data promise a safe and scalable approach for learning and evaluating policies that act in the physical world. However, they still struggle with long-horizon rollouts and out-of-distribution generalization, and they remain computationally demanding at inference time. Typically, video world models make predictions in the latent space of a reconstruction-trained autoencoder, which however is not directly optimized for dynamics modeling. On the other hand, world models designed for latent planning usually optimize latents to be predictable, but they forgo accurate prediction of observations in pixel space. In this paper, we show that it is possible to achieve the best of both worlds: a latent geometry optimized to make future prediction as simple as possible without sacrificing accurate prediction of future observations. More precisely, we find that temporal objectives for representation learning, paired with a past-conditioned decoder architecture, lead to consistent improvements in generative video world models at the same training budget. The gains include up to 5x cheaper inference, over 7x faster training, and 15-24% lower pixel and perceptual errors out of distribution and over long horizons. Our world models also agree more closely with real-world policy evaluation when using a vision-language model to judge task success from their pixel-space predictions. Overall, our study indicates that temporal predictability is an important design criterion for the latent space of video world models, and provides concrete guidance on how to incorporate it into the training of autoencoders.

OSWAM: An Effective World Action Model with One-Step Action Generation

2026-10-26 cs.CVcs.RO 👤 Guo-Wen Zhang (h=0)
Guowen Zhang, Liyi Chen, Xu Hu, Pengfei Wang, Jingfan Chen
Core Contributions
  • Identifies why direct-policy world action models need many denoising steps: random noise has no structured action prior, so coherent sequences must be recovered iteratively.
  • Imagination tokens initialize the diffusion process with physical priors and action-conditioned representations, and privileged-information distillation with future conditioning adds future awareness.
  • Matches or beats multi-step direct-policy and joint WAMs in driving and embodied control with about 3x speedup, and runs compiled inference at 40 ms on an RTX 5090.
Show abstract
Direct-policy World Action Models (WAM) have demonstrated the benefits of decoupling action generation from explicit future visual prediction for efficient inference in autonomous driving and embodied control. However, they still require tens of denoising steps to achieve competitive action quality, incurring substantial latency and hindering real-time deployment. Simply reducing the number of denoising steps will collapse the nonlinear multi-step denoising trajectory into coarse updates, substantially degrading action quality. To reduce the denoising steps while enabling real-time action generation, we present an effective world action model with one-step action generation, namely OSWAM, for autonomous driving and embodied control. We argue that it is the absence of structured action priors in random noise initialization that forces direct-policy WAMs to recover coherent action sequences through iterative denoising. Hence, we construct imagination tokens to initialize the diffusion process, providing physical priors and action-conditioned representations. We further introduce privileged information distillation with future conditioning to enhance future-aware action generation. As a result, OSWAM can efficiently and effectively generate high quality actions in just one diffusion step. Our experiments demonstrate that OSWAM achieves comparable or even better results in both autonomous driving and embodied control than direct-policy WAM and Joint-WAM that require tens of steps. It obtains approximately $3\times$ speedup over direct-policy WAM baselines and supports real-time compiled inference with latency as low as 40ms on an NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU. The project page is available at https://gwenzhang.github.io/OSWAM/

Adapt for Planning, Not just Prediction: Optimistic Test-Time World Model Adaptation

2026-10-26 cs.LGcs.AIcs.RO 👤 Akshay Mete (h=0)
Akshay Mete, Dileep Kalathil, P. R. Kumar
Core Contributions
  • Observes that test-time adaptation of a world model for planning is closed-loop, so prediction-only objectives fit only the model's own chosen actions and can stay wrong about actions that would reach the goal.
  • Borrowing reward-biased maximum likelihood from adaptive control, OpTTA adds a planner goal-reaching cost on candidate rollouts to the prediction loss, biasing toward optimistic models without extra interaction.
  • Across visual and environment shifts and three JEPA world models, it raises planning success 13.5 points over a frozen model versus 6.9 for the prediction-only AdaJEPA when adapting the predictor.
Show abstract
Latent world models are typically pre-trained offline and kept frozen at test time, so planning with them can degrade under distribution shift. Test-time adaptation (TTA) can mitigate this degradation by updating the model during deployment. Unlike pre-training on fixed data, TTA for planning is closed-loop: the planner uses model rollouts to select actions, and the resulting transitions drive the next update. This raises the question: which objective should guide TTA for planning? Under a prediction-only objective, the model is fit only to the outcomes of its own actions, so it can remain wrong about the actions that would reach the goal, and low prediction error need not translate into better planning. Inspired by reward-biased maximum likelihood from adaptive control, we introduce Optimistic Test-Time Adaptation (OpTTA). Its objective has two terms, the prediction loss on observed transitions and the planner's goal-reaching cost on its candidate rollouts, both minimized over the model parameters. The planning term biases adaptation toward models that are optimistic about reaching the goal, without any additional environment interaction. Across visual and environment shifts on manipulation, reaching and navigation tasks, OpTTA raises final planning success over the frozen model by 13.5 percentage points on average, compared with 6.9 for the prediction-only objective (AdaJEPA), when only the predictor is adapted. When the encoder is adapted as well, the gains are 13.5 and 7.5 points. These experiments span three JEPA world models with different encoders and training objectives and, for temporal straightening, both CEM and gradient-based planners. Our results suggest that test-time adaptation of world models for planning benefits from an optimistic, task-aware term in addition to the prediction loss.

Profiling a Frontier Agent with EBench: From Direct Control to Harness with Policy

2026-10-26 cs.ROcs.AI 👤 Jian-Ming Ma (h=3)
Yifei Yao, Yude Zou, Guanbo Jiao, Xing Gao, Ning Gao
Core Contributions
  • Profiles a frontier multimodal model as a robot controller under the same observation protocol as post-trained policies, separating direct waypoint control from a harness that can call a VLA or WAM.
  • Direct control leads on short-horizon tasks and varies least across generalization dimensions, but trails on long-horizon, tabletop and high-precision contact-rich tasks.
  • Letting the agent invoke a post-trained policy matches the strongest compared policies at much lower token cost, though poor invocation timing, fixed instructions and grasp disruption on handback keep it below per-task upper bounds.
Show abstract
Frontier multimodal large language models (MLLMs) can control robots without robot-specific training, but how their capabilities compare and interact with post-trained vision-language-action (VLA) and world action model (WAM) policies across manipulation demands remains to be thoroughly evaluated. In this report, we evaluate GPT-6-Astra on EBench as the core of an agent that controls a robot through an agent harness under the same visual and proprioceptive observation protocol as post-trained policies. We study two agent configurations: direct control, in which the agent generates waypoint commands directly, and harness with policy, in which it can also invoke a post-trained VLA or WAM policy for action execution. Under direct control, the agent achieves competitive overall performance relative to post-trained policies. It leads on short-horizon tasks and exhibits the least performance variation across generalization dimensions, whereas it underperforms post-trained policies on long-horizon tasks, tabletop manipulation, and high-precision contact-rich tasks. In the harness with policy, invoking a post-trained VLA or WAM combines the complementary strengths of both components, raising overall task success to the level of the strongest compared post-trained policies while substantially reducing token cost. However, performance remains below the per-task upper bound of the standalone components due to suboptimal policy invocation, fixed-instruction execution without subgoals, and grasp disruption when the agent resumes control. Overall, our study characterizes the capability boundary of a frontier MLLM agent and suggests directions for improving future agent harness design. Project page: https://internrobotics.github.io/EBench-Astra-Tech-Report/

RoboDoctor: Training-Free Recursive Self-Improvement for Robot Policies

2026-10-26 cs.ROcs.CV 👤 Ji-Xin Yan (h=1)
Heyang Yu, Haomin Li, Sihang Li, Jixin Yan, Shiyun Peng
Core Contributions
  • Treats execution time as where adaptation happens: weights stay fixed while feedback from physical interaction revises how the policy and an agent are combined.
  • Linked experience records keep the reason for a decision, the method tried, its outcome and conditions for reuse, so lessons transfer across episodes instead of being forgotten.
  • On 54 RoboDojo-Sim entries it reaches a 57.10 five-dimension mean score with 49.44% native success, though the authors note controlled trials are needed to isolate the cross-episode experience effect.
Show abstract
Generalist robot policies and language-model agents provide complementary capabilities, but completing a task also requires adapting their use to the consequences of physical interaction. We study execution as a locus of adaptation: feedback guides the current action and supplies evidence for revising subsequent decisions under fixed model weights. We introduce RoboDoctor, a training-free framework that connects diagnosis, grounded action, and persistent experience across three revision targets. The harness allocates control between policy and agent; intervention determines when to assist while preserving useful policy behavior; and operation refines physical methods through observed effects. Linked experience records retain the reason for a decision, the attempted method, its outcome, and conditions for reuse. On a curated archive of 54 RoboDojo-Sim task entries, RoboDoctor achieves a five-dimension mean score of 57.10, with a native success rate of 49.44%. Recorded interactions illustrate strategy changes, selective correction, and grasp revision. These findings characterize execution-time adaptation in the evaluated system; controlled trials remain necessary to isolate the contribution of cross-episode experience.

VISTA: Active Exploration for Self-Improvement in Embodied Agents

2026-10-26 cs.RO 👤 Si-Min Huang (h=4)
Zhang Zhang, Shengjun Zhang, Simin Huang, Min Chen, Chong Xia
Core Contributions
  • Replaces post-hoc reflection on static rollout logs with a meta-agent that actively generates new rollouts to probe failures and recovery behavior before committing a harness change.
  • A harness graph records the lineage of proposed harnesses so the meta-agent can reuse effective mechanisms and avoid re-exploring failed directions.
  • Improves success on LIBERO-Pro, RoboCasa and RoboTwin with fewer simulator rollouts than static reflection, making self-improvement cheaper as well as better.
Show abstract
Agentic embodied intelligence plays a foundational role in open-ended, dynamic physical environments. Rather than treating policy learning as an end-to-end mapping from instructions to actions, recent studies reveal that embodied systems depend not only on pre-trained foundation models, but also on harness. Prior approaches largely treat rollouts as static logs for post-hoc reflection, producing harness updates without autonomously testing and refining them through subsequent embodied interaction. To address this challenge, we propose VISTA, a meta-agent framework that transforms post-hoc rollout reflection into active self-exploration for embodied harness improvement. Specifically, VISTA maintains a harness graph that records the lineage of proposed harnesses, which enables the meta-agent to retrieve prior hypotheses, build on effective mechanisms, and avoid repeatedly exploring failed directions. Each iteration begins from a retained harness version and its execution evidence. Rather than directly committing an update from these historical trajectories, the meta-agent actively explores in the environment. It autonomously generates new rollouts, probes execution failures and recovery behaviors, and iteratively modifies the harness based on the resulting evidence. The refined harness is then evaluated afresh and added to the harness graph. We evaluate VISTA on the LIBERO-Pro, RoboCasa, and RoboTwin benchmarks. Experimental results demonstrate that VISTA progressively improves task success and is more rollout-efficient than static reflection, achieving higher success rates with fewer simulator rollouts.

FISRF: Flexible Instructional Scaffolding Reflection Framework for Robotics Task Planning with Large Language Model

2026-10-26 cs.RO 👤 Qing-Miao Liang (h=0)
Qingmiao Liang, Jiatao Zhang, Runxin Zou, Yufan Song, Wei Song
Core Contributions
  • Argues that unguided LLM self-reflection accumulates erroneous experience early in a new environment, and replaces it with an agent-mentor design inspired by the Zone of Proximal Development.
  • Estimates what the robot can currently do alone and adaptively picks reflection strategy and mentor guidance, so scaffolding fades as competence grows.
  • In ALFWorld and on real robots it cuts error-correction rounds by 37% and redundant reasoning by 25.1% while raising task success by 8%.
Show abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) are widely adopted for reflective error correction in task planning; however, existing approaches primarily rely on autonomous exploration, rendering them prone to accumulating erroneous experiences during early interactions with new environments. To address this limitation, we propose the Flexible Instructional Scaffolding Reflection Framework (FISRF), an agent--mentor architecture that estimates the robot's Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD) in task planning and adaptively selects reflection strategies and mentor guidance, thereby facilitating the accumulation of effective error-correction experience. Experiments in ALFWorld and on real-world robotic platforms demonstrate that FISRF reduces the average number of error-correction rounds by 37% and redundant reasoning by 25.1%, while improving the overall task success rate by 8%. TrySee: Modular Perception-Enhanced VLM Grounding for Closed-Loop Robot Task Planning in Specialized Domains

2026-10-26 cs.ROcs.AI 👤 Elias Hofele (h=0)
Elias Hofele, Thomas Trapp, David Timmermann, Tristan Schnell, Georg Heppner
Core Contributions
  • Pairs a general VLM planner with replaceable domain-specific perception modules, so specialized imagery is preprocessed before the VLM must interpret it.
  • A cyclic closed-loop pipeline with dynamic preprocessing selection keeps the system reactive and scalable without VLM fine-tuning or end-to-end demonstrations.
  • Evaluated across five very different domains, including a real robot disassembly task in a brownfield environment with little domain-specific training data.
Show abstract
Vision Language Models (VLMs) have shown impressive capabilities in high-level reasoning and task planning for robots. However, specialized domains remain a challenge for VLMs' image interpretation and grounding abilities. We introduce TrySee, a modular closed-loop robot task planning pipeline integrating general-purpose VLM reasoning with domain-specific perception and established robotics components. The system consists of an inherently reactive cyclic planning pipeline, augmented with domain-specific modular vision preprocessing, to provide online grounding. Finally, a dynamic preprocessing selection mechanism contributes to the scalability of the pipeline. The system requires neither VLM fine-tuning nor end-to-end demonstrations and adapts through replaceable perception modules and motion planning. The approach is evaluated on planning tasks across 5 widely different domains, including a real-world robot disassembly task. The field tests in a brownfield environment demonstrate the approach's practicality in integrating VLM planning into specialized robotic systems with limited domain-specific training data.

EmbodiedLab: Benchmarking Embodied Agents That Act to Know

2026-10-26 cs.RO 👤 Yin-Peng Dong (h=6)
Hongcheng Gao, Zelin Zheng, Alex Zhou, Jay Zhu, Shijia Ge
Core Contributions
  • Defines an 'act to know' setting: the robot must run physical experiments to discover quantities, which manipulation benchmarks (target given) and discovery benchmarks (symbolic queries) both skip.
  • Resampling system parameters for every instance means the answer cannot be memorized, yet it can be scored exactly; 30 experiments over five disciplines and 14 apparatus platforms, all verified solvable.
  • Even the strongest of six frontier agents succeeds under half the time with native control; most failures come from interacting with the apparatus, and distilled manipulation skills help weak agents more than better perception or planning.
Show abstract
Embodied agents are expected to accomplish real-world goals by deciding for themselves how and when to interact with their surroundings. For many such goals, changing the physical state of the world is the means rather than the end: the agent changes the state of a system through interaction, observes the feedback, and explores until it either achieves the goal itself (e.g., discovering physical laws) or learns what the goal requires; it acts to know. Manipulation benchmarks stop at acting to change, with the target state given; scientific discovery benchmarks evaluate knowing but replace physical experimentation with symbolic actions or numerical queries. We introduce EmbodiedLab, a benchmark in which a robot must act to know. Quantitative science experiments make this setting controllable: the parameters of the system are resampled for every instance, so the result cannot be known in advance, yet it can be scored exactly. Across 30 experiments in five disciplines on 14 apparatus platforms, the agent receives an objective and the apparatus, chooses its own procedure, and operates a physics-based simulated laboratory, reading the instruments through its cameras. Evaluation checks what the agent came to know, grounded in readings recorded during the episode, as well as the physical state the task requires; every experiment is verified to be solvable. We evaluate six frontier models in their developers' coding agents with native observation and control, with added perception and motion planning, and with manipulation skills distilled from the strongest agents. With native control, even the strongest agent succeeds less than half the time; for every agent, most failures arise in interacting with the apparatus, and manipulation skills help weaker agents more than perception and planning. The benchmark and its evaluation code are available at https://github.com/HexaFuture/EmbodiedLab.

RobotAgentBench: Towards Open-world Robot Autonomy

2026-10-26 cs.ROcs.AI 👤 Wen-Yi Wu (h=6)
Lingjun Mao, Jinglin Cao, Lukun He, Wenpeng Xu, Yuchen Yan
Core Contributions
  • Evaluates whole robot agents rather than isolated skills, spanning task discovery, context reasoning, manipulation, failure recovery and robustness across 50 household layouts and 90 atomic, 18 short-horizon and 8 long-horizon tasks.
  • Builds layouts through an agent-assisted, human-in-the-loop real-to-sim pipeline, so interactive objects and physically grounded tasks come with less manual authoring.
  • Finds a large gap between manipulation skill and autonomy, with agentic architectures and recursive self-improvement giving clear advantages over standalone VLA/WAM models.
Show abstract
Recent advances in vision-language-action (VLA) and world-action models (WAMs) have significantly improved robotic manipulation, yet deploying robots in the open world demands a higher level of autonomy beyond executing predefined tasks. Robot agents offer a promising paradigm for this transition, integrating task discovery, contextual reasoning, physical execution, and adaptive recovery into a closed interaction loop. However, existing benchmarks largely evaluate isolated capabilities, failing to capture the complexity of open-world robot autonomy. Multi-objective reinforcement learning is a fundamental problem for modern AI, with applications spanning post-training, reasoning models, and robotic control. A central challenge is to learn policies that balance competing objectives, such as helpfulness versus honesty in safety and alignment, or speed versus energy in robotics. The optimal policy can vary sharply with objective-weighting combinations, and the extent of this variation depends crucially on the degree of conflict among objectives, making per-preference policy learning sample-inefficient. In this paper, we introduce objective-conflict coefficients and derive near-optimal regret bounds for online settings using this measure, thereby quantifying the impact of misalignment on learning efficiency. Then, we design routed ensemble policy learning, an algorithm that maintains a small set of basis policies and dynamically routes trajectories to the policy with the highest potential reward. Finally, we consider trajectory selection as another approach to mitigate objective conflict, and design a kernel surrogate trajectory attribution algorithm that estimates how feedback signals from a set of trajectories influence policy learning on other trajectories. The kernel can capture nonlinear interactions such as synergy and antagonism between trajectories. We evaluate our approach across diverse environments and benchmarks: routed ensembles improve average performance by 4.3% over baselines for online environments from Meta-World and MO-Gymnasium, and kernel surrogate attribution improves correlation with leave-one-out analysis over existing baselines by 50%. When applied to trajectory selection, kernel-based attribution yields a 27% improvement in reward over baseline attribution methods.
