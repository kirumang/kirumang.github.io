🔭 Research Landscape

The dominant theme is making world-action models (WAMs) lean and useful. TAPESTRY (#4) turns a pretrained video diffusion transformer into a policy by treating each action as a single patch token, then drops video generation at inference to emit an action chunk in one DDIM step (84 ms). GAWM (#27) goes smaller still: fewer than 100M parameters, a frozen DINOv3 encoder and no robot or video pretraining, yet it beats billion-parameter WAMs on RoboTwin 2.0. Trace-WAM (#28) argues the video target is the wrong thing to predict and swaps in future 3D point motion as an auxiliary signal, while SWIM (#18) makes the same argument for JEPA world models by learning which state features matter from an inverse-dynamics model. Taken together, these papers say the useful content of a world model is the task-relevant dynamics, not pixels, and that this is what lets it shrink.

A second thread is what to do with a policy after it is trained. Best-of-N analysis (#22) shows the standard way of measuring selection headroom overstates it (true selectable share 24-66%), and a fail-gated selector lifts closed-loop success from 76.9% to 81.6%. R2A (#9) retrieves motion primitives from training demonstrations to correct a frozen VLA under visual shift (up to +40% real-world), VLA-Stem (#16) bolts on an RL reflex layer for obstacles (52.8% vs 23.7% collision-free success) and AFIL (#7) steers sampling away from failure modes. All four leave base weights alone, a pragmatic stance that contrasts with TOPS (#17) and ThinkWorld (#30), which instead reshape the generative prior or the reasoning space during training.

Data and embodiment transfer form the third theme. OpenJaws (#2), RoboSTD (#5), HumiTransfer (#6), Ego-CEVR (#14) and CARD (#3) each find a different way around robot-in-the-loop teleoperation, and CARD's finding that kinematic retargeting keeps contact on under 10% of frames is a useful warning that geometry alone is not enough for dexterity. That points toward the contact-rich results in #12 (95.2% of 4,800 in-hand goals, drops cut 75% by a safety filter) and the sobering base-compliance measurements in #24, where only 54-70% of a 100 N command reached the workpiece. The field is converging on cheap human data plus explicit contact and safety modeling as the route to reliable manipulation.

OmniQuad: a Wheeled-Legged Hybrid Robot with Omnidirectional Wheels

2026-10-28 cs.RO 👤 M. Garabini (h=30)
Francesco Iotti, Alok Ranjan, Franco Angelini, Manolo Garabini
Core Contributions
  • Converts a pretrained video diffusion transformer into a policy by mapping each action timestep to a single patch token through a linear projection into the VAE patch space, so one backbone handles both video and control.
  • Trains with three diffusion objectives per minibatch (video, joint video+action, action-only), letting human and robot video supply dynamics priors without requiring action labels everywhere.
  • At inference it skips video generation and denoises an action chunk in a single DDIM step, 84 ms per chunk on consumer hardware, much cheaper than joint video-action policies.
Show abstract
Combining wheeled and legged locomotion within a single robotic platform offers the potential to harness the advantages of both systems. The development of such hybrid systems remains an active area of research. While legged robots exhibit remarkable adaptability for traversing uneven and rugged terrains, their relatively low speed limits their practicality in indoor environments. Conversely, wheeled robots demonstrate higher efficiency on flat surfaces but often encounter difficulties when navigating obstacles such as steps. This paper introduces OmniQuad, a novel hybrid robot that integrates mecanum wheels with legged locomotion to exploit the benefits of both modalities. Mecanum wheels provide omnidirectional mobility, enhancing the robot's manoeuvrability by enabling agile movement in confined spaces and the ability to maintain a consistent orientation during task execution. In this work, we first present the design of a custom wheeled-foot featuring mecanum wheels. Subsequently, we describe the complete robot assembly, comprising these wheeled feet and EM-Act actuators. Finally, we detail the control scheme developed to govern the OmniQuad and evaluate its performance through experimental trials conducted in both indoor and outdoor environments.

MATCH: Execution-Aware Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning for Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environment

2026-10-28 cs.RO 👤 Shu-Hao Ye (h=4)
Shuhao Ye, Yufei Wei, Demao Wang, Shichao Zhai, Rong Xiong
Core Contributions
  • Shows a world-action model does not need a billion-parameter backbone: GAWM predicts future observations in the latent space of a frozen DINOv3 encoder with under 100M parameters and no robot or video pretraining.
  • Compresses each camera view into a few tokens and supervises forecasts at two future horizons with dense patch features of future frames, forcing the tokens to encode future spatial layout; an action decoder regresses action chunks from current and predicted tokens.
  • Outperforms billion-parameter WAMs on RoboTwin 2.0, is comparable to billion-parameter VLAs on LIBERO and has lower action-prediction error and latency than a fine-tuned VLA on a dual-arm robot; the latent world model helps most when data or training time is limited.
Show abstract
Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLN-CE) requires instruction-consistent planning and reliable execution under collision constraints. Yet many hierarchical approaches optimize the planner while keeping execution fixed. We introduce MATCH, a framework that jointly learns a language-conditioned planner and a goal-conditioned primitive-action executor. An execution-aware topological graph represents start-dependent execution options and integrates executor value estimates with realized outcome memory, enabling the planner to select execution requests and refine their targets through bounded continuous offsets. A transition-aware dense macro reward balances ordered progress along the reference route against execution residual, encouraging executable requests without substituting local success for instruction following. A staged curriculum culminates in synchronized on-policy updates, with the executor learning on planner-generated requests and the planner adapting its requests to the executor's current behavior. With 0.5B parameters, MATCH achieves state-of-the-art performance on the R2R-CE and RxR-CE benchmarks. Preliminary real-robot deployments further demonstrate its practical potential.

Selective Sliding Mode Control Enhances Robustness of Aerial Robots Under Disturbances

2026-10-28 cs.ROeess.SY 👤 Anoop Kiran (h=3)
Anoop Kiran, Thales C. Silva, Nora Ayanian
Core Contributions
  • Argues future-video prediction wastes capacity on background appearance, and adds future 3D motion prediction (point correspondence, image-plane displacement, depth change) as an auxiliary objective focused on interaction dynamics.
  • A three-branch Mixture-of-Transformer jointly learns video, motion and action, but the action expert does not consume predicted trajectories, so inference is still one visual pass plus action denoising.
  • Shows strong in-distribution and out-of-distribution results on RoboCasa-GR1, LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus, with clearest gains over Fast-WAM when demonstrations are limited or distribution shifts occur.
Show abstract
Quadrotor control under disturbances such as wind gusts and payload variations remains challenging. First-order sliding mode control (SMC) and matched/unmatched uncertainty distinction are classical approaches; however, neither tells a practitioner which loops in a cascaded controller should be made robust to sustain disturbances, how to tune those loops, nor what the additional costs are on an embedded processor. Applying robust control uniformly across all axes addresses disturbances indiscriminately, adding tuning effort on axes where no disturbance acts. On inherently underactuated vehicles, disturbances along some axes cannot be opposed directly at all. We propose a principled methodology for deciding where robust control belongs in a cascaded architecture, with all control running onboard the vehicle. We recover the acting disturbance from PID hover flights under disturbance by inverting the equations of motion, use the recovered signal to identify and quantify the excited axes, and deploy robust control where it counts. Experiments on a Crazyflie 2.1 quadrotor under slung-payload and oblique-wind disturbances show tracking improvements concentrated on the axes the rule selects, and gain sweeps confirm that the switching gain reduces error on matched axes but has no effect on an excited unmatched axis, as the analysis predicts.

Minimum-Time Escape from the Conic Field of View of a Reeds--Shepp Pursuer: the Faster Evader

2026-10-28 cs.ROmath.OC 👤 Ubaldo Ruiz (h=9)
Ubaldo Ruiz
Core Contributions
  • JEPA world models predict embeddings but need not encode task-relevant state; hand-picked or uniformly weighted state supervision overcounts redundant features and lets irrelevant ones obscure differences.
  • SWIM learns state-feature weights with an inverse-dynamics model that predicts recorded actions from states and future displacements, then uses the weighted metric to align latent and state distances.
  • The inverse-dynamics model is training-only, so planning from images costs nothing extra; it improves planning success over uniform weighting on five simulated manipulation tasks.
Show abstract
We continue the study of minimum-time escape of an omnidirectional evader from the forward conic field of view of a Reeds--Shepp pursuer, now for a faster evader, and solve the game for every speed ratio $ν>1$ and every half-angle $0<α<π/2$: the escape time is characterized by explicit characteristics and the selection of roots of a scalar equation, and it is the value of the game in nonanticipative strategies at every state. Above the end $E$ of the evader's dispersal line on the front axis, the evader chases the reversing pursuer along a universal line fed by tributaries. The characteristic from the boundary of the usable part is an involute of a circle of radius $ν$ ending at a cusp, and two explicit thresholds decide whether it reaches the axis before the cusp and whether the jump of the escape time across it ends before the cusp or runs to it; in the latter case primaries fold back next to the barrier. The pursuer's optimal strategy is the relay of the slower case. The evader follows the tributaries and switches once to a primary, or commits in advance to a heading certified by the scalar equation of the primaries. Level-set and semi-Lagrangian computations are compared with the value.

Self-Supervised Doppler-Guided RF Odometry

2026-10-28 cs.ROcs.LG 👤 Hua-Cheng Zeng (h=6)
Kunzhe Song, Jialuo Du, Jingkai Lin, Huacheng Zeng
Core Contributions
  • Shows that the usual headroom estimate for best-of-N selection (run each candidate once, keep the best) conflates candidate quality with downstream randomness, so it overstates what a selector can realize.
  • Introduces a denoised counterfactual protocol (reproducible branching, repeated continuations, held-out oracles, common random numbers) over 100,000+ rollouts and four WAMs; selectable share of native samples is only 24-66% for three WAMs.
  • Finds that imagined futures from action-conditioned WAMs carry as much outcome information as real futures, which Cosmos Policy's value head leaves unused.
  • A fail-gated selector that deviates only when failure is predicted raises success from 76.9% to 81.6% on training tasks and 62.6% to 69.8% on unseen tasks.
Show abstract
Radio-frequency (RF) sensing enables robot ego-motion estimation in darkness, smoke, and fog, yet RF odometry remains challenging because multipath and moving reflectors make cross-frame matching highly ambiguous. Existing methods often rely on auxiliary inertial sensors or ground-truth pose supervision. We present RFWalk, a self-supervised, RF-only odometry framework that models dense correspondences across range-azimuth frames as random walks on a space-time graph and learns them from unlabeled sequences through temporal cycle consistency. To rule out correspondences that are cycle-consistent but physically implausible, we introduce a Doppler consistency loss that constrains correspondence-induced motion using radial velocity measured by the RF sensor itself. Ego-motion is then recovered through robust $\mathrm{SE}(2)$ registration that suppresses uncertain correspondences and those arising from independently moving objects. Without pose labels or auxiliary sensors, RFWalk remains competitive with supervised baselines in static scenes and reduces absolute trajectory error by 58% in dynamic scenes with moving pedestrians.

Value Function-Guided MPPI for Hierarchical Planning and Control in Off-Road Autonomous Navigation

2026-10-28 cs.RO 👤 Jeff G. Schneider (h=3)
Anoushka Alavilli, Raymond Song, Guanya Shi, Jeff Schneider
Core Contributions
  • Represents each action thought as a Chain-of-Dynamics, a sequence of discrete motion tokens capturing intermediate dynamics from the current world to a future state, usable as a common action representation across embodiments.
  • Reinforces CoDs by predicting the future each induces and rewarding those that match the observed outcome, so human videos without action labels still give direct supervision from state transitions.
  • Human and robot experience thus refine one reasoning space; the abstract reports effective human-to-robot transfer and generalization to unseen tasks in simulation and real robots, but gives no numbers.
Show abstract
Autonomous navigation in off-road terrain is commonly achieved using hierarchical control and planning architectures that rely on explicit cost maps and one-way information flow from a high-level planner to a low-level controller, decoupling planning from the true execution cost experienced during control. We propose a hierarchical planning and control framework in which information flows bidirectionally, allowing the low-level controller to guide trajectory selection at the planning level. The highest level of the hierarchy is a Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control-style planner that determines optimal trajectories and passes these waypoints to a pretrained low-level reinforcement learning (RL) controller. Rather than drawing from an explicit cost map, our MPPI planner leverages the value function of the RL controller as an implicit cost representation. In this formulation, MPPI serves as a trajectory exploration mechanism, while the low-level controller's value function guides the search. To account for the distribution shift seen by the value function between training and evaluation, we propose an online policy evaluation process that adapts the value function to unseen driving experiences, and we demonstrate this adaptation in unseen off-road simulation environments. We evaluate our approach in a high-fidelity off-road driving simulator across a range of challenging terrain conditions and compare its performance against a baseline MPPI controller. The results demonstrate that our method achieves safer and more effective navigation to goal locations while eliminating the need for explicit cost maps and enabling tighter coupling between planning and control.

When "No Worries" Went Wrong: HRI Lessons for Foundation-Model Robots

2026-10-28 cs.RO 👤 Eliahu Khalastchi (h=9)
Eliahu Khalastchi
Core Contributions
  • Questions the default of one shared Gaussian source and linear path for all tasks in flow-based multi-task policies, and adapts both per task without changing the architecture.
  • Builds multimodal task sources from reverse-mapped expert action sequences so the language instruction chooses where generation starts, then reshapes the transport schedule using source-target covariance mismatch, with an analysis showing a lower bound on velocity-prediction risk.
  • With one integration step lifts LIBERO-10 success from 53.44% (ten-step flow matching) to 69.67% at about one-sixth the inference time, and on a real upper-drawer task from 20% to 80%; gains transfer to pi0.5.
Show abstract
When a person declined our robot's invitation to play, Alice turned her shoulder away and said "No worries, it's all good." Only after a brief pause did she drive off. The words and gestures were plausible but their order changed the message. In another episode, Alice informed a host that his visitor had arrived---while the visitor stood right beside him. Both came from a three-year ROS 2 HRI consortium that integrated large language and vision models as they emerged. Today's foundation models give robots broader capabilities to perceive their surroundings, reason about situations and generate behavior. Yet capability alone does not ensure that a changed situation shapes the action that reaches a person. Evidence may never be acquired, or a purpose or constraint may fail to survive planning and execution. A system may even report Success when an action no longer serves its intended purpose. Drawing on the consortium's engineering experience, we develop five design lessons about attention, knowledge, commitments, embodied timing and action authority. We then introduce TRACE, a lightweight review that changes one interaction premise, follows it to a decision or enforcement boundary, and produces an actionable architecture finding. A bounded analysis of a published LLM-generated behavior-tree system illustrates how an available clarification mechanism can go unused. The engineering lesson is to preserve accountability boundaries, not the computational pipelines that once exposed them.

2026-10-28 cs.ROcs.AIcs.LG 👤 David Fridovich-Keil (h=22)
Jacob Levy, Arthur Bouton, Tristan D. Hassler, Hari Nayar, David Fridovich-Keil
Core Contributions
  • Treats VLA robustness to visual shift as a retrieval problem in motion space rather than trying to clean up the visual features that the shift corrupts.
  • Organizes successful demonstrations into an offline spatiotemporal motion primitive graph; a query-dependent GNN localizes current execution in it and retrieves compatible future primitives.
  • A learned dimension-wise gate blends retrieved priors with the frozen VLA's actions, keeping local reactivity; across GR00T N1.7, pi0.5 and StableVLA it gains up to +12% in simulation and +40% in real-world tests with no fine-tuning.
Show abstract
Future planetary rovers will traverse steep, deformable terrain where slip, sinkage, and terrain deformation can threaten mobility. Although simulation can capture some wheel-soil effects, unmodeled deformable-terrain phenomena create sim-to-real gaps requiring deployment-time adaptation. Yet, rover operations leave little room for extensive trial-and-error. We introduce TERRA (Targeted Exploration for Reward-Relevant Adaptation), an online adaptation framework that efficiently seeks deployment data exposing task-relevant model errors and corrects them. TERRA adapts a simulation-trained world model with a reward-weighted prediction loss and trains a loss predictor to estimate where future performance-relevant errors are likely to occur, using this signal to guide exploration during MPC. In simulation, TERRA reaches approximately 90% slope-climbing success within five trials under severe out-of-distribution pretraining conditions, outperforming exploration baselines in reward, completion time, and energy to first success. On the ERNEST rover, TERRA recovers coordinated active-suspension locomotion on a deformable Mars-simulant slope within three to five real-world trials. HERON: Real-Time Obstacle-Aware Reachability Maps for Mobile Manipulators

2026-10-28 cs.RO 👤 Jean-Philippe Roberge (h=10)
G. Dupoiron, F. Michaud, J. P. Roberge
Core Contributions
  • Adds obstacle avoidance to VLAs without fine-tuning them, which would need costly dynamic-obstacle data and could erode original task skill.
  • Converts VLA action chunks into motion references and trains an RL "Stem" policy, conditioned on proprioception and local obstacle information, to adjust timing and path while preserving task intent; training combines teacher-student distillation with curriculum learning.
  • Reaches 52.8% collision-free task success across six constrained simulation settings versus 23.7% for the strongest safety baseline, with base performance preserved when no obstacles are present, and fewer collisions in real-world tests.
Show abstract
Mobile manipulator robots rarely reposition their base during manipulation, because doing so requires real-time knowledge of how well the arm can reach its surroundings among obstacles. Reachability maps (RMs) encode this knowledge, but existing methods are slow, coarse (typically 5 cm), or ignore real obstacles. This article asks whether a neural network can predict an obstacle-aware RM at 2 cm resolution from a live depth camera, on embedded hardware, fast enough for online use. We present HERON, a 3D U-Net trained on RMs computed with the GPU-accelerated inverse kinematics of cuRobo, first on synthetic scenes and then on real depth data, and deployed on a Jetson AGX Orin with TensorRT and INT8 quantization. HERON predicts the RM of a 2.56 m cube around the arm in 115 ms on average, for a measured system rate of 8.57 Hz, close to the 10 Hz update rate of the obstacle map, whereas computing the same map with cuRobo takes about 90 min. Against the cuRobo ground truth, 95% of the voxels lie within 1 class out of 51 with the FP32 model and within 3 classes with the INT8 model, with a Pearson correlation of 0.99 and a quadratic weighted kappa above 0.9. Accuracy degrades mainly at the workspace boundaries.

2026-10-28 cs.RO 👤 M. T. Masouleh (h=23)
Ali Sabzejou, Farbod Azimmohseni, Mehdi Tale Masouleh, Ahmad Kalhor
Core Contributions
  • Note: the paper's listed title describes primitive-motion transfer, but the abstract describes AFIL, a failure-informed method for VLAs; these bullets follow the abstract.
  • Success-only behavioral cloning gives no corrective signal, so small errors compound; AFIL has the pretrained VLA generate its own failure rollouts online, avoiding handcrafted failure modes or human recovery data.
  • Trains dual action generators (success and failure) on a shared vision-language backbone, so the failure branch adds little parameter overhead.
  • During sampling the failure generator steers actions away from failure-prone regions, with strength set per diffusion step by the distance between the two distributions; reported to improve success on short- and long-horizon in- and out-of-domain tasks.
Show abstract
Robotic grasping relies on large manually annotated datasets, which are costly to create. We introduce a straight-skeleton-based algorithm for generating two-finger grasp keypoints and identifying regions suitable for stable, feasible grasps. Our automated pipeline produces consistent grasp annotations without human intervention. We also propose the Skeleton-based Generative Grasp Convolutional Neural Network (SkelGG-CNN), a 4-DoF architecture that learns geometric grasp features from skeleton-based guidance. To extend auto-labeling to 6-DoF grasping, we develop a generalized quasi-3D skeletonization algorithm that operates directly on point clouds. Using automatically generated 4-DoF labels, SkelGG-CNN achieves accuracies of 95.42%, 96.55%, and 97.1% on Jacquard V1, Jacquard V2, and Cornell, respectively. On the GraspNet novel split, 6-DoF auto-labeling achieves an average precision of 18.33, comparable to state-of-the-art model-based approaches. In simulation, the 6-DoF method achieves success rates of 98.4% on Dex-Net and 89.1% on EGAD!. Real-world experiments with a Delta parallel robot show success rates of 97.4% on single household objects and 95.0% in cluttered scenes, outperforming state-of-the-art methods without domain adaptation. The 4-DoF pipeline adapts across environments and produces annotations approaching human-level precision, enabling efficient training of lightweight models on small datasets. The generalized 6-DoF method provides scalable, geometry-aware annotations for cluttered 3D scenes.

ELEVATE: Agentic Controller Acquisition from Task Failure for Quadrupedal Manipulation

2026-10-28 cs.RO 👤 Ding Zhao (h=38)
Shiqi Liu, Radu Corcodel, Ding Zhao
Core Contributions
  • Existing retargeting copies kinematics and ignores force, so retargeted hands lose contact; CARD instead sets explicit contact-force targets alongside joint motion.
  • Unifies video, motion capture and tactile-glove data through one hand-contact representation, and adds Vid-HACO to infer contact from video that has no tactile signal.
  • Maintains contact on 43-78% of contact frames versus under 10% for kinematic and 22.5% for object-aware baselines; plugged into physics-based retargeting, strict success on GRAB rises from 12% to 32%.
  • A tactile tracking controller is shown to be necessary to reproduce demonstrated forces during open-loop hardware replay.
Show abstract
Quadrupedal manipulators can extend their workspace through whole-body interaction, but task-planning agents' capabilities remain limited by their available controllers. We present ELEVATE, a framework that uses persistent failures during task-program refinement to identify and acquire missing whole-body control capabilities. When these failures suggest a missing physical capability, a coding agent formulates and iteratively refines a reinforcement-learning problem, specifying behavior-specific rewards, curricula, and termination conditions. Validated controllers are added to the capability library and composed with existing controllers to retry the task. Starting from a single pretrained loco-manipulation controller, ELEVATE autonomously acquires controllers for wall-supported elevation, supported manipulation, and wall descent across a sequence of seven simulation tasks. These controllers are reused in subsequent reaching and object-manipulation tasks. On the five tasks beyond the initial controller's workspace, ELEVATE achieves mean success rates of 98.4%-100% across five independent runs, while coding-agent baselines with a fixed controller library fail to complete these tasks. The synthesized task programs, together with the acquired controllers, also transfer to a physical quadrupedal manipulator without real-robot training or fine-tuning, achieving 18/20 successes in rack retrieval and 16/20 in floor-to-rack placement. Videos and demonstrations are available at https://agentic-quad-elevate.github.io/public/.

In this field report, we present SKiD-SLAM, a distributed multi-robot LiDAR simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) system for such environments, and report its deployment across two mission concepts: cave exploration and scientific surface exploration. 2026-10-28 cs.RO 👤 Yun-Fan Ren (h=17)

Core Contributions

  • Replaces explicit hand-motion triggers with a visual imitation policy that maps palm-camera images straight to joint positions, inferring intent from both hand and object.

Show abstract
Reliable obstacle avoidance is essential for safe quadrotor flight in unknown environments, yet conventional frame-based cameras remain challenged by motion blur, limited dynamic range, and latency under fast motion. Event cameras, with sub-millisecond latency, high dynamic range, and negligible motion blur, offer a promising alternative, but leveraging them for closed-loop control remains difficult. Zero-shot vision-and-language navigation (VLN) requires an agent to use past observations without navigation-specific training. We study what information an 8B-scale navigation system should retain and how it should be organized for subsequent decisions. Our agent combines instruction progress, hierarchically compressed observation history anchored to poses, and accumulated geometry in complementary task, temporal, and spatial memories. An 8B observer summarizes observations, and an 8B reasoner uses the memories to select waypoint intents or request additional views, a geometric controller executes the selected targets. A semantic information perspective motivates the distinction between retaining evidence, making it accessible to the reasoner, and acquiring further observations. On R2R-CE val-unseen, the complete system achieves the highest among the evaluated Qwen3-based zero-shot baselines. Removing temporal memory reduces SR and sharply increases average path length from 16.9 to 39.1m, removing the spatial-map and planning component reduces SR as well. Real-world experiments on a Unitree Go2 show that our zero-shot method achieves higher success rates than the post-trained baseline in both corridor and office environments, suggesting better transfer to the evaluated real-world settings. As a complementary evaluation, LAW2 uses paired instruction interventions to examine behavioral responses, instruction following ability beyond endpoint success. These results support preserving task progress, observation history, and geometry in structured memory for zero-shot navigation with smaller open models.
