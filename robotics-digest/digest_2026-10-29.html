🔭 Research Landscape

The dominant theme is making world-action models (WAMs) lean and useful. TAPESTRY (#4) turns a pretrained video diffusion transformer into a policy by treating each action as a single patch token, then drops video generation at inference to emit an action chunk in one DDIM step (84 ms). GAWM (#27) goes smaller still: fewer than 100M parameters, a frozen DINOv3 encoder and no robot or video pretraining, yet it beats billion-parameter WAMs on RoboTwin 2.0. Trace-WAM (#28) argues the video target is the wrong thing to predict and swaps in future 3D point motion as an auxiliary signal, while SWIM (#18) makes the same argument for JEPA world models by learning which state features matter from an inverse-dynamics model. Taken together, these papers say the useful content of a world model is the task-relevant dynamics, not pixels, and that this is what lets it shrink.

A second thread is what to do with a policy after it is trained. Best-of-N analysis (#22) shows the standard way of measuring selection headroom overstates it (true selectable share 24-66%), and a fail-gated selector lifts closed-loop success from 76.9% to 81.6%. R2A (#9) retrieves motion primitives from training demonstrations to correct a frozen VLA under visual shift (up to +40% real-world), VLA-Stem (#16) bolts on an RL reflex layer for obstacles (52.8% vs 23.7% collision-free success) and AFIL (#7) steers sampling away from failure modes. All four leave base weights alone, a pragmatic stance that contrasts with TOPS (#17) and ThinkWorld (#30), which instead reshape the generative prior or the reasoning space during training.

Data and embodiment transfer form the third theme. OpenJaws (#2), RoboSTD (#5), HumiTransfer (#6), Ego-CEVR (#14) and CARD (#3) each find a different way around robot-in-the-loop teleoperation, and CARD's finding that kinematic retargeting keeps contact on under 10% of frames is a useful warning that geometry alone is not enough for dexterity. That points toward the contact-rich results in #12 (95.2% of 4,800 in-hand goals, drops cut 75% by a safety filter) and the sobering base-compliance measurements in #24, where only 54-70% of a 100 N command reached the workpiece. The field is converging on cheap human data plus explicit contact and safety modeling as the route to reliable manipulation.

Securing the Robotics Supply Chain

2026-10-29 cs.RO 👤 H. Christensen (h=60)
Cindy L. Bethel, Henrik I. Christensen, Odest Chadwicke Jenkins, George Pappas, Holly Yanco
Core Contributions
  • Converts a pretrained video diffusion transformer into a policy by mapping each action timestep to a single patch token through a linear projection into the VAE patch space, so one backbone handles both video and control.
  • Trains with three diffusion objectives per minibatch (video, joint video+action, action-only), letting human and robot video supply dynamics priors without requiring action labels everywhere.
  • At inference it skips video generation and denoises an action chunk in a single DDIM step, 84 ms per chunk on consumer hardware, much cheaper than joint video-action policies.
Show abstract
This paper sets out what a sustained federal research and investment agenda would need to include to close that gap--not within a single budget cycle, but over the decade such an undertaking will realistically require. It identifies where the gap is most pronounced today: in the methods needed to verify and certify components of uncertain origin, in the foundational hardware research that has long been underfunded relative to software, in the physical infrastructure researchers need to test systems at scale, and in the workforce required to build and sustain all of it. Robotics should not be treated merely as a downstream beneficiary of a broader artificial intelligence investment strategy. It constitutes its own supply chain, with its own points of failure, and merits its own sustained federal commitment.

TouchBridge: Tactile-Conditioned Human-to-Robot Transfer for Dexterous Manipulation

2026-10-29 cs.RO 👤 Zhe-Cheng Yuan (h=18)
Haozhe Zhang, Gu Zhang, Zirui Zou, Yuzheng Chen, Jinlong Wu
Core Contributions
  • Shows a world-action model does not need a billion-parameter backbone: GAWM predicts future observations in the latent space of a frozen DINOv3 encoder with under 100M parameters and no robot or video pretraining.
  • Compresses each camera view into a few tokens and supervises forecasts at two future horizons with dense patch features of future frames, forcing the tokens to encode future spatial layout; an action decoder regresses action chunks from current and predicted tokens.
  • Outperforms billion-parameter WAMs on RoboTwin 2.0, is comparable to billion-parameter VLAs on LIBERO and has lower action-prediction error and latency than a fine-tuned VLA on a dual-arm robot; the latent world model helps most when data or training time is limited.
Show abstract
Human demonstrations offer an efficient and scalable source of data for dexterous robot manipulation, but differences in sensing and hand morphology complicate the transfer of contact-rich interactions. We present TouchBridge, a hardware--software co-designed framework for human-to-robot data transformation. A wearable human demonstration interface and a corresponding robot interface establish cross-embodiment correspondence in visual and fingertip tactile sensing. We introduce Tac-Retarget, a tactile-conditioned retargeting method that uses measured contact to align fingertip contact patches and preserve their spatial relations within and across hands. Unlike object-aware or simulation-based transfer pipelines, Tac-Retarget requires no object mesh, object-pose estimate, or physics simulation for retargeting, enabling direct and scalable conversion of human demonstrations into robot-domain training data. Combined with visual synthesis and camera-anchored action conversion, TouchBridge produces robot-domain demonstrations for fine-tuning a pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) model. Across four real-world contact-rich tasks, policies fine-tuned solely on TouchBridge-converted human demonstrations achieve a 72.5% average success rate, compared with 77.5% using an equal number of teleoperated robot demonstrations, while human demonstration collection is 7.0x faster. Under the same policy and training setup, Tac-Retarget outperforms DexPilot and UniDex by 25.0 and 42.5 percentage points, respectively.

Compressed Orientation Reachability Maps for Sub-Second Robotic Needle Entry Point Planning

2026-10-29 cs.ROcs.CGeess.SY 👤 F. Mathis-Ullrich (h=18)
Prabhu Nithin Gollapudi, Andreas Maier, Franziska Mathis-Ullrich, Water Zhao, Jia Wang
Core Contributions
  • Argues future-video prediction wastes capacity on background appearance, and adds future 3D motion prediction (point correspondence, image-plane displacement, depth change) as an auxiliary objective focused on interaction dynamics.
  • A three-branch Mixture-of-Transformer jointly learns video, motion and action, but the action expert does not consume predicted trajectories, so inference is still one visual pass plus action denoising.
  • Shows strong in-distribution and out-of-distribution results on RoboCasa-GR1, LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus, with clearest gains over Fast-WAM when demonstrations are limited or distribution shifts occur.
Show abstract
In the context of multi-stage trajectory planning for robotic percutaneous liver interventions, identifying a kinematically feasible entry point remains a critical computational bottleneck. Existing methods rely on exhaustive inverse kinematics or randomized searches, incurring delays of 13 to 27 seconds per target and limiting real-time intraoperative adaptation. This paper introduces an Orientation-Based Reachability Map (ORM) that precomputes the kinematic feasibility of a 6 Degrees of Freedom (DoF) serial manipulator across 188,873 workspace positions and encodes orientation feasibility into a compact, indexed 83 MB map. A Just-In-Time (JIT) compiled runtime pipeline replaces live optimization with near-instantaneous array lookups. Experimental evaluation demonstrates a reduction in planning latency from 13.5-26.9 s to 0.289-0.349 s per target while maintaining high geometric fidelity, achieving a mean convex hull volume overlap ratio of 0.933 and a mean alpha-shape volume ratio of 0.976 relative to the baseline reachable point cloud. The proposed ORM framework enables sub-second entry point planning and provides an efficient computational framework for robotic needle insertion in percutaneous liver ablation.

Improving Fine-Grained Manipulation through Demonstration Expansion and Execution Design

2026-10-29 cs.RO 👤 Dong-Ze Lian (h=23)
Jingxu Gu, Dongze Lian
Core Contributions
  • JEPA world models predict embeddings but need not encode task-relevant state; hand-picked or uniformly weighted state supervision overcounts redundant features and lets irrelevant ones obscure differences.
  • SWIM learns state-feature weights with an inverse-dynamics model that predicts recorded actions from states and future displacements, then uses the weighted metric to align latent and state distances.
  • The inverse-dynamics model is training-only, so planning from images costs nothing extra; it improves planning success over uniform weighting on five simulated manipulation tasks.
Show abstract
Fine-grained manipulation requires a robot policy to follow instructions, remain robust to visual variations, and translate predicted actions into precise robot control. We present Demonstration Expansion and Execution Design (DEED), a framework for adapting a pretrained vision-language-action policy to the five MetaFine tasks through structured demonstration expansion and execution design. DEED generates demonstrations for different instructions from the same initial scene and renders each physical trajectory under different camera positions and lighting conditions while preserving its original states and action targets. A hierarchical sampling strategy balances instructions, trajectories, and visual conditions during supervised adaptation. At deployment, DEED converts predicted action chunks into robot commands using inverse kinematics, gripper mappings selected for each task, and closed-loop replanning. DEED achieves an official Overall score of 85.55%. Fixed-policy evaluations show that performance varies with execution horizon, denoising budget, and gripper mapping.

Data-Driven or Model-Constrained? Comparing Airflow Estimation Methods under Sparse Sensing

2026-10-29 cs.RO 👤 J. Monroy (h=20)
Dominik Aigner, Thomas Wiedemann, Javier Monroy, Achim J. Lilienthal
Core Contributions
  • Shows that the usual headroom estimate for best-of-N selection (run each candidate once, keep the best) conflates candidate quality with downstream randomness, so it overstates what a selector can realize.
  • Introduces a denoised counterfactual protocol (reproducible branching, repeated continuations, held-out oracles, common random numbers) over 100,000+ rollouts and four WAMs; selectable share of native samples is only 24-66% for three WAMs.
  • Finds that imagined futures from action-conditioned WAMs carry as much outcome information as real futures, which Cosmos Policy's value head leaves unused.
  • A fail-gated selector that deviates only when failure is predicted raises success from 76.9% to 81.6% on training tasks and 62.6% to 69.8% on unseen tasks.
Show abstract
Airflow-driven advection is the primary mechanism for gas and aerosol dispersion in real-world scenarios, making accurate airflow estimation essential for ventilation monitoring, gas source localization, and contaminant spread analysis. Since obtaining airflow maps via high-resolution spatial sensing is usually infeasible in practice, flow field estimation methods must cope with sparse measurements by introducing statistical or physical priors to compensate for limited data. Existing approaches mainly differ in how they incorporate fluid-flow constraints and environmental geometry. However, due to the lack of systematic comparisons across this spectrum of methods, the potential benefits of physics-based priors for airflow estimation under sparse sensing remain underexplored. To address this question, we quantitatively evaluate four airflow estimators across three simulated 2D scenarios under steady-state conditions and varying sampling densities. The methods range from Gaussian Process Regression and graph-based Gaussian Markov Random Fields to two Navier-Stokes-based approaches, including a novel strong-form residual minimization variant. Our results show that physical priors provide the greatest benefit at low sampling densities by compensating for missing spatial information. Although data-driven Gaussian Process Regression becomes increasingly competitive as sensor coverage increases, model-based estimators retain an accuracy advantage across the evaluated scenarios.

Kairos: Semantics-Aware Orchestration of Planning Engines across Classical, Numeric, Temporal and Hybrid PDDL

2026-10-29 cs.AIcs.RO 👤 F. Rodríguez-Lera (h=15)
Francisco Martín Rico, Esther Aguado González, Francisco Miguel Moreno Olivo, Rodrigo Pérez Rodríguez, José Miguel Guerrero Hernández
Core Contributions
  • Represents each action thought as a Chain-of-Dynamics, a sequence of discrete motion tokens capturing intermediate dynamics from the current world to a future state, usable as a common action representation across embodiments.
  • Reinforces CoDs by predicting the future each induces and rewarding those that match the observed outcome, so human videos without action labels still give direct supervision from state transitions.
  • Human and robot experience thus refine one reasoning space; the abstract reports effective human-to-robot transfer and generalization to unseen tasks in simulation and real robots, but gives no numbers.
Show abstract
Planning at run time, as in robots that re-plan while they act, needs a planner that reads the PDDL its users write (durative actions, numeric resources, time windows, continuous change), answers quickly, and delivers only validated plans. Specialised planners are strong on individual fragments of PDDL, but they differ in coverage, latency and in what their failures mean. We study whether selecting heterogeneous engines from the semantics of each task can give broad coverage of PDDL, from STRIPS to PDDL+, while remaining competitive with specialised planners and making explicit what each answer guarantees. Kairos profiles each task to decide which of five engines apply (ground heuristic search, compressed and snap-action temporal search, time steps for hybrid dynamics, lifted search) and schedules them under one budget of time and memory. Every candidate plan is checked by a validator independent of the planner's transformations, and "unsolvable" is reported only when a search whose exhaustion is a proof exhausts its space. On 1,756 tasks from nine comparative suites, Kairos solves more tasks at the full limits than the strongest evaluated planner of each suite, with the largest differences on numeric, temporal, PDDL3 and PDDL+ planning, and it also solves the most tasks at 100 ms and 1 s. Resampling over domains, the gain is above zero in six of the nine suites, while on classical planning against LAMA it is within the noise. A further 450 feature-oriented tasks and 26 one-feature probes show that Kairos handles all seventeen features probed, where no other planner evaluated handles more than eight. Ablations identify compressed temporal search and the novelty fallback as major contributors, and a comparison with VAL traces every disagreement to a PDDL+ convention or to a documented VAL defect. Planner and benchmark are open source.

RAIBO-Wagon: Real-Time Navigation System for a Legged Robot Towing a Wagon

2026-10-29 cs.RO 👤 Jemin Hwangbo (h=26)
Insung Yang, Eunchae Kim, Jungwoo Hur, Seunghun Jeon, Donghyuk Choi
Core Contributions
  • Questions the default of one shared Gaussian source and linear path for all tasks in flow-based multi-task policies, and adapts both per task without changing the architecture.
  • Builds multimodal task sources from reverse-mapped expert action sequences so the language instruction chooses where generation starts, then reshapes the transport schedule using source-target covariance mismatch, with an analysis showing a lower bound on velocity-prediction risk.
  • With one integration step lifts LIBERO-10 success from 53.44% (ten-step flow matching) to 69.67% at about one-sixth the inference time, and on a real upper-drawer task from 20% to 80%; gains transfer to pi0.5.
Show abstract
Legged robots have demonstrated advanced capabilities across a wide range of real-world tasks, yet much of this progress concerns the robot controlling only itself. Once the robot is coupled to an external object -- particularly an underactuated one -- the dynamics of the combined assembly become more complex. Towing a wagon is a common practical scenario, where coupled dynamics make reliable navigation challenging. This difficulty is amplified in confined spaces, where collision-free navigation requires finding a narrow set of feasible paths under strict real-time constraints. To address this, we propose a navigation framework that combines Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) with Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control over a learned Forward Dynamics Model (FDM). The CFM model mitigates the initialization problem of MPPI and accelerates its convergence, while MPPI with the FDM recovers feasible solutions beyond the CFM model's training distribution by iterative refinement. We integrate the framework into a full-stack navigation system for RAIBO-Wagon, a quadruped towing a custom wagon, and validate it in real-world outdoor experiments on 15 m-long courses through confined spaces, with all sensing and computation performed fully onboard. The system achieves an 85% success rate across 40 trials, towing a total load of up to 40 kg -- nearly 100% of RAIBO's body weight.

2026-10-29 cs.ROeess.SY 👤 Tevfik Bultan (h=41)
Xi Zheng, Ivan Ruchkin, Hong Jin Kang, Tevfik Bultan, Vijay Ganesh
Core Contributions
  • Treats VLA robustness to visual shift as a retrieval problem in motion space rather than trying to clean up the visual features that the shift corrupts.
  • Organizes successful demonstrations into an offline spatiotemporal motion primitive graph; a query-dependent GNN localizes current execution in it and retrieves compatible future primitives.
  • A learned dimension-wise gate blends retrieved priors with the frozen VLA's actions, keeping local reactivity; across GR00T N1.7, pi0.5 and StableVLA it gains up to +12% in simulation and +40% in real-world tests with no fine-tuning.
Show abstract
Learning-enabled cyber-physical systems (CPS), including autonomous vehicles and UAVs, increasingly incorporate foundation models, yet fragmented assurance practices provide insufficient support for system-level guarantees. We argue that restoring engineering control across stochastic neural, symbolic, and physical boundaries requires a shift from model-centric evaluation to system-level assurance. We introduce the Foundation Model Assurance Stack, connecting three directions: (i) neuro-symbolic architectures that constrain foundation models through explicit symbolic world models, reasoning layers, and interfaces; (ii) semantically grounded, conformance-based testing that combines formal specifications and symbolic world models with foundation-model-assisted specification mining, scenario generation, and test synthesis; and (iii) compositional verification and runtime assurance that target formally tractable components and interfaces through modular contracts, equivalence checking, verified synthesis, uncertainty-aware reasoning, and runtime monitoring and enforcement. Foundation models serve as constrained assistants whose outputs are independently checked wherever correctness can be formally specified. We identify research challenges in specification engineering, abstraction fidelity, evidence composition, and lifecycle certification, and outline a staged agenda toward trustworthy, deployable, and certifiable learning-enabled CPS.

From Reasoning to Action: Consequence-Grounded Alignment for Vision-Language-Action Models

2026-10-29 cs.RO 👤 Zhengping Che (h=29)
Yixue Zhang, Wenke Xia, Kun Wu, Wenbo Yu, Pei Ren
Core Contributions
  • Adds obstacle avoidance to VLAs without fine-tuning them, which would need costly dynamic-obstacle data and could erode original task skill.
  • Converts VLA action chunks into motion references and trains an RL "Stem" policy, conditioned on proprioception and local obstacle information, to adjust timing and path while preserving task intent; training combines teacher-student distillation with curriculum learning.
  • Reaches 52.8% collision-free task success across six constrained simulation settings versus 23.7% for the strongest safety baseline, with base performance preserved when no obstacles are present, and fewer collisions in real-world tests.
Show abstract
Reasoning informs decision-making by connecting intended actions with their expected consequences. Recent vision-language-action (VLA) models incorporate embodied chains of thought (ECoTs) to guide action generation. However, such guidance remains implicit, as actions are conditioned on ECoTs rather than explicitly optimized to align with them. We introduce CoRAct, a consequence-grounded reasoning-to-action alignment framework that explicitly aligns reasoning and action through reward-guided optimization in action noise space. CoRAct formulates two physically grounded ECoT components as verifiable signals: movement specifies intended motion, while waypoints encode expected spatial consequences. A unified Mixture-of-Transformers jointly models ECoT reasoning, action generation, and action-conditioned future prediction. For candidate actions generated from sampled noise, CoRAct computes alignment rewards based on movement consistency and agreement between predicted outcomes and specified waypoints. These rewards guide group-relative optimization of the initial noise distribution, steering action generation toward consistency with intended actions and expected consequences. Experiments across RoboCasa, VLABench, and real-world manipulation tasks demonstrate improvements in task success, generalization, and reasoning-execution alignment over strong ECoT-based VLA baselines. CoRAct also grounds externally provided ECoTs in executable behavior, enabling zero-shot adaptation to novel scenarios by leveraging knowledge from powerful vision-language models. Project page and dataset: https://onlyxingxingzyx.github.io/CoRAct/

Adaptive search with path-constrained mobile sensors using multi-arm bandits

2026-10-29 cs.ROcs.LGmath.OC 👤 S. di Cairano (h=43)
Abraham P. Vinod, Parth K. Thaker, Gautam Dasarathy, Stefano Di Cairano
Core Contributions
  • Note: the paper's listed title describes primitive-motion transfer, but the abstract describes AFIL, a failure-informed method for VLAs; these bullets follow the abstract.
  • Success-only behavioral cloning gives no corrective signal, so small errors compound; AFIL has the pretrained VLA generate its own failure rollouts online, avoiding handcrafted failure modes or human recovery data.
  • Trains dual action generators (success and failure) on a shared vision-language backbone, so the failure branch adds little parameter overhead.
  • During sampling the failure generator steers actions away from failure-prone regions, with strength set per diffusion step by the distance between the two distributions; reported to improve success on short- and long-horizon in- and out-of-domain tasks.
Show abstract
We study a constrained sequential decision problem for detecting regions of interest from noisy online measurements collected by a team of energy-constrained mobile sensors. The sensor motion is restricted to a precomputed set of feasible paths that satisfy mobility constraints, limited available energy by requiring return to charging stations, and avoidance of known obstacles. We cast the resulting active path selection as a multi-armed bandit problem and propose an iterative search algorithm that adaptively assigns paths using confidence bounds computed from the collected data. We also propose a graph-based method to construct low-cardinality path covers under the team's constraints. We establish finite-time and anytime probabilistic correctness guarantees for the proposed algorithm, derive non-asymptotic upper bounds on the times required to detect all regions of interest and to label all nodes, and show that these bounds match algorithm-independent lower bounds up to constant factors for a broad class of problem instances. Using numerical simulations, we illustrate the behavior of the proposed approach and the effect of path redundancy and team size on search performance.

Decoding Geometry, Not Tokens: Efficient VLMs for Robot Affordance and Trajectory Prediction

2026-10-29 cs.ROcs.CV 👤 S. Sridharan (h=66)
Akila Ayanthi, Darshana Priyasad, Tharindu Fernando, Dimity Miller, Sridha Sridharan
Core Contributions
  • Existing retargeting copies kinematics and ignores force, so retargeted hands lose contact; CARD instead sets explicit contact-force targets alongside joint motion.
  • Unifies video, motion capture and tactile-glove data through one hand-contact representation, and adds Vid-HACO to infer contact from video that has no tactile signal.
  • Maintains contact on 43-78% of contact frames versus under 10% for kinematic and 22.5% for object-aware baselines; plugged into physics-based retargeting, strict success on GRAB rises from 12% to 32%.
  • A tactile tracking controller is shown to be necessary to reproduce demonstrated forces during open-loop hardware replay.
Show abstract
Predicting where to grasp and how to move is fundamentally a geometry problem. Vision-language models treat it as language generation instead. They tokenize spatial coordinates into text and decode them autoregressively. This mechanism ignores spatial structure and truncates continuous precision. Errors compound across coordinate tokens during decoding. In this paper, we show that compact off-the-shelf VLMs with 0.5-2B parameters already encode rich spatial information in their intermediate hidden states. Autoregressive decoding is the dominant bottleneck for trajectory prediction, while affordance prediction depends additionally on feature depth and decoder architecture. We expose the latent spatial information with a lightweight, geometry-compatible prediction head that attaches to a frozen backbone. It reads features from an intermediate hidden layer, conditions a learned query on the task instruction and refines it via cross-attention over the visual tokens to regress affordance boxes and trajectory waypoints directly as continuous values, without modifying the backbone. We evaluate on ShareRobot-Bench and AGD20K. Our 2B variant reduces trajectory error by over 60% and improves ShareRobot-Bench affordance mAP by 85% relative to the autoregressive baseline. It approaches RoboBrain on trajectory prediction (0.251 vs 0.191 DFD) at 3.8x higher throughput. These results demonstrate a favorable accuracy-efficiency trade-off for instruction-conditioned 2D geometric prediction.

Learned Terminal Costs for Short-Horizon Model Predictive Control of Autonomous Trucks in Interactive Highway Traffic

2026-10-29 cs.LGcs.AIcs.ROeess.SY 👤 Nikolce Murgovski (h=35)
Hooman Hamze, Benjamin Houshmand, Deepthi Pathare, Erik Börve, Morteza Haghir Chehreghani
Core Contributions
  • Sets a hard benchmark for in-hand manipulation: reaching arbitrary object poses at any wrist orientation through long goal sequences, not just spinning an object.
  • The sim-only recipe relies on diverse resets and goals from generated grasp datasets and an adaptive friction curriculum, and transfers zero-shot to a real LEAP hand using mocap or a single wrist depth camera.
  • Over 13.3 hours and 4,800 unseen goals the state-based policy reaches 95.2% of goals within 2 cm and 0.4 rad; a learned residual safety filter with tunable conservativeness cuts drops 75% while still reaching 91.5%.
  • Also demonstrates portable skills such as screwing in a lightbulb using only a wrist camera, and releases the full recipe.
Show abstract
Autonomous highway driving for heavy-duty trucks is a challenging control problem. The greater mass, dimensions and intricate dynamics increase the requirements for cooperation from surrounding traffic in critical maneuvers, requiring a careful balance between safety constraints and efficient long-horizon decision making. Model Predictive Control (MPC) provides rigorous constraint satisfaction but relies on terminal cost which can limit its effectiveness at short, computationally affordable horizons. Reinforcement Learning (RL) captures long-term driving behavior but offers no constraint guarantees and tends toward overly conservative policies when trained under sparse collision penalties. In this paper, we propose a hybrid MPC and RL framework for highway driving in autonomous trucks, where a finite-horizon MPC, solved via dynamic programming, uses a pre-trained RL model for terminal cost estimation. We evaluate the framework in a simulation environment with reactive surrounding traffic and compare it against pure MPC with zero terminal cost, rollout with terminal cost estimation using a base policy, and pure RL. Results show that the hybrid approach achieves zero collisions with the lowest cost of any configuration.

This work proposes a novel conveyor belt mechanism enabling continuous, active repositioning of functional elements along the body of a tendon-driven continuum robot. Up to three conveyor belt mechanisms are integrated within a 9 mm diameter continuum robot. We demonstrate equivalent or reduced conveyor belt actuation forces compared to robot tendon actuation forces. Human cognition flexibly draws on a shared perceptual foundation to understand environments, attend to referenced entities, reason about spatial relationships, and act. Endowing aerial robots with an analogous functional organization remains challenging, as existing systems are often specialized for individual capabilities, dependent on offboard computation, or unable to sustain closed-loop operation on resource-constrained platforms. We present OpenFlyV2, a unified onboard vision-language-action architecture that uses a shared vision-language representation to support semantic understanding, language-grounded perception, spatial reasoning, and continuous aerial control within a single model. A compact action expert converts the shared multimodal context into short-horizon flight waypoints, while motion-aware temporal memory and action priors reduce the computational cost of closed-loop execution. We construct \dataset{}, a simulation-based dataset comprising approximately 70,000 navigation trajectories and 60,000 vision-language instruction--response pairs. OpenFlyV2 generates action chunks, each comprising 12 waypoints, entirely onboard at 10 Hz using an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX. It transfers zero-shot from simulation to physical quadrotor flight, achieving a 48\% navigation success rate across four real-world environments without real-world aerial training data. Extensive results demonstrate that broad vision-language competence and continuous closed-loop flight can coexist within a single model on a resource-constrained aerial platform. Code will be made publicly available at https://github.com/Ideal-ljl/AeroBrain.
