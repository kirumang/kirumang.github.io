🔭 Research Landscape

The strongest cluster today is whole-body mobile manipulation fed by human data. Pixel (#2) and Pixel-UMI (#1), from the same group, argue that hardware, data collection, controller and policy must be co-designed: Pixel builds a low-cost wheeled humanoid with human-aligned kinematics, while Pixel-UMI retargets sensorized human demonstrations with scene geometry, masks out views corrupted by residual retargeting error, and closes the gap with DAgger corrections to reach 87.0% average task progress. TAMPAgent (#5) approaches the same household mobile-manipulation problem from the planning side, letting an LVLM agent write a partial belief-space plan that classical TAMP search completes, and VerifLM (#20) turns an edited image into a goal pose that must survive parallel simulated worlds. The common move is to stop trusting a single learned guess and surround it with structure, whether kinematic retargeting, symbolic search or physical verification.

A second theme is the quiet maturation of evaluation for foundation policies. D&A (#3) shows that a VLM reward model improves when it first writes down an absolute definition of success and only then scores, WISE (#6) raises closed-loop success on RoboLab from 6.0% to 24.0% by picking among four imagined rollouts, and VLALagBench (#28) quantifies that a 100 ms inference delay can nearly halve success on moving targets. Read together they say the bottleneck is no longer producing plausible outputs but knowing which output to execute and when: reward judgement (#3), candidate selection (#6) and latency budgeting (#28) are all about selection and timing, not model size. The Cosmos/DreamZero setting of #6 and the asynchronous-execution findings of #28 are directly complementary.

Third, contact sensing keeps getting cheaper and more informative. PanoTac (#8) delivers a 12x32 three-axis taxel map for under $50 per fingertip and beats vision-only policies by 46.7 points, ToFTouch (#12) adds pre-contact sensing, and ForceLDM (#19) distils privileged future force into a student that only sees the present. Park et al. (#9) add a cautionary physical angle: behavior cloning degrades when a heavy arm meets light objects while RL does not, hinting that hardware and demonstration choice matter as much as data volume. Around these, a long tail of control-theoretic and benchmark papers (#11, #18, #24, #26, #29, #30) shows steady investment in certificates, open testbeds and datasets that include failures.

Pixel-UMI: Learning Environment-Aware Whole-Body Manipulation from Sensorized Human Demonstrations

2026-11-02 cs.ROcs.AIcs.CVcs.LGeess.SY 👤 Jason Jingzhou Liu (h=n/a)
Jason Jingzhou Liu, Tony Tao, Deepak Pathak, Ruslan Salakhutdinov
Core Contributions
  • Tackles the embodiment gap in human-to-robot transfer: Environment-Aware Retargeting uses reconstructed scene geometry plus head and gripper trajectories to choose robot posture and produce collision-aware joint and base targets, rather than copying human poses.
  • Retargeting-Error-Aware Masking feeds the residual pose error back into training so the policy relies less on camera views that will not match at deployment, a failure mode most UMI-style pipelines ignore.
  • Adds on-policy human corrections via DAgger to cover contact-rich interactions that estimated actions cannot capture.
  • Reaches 87.0% average task progress over five tasks spanning articulated objects, deformable objects, constrained reaching, whole-body collision avoidance and bimanual contact.
Show abstract
Sensorized human demonstrations offer scalable data for mobile manipulation, but embodiment differences complicate policy learning. We present Pixel-UMI, a pipeline for learning environment-aware whole-body policies from human data. We first introduce Environment-Aware Retargeting (EAR), which combines reconstructed scene geometry with demonstrated head and gripper trajectories to determine robot posture and generate collision-aware joint and base targets. Delay-calibrated action assignment converts these trajectories into actions. However, kinematic differences can leave residual pose errors, creating viewpoint mismatches between human demonstrations and robot observations. We therefore introduce Retargeting-Error-Aware Masking (REAM), which uses these errors during training to reduce reliance on mismatched views at deployment. Finally, because estimated actions cannot fully capture contact-rich interactions, we collect on-policy human corrections through DAgger to improve performance. We evaluate Pixel-UMI on five challenging tasks spanning articulated-object manipulation, deformable-object handling, constrained-space reaching, whole-body collision avoidance, and contact-rich bimanual coordination, achieving 87.0% average task progress. Video results are available at https://pixel-robot.com/pixel-umi

Pixel: A Co-Designed Full-Stack Robot System for Learning General-Purpose Mobile Manipulation

2026-11-02 cs.ROcs.AIcs.CVcs.LGeess.SY 👤 Tony Tao (h=n/a)
Tony Tao, Jason Jingzhou Liu, Ruslan Salakhutdinov, Deepak Pathak
Core Contributions
  • Argues that mobile-manipulation progress is limited by mismatches between independently developed hardware, data interfaces, controllers and policies, and builds all of them together as one system.
  • Pairs a low-cost wheeled humanoid with human-aligned kinematics and compliant grippers with several teleoperation and robot-free data-collection interfaces that feed a single policy pipeline.
  • A unified whole-body controller and a hierarchical stack provide language-conditioned visuomotor control and long-horizon execution.
  • Demonstrates behaviours needing dexterity, active perception, navigation and whole-body coordination, positioned as an extensible research platform rather than a single result.
Show abstract
General-purpose mobile manipulation requires a combination of capable robot hardware, scalable demonstration collection, expressive whole-body control, and effective policy learning. Yet these components are often developed independently, creating mismatches that limit the capabilities of the complete system. We present Pixel, a co-designed full-stack system for learning general-purpose mobile manipulation. Pixel combines a low-cost wheeled humanoid with human-aligned kinematics and compliant grippers, multiple teleoperation and robot-free human data-collection interfaces, a unified whole-body controller, and a common policy learning pipeline across human and robot demonstrations. A hierarchical learning stack further enables language-conditioned visuomotor control and long-horizon task execution. We evaluate the system across hardware, data collection, and policy learning, and demonstrate mobile-manipulation behaviors requiring dexterity, active perception, navigation, and whole-body coordination. We hope that Pixel provides an integrated and extensible foundation for studying how robot hardware, data, control, and learning can be designed together for robot learning. More results at https://pixel-robot.com/pixel

TAMPAgent: Agentic Belief-Space Planning for Mobile Manipulation

2026-11-02 cs.RO 👤 Utkarsh A. Mishra (h=n/a)
Utkarsh A. Mishra, Alperen Degirmenci, Yongxin Chen, Danfei Xu, Tucker Hermans
Core Contributions
  • Identifies why LVLM agents with direct tool calling fail outside tabletops: they cannot reason about geometric uncertainty, while classical TAMP handles partial observability but is slow and needs closed worlds.
  • The agent writes only a partial plan of parameterised observe and execute actions, classical search completes it, and the agent then binds geometric parameters through its perception, planning and control tool API.
  • Closed-loop belief updates interleave observation and manipulation, so information gathering becomes a first-class action.
  • Outperforms direct tool-calling agents on household mobile-manipulation tasks on Rainbow Y1 and Unitree G1 platforms.
Show abstract
Open-world robotic mobile manipulation requires acting in physical environments with only partial information. While recent Large Vision-Language Model (LVLM) agents show strong open-world reasoning capabilities, existing architectures that rely on direct tool calling struggle with geometric uncertainty and thus are limited to fully-observable tabletop environments. In contrast, Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) systems can solve long-horizon tasks under partial observability but incur high computational costs and require closed worlds. We propose \textbf{TAMPAgent}, an agentic planning framework that unifies the reasoning of LVLMs with the structured constraint satisfaction of TAMP systems. We endow an LVLM agent with a belief-space planning model operating over an extensible API of perception, planning, and control tools. The agent formulates a partial task plan composed of parameterized observation and execution actions, which is subsequently completed using classical search algorithms. After extracting the plan's geometric constraints, the agent leverages its tool API to bind valid parameter values, satisfying feasibility requirements and producing an executable motion plan. Upon execution, the agent continuously updates its belief history and repeats this closed-loop process by interleaving observation and manipulation actions. Evaluated across complex household mobile manipulation tasks on Rainbow Y1 and Unitree G1 platforms, TAMPAgent significantly outperforms direct tool-calling agentic baselines, demonstrating that integrating TAMP constraint satisfaction into agentic planning loops is essential for unstructured, partially observable environments.

VerifLM: Physically Verified Image-Edit-Guided Goal Pose Generation for Robotic Manipulation

2026-11-02 cs.RO 👤 Xuanyou Chen (h=n/a)
Xuanyou Chen, Jinyu Chen, Glen Chou
Core Contributions
  • Treats goals that are easier to draw than specify: an edited image yields an initial relative pose between active and passive objects.
  • A VLM writes axis-wise sampling constraints and a task-specific validation program, and candidate 6-DoF poses are checked in a physics simulator.
  • Without meshes or physical parameters it reconstructs geometry from RGB-D and runs parallel worlds with varied physics, ranking candidates by how many worlds satisfy the program.
  • Best task success on eight of nine tasks, with the largest gain where precise orientation and contact relations matter.
Show abstract
Many manipulation goals are easier to visualize than to specify numerically through explicit geometric constraints. We present VerifLM, a framework that converts image-edited goal configurations into physically verified 6-DoF object poses for planning. Using an edited image, VerifLM recovers an initial relative pose between an active object and a passive object, then prompts a vision-language model to generate axis-wise sampling constraints and a task-specific validation program. Candidate poses are evaluated in a physics simulator, and the VLM selects the most task-consistent retained candidate. To operate without predefined object meshes or physical parameters, VerifLM reconstructs metric object geometry from RGB-D observations and constructs multiple hypothetical simulations with varying physics and geometry. Parallel-world validation evaluates candidates across geometry--physics variations and ranks them by the number of worlds in which they satisfy the validation program. We evaluate VerifLM on nine manipulation tasks. VerifLM achieves the highest task success on eight of nine tasks, with the largest performance gain on goals requiring precise orientation and contact relationships.

D&A: Decoupling Description and Assessment Enables VLMs as Zero-Shot Robotic Reward Models

2026-11-02 cs.ROcs.AIcs.CLcs.LGeess.SY 👤 Marius Memmel (h=n/a)
Marius Memmel, Shawn Yin, Urvi Rutia, Angad Wadhwa, Eldon Li
Core Contributions
  • Diagnoses why VLM progress estimators hallucinate success: implicit success assumptions or relative temporal ranking anchor the score to the video's own timeline, so failed runs still look like they progress.
  • Decouples the job into an objective text description of scene dynamics followed by scoring against an explicit rubric, forcing the model to commit to an absolute definition of success first.
  • Purely prompt-based, so it needs no robot data or per-model post-training, unlike trained reward models.
  • Matches or beats post-trained reward models across tasks, embodiments and benchmarks while penalising failed executions, and its rewards support efficient downstream RL.
Show abstract
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) hold promise for zero-shot video task-progress estimation in robot learning, yet existing frameworks frequently suffer from confirmation bias, hallucinating progress in failed executions due to implicit success assumptions or relative temporal ranking designs. Naive prompting of frontier models collapses into a binary success detector that fails to capture nuanced, non-linear progress and failures. While post-training approaches can mitigate this through explicit training, they demand expensive real-world robotics data and require computationally intensive post-training for every individual base model. To resolve these limitations, we introduce D&A (Describe & Assess): our approach decouples progress estimation into an objective, task-grounded textual description of scene dynamics followed by an assessment phase using explicit rubrics. By compelling the VLM to synthesize an explicit, text-based definition of absolute success before scoring, the framework anchors evaluations to an external, global criterion rather than the video's internal timeline. Empirical results demonstrate that our method calibrates zero-shot estimates and mitigates confirmation bias, providing fine-grained, partial progress metrics that accurately penalize failed executions while correctly interpolating successful runs. Being purely prompt-based, D&A generalizes across tasks, embodiments, and benchmarks---matching or surpassing post-trained reward models---and its reward estimates enable efficient downstream reinforcement learning.

Seeing Through the Illusion: Test-Time Scaling for World Action Models

2026-11-02 cs.RO 👤 Cheikh Ahmed (h=n/a)
Cheikh Ahmed, Mahdi Mostajabdaveh, Milad Yazdani, Zirui Zhou
Core Contributions
  • Points out that a world action model's imagined future need not be realized by the action it co-generated, so plausible videos can mislead execution.
  • WISE samples several rollouts and scores each on action validity and temporal regularity, inverse-dynamics consistency with the imagined motion, and instruction-conditioned task progress, executing the best while the WAM stays frozen.
  • With only four candidates, success on RoboLab with Cosmos 3 Edge rises from 6.0% to 24.0%, and mean LIBERO success with DreamZero from 75.1% to 78.3%.
  • Ablations show the three scores rank candidates differently and task progress carries the largest weight, so the gain comes from combining complementary checks.
Show abstract
World action models (WAMs) jointly generate actions and imagined futures, but a plausible prediction need not be realized by its co-generated action. We introduce WISE (\emph{World Action Inference Scaling through Evaluation}), a pre-execution selector that scores each sampled rollout on action validity and temporal regularity, inverse-dynamics consistency between the proposed action and the imagined motion, and instruction-conditioned task progress, and executes the highest-scoring candidate while keeping the WAM frozen. With four candidates per decision, WISE raises closed-loop success from 6.0\% to 24.0\% on RoboLab with Cosmos~3 Edge and from 75.1\% to 78.3\% mean success on LIBERO with DreamZero, and exceeds the best existing selector in aggregate by 4.0 and 0.9 points. Component ablations and agreement analyses show that the three scores rank candidates differently and that task progress carries the largest weight in the fused decision.

VLALagBench: Understanding When and Why VLA Inference Latency Matters

2026-11-02 cs.RO 👤 Qi Wen (h=n/a)
Qi Wen, Borui Xu, Hussein Amro, Hongshi Tan, Hanmin Lin
Core Contributions
  • Quantifies how VLA inference latency affects success, across task types, object speeds, sync versus async execution and policies.
  • A 100 ms delay can nearly halve success on moving targets, and asynchronous overlap recovers some at the cost of more frequent inference.
  • An empirical model predicts success from limited calibration data with RMS error of 5.7 to 7.8% across policies.
  • Predictions help choose algorithm configurations given a system's latency and per-robot throughput, supporting algorithm-system co-design.
Show abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) models offer a promising path toward general-purpose robot manipulation. While inference latency can affect task success in a constantly evolving physical world, the relationship between latency and task success rate remains poorly understood. In this paper, we introduce VLALagBench, a comprehensive benchmark for studying VLA inference latency sensitivity across task types, object speeds, execution modes (synchronous and asynchronous), and VLA policies. Using VLALagBench, we find that even a modest inference latency of 100 ms can nearly halve task success on moving targets, with sensitivity varying substantially across tasks and policies. These differences reflect task-specific requirements for precision and continuous motion. By overlapping inference with action execution, asynchronous execution can recover some of the lost success at the cost of more frequent inference requests. Building on these findings, we develop an empirical model that predicts task success from limited calibration data, achieving root-mean-square prediction errors of only 5.7-7.8% across various VLA policies. Given an inference system's latency and per-robot throughput, these predictions guide the selection of system-specific VLA algorithm configurations to maximize task success. We believe VLALagBench and these findings provide a solid foundation for future robotics algorithm-system co-design.

Fine-grained manipulation couples instruction grounding, local visual precision, and sustained execution. We present FineCraft, a framework that adapts pretrained models for fine-grained manipulation in the MetaFine Challenge. Toward Uncertainty-Aware, Physics-Grounded Human World Models

2026-11-02 cs.RO 👤 Yufei Zhang (h=n/a)
Yufei Zhang, Zijun Cui
Core Contributions
  • A position-style abstract with no new experiments, arguing that two published models are the two halves of a human world model for robots: a belief over body state and a dynamics model.
  • KNOWN estimates aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty in 11.6 ms per frame, and epistemic-driven refinement cuts error on out-of-distribution inputs from 81.3 to 70.3 mm.
  • PhysMoP solves Euler-Lagrange equations with learned terms, reducing 80 ms prediction error from 9.6 to 2.1 mm on Human3.6M and from 12.1 to 0.7 mm on 3DPW.
  • Defines the interface and three mechanisms for injecting KNOWN's uncertainty into PhysMoP, plus an evaluation protocol on in-the-wild video.
Show abstract
A robot that acts around people needs a world model of the human: a belief over the person's body state and a dynamics model that predicts where that state goes next. This abstract argues that two published components form exactly these two halves, and specifies how to couple them. KNOWN [1] reconstructs the 3D human body while estimating both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty in real time (11.6 ms per frame); its aleatoric term identifies unreliable observations, its epistemic term identifies inputs from low-density regions of the training data, and refinement driven by the epistemic term reduces the error on such inputs from 81.3 to 70.3 mm. PhysMoP [2] predicts future body configuration by solving the Euler-Lagrange equations of the body with learned dynamics terms, reducing the 80 ms prediction error from 9.6 to 2.1 mm on Human3.6M and from 12.1 to 0.7 mm on 3DPW. We define the interface between the two, propose three mechanisms by which KNOWN's uncertainty enters PhysMoP, and specify an evaluation protocol on in-the-wild video. No new experimental results are reported here.

PanoTac: A Dense Three-Axis Tactile Sensor for Robust Touch-Centric Manipulation

2026-11-02 cs.ROcs.AIcs.LG 👤 Binglin (h=n/a)
Binglin, Wang, Xiaole, Jiang, Zicheng Teng
Core Contributions
  • Targets a gap between cheap tactile sensors that give only coarse force and rich ones that are costly: a compliant fingertip yields a dense 12x32 map with normal plus two shear components per taxel at 60 Hz for under $50.
  • Characterises repeatability across sensor instances and contact locations, which is rarely reported yet decides whether policies transfer between fingertips.
  • In test-tube preparation, grain scooping and salt pouring the visuo-tactile policies beat vision-only baselines by 46.7 points on average.
  • Ablations show removing either spatial contact information or shear feedback hurts, so both dense geometry and force direction matter.
Show abstract
Contact-rich manipulation benefits from tactile feedback that captures both detailed contact geometry and interaction forces. When scooping grains, contact patterns help infer spoon orientation, while resistance transmitted through the grasp guides motion. To provide these complementary sensing capabilities, we present PanoTac, a low-cost, compliant tactile fingertip designed for dense three-axis tactile sensing. Each fingertip provides a $12 \times 32 \times 3$ tactile representation, with one normal ($z$) and two shear ($x$, $y$) response components at each taxel. The sensor operates at 60 Hz and costs less than $50 per fingertip. Sensor characterization quantifies response repeatability and consistency across sensor instances and contact locations. We integrate PanoTac into a robotic system with a visuo-tactile pipeline. Across test-tube preparation, grain scooping, and salt pouring, the resulting policies outperform vision-only baselines by an average of 46.7 percentage points. Ablations show that removing either spatial contact information or shear feedback reduces overall performance across the task suite. Our project page is available at https://panotac.github.io.

Exploring Proximity and Tactile Sensing for Robotic Manipulation

2026-11-02 cs.RO 👤 Eric T. Chang (h=n/a)
Eric T. Chang, Bianca Aumann, Zhanpeng He, Mohamed Elnoor, Thomas Liang
Core Contributions
  • Argues pre-touch sensing lets a hand anticipate contact, and combines three 4x4 multizone time-of-flight sensors with an optical pressure array in one gripper-mounted sensor.
  • Anticipatory closure during approach cuts pre-grasp cycle time by 7.4%.
  • For extraction from clutter, ToF feedback gave successful retrieval over plus or minus 2 cm depth offsets, with standard deviation under 0.4 cm, where baselines failed.
  • Insertion into tight clutter is explored qualitatively, showing what proximity plus pressure reveals along the trajectory.
Show abstract
Pre-touch sensing, or sensing information about an object before contact, is an attractive modality for robot hands as it can allow a robot to anticipate and respond to a contact before it happens. In this work, we introduce ToFTouch, a multimodal tactile sensor combining three multizone (4$\times$4 resolution) time-of-flight sensors with an optics-based pressure sensing array. We mount this sensor on a parallel jaw gripper and demonstrate its utility on three distinct tasks: (i) pre-grasp adjustment, (ii) item extraction from clutter, and (iii) item insertion into tight spaces. For pre-grasp adjustment, ToF feedback reduced cycle time by 7.4$\%$ by enabling anticipatory gripper closure during approach. For extraction, ToF sensing enabled successful retrieval across all tested depth offsets ($\pm$2 cm) deviating from the nominal pick point, including conditions where baseline approaches failed, with standard deviations below 0.4 cm. For insertion into dense clutter, we explore the proximity and tactile information provided from ToFTouch during an insertion trajectory. The results demonstrate the utility of multizone ToF sensing in a manipulation environment.

ForceLDM: A Force-aware Latent Dynamics Model for Contact-Rich Manipulation

2026-11-02 cs.RO 👤 Peiyuan Tang (h=n/a)
Peiyuan Tang, Yao Li, Xiaodong Zhang, Wuyang Zhang, Qin Xia
Core Contributions
  • Observes that force and tactile signals are usually extra observations rather than tools for modelling future interaction dynamics.
  • A teacher with privileged future force/torque and optical flow is distilled into a student that sees only current observations, avoiding the need to predict raw future sensor data that entangles irrelevant detail.
  • Curriculum-based progressive noise injection stops the policy from over-relying on the distilled future features.
  • Beats state-of-the-art baselines on five real-world contact-rich tasks, generalises to novel objects and stays robust to perturbations.
Show abstract
Contact-rich manipulation requires fine-grained control and complex physical interactions with the environment, posing significant challenges for robot policy learning. Vision-only policies struggle with such tasks, as contact states and interaction forces are difficult to infer from visual observations alone. Existing methods mitigate this issue by incorporating force or tactile sensing. However, they often treat these signals as auxiliary observations without fully exploiting their role in modeling future interaction dynamics. To address this, we propose ForceLDM, a force-aware latent dynamics model that learns future contact and motion dynamics to guide action generation. Rather than directly predicting future observations, which can entangle task-relevant dynamics with irrelevant sensory details, ForceLDM learns a compact representation of future dynamics through knowledge distillation. Specifically, a teacher network with privileged future force/torque and optical flow signals extracts future dynamic features, which are then distilled into a student network trained only on current observations. This enables the student model to reason about upcoming contacts and physical dynamics without relying on future sensory inputs at deployment. To improve robustness and generalization, we further introduce a curriculum-based progressive noise injection strategy to mitigate over-reliance on future features. Experiments on five real-world contact-rich manipulation tasks demonstrate that ForceLDM outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, generalizes effectively to novel objects, and remains robust to environmental perturbations. Project page and code are available at https://tpy001.github.io/ForceLDM/.

When Robots Outweigh Objects: How Inertia Mismatch Shapes Robot Policy Learning

2026-11-02 cs.RO 👤 Younghyo Park (h=n/a)
Younghyo Park, Antonia Bronars, Pulkit Agrawal
Core Contributions
  • Asks whether heavy arms impose a hidden learning cost, since they can knock light objects out of place and learning has so far masked the problem with data.
  • Controlled simulation shows RL succeeds across mass combinations, whereas behavior cloning does better when a lighter robot moves a heavier object, and more data narrows but does not close the gap.
  • Behavior cloning also degrades when motor gearing reflects more inertia even at equal weight, linking actuator design to imitation quality.
  • Offers fixes for existing hardware: pick a lower-inertia workspace or steer demonstrators to lower-inertia solutions, both of which make demonstrations easier to imitate.
Show abstract
Conventional robot arms can be extremely heavy compared with the everyday objects they manipulate, creating a significant inertia mismatch at contact. Even a small accidental bump from a heavy arm can knock a light object out of place. Modern learning methods have nevertheless demonstrated a wide range of successful manipulation behaviors on these platforms, suggesting that even extreme inertia mismatch can be overcome with sufficient data. However, it remains unclear whether high inertia imposes a hidden cost on learning, potentially worsening scaling with data or lowering the performance ceiling of modern robot learning methods. We investigate this question through controlled simulation experiments that vary robot and object masses. We find that reinforcement learning succeeds across a broad range of mass combinations, while behavior cloning learns more reliable policies when a lighter robot manipulates a heavier object, and more data narrows the resulting gap without closing it. Behavior cloning likewise degrades on a simulated robot arm as its motors reflect more inertia through their gearing, even though its weight stays the same. Finally, because new hardware is rarely an option, we explore ways to lower contact inertia on the arm one already has, either by choosing a lower-inertia workspace before collecting data or by steering the demonstrator toward lower-inertia solutions of the task. In both simulated cases, the lower-inertia demonstrations are imitated more reliably. Project website: https://younghyopark.me/inertia-mismatch/

The framework is designed as an easy-to-use tool requiring only a small number of intuitive user-defined parameters to automatically generate large sets of distinct navigation scenarios satisfying prescribed geometric and kinematic constraints. By relying on explicit, seed-controlled algorithmic procedures rather than data-driven models, the generated scenarios remain transparent, reproducible, and configurable, making the framework well suited for telepresence and human-subject experiments. The code is available at https://github.com/JucciK/Trails .
