🔭 Research Landscape

The dominant theme today is policies that improve themselves after pretraining. Reinforcement Learning Enables Scaling Generalist Robot Policy Improvement (#3) shows that a multi-task base policy can be improved online across many tasks at once, and that value-based RL plus a diverse training task distribution is what lets gains spill over to tasks never trained on. Compounding Physical RSI (#13) attacks the same problem from the failure side: it localizes which stage of a task broke, fixes it with behavior-regularized offline RL, and then interpolates weights with the previous generation to avoid forgetting, topping the 42-task RoboDojo-Sim leaderboard. PyRUA-REAL (#7) pursues the code-as-policy version of recursive self-improvement on real robots (91.1% over eight tasks), while Rethinking Self-Repair (#5) is a useful cold shower: with Qwen2.5-Coder-7B, template feedback recovers 85.3% of failures versus 68.3% for plain resampling, but generic static guidance does about as well, so much of the apparent value of failure feedback may be extra sampling and task hints.

A second thread is that evaluation and infrastructure are becoming first-class research objects. VABAR (#4), RoboCasa-Mem (#21), and the failure-boundary profiles of #22 each argue that a single success or driving score hides how agents actually fail, whether that is brittle code policies, missing memory, or an agent that never stops at a blocked corridor. Where Is VLA Inference Time Spent? (#9) does the same for deployment cost, showing that optimizations such as CUDA graphs, pruning and quantization interact differently across seven VLAs and four GPU families. On the simulation side, Cosmos interactive world simulation (#16) and TwinBridge (#24) show two ways to make simulators useful for training: learned action-conditioned video, or Gaussian-splat digital twins that supply policy initialization and dense rewards, reaching 98% success in at most 25 real minutes per task.

Finally, the physical-embodiment papers show a pragmatic turn toward structure rather than scale alone. HALO (#2) synthesizes vision-language-motion data across a thousand procedural homes and transfers zero-shot at 91% in unseen real homes, soccerbot (#19) constrains RL inside a latent human-motion manifold, and cuRoboV2-Fast (#1) plans torque-limited motions in 4.4 ms. Tactile and contact work (#12, #15) similarly asks which physical signal matters, rather than adding more sensors, and the safety papers (#6, #28, #30) wrap learned components in conservative guarantees. The picture is a field where learning supplies the capability and carefully chosen structure, evaluation and safety margins decide whether it can be trusted.

Reinforcement Learning Enables Scaling Generalist Robot Policy Improvement

2026-11-04 cs.LGcs.RO 👤 Michal Korniak (h=n/a)
Michal Korniak, Marco Bagatella, Ralf Römer, Michal Nauman, Yarden As
Core Contributions
  • Asks a question prior single-task RL work skips: can a multi-task generalist policy be improved online on many tasks simultaneously, instead of overfitting to one deployment task?
  • Builds structured evaluations and systematically ablates value estimation, policy extraction and resource allocation, producing a concrete recipe instead of a one-off result.
  • Finds that improvement generalizes to tasks not seen in online training, but only when the training task distribution is diverse and value-based RL is used, a useful guide for how to collect deployment experience.
  • Presented as among the first evidence of online generalist improvement for robot foundation models, shifting the adaptation question from per-task fine-tuning to fleet-wide improvement.
Show abstract
The introduction of generalist robot policies has enabled a single, large model to learn to perform a variety of tasks from a vast pre-training corpus. While these models often exhibit powerful zero-shot generalization capabilities, they typically require an adaptation phase to achieve sufficient performance in deployment. Iterated self-improvement via reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated promise in enabling this adaptation, yet such approaches have been largely explored in single-task settings - where the goal is to simply improve on a narrow deployment task - which lies in stark contrast to the multi-task capabilities of the base policy. In this work we ask whether generalist robot policies are capable of multi-task, generalist improvement in deployment - targeting many diverse tasks simultaneously - and what algorithmic recipe enables this. To answer this question, we first develop a set of structured evaluations to test generalist improvement capabilities, and systematically evaluate algorithmic and practical design choices regarding value estimation, policy extraction and resource allocation. We show that not only can generalist policies be improved online across diverse tasks, but that the right recipe can enable generalization and increase performance on tasks not considered during online training. We characterize when this generalization occurs and show that both the training task distribution and the use of value-based RL are critical. To the best of our knowledge, this is among the first evidence for online generalist improvement for robot foundation models.

Compounding Physical RSI: Turning Execution Failure into Inherited Capability

2026-11-04 cs.RO 👤 Yixuan Yang (h=n/a)
Yixuan Yang, Qiang Chen, Run Shao, Shuwei Dong, Wenhao Li
Core Contributions
  • Exploits that manipulation success is roughly a product of stage pass rates, so fixing one bottleneck reveals the next and gains can compound across generations.
  • Automated capability-gap assessment traces failed rollouts to the specific stage where progress broke, solving the credit-assignment problem terminal success signals leave open.
  • The remedy stage refines the policy with behavior-regularized offline RL on curated stage-relevant experience, avoiding costly online rollouts at every step.
  • A retention stage interpolates weights between reference and refined policy, evaluates candidates across the suite and promotes the best, guarding against forgetting; it ranks first on RoboDojo-Sim (42 tasks) with overall score 26.15 and 20.70% success, 5.82 points ahead.
Show abstract
Generalist robot policies are trained on static offline datasets, yet physical manipulation unfolds sequentially across stages. Because a weakness in a downstream stage remains unobservable until the policy passes earlier ones, each resolved bottleneck exposes the next hidden failure mode. This staged structure calls for Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI), in which a policy iteratively executes, diagnoses failures, and updates itself. Moreover, because task completion behaves like a product of stage-level pass rates, recursive gains can multiply across generations. In physical environments, three bottlenecks obstruct compounding: terminal execution feedback rarely localizes the stage that caused a failure, failed trajectories do not supply the corrective action, and updating shared parameters can degrade previously mastered skills. We propose Compounding Physical RSI, a self-evolving policy framework that turns execution failures into heritable capability. First, automated capability-gap assessment traces failed rollouts to the task stage where progression breaks down and formulates a structured improvement objective. Second, conditioned on this objective, our remedy stage curates validated, stage-relevant experience and refines the policy via behavior-regularized offline reinforcement learning, without requiring costly online rollouts at each optimization step. Third, to safeguard existing skills, our retention stage constructs weight-space candidates between the inherited reference policy and the refined policy, evaluates them across the task suite, and promotes the best candidate as the reference state for the next generation. Evaluated across all 42 tasks of the RoboDojo-Sim benchmark, CSU-AI-07 ranks first on the leaderboard with a three-seed mean Overall Score of 26.15 and Success Rate of 20.70%, outperforming the next-best entry by 5.92 points and 5.82 percentage points.

PyRUA-REAL: Robot Exploration and Adaptive Learning through RSI in the Real World

2026-11-04 cs.RO 👤 Qiang Wang (h=n/a)
Qiang Wang, Jianxin Bi, Shulong Jiang, Ruiyang Si, Wenbo Huang
Core Contributions
  • Treats embodied recursive self-improvement as a loop where coding agents explore physically, refine code from execution feedback, and store experience as persistent memory and reusable executable skills.
  • Studies what makes the loop work: how agents collaborate, which execution feedback helps, and how task specs and earlier skills affect robustness and transfer.
  • Resulting programs are deployed without an online agent in the loop, so the gain is efficient standalone skills rather than ongoing LLM calls.
  • Reports 91.1% average success on eight real tasks and 84.4% on 42 RoboDojo tasks.
Show abstract
Coding agents powered by models such as GPT-6-Astra connect spatial reasoning with executable robot behavior, raising the question of how such agents can produce lasting improvements in robot capabilities from physical interaction. In this work, we study embodied recursive self-improvement (RSI) through real-world exploration, code refinement, and deployment using PyRUA-REAL, a framework in which coding agents retain interaction experience as persistent memory and reusable executable skills that inform subsequent exploration and improvement. Our empirical study places physical manipulation at the center of the improvement process, examining how agents collaborate, what kinds of execution feedback support effective code refinement, and how task specifications and previously acquired skills influence robustness and task transfer. We further investigate the efficiency and capability of the resulting programs when deployed without involving an online agent for decision-making. Across eight real-world tasks, PyRUA-REAL achieves an average success rate of 91.1%. In complementary evaluation on 42 tasks from the RoboDojo benchmark, it achieves an average success rate of 84.4%. This study provides empirical guidance for turning physical exploration into reusable robot skills that support continued improvement and robust, efficient deployment.

Rethinking Self-Repair in Code-as-Policy Agents: What Drives Retry Success?

2026-11-04 cs.RO 👤 Siddharth Aphale (h=n/a)
Siddharth Aphale, Ayushman Singh
Core Contributions
  • Separates three effects usually bundled together in retry loops: failure-specific feedback, extra sampling and task guidance.
  • On 252 identical initial failures, Qwen2.5-Coder-7B recovers 85.3% in five retries with template feedback versus 68.3% by plain resampling, and the gap persists under equal token budgets.
  • Yet feedback from other episodes and static task guidance give similar recovery, and matching the coarse failure label shows no detected benefit, which questions the claim that the specific diagnosis is what helps.
  • Template feedback even hurts a nut-insertion task needing a rim grasp because its instruction assumes a center grasp, so self-repair studies should include resampling, mismatched feedback and concise static guidance as baselines.
Show abstract
Code-as-policy agents often use execution feedback to retry failed programs. Additional sampling and task guidance can also improve success, leaving the contribution of failure-specific feedback unclear. We study this question in simulated robot manipulation. A fixed lookup selects feedback templates using failure labels and the API variant. A frozen code model generates a fresh program after each scene reset. On the same 252 initial failures, Qwen2.5-Coder-7B recovers 85.3% within five retries, compared with 68.3% for resampling with the same retry cap. The advantage persists under fixed total-token budgets. Feedback from other episodes and fixed task guidance yield similar recovery point estimates, with no detected benefit from matching the coarse failure label. In a separate study, a fixed guided prompt used from the first attempt achieves an observed overall success rate similar to that of template-based feedback. Template-based feedback also lowers recovery on a nut-insertion task requiring a rim grasp, consistent with an incompatible center-grasp instruction. Evaluations of robot self-repair should include resampling, mismatched feedback, and concise, task-compatible static guidance as baselines.

$A^3$-VLA: Automatic Perception-Guided Attention Alignment for Robot Manipulation

2026-11-04 cs.RO 👤 Shengzhe Zhang (h=n/a)
Shengzhe Zhang, Qi Zhang, Dazhong Shen, Chao Wang, Shaopeng Zhai
Core Contributions
  • Argues that the action-prediction loss gives no fine-grained perceptual supervision, so VLAs latch onto task-irrelevant visual shortcuts instead of target objects and interaction regions.
  • Derives guidance from the manipulation itself: interaction-induced visual changes give candidate target and robot regions, refined offline by a segmentation model, with no manual annotation.
  • Two losses use these regions: an attention allocation loss for language or robot-state queries, and a contrastive loss aligning target regions with instructions and separating distractors.
  • Improves several VLA backbones with average success gains of 6.5% to 25.2% across simulated and real benchmarks.
Show abstract
Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs) enable robots to perform manipulation tasks by leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to process visual observations and language instructions. However, the action prediction objective itself lacks fine-grained task-specific perceptual supervision, making it prone to relying on task-irrelevant visual shortcuts, leading attention away from target objects and key interaction areas, thereby limiting its operational performance and generalization ability. To address this, we propose Automatic Perception-Guided Attention Alignment for Robot Manipulation ($A^3$-VLA), a framework that derives task-specific visual guidance from the manipulation process itself. Specifically, $A^3$-VLA first generates heuristic candidate regions for target objects and the robot from interaction-induced visual changes, and refines them with a visual segmentation model offline. These regions serve as explicit perceptual guidance through two complementary objectives: an attention allocation loss that encourages sufficient focus on task-critical regions when language or robot state tokens query the visual input, and a contrastive learning loss that aligns the representations of candidate target regions with language instructions while separating them from task-irrelevant regions. Extensive experiments on simulated and real-world manipulation tasks demonstrate that $A^3$-VLA consistently improves multiple VLA backbones, with an average success rate gain of 6.5% - 25.2% across benchmarks.

Where Is VLA Inference Time Spent? Bottlenecks and Optimization Interactions Across GPUs

2026-11-04 cs.RO 👤 Muhammad Danish Waseem (h=n/a)
Muhammad Danish Waseem, Ahmed Ali-Eldin Hassan
Core Contributions
  • Fills a gap in VLA efficiency research, where optimizations are usually reported on one GPU and a few models, by measuring seven optimization families on seven VLAs across four GPU families from H100 to Jetson Thor.
  • Decomposes latency into CPU launch overhead, memory bandwidth and kernel efficiency, explaining why the same technique helps on one device and barely on another.
  • Shows that optimizations interact: applying one changes how much another gains, so stacking them naively can disappoint.
  • Distills this into VLA-Triage guidelines for stacking optimizations under a latency budget, including which combinations need testing in the deployed execution mode.
Show abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) models map camera observations and language instructions to robot actions. However, their large backbones and repeated action generation can make inference take longer than the robot's real-time requirements for its next action. To reduce the load of VLA computations, researchers have proposed multiple optimization approaches, including quantization and pruning. These optimizations can be combined with other general optimizations such as better kernel compilation or using CUDA graphs instead of the eager mode. However, most of these optimization results are usually reported on a single GPU type, typically for a few VLA models, with no analysis of what the real bottlenecks are for a model on a particular hardware or how these optimizations would generalize beyond the reported results. In this paper, we provide a comprehensive measurement study of seven families of inference optimization methods, from CUDA graphs and compilation to token pruning and quantization, on seven state-of-the-art VLAs evaluated on four GPU families ranging from a datacenter H100 to an embedded Jetson Thor. We analyse how CPU launch overhead, memory bandwidth, and kernel efficiency jointly shape optimization gains, and how applying one method changes the benefit of another. From this analysis, we derive VLA-Triage, guidelines for stacking optimizations under latency budgets. The guidelines identify which stages can provide the required savings and which combinations need testing in the deployed execution mode.

Detecting Silent Action-Interface Mismatch with Calibrated Relational World Models

2026-11-04 cs.ROcs.LG 👤 Rui Zhu (h=n/a)
Rui Zhu, Xurui Li, Haixu Tang
Core Contributions
  • Targets a quiet failure mode: actions that pass shape and range checks but have wrong semantics such as permuted axes, flipped signs, rescaled rotations or reversed gripper polarity.
  • Learns state-action-effect relations from clean data as offline contracts, calibrating alarms at a 2.5% clean false-positive rate.
  • On ten LIBERO Spatial tasks, a joint Gaussian mixture reaches 61.0% recall versus 46.1% for a ridge forward model, and its exact conditional reaches 65.7%, though joint scoring localizes the faulty field slightly better (95.8% vs 94.6%).
  • Candid about limits: DROID, HYDRA tasks, small offsets and short temporal faults fail the gates, so it is a bounded offline diagnostic rather than cross-robot safety.
Show abstract
A robot action can satisfy shape and range constraints yet carry the wrong semantics: axes may be permuted, signs inverted, rotations rescaled, or gripper polarity reversed. We study learned state-action-effect relations as offline contracts for detecting these mismatches and ranking their likely fields. Clean held-out transitions calibrate alarms; a fixed inverse vocabulary supports closed-set localization. On ten LIBERO Spatial tasks comprising 432 source episodes, a capacity-controlled comparison at 2.5% empirical clean false-positive rate gives 46.1% recall for a ridge forward residual, 61.0% for a four-component joint Gaussian mixture, and 65.7% for the exact conditional of that same mixture. The joint score retains a 14.9 percentage-point advantage over ridge, while conditioning improves recall by a further 4.7 points. Joint scoring instead has higher localization: 95.8% versus 94.6%. These matched-rate comparisons are retrospective diagnostics, not deployment calibration guarantees. Earlier Object/Goal results broaden tasks within the LIBERO convention; DROID, all three HYDRA tasks, short temporal faults, small offsets, exact repair, and simulated mitigation fail their respective gates, while xArm contributes one task only. The evidence supports a bounded offline diagnostic and a detection-localization tradeoff, rather than joint-density superiority or cross-robot safety.

Humanoid robots have the potential to automate a wide range of physical tasks. H^2MARL: Hierarchical Heterogeneous Multi-agent Reinforcement Learning for Humanoid Robot Control

2026-11-04 cs.RO 👤 Xuemin Hu (h=n/a)
Xuemin Hu, Kaixin Li, Qingqing Xia, Yuxin Wang, Wei Xie
Core Contributions
  • Treats each torque-actuated joint DoF as an agent conditioned on a descriptor of its actuation, kinematic and dynamic properties, rather than using coarse limb-level partitions or generic heterogeneous-agent algorithms.
  • A closed-loop policy-executor co-optimization jointly adapts the high-level pose planner and low-level joint executor on shared trajectories, reducing the planner-executor distribution mismatch of independently trained hierarchies.
  • In MuJoCo and Isaac Gym it improves locomotion stability, gait coordination and motion quality over homogeneous, coarse-grained and independently optimized variants.
Show abstract
Coordinated whole-body control of humanoid robots remains challenging due to high-dimensional coupled dynamics and substantial physical heterogeneity across joint degrees of freedom (JDoFs). Existing multi-agent reinforcement learning methods either employ coarse agent partitions or directly apply generic heterogeneous-agent algorithms, while hierarchical controllers commonly optimize high-level planning and low-level execution independently, leading to uncoordinated motion. In this paper, we propose H^2MARL, a hierarchical heterogeneous multi-agent reinforcement learning framework with two key mechanisms. We first formulate humanoid whole-body control using a JDoF-level physical-heterogeneity-aware policy factorization, where each torque-actuated JDoF is conditioned on an explicit descriptor of its actuation, kinematic, and dynamic properties. Moreover, we develop a closed-loop policy-executor co-optimization mechanism that jointly adapts the high-level pose planner and the low-level joint executor using shared interaction trajectories, thereby reducing the distribution mismatch between planning and execution. Extensive experiments in MuJoCo and Isaac Gym demonstrate that H^2MARL consistently improves locomotion stability, gait coordination, and motion quality over homogeneous, coarse-grained, and independently optimized alternatives.

cuRoboV2-Fast: Fast Torque-Limited Collision-Free Motion Planning

2026-11-04 cs.RO 👤 Balakumar Sundaralingam (h=n/a)
Balakumar Sundaralingam, Adithyavairavan Murali, Stan Birchfield
Core Contributions
  • Unlike cuRoboV2, which paid for torque limits and payload awareness with slow refinement, this replaces trajectory parameterization with B-splines and adds differentiable inverse dynamics, so fewer iterations are needed and payload changes are handled directly.
  • TimeOpt retimes motion along a fixed path under sampled velocity, acceleration, jerk and torque limits, and uses local spline support to turn the problem into banded Newton systems that map well to GPUs.
  • With a 3 kg payload it plans in 4.4 ms on an RTX 5090 (14.7x faster than cuRoboV2), and on a Jetson AGX Orin it still beats cuRoboV1 + TOPPRA run on the desktop GPU, which matters for onboard planning.

Show abstract
We present cuRoboV2-Fast, a GPU-accelerated planner that reduces both planning latency and execution time for collision-free, payload-aware, torque-limited motions. B-spline trajectory optimization reduces the required iterations, while accelerated robot computations and differentiable inverse dynamics reduce refinement cost and accommodate changing payloads. TimeOpt optimizes traversal speed along the fixed path under sampled velocity, acceleration, jerk, and torque constraints, exploiting local spline support to solve banded Newton systems efficiently on the GPU. With a 3,kg payload, cuRoboV2-Fast achieves 4.4 ms mean planning time on RTX 5090, 14.7x faster than cuRoboV2. Our method on Jetson AGX Orin plans faster than cuRoboV1 + TOPPRA on RTX 5090. A stereo-camera-based deployment on YAM achieves 5.93 s mean completed pick-and-drop cycles, 34.6% shorter than cuRoboV2. ConDR: Conservative Reachability through Neural Hamilton-Jacobi Subsolutions

2026-11-04 cs.ROcs.LGeess.SY 👤 Kendra Givens (h=n/a)
Kendra Givens, Taylor T. Johnson, Bryan C. Ward, Thomas Beckers
Core Contributions
  • DeepReach-style neural HJ reachability penalizes the residual symmetrically, so the learned value can sit above the true one and label unsafe states safe; ConDR uses a one-sided loss that pushes the residual to a margin epsilon below zero.
  • Enforces the obstacle branch and terminal condition by construction and blocks the degenerate 'everything unsafe' solution, so conservatism is not obtained trivially.
  • Proves the margin-shifted solution is a subsolution whose unsafe set contains the true one, with value within epsilon times tau of the true value, giving a formal safety direction that earlier learned reachability lacked.
  • On Air3D, Air6D, a 9D multi-vehicle system and a 13D quadrotor it has the lowest misclassification and collision rates, and at matched false-negative rates its unsafe sets are smaller than baselines'.
Show abstract
Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) Reachability identifies the unsafe states of a dynamical system from which it can be forced into failure under worst-case disturbance. However, classical grid-based solvers scale exponentially with state dimension. DeepReach improves scalability by replacing the grid with a neural value function trained on the HJ-Isaacs (HJI) variational inequality (VI), but penalizes both sides of the residual equally. This allows the learned value to lie above the true one and unsafe states to be misclassified as safe. We propose Conservative DeepReach (ConDR) which aims to learn a subsolution of the HJI VI such that the learned unsafe set contains the true one. ConDR enforces the obstacle branch and terminal condition by construction, pushes the HJI residual to a safety margin $ε$ below zero with a one-sided loss, and prevents the degenerate solution of classifying everything as unsafe. We prove that the exact solution of the margin-shifted VI that ConDR trains toward is a subsolution of the original VI so its unsafe set contains the true one and its value lies within $ετ$ of the true value function. We compare against DeepReach and MADR in the established systems of Air3D, Air6D, a 9D multi-vehicle system, and a 13D quadrotor. ConDR reports the lowest rate of unsafe state misclassified and the lowest closed-loop collision rates with at most a small increase in intervention. On Air3D and Air6D, ConDR tolerates model mismatch that scales with $ε$. At matched false-negative rates, ConDR's unsafe sets are smaller than the baselines showing the safety is not obtained by trivial over-approximation.

Dynamic Shape Control of Soft Trunk Robots

2026-11-04 cs.RO 👤 Hehui Zheng (h=n/a)
Hehui Zheng, Marc Stampanoni, Roshan S. Kaundinya, Ronan Hinchet, George Haller
Core Contributions

Dexterous robotic hands are increasingly coupled to large-scale human data and foundation models, yet progress in hardware, representation learning and evaluation is often assessed separately. This survey reviews work from January 2024 to early November 2026, with earlier studies included to establish technical lineages. We organize the literature into five interdependent layers: hardware; data collection and curation; cross-embodiment transfer; learning and foundation models; and evaluation and applications. Human hand-object understanding and tactile sensing provide two cross-cutting perspectives. A system-level architecture, comparative tables and five case analyses connect design choices across these layers. We distinguish within-study experimental comparisons from heterogeneous corpus statistics, vendor specifications and company-reported results. Three conclusions emerge. First, the value of human data depends on kinematic, visual and contact alignment, not recording volume alone. Second, shared action representations and foundation policies do not remove embodiment-specific constraints on force, latency and contact. Third, task completion is insufficient to establish physical correctness or deployment readiness. Evidence remains limited for zero-shot transfer of contact-rich skills between morphologically different hands, long-duration reliability, and cross-sensor tactile generalization. We identify eleven open problems and propose reporting interfaces and controlled comparisons that can make full-stack progress more measurable. The review is a structured narrative synthesis rather than a pooled performance ranking or an exhaustive systematic review.
