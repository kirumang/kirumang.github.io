🔭 Research Landscape

Today's batch is dominated by a single question: how do we make Vision-Language-Action models trustworthy and cheap after pre-training? Seven papers attack different layers of it. OopsieRecover (#1) shows that a handful of corrective demonstrations can generalize if corrections are stored as semantic, intent-level costs rather than raw observation-action pairs; VLA-Tug (#4) tackles the complementary runtime side with a calibrated latent probe that predicts failures early and names the failure mode; T3R (#5) cuts the compute side by pruning 85% of scene tokens without retraining; and SLA (#9) and the shape-teacher paper (#18) both inject 3D geometry through training-only alignment losses, leaving the deployed inference graph untouched. That shared design pattern - auxiliary supervision that is discarded at test time - also appears in NegWAM (#26), STEP (#28) and La-JEPA (#21), where future prediction is trained but skipped at inference.

A second thread is a quiet challenge to the 'bigger generative backbone' default. La-JEPA (#21) trains a compact policy from scratch in V-JEPA feature space and beats far larger policies, arguing that architectures should be designed for control rather than inherited from generation. Latent RL Priors (#2) similarly shows that a flow-matching decoder distilled from privileged RL, frozen and driven by a thin visual interface, beats full fine-tuning of pi0.5 and GR00T with 5-10x fewer demonstrations. GPWM (#15) and VFlash (#3) push the same idea on the representation side: model physics directly as 3D particle motion, or decode whole visual plans in one pass, instead of generating pixels or one token at a time. Where world models were once video generators, they are increasingly structured, control-oriented latent or geometric objects.

The third, more reflective theme is measurement. RoboARCade (#20) benchmarks LLM agents as robot policies and finds they claim success on 24-39% of subtasks they actually failed; the criteria-exposure study (#16) shows a coding agent that sees the verifier gains on it but not on slightly rephrased criteria; the silent-fault paper (#22) shows learning-curve checks catch at most one in ten injected environment-boundary bugs; and USB Gym (#14) honestly reports that stitching together 20 published components still closes only 16% of the oracle gap. Together with VisionSIM (#11) exposing the failure modes idealized sensor simulators hide, the batch suggests the field's evaluation infrastructure is now the bottleneck, and that results should be read with the verifier in mind. Meanwhile humanoids (#6, #28, #30) are moving from solo locomotion toward perceptive and socially responsive behavior, and multi-robot work (#8, #13, #24, #25, #29) is converging on compact, shareable summaries - latents, value functions, port-to-port indices - as the currency of cooperation.

OopsieRecover: Learning Transferable VLA Corrections from Semantic Interventions

2026-11-05 cs.RO 👤 Arpit Bahety (h=n/a)
Arpit Bahety, Skand Peri, Sriniket Ambatipudi, Chen Xu, Haruki Nishimura
Core Contributions
  • Instead of binding a correction to the exact observation where it was demonstrated (as DAgger-style fine-tuning does), it abduces the intent behind a few corrective demos and stores it as dense spatial representations plus conditional costs - so the correction follows the semantics, not the pixels.
  • The costs mark when a behavior is undesirable and push the VLA toward better action regions, which is why a handful of demos transfers to new objects, poses and scenes without fresh corrective data per variation.
  • Evaluated over 6 tasks with object-pose, scene and related-task shifts; it targets the practical pain that DAgger-like recovery needs tens to hundreds of interactions and still does not generalize.
Show abstract
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models can perform a wide range of robotic manipulation tasks, but often fail during deployment. Teaching them to recover from these failures requires substantial corrective data: existing approaches learn to recover from failures by directly associating corrective actions with the observations in which they were demonstrated. For instance, DAgger-like fine-tuning can require tens to hundreds of interactions, yet the resulting policy may still fail to generalize the learned corrections beyond the demonstrated scenarios. We hypothesize that semantics can capture the underlying reasons for failures and their corrections, enabling policies to learn from only a handful of corrective demonstrations and generalize those corrections to novel objects, and scene variations. We propose OopsieRecover, a framework that uses abduction to infer the intent behind a small set of corrective demonstrations and encodes the inferred knowledge as dense spatial representations and conditional costs. These costs capture when a behavior is undesirable and steer the policy toward more desirable action regions. We experiment across 6 tasks and variations in object poses, scenes, and related tasks, and find that only a handful of corrective demonstrations can enable a VLA to overcome failures across a broad range of novel situations, without requiring new corrective data for each variation. Please refer to https://robin-lab.cs.utexas.edu/oopsierecover for videos.

Failure Prediction and Classification in Vision-Language-Action Models Via Calibrated Latent Probing

2026-11-05 cs.ROcs.AI 👤 Michal P. Podolinsky (h=n/a)
Michal P. Podolinsky, Yunhao Yang, Daniel Little, Hong Lu, Rohan Siva
Core Contributions
  • VLA-Tug is a lightweight probe over a VLA's latent layers that acts as a runtime monitor with statistical guarantees: it bounds missed-detection and false-alarm rates rather than just emitting a score.
  • Supervising on the pre-failure segment of trajectories lifts early detection at matched false-positive rate by up to 30% over the strongest learned baseline - the point is warning before the failure, not labeling it afterwards.
  • Under shift to unseen tasks the error bounds still hold in up to 86% of runs versus at most 21% for baselines, which is the property that makes a monitor deployable.
  • It also predicts the failure mode (top-3 accuracy up to 44% above majority baseline on unseen tasks) to enable lightweight steering, and releases 3,180 annotated episodes.
Show abstract
Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) enable general-purpose embodied intelligence, but their performance is brittle under distribution shift, where unanticipated failures can damage equipment or endanger people. Deploying VLAs in real-world systems therefore requires a runtime monitor that is timely, calibrated, and informative while being efficient enough to run on the edge. Existing methods fail to fulfill these requirements. We present VLA-Tug, a lightweight probe over the latent layers of a VLA backbone that improves early failure detection and bounds the missed-detection and false-alarm rates, with minimal inference overhead. We show that supervising on the pre-failure segment of a trajectory increases early failure detection at matched false-positive rates by up to 30% over the strongest learned baseline. Under shift to unseen tasks, the bounds on missed-detection and false-alarm rates remain valid in up to 86% of runs, versus at most 21% for baselines. Unlike existing methods, VLA-Tug predicts the failure mode to enable corrective actions, raising top-3 accuracy on unseen tasks by up to 44% over a majority baseline. VLA-Tug enables real-time monitoring where the well-timed and informative probe supports lightweight steering that improves task success. We release a dataset of 3,180 annotated manipulation episodes to support future work on latent failure detection in VLAs at https://vla-tug.github.io.

T3R: Training-Free Two-Stage Token Refinement Towards Efficient and Robust VLA Models

2026-11-05 cs.ROcs.CV 👤 Mohammed Hassan (h=n/a)
Mohammed Hassan, Zhenyang Chen, Zheng Wang, Zhixin Zhu, Tianlong Chen
Core Contributions
  • Argues VLA inference wastes effort in two distinct ways - spatially irrelevant tokens and misallocated attention over the kept ones - and fixes each with a training-free stage.
  • Stage one uses segmentation to prune background patches; stage two biases attention toward action-critical regions and the language instruction using saliency, so pruning cleans the candidate set and biasing sharpens it.
  • On LIBERO-Spatial it drops 85% of scene-camera tokens while matching unpruned success, and gains up to 7 points under out-of-distribution object placement - pruning acts as robustness, not only compression.
  • Overhead is 7.8 ms per call, with up to 3.3x LLM-forward speedup at higher resolution; consistent across LIBERO, SIMPLER, RoboTwin and three backbones, strongest for multi-camera setups.
Show abstract
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models enable end-to-end robot control, but inference is increasingly expensive. Each decision processes hundreds of visual tokens, often across multiple cameras, leading to substantial computation while allowing irrelevant background content to distract action prediction under distribution shift. We argue that these issues stem from two complementary forms of redundancy: many visual tokens are spatially irrelevant, while attention over the remaining tokens is often misallocated. Based on this observation, we propose T3R, a Training-Free Two-Stage Token Refinement framework. In the first stage, segmentation-guided token pruning removes irrelevant visual patches to reduce computation and eliminate distracting context. In the second stage, saliency-guided attention biasing reallocates attention toward action-critical visual regions and language instructions within the retained tokens. Together, pruning produces a cleaner candidate set, while attention biasing further emphasizes the most decision-relevant evidence, yielding a better accuracy-efficiency frontier than either stage alone. On LIBERO-Spatial, T3R removes 85% of scene-camera visual tokens while essentially matching the unpruned success rate, and improves success by up to 7 percentage points under out-of-distribution object-placement perturbations. The timing comparison attributes 7.8 ms per inference call to pruning and attention-bias application. As input resolution increases, LLM-forward profiling shows up to a 3.3x speedup over the unpruned baseline. Evaluations across LIBERO, SIMPLER, and RoboTwin with three VLA backbones show the strongest accuracy-efficiency benefits in multi-camera settings. These results demonstrate that training-free token refinement is a practical approach to efficient and robust VLA inference.

SLA: Scaling Spatial-Language-Action Model via Continual Pre-training

2026-11-05 cs.RO 👤 Fuhao Li (h=n/a)
Fuhao Li, Wenxuan Song, Qizhou Huang, Ziyang Zhou, Jingbo Wang
Core Contributions
  • VLA backbones inherit 2D vision-language features and add spatial supervision only at task-specific fine-tuning; SLA moves spatial alignment into continual pre-training so 3D understanding becomes a transferable capability.
  • Starting from pi05, it aligns intermediate visual embeddings with a frozen 3D perception model's features while still optimizing actions, using feature-dependent gates to aggregate multiple VLA layers.
  • The 3D teacher uses whatever geometry is available (partial depth, camera calibration) across heterogeneous robot datasets, and probabilistic state dropout forces reliance on vision rather than memorized trajectories.
  • All auxiliary parts exist only in training, so pretrained VLA weights and the original inference architecture are reused unchanged - no deployment cost for the added geometry.
Show abstract
Reliable robotic manipulation requires both semantic understanding and precise control in three-dimensional space. However, vision-language-action (VLA) foundations largely inherit visual representations from 2D vision-language pre-training, while spatial supervision is often introduced only during task-specific adaptation. Developing spatial understanding as a transferable foundation-model capability therefore calls for learning from broader robot experience. We present SLA, a Spatial-Language-Action Model that scales implicit spatial alignment to continual pre-training and maintains it during downstream post-training. Initialized from a pretrained pi05 policy, SLA jointly optimizes action prediction and alignment between intermediate visual embeddings and spatial representations from a frozen 3D perception model. We curate and unify heterogeneous robot datasets to support spatial learning across diverse embodiments, tasks, and scenes. To accommodate this diversity, we introduce feature-dependent gates that adaptively aggregate visual embeddings from multiple VLA layers for spatial supervision. The teacher further exploits partially available depth and camera calibration alongside RGB observations, enabling effective use of heterogeneous geometric annotations. Probabilistic state dropout encourages reliance on visual cues and mitigates overfitting to demonstration trajectories. All auxiliary components are used only during training, allowing direct reuse of pretrained VLA weights and preserving the original inference architecture.

Aligning Policy Encoders with a Shape Teacher

2026-11-05 cs.ROcs.AIcs.CV 👤 Madelon Bernardy (h=n/a)
Madelon Bernardy, Mohammad Mahdi Derakhshani, David M Knigge, Riccardo Valperga, Artem Moskalev
Core Contributions
  • Asks whether an image-only policy can gain 3D shape awareness during training while keeping a purely image-based deployment interface (no depth or point clouds at test time).
  • Shape-teacher alignment uses Point-MAE features of individual objects as targets for the matching image patches in the policy encoder, transferring geometric structure without changing inputs or architecture.
  • On LIBERO the resulting representations are more organized around object shape and parts; for pretrained policies convergence is 4.7x faster on Object and 5.6x faster on Spatial suites, with higher success rates.
Show abstract
3D-aware representations can improve robotic manipulation by providing geometric information that is robust to changes in viewpoint, object position, and occlusion. However, using 3D inputs at deployment requires additional sensing and processing, making image-based policies more practical. We ask whether an image-based policy can acquire 3D shape information during training while retaining a purely image-based interface at deployment. We propose shape-teacher alignment, a training-time mechanism that uses a pretrained 3D shape representation as an alignment target for an image-based policy encoder. Specifically, we use Point-MAE features from individual objects as targets for corresponding image patches, transferring geometric structure to the policy without changing its deployment inputs or architecture. On LIBERO, shape-teacher alignment produces image representations that are more structured around object shape and corresponding parts, while substantially accelerating policy convergence and improving success rates. For pretrained policies, convergence is accelerated by 4.7x and 5.6x on the Object and Spatial suites, respectively, without requiring the shape teacher or depth input at deployment.

La-JEPA: Compact Robot Policies with Latent World Modeling

2026-11-05 cs.ROcs.CVcs.LG 👤 Mateo Clémente (h=n/a)
Mateo Clémente, George Z. Xue, Amin Abyaneh, Liheng Ma, Rui Heng Yang
Core Contributions
  • Questions whether policy architecture should be inherited from video generators or VLMs; La-JEPA trains a compact policy transformer from scratch in the feature space of a frozen V-JEPA 2.1 encoder.
  • Follows Fast-WAM: it predicts future features jointly with actions during training but generates only actions at inference, so inference cost stays low.
  • Among policies without embodied pretraining it is state of the art on clean and randomized RoboTwin 2.0 and LIBERO-Plus with far fewer parameters, faster than Fast-WAM, and comparable to pi0.5 on four real-robot tasks.
  • Ablations show JEPA features beat video-VAE latents, especially out of distribution; it also corrects the officially released LIBERO-Plus evaluator, which corrupts instructions for most variants, and re-evaluates baselines.
Show abstract
World-action models adapt pretrained video generators, and vision-language-action models build on pretrained vision-language backbones; both tie policy architecture and inference cost to large transformers designed for generation rather than control. It remains unclear how much these generation-oriented choices affect control. In this work, we introduce Latent Action JEPA (La-JEPA), a compact world-action model whose policy transformer is trained from scratch in the feature space of a pretrained V-JEPA 2.1 encoder. Following Fast-WAM, La-JEPA learns to predict future features jointly with actions during training and generates only actions at inference. Among policies without embodied pretraining, La-JEPA achieves state-of-the-art performance in clean and randomized RoboTwin 2.0 scenes and on LIBERO-Plus with correct instructions, while using far fewer parameters than the strongest competing policies. It runs substantially faster than Fast-WAM at comparable performance and outperforms the closest JEPA-based methods, LeapBot-WA and JEPA-WAM, on LIBERO-Plus and RoboTwin 2.0 without their pretrained policy backbones. On four real-robot tasks, its average success is comparable to pi0.5 and above non-embodied-pretrained baselines. Through a design study of the choices behind La-JEPA, we show that JEPA features outperform video VAE latents, most clearly out of distribution, and that training-time future prediction choices significantly impact the policy even when future prediction is skipped at inference. We correct the LIBERO-Plus instructions, as the officially released evaluator corrupts them for most variants, and re-evaluate several strong open baselines. Without a generative pretrained policy backbone, La-JEPA is better and faster than far larger policies, suggesting that robot foundation models may benefit from architectures designed for control rather than inherited from generation.

VFlash: Fast, Accurate Visual Planning via Flash Primitive Decoding

2026-11-05 cs.ROcs.CV 👤 Yu-Cheng Chou (h=n/a)
Yu-Cheng Chou, Shihao Wang, Jin Huang, Chi-Pin Huang, Chiao-An Yang
Core Contributions
  • Autoregressive VLMs emit visual plans (points, boxes, trajectories) one coordinate token at a time; VFlash exploits the known structure of these primitives and predicts a whole primitive in a single forward pass with a lightweight drafter.
  • Unlike speculative decoding, it does not require agreeing with the big model's exact token sequence - structurally valid predictions are accepted directly, and the drafter trains on annotated primitives, trading fidelity to the sequence for fidelity to the geometry.
  • Reduces error versus autoregressive decoding on 2D/3D trajectory tasks, with a preliminary ~25x generation speedup for 3D point-motion forecasting.
  • Downstream, a world action model conditioned on VFlash plans reaches 70.2% average success across four MolmoSpaces suites versus 62.6% for the baseline, so the speed does not come at the cost of usefulness.
Show abstract
Models for physical AI tasks, such as robotics and autonomous driving, need not reason solely in language. They can use visual plans--points, bounding boxes, and trajectories--to represent spatial goals and anticipate motion. However, autoregressive vision-language models generate these plans one coordinate token at a time, making richer predictions increasingly costly. We introduce VFlash, a method for fast, accurate visual planning through Flash Primitive Decoding. VFlash exploits the known structure of visual primitives to predict their coordinates jointly, using a lightweight drafter that generates an entire primitive in one forward pass. The layout adapts to the primitive's type, dimensionality, and length, supporting compact boxes and long, variable-length trajectories. Crucially, spatial accuracy need not require reproducing an autoregressive model's exact token sequence. VFlash therefore trains on annotated primitives and directly accepts structurally valid predictions without target-token agreement. Across the evaluated 2D and 3D robot trajectory tasks, VFlash consistently reduces prediction errors relative to autoregressive decoding. Preliminary single-rollout measurements indicate approximately 25x faster generation for 3D point motion forecasting. A world action model conditioned on VFlash plans achieves 70.2% average success across four MolmoSpaces task suites, compared with 62.6% for the baseline.

Latent RL Priors for Data-Efficient Dexterous Manipulation

2026-11-05 cs.RO 👤 Yixiao Wang (h=n/a)
Yixiao Wang, Peiyi Chen, Mustafa Mukadam, Shreekant Gayaka, Masayoshi Tomizuka
Core Contributions
  • Splits the problem cleanly: control knowledge is distilled from large-scale privileged RL into a flow-matching action decoder, then frozen, so vision only has to learn a map from images to the decoder's latent context.
  • The visual interface is trained with action supervision alone - no privileged labels or latent targets - and feeding vision straight into the latent beat routing it through the pretrained state encoder on both adaptation quality and stability.
  • In the low-data regime it outperforms full fine-tuning of pi0.5 and GR00T N1.7, and on door lifting and bimanual assembly it surpasses full-data baselines with 10x and 5x fewer demonstrations.
  • Probing shows contact-force information stays linearly accessible in the decoder and the policy is robust to unseen mass changes, evidence the RL-acquired control knowledge is actually retained rather than overwritten.
Show abstract
Learning dexterous manipulation from limited visual demonstrations is challenging because policies must acquire both perceptual grounding and precise control, while reinforcement learning (RL) in simulation can acquire rich control behaviors from large-scale interaction with privileged information. We introduce \textbf{Latent RL Prior}, a two-stage framework that reuses this control knowledge for data-efficient visual policy learning. During privileged pretraining, we distill large-scale RL rollouts into a flow-matching action decoder conditioned on proprioception and latent task context. During visual adaptation, we freeze the decoder and learn a visual interface that maps images to its latent context using action supervision alone, without privileged labels or latent targets. The same decoder can further be shared across arms by learning coordinated visual contexts for bimanual manipulation. Across three simulated tasks, our method outperforms full-parameter fine-tuning of $π_{0.5}$ and GR00T N1.7 in the low-data regime; on door lifting and bimanual assembly, it surpasses full-data baselines using $10\times$ and $5\times$ fewer demonstrations, respectively. Further analysis shows that direct visual-to-latent conditioning improves adaptation performance and training stability over routing visual predictions through the pretrained state encoder. Robustness to unseen mass variation and greater linear accessibility of contact-force information in decoder representations further provide evidence of retained RL-acquired control knowledge. Project website: https://yixiaowang7.github.io/latent-rl-prior-page/.

MOMASPLAT: Dynamic Novel-View Demonstration Augmentation for Mobile Manipulation

2026-11-05 cs.ROcs.AIcs.CV 👤 Sateesh Kumar (h=n/a)
Sateesh Kumar, Arnav Balaji, Arpit Bahety, Skand Peri, Georgios Pavlakos
Core Contributions
  • Imitation policies are brittle to camera viewpoint, and mobile manipulators rarely stop at the demonstrated base pose; earlier novel-view augmentation assumes static scenes, so the post-contact phase - the critical part - goes un-augmented.
  • MOMASPLAT reconstructs scene and manipulated object separately in 3D (Gaussian splatting plus feed-forward 3D foundation models), then re-renders them from new base poses with kinematics-based action retargeting to produce consistent synthetic demos.
  • The insight is that ordinary mobile-manipulation data collection already provides the multi-view coverage needed - no extra capture effort.
  • On five real tasks (rigid and articulated objects, three scenes) it more than doubles the strongest baseline's average success and keeps non-trivial success at large base displacements where all baselines fail.
Show abstract
Visuomotor policies trained through imitation learning are highly sensitive to changes in camera viewpoint. This is particularly problematic for mobile manipulation (MoMa), where robots rarely execute manipulation from the exact same base pose used during demonstration collection due to navigation uncertainty, localization drift, and environmental constraints. Recent approaches use novel-view synthesis (NVS) to augment demonstrations with unseen viewpoints, but they assume static scenes and therefore fail to re-render the manipulated object during the post-contact interaction phase, leaving the most critical parts of the demonstration unaugmented. We present MOMASPLAT, a novel-view demonstration augmentation framework for mobile manipulation that synthesizes full manipulation trajectories from unseen robot base poses, including dynamic object interactions. Our key insight is that standard MoMa data collection naturally provides the multiview observations needed to reconstruct both the static scene and the manipulated object in 3D. MOMASPLAT combines 3D Gaussian Splatting, feed-forward 3D foundation models, and kinematics-based action retargeting to separately reconstruct the scene and manipulated object, render them from novel viewpoints, and generate geometrically consistent synthetic demonstrations paired with transformed actions. We evaluate MOMASPLAT on five real-world mobile manipulation tasks involving both rigid and articulated objects across three scenes, and validate the fidelity of its synthesized demonstrations against ground truth in simulation. MOMASPLAT substantially improves robustness to unseen base poses, more than doubling the average success rate of the strongest baseline and maintaining non-trivial success at large base displacements where all baselines fail, while reducing the need for largescale real-world data collection.

GPWM: Modeling the World as 3D Particle Motion

2026-11-05 cs.RO 👤 Robert Ren (h=n/a)
Robert Ren, Matthew Bronars, He Zhu, Yikai Tang, Jianing Zhang
Core Contributions
  • GPWM predicts scene evolution by generating the motion of 3D particles and rigid bodies directly in physical space, not appearance in video, with one representation covering rigid, deformable, cloth and granular materials.
  • An object-aware multi-resolution transformer combines flow matching with diffusion forcing to roll out coherent trajectories well beyond the training horizon; trained on ParticleWorlds, a 25.5M-frame corpus.
  • Beats the best prior learned-dynamics baseline in all six physical domains (31% lower error on average), and a single Unified model with one set of weights matches or beats per-domain specialists, even its own in two domains.
  • Planning searches the model's latent noise instead of the action space, substantially outperforming action-space search on pushing and picking where contact sequences are hard to find, with open-loop goal-directed execution and no goal-specific policy.
Show abstract
We introduce GPWM, a Generative Particle World Model that predicts how 3D scenes evolve by generating the motion of particles and rigid bodies over time. Unlike video world models that generate scene appearance, GPWM operates directly in 3D physical space. A single representation and architecture cover rigid, deformable, cloth, and granular materials, in both passive physics and robotic interaction. GPWM is an object-aware multi-resolution transformer that combines flow matching with diffusion forcing to generate coherent physical trajectories far beyond its training horizon. We train and evaluate GPWM on ParticleWorlds, a 25.5-million-frame corpus spanning diverse geometries, materials, interactions, and robot embodiments. GPWM outperforms the best prior learned-dynamics baseline in each of six physical domains, reducing prediction error by 31% on average. Breaking from the domain-specific setting of prior methods, a single Unified GPWM models these heterogeneous regimes with one set of weights, largely outperforming per-domain baselines and even surpassing our own specialist models in two domains. GPWM further generalizes to unseen geometries, physical properties, object counts, and rollout horizons. Beyond prediction, GPWM enables contact-rich planning by learning a generative prior over joint actuator-object motion. Instead of searching over unconstrained actions, planning searches the model's latent noise for interactions that achieve a desired object-space goal. With the same world model, noise-space planning substantially outperforms action-space search on pushing and picking, where complex contact sequences are hard to discover. Executed open-loop in simulation, the resulting plans achieve goal-directed manipulation without reference action trajectories or goal-specific policies.

SHORE: Stability-Hybrid On-Policy Reinforcement Learning for Online Bin Packing

2026-11-05 cs.ROcs.LG 👤 Chang Shi (h=n/a)
Chang Shi, Ann Majewicz Fey
Core Contributions
  • In online bin packing a collapse ends the episode, yet prior methods use hard stability masks or geometric rules that cannot trade risk against volume when jointly choosing box, orientation and placement.
  • SHORE learns a placement's stability probability with a 3D U-Net (trained on executed outcomes and simulated alternatives) and multiplies it into an on-policy return estimate inside the actor-critic's values and prior, with gates guarding against an untrained model.
  • Reaches 73% space utilization in 0.4M environment steps versus ~1M without stability modeling; on the 60-box set it achieves 0.734 versus 0.588 for GOPT.
  • Honest trade-off: placement-failure frequency is higher (1.03% vs 0.63%) - it deliberately accepts more risk for more packed volume - and it needs no extra search at deployment; shown on a real robot.
Show abstract
Online 3D bin packing with a processing buffer requires choosing which box to pack, its orientation, and its placement while keeping the stack stable under unknown future arrivals. A collapse ends the episode and forfeits the remaining volume. Existing methods use geometric rules or hard stability masks, limiting how risk can influence the joint choice of box, orientation, and placement. We propose Stability-Hybrid On-Policy Reinforcement Learning (SHORE), which combines a learned stability probability of a placement with an on-policy estimate of its return conditional on stability. A 3D U-Net learns stability from executed outcomes and simulated alternatives, revealing outcomes of actions underrepresented in early rollouts. A discrete on-policy actor--critic uses stability predictions in both its action values and policy prior, with gates that limit the effect of an untrained or changing model. In deployment, SHORE does not require additional search or simulation overhead. In Isaac Gym, SHORE reaches 73\% space utilization by 0.4M environment steps, while the same learner without stability modeling reaches that level at about 1M. 2026-11-05 cs.AIcs.RO 👤 Wenbo Huang (h=n/a)
Wenbo Huang, Jianxin Bi, Shunyu Yang, Ruiyang Si, Rui Ni

  • Controlled experiments show that with exposure the agent quickly scores higher under the given criteria, but the advantage vanishes when criteria are slightly modified without leaving the instruction.

Show abstract
Self-improving embodied agents are increasingly being developed and compared on robot simulation benchmarks. Several recent works expose the benchmark's success criteria to the learning agent, a practice we call Criteria Exposure (CE). These criteria, originally written to verify policies trained by imitation learning, often demand more than the corresponding language instructions, so a score from such a verifier may not faithfully reflect whether the instructed task was completed, especially for robot-control programs written by an agent. As embodied coding agents report ever higher benchmark scores, a natural question arises: when a coding agent is exposed to the benchmark's criteria, does it generate code that better performs the instructed task, or code that simply overfits to the verification criteria? We conduct controlled experiments with a self-improving agent with and without CE, and find that with CE the agent quickly produces code with a higher success rate under the given criteria, but this advantage disappears when the criteria are changed slightly without departing from the original instructions. More importantly, the skills the agent develops under CE transfer poorly across tasks. We further propose instruction-faithful criteria and show that they better reflect the performance of agent-generated programs.

Show abstract
Robot reinforcement learning increasingly runs environments outside the trainer: in another process, on another machine, in another language. Many faults at this boundary raise no error. We make the boundary a wire protocol and ask what notices a fault in it. On Pendulum, HalfCheetah, Hopper and a 7-joint arm (Pusher), we injected up to 23 faults into what crosses the wire and 2 into the trainer's log, from one value to every value. Unless every value was wrong, a learning-curve threshold caught at most one run in ten, and a comparison with ten fault-free seeds at most 21%. A clean evaluation of all 1,060 final policies agreed with the curves: of the 728 boundary-fault runs both curve checks passed, 723 scored no more than three standard deviations below fault-free seeds, though nearly all ended on other weights and, for reward faults, logged other returns. Checks of the run itself, not of its outcome, saw every fault. A step ledger kept on both sides and compared step by step caught and located every fault that crossed, on Pendulum, Hopper and Pusher on one machine and on Pendulum across two, with no fault-free run and, on Pendulum, a median 3% more training time. An episode record kept at the environment caught the faults in the trainer's log. We list such faults found in practice, in Gymnasium 1.3 and our own code and, reported by others, in five multi-agent libraries, and recommend a step ledger and episode counts kept at the environment.

Show abstract
Haptic interactions, such as touching an object in virtual reality, involve detecting a plethora of sensations through the skin. Whilst tactile displays exist to simulate and manipulate all of these cues individually, little progress has been made constructing devices that can control multiple cues simultaneously. This paper presents a tactile display technology that offers both softness and temperature change. A prototype display, based on particle jamming and the thermal transfer of heated and cooled water, is evaluated in terms of its ability to change both stiffness and temperature. Mechanical evaluations demonstrate controllable stiffness increase of 300%, whilst thermal characterisation showed strong surface uniformity and a range of achievable temperatures from 10°C up to 50°C, with closed-loop control maintaining a 0.2°C error from a setpoint. This work advances the design of multimodal tactile displays with applications to perceptual research, medical simulation, and affective haptics.

Transformer-Based Trajectory Prescription for sEMG-Optimizing Robot-Mediated Rehabilitation

2026-11-05 cs.ROcs.HCcs.LG 👤 Gabriel Parra (h=n/a)
Gabriel Parra, Kimia Khoshnami, Daniel S. Brown, Laura A. Hallock
Core Contributions
  • Rehabilitation robots usually prescribe kinematic trajectories; this work instead maps a user's muscle activations (sEMG) to contact-force trajectories, aiming at the neuromotor activations thought to drive recovery.
  • A transformer encoder-decoder with rotary embeddings builds a personalized latent of user-specific neuromotor factors, and flow matching yields a distribution over plausible prescriptions.
  • With 16 healthy participants and 15 isometric tracking tasks, trajectory error is under 8% on held-out tasks and users, with instantaneous deterministic inference and sub-second sampling; fine-tuning is feasible within a session.
  • In a live test participants reached target activations with only 15% accumulated error - a proof of concept (healthy subjects only), with data and code open-sourced in OpenRobotRehab.
Show abstract
Robot-mediated rehabilitation has the potential to deliver high-intensity, individualized therapy, but existing systems (which have not consistently improved outcomes) typically prescribe kinematic trajectories rather than explicitly targeting the neuromotor activations thought to support recovery. To address this gap, and ultimately enable activation-optimizing rehabilitation robot behavior, we present a personalizable transformer-based model mapping individuals' muscle activations (as measured via surface electromyography) to prescribed contact force trajectories when interacting with an end-effector rehabilitation robot. The model uses a transformer encoder-decoder leveraging rotary positional embeddings (RoPE) to generate a latent representation of trajectory-relevant and user-specific neuromotor factors, and augments trajectory prediction with flow matching to produce a distribution over plausible prescriptions biased toward the training distribution. Using data from 16 healthy participants completing 15 isometric trajectory tracking tasks, we evaluate generalization across held-out tasks and users, as well as the feasibility of user-specific fine-tuning within a therapy-session timescale. The model achieves mean trajectory prediction errors of <8% on held out tasks and users, with effectively instantaneous deterministic inference and sub-second sampling of trajectory distributions. An initial test using the model during live rehabilitation tasks demonstrates that, on average, participants reach the target neuromotor activations with just 15% error accumulated throughout the task. These results provide a proof of concept for data-driven, activation-targeted trajectory prescription in robot-mediated rehabilitation and establish a foundation for future validation in clinical populations. Data and code have been open-sourced as part of the OpenRobotRehab project on SimTK.

Show abstract
Robot Operating System (ROS) is becoming a standard in robotics courses at higher education, but its installation, configuration and distributed programming model can introduce a significant entry barrier in the new landscape of AI-based master and degree studies, where many students do not have a strong informatics background. This paper presents an educational experience that enables students to program a TurtleBot3 Burger directly in Python, while preserving a direct transfer path from simulation to the physical robot. The approach is based on a custom library that mirrors the Webots API, allowing the same controller code to be executed in the simulator and on the real TurtleBot3 with minimal modification. The library provides access to the robot motors, wheel encoders, inertial sensors and LiDAR, and is complemented by scripts that automate deployment and execution on the physical platform. It was used in a master-level course on introductory autonomous vehicles, and initial student feedback suggests that the approach reduces the perceived complexity of ROS-related infrastructure and helps learners focus on algorithmic logic and real-world behaviour.
