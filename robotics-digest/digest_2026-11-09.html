Research Landscape

The dominant thread in this batch is how to specialise a generalist policy without paying for retraining. Four papers attack it from different sides. Distilling Instructions into Weights (#3) edits pi0.5's MLPs in closed form to fix a language-binding error, lifting held-out success from 27 to 106 of 128 seeds; World In-Context Learning (#13) goes the opposite way and leaves weights frozen, writing simulator rehearsal into a Task Memory to reach 83.4% on all 50 RoboTwin 2.0 tasks from one demo; Thermo-VLA (#5) grafts a new physical attribute into the token stream via gated residuals; and VLA Adaptation for Robotic Chess (#8) supplies the sobering empirical baseline - LR cool-down and sim pretraining matter, while several self-improvement methods did not beat plain BC. Read together, they suggest the field is moving from 'scale the base model' to 'diagnose and patch the specific failure', with weights, context and input modality as competing patch surfaces.

A second theme is world models that know what they don't model. Library of Hands (#19) argues that most deployment breakage is in the command-to-action map rather than the scene dynamics, and fixes it training-free by reading the 'hand' through a frozen LeWM; Sigma-LeWM (#18) strengthens the same LeWM latent with backward and inverse-dynamics losses; SWAM (#11) shows action training can erode the language-video alignment world-action models inherit, and that egocentric plus UMI data is the cheapest fix; and Simulation-aided RL with Control Variates (#21) uses simulators only to cut variance, never to set the target. A parallel structure appears on the classical side, where Lie-group formulations do heavy lifting: zonotopic reachability on matrix Lie groups (#4), Galilean-group spatiotemporal calibration (#24) and LawKAN's group-law layers (#23) all replace ad-hoc parameterisations with exact algebraic structure.

Finally, there is an unusually strong evaluation-hygiene undercurrent. #3 reports that rerunning unedited weights flips about a third of paired outcomes; #8 finds validation loss useless for checkpoint selection; #16 proposes stochastic real-world challenges as a physical hidden test set; and #20 shows that 'valid typed plan' and 'task reported complete' can both hold while a fleet does the wrong thing. On the sensing side, tactile research is splitting between better transduction physics (PCTac's force-encoding structural colour, #6) and sensor-agnostic tokens (LingBot-Touch, #15, aligned in spirit with UniOMA's GW regulariser, #29). Navigation, meanwhile, is converging on long memory and cross-embodiment transfer - Compass 1's two-minute history across four robot heights (#7) and LHNav's brain/cerebellum/brainstem humanoid stack (#2).

Distilling Instructions into Weights: Closed-Form Edits Fix a Robot Policy's Misreading

2026-11-09cs.ROcs.AIcs.LG👤 Esmaeil Seraj (h=14)
Ali-Adeeb Abbas, Esmaeil Seraj
Core Contributions
  • Shows a generalist VLA's failure can be a language-binding bug: replaying 21 decisions under rewordings reveals pi0.5 attaches "in the bowl" to the wrong object, which is why it drops the cube early.
  • Repairs it with a closed-form, gradient-free and data-free edit that copies the per-layer effect of a clarifying rewording into the language model's MLP weights - success on 128 held-out seeds rises from 27 to 106 (81 fixed, 2 broken, p=7e-22), while an equal-norm random edit does nothing.
  • Localisation matters: the same edit in the action expert or head helps less or not at all, and edits targeting perception, grasp, contact or gripper threshold fail - weight editing works when a known-good input already exists to distil.
  • A sobering evaluation lesson: rerunning unedited weights flips ~1/3 of paired outcomes, so the authors argue for paired seeds, unedited reruns and held-out confirmation as standard practice for VLA claims.
  • Of seven blind Claude Opus 'editors', only the one told about the misreading produced a passing edit - diagnosis, not search, is the bottleneck.
Show abstract
Generalist robot policies often fail at one step of a task they can otherwise perform. We ask whether such a failure can be repaired by editing the policy's weights directly, without gradients, training data, or a change to the instruction. Told to put the cube then the banana in the bowl, a zero-shot $π_{0.5}$ vision-language-action policy in RoboLab releases the cube before it reaches the bowl. Replaying 21 of these decisions under rewordings shows that it binds "in the bowl" to the wrong object. Closed-form instruction distillation copies the per-layer effect of an explicit rewording into the language model's MLP weights. On 128 held-out seeds, success rises from 27 to 106 episodes, with 81 fixed and 2 broken ($p=7\times10^{-22}$), while an update of the same size in random directions leaves success unchanged. The edit also lifts two other tasks without detectable harm on the remaining 13, and a variant that stores 0.16% of the parameter count keeps most of its effect. Placed in the action expert or action head instead, the same distillation helps less or not at all. Our released tool reproduced the fix end to end on new held-out seeds, from 8/64 to 36/64. Edits aimed at perception, a missing grasp, contact physics, or the gripper threshold did not help, and of seven blind Claude Opus editors, only the one given the misread instruction produced an edit that passed held-out scoring. These results suggest that weight edits work best when a known-good input already exists to distil. Because reruns with unchanged weights flip about a third of paired outcomes, evaluations should pair seeds, rerun the unedited policy, and confirm gains on held-out seeds.

Thermo-VLA: A Thermal-Aware Vision-Language-Action Model with Gated Residual Modulation for Robotic Manipulation

2026-11-09cs.ROcs.AI👤 Chen-Cheng Dong (h=7)
Jingxuan Chen, Junshi Luo, Chencheng Dong, Xiaojian Nie, Tongge Huang
Core Contributions
  • Rather than feeding thermal images as just another visual stream, the ThermoRes encoder decomposes them into hot and cold attribute maps and injects them into RGB tokens through language-conditioned gated residual modulation.
  • This makes temperature a reasoning variable ("pick the warmer cup", relational comparisons) instead of a texture cue, which is where naive RGB-T fusion falls short.
  • On a dual-arm Unitree G1 across four thermal-dependent tasks it averages 0.86 success, +18 points over a pi0.5 baseline given raw RGB-thermal input, and keeps 0.78 under unseen objects/scenes.
  • The same modulation transferred to colour attributes with consistent gains in sim and real - suggesting a general recipe for grafting any scalar physical attribute onto a VLA.
Show abstract
Recent Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have begun incorporating thermal sensing, but existing approaches typically treat thermal images as ordinary visual inputs, providing limited support for temperature-aware relational reasoning in manipulation. To address this limitation, we propose Thermo-VLA with a ThermoRes Encoder, which decomposes thermal images into cold and hot attribute representations and injects them into the RGB token through language-conditioned residual modulation, enabling temperature-driven target selection and continuous manipulation. We further construct six real-world robotic tasks on a dual-arm Unitree G1 platform, spanning basic grounding to relational reasoning. Experiments show that Thermo-VLA achieves an average success rate of 0.86 across four thermal-dependent tasks, outperforming a pi_0.5 baseline that directly incorporates RGB-thermal observations by 18 percentage points. Under unseen-object and scene variations, Thermo-VLA maintains an average success rate of 0.78, achieving the best generalisation performance among the compared methods. Beyond thermal sensing, we transfer the same residual modulation mechanism to colour attributes and observe consistent improvements in both simulation and real-robot experiments, indicating its potential to generalise beyond thermal attributes. Project website: https://j-x-chen.github.io/Thermo-VLA-web/

VLA Adaptation with Real and Simulated Data: A Case Study on Robotic Chess

2026-11-09cs.ROcs.LG👤 Milan Straka (h=5)
Matěj Kripner, Milan Straka
Core Contributions
  • A careful empirical study of the unglamorous decisions in VLA fine-tuning (data size, update budget, checkpoint choice, sim data) on a chess-king move task that needs both precision and symbolic spatial grounding ("e4 to a5") on a low-cost SO-101 arm.
  • Practical headline: a short learning-rate cool-down adds up to 31 points and is necessary for fair checkpoint comparison, while validation loss and action error are unreliable selectors.
  • Pretraining on demos from a randomised scripted sim expert helps substantially when real data is scarce (up to 256 teleop demos tested, 10 policies x 25 trials).
  • Valuable negative result: filtered BC on own rollouts, RECAP-style advantage conditioning, automatic-coach corrections and residual RL all failed to beat plain BC here - a useful counterweight to self-improvement claims.
Show abstract
Vision-language-action (VLA) models are trained as general-purpose foundation models for robot learning, and require fine-tuning for each downstream task and robot. This raises practical questions such as how much data and training updates are needed, how checkpoints should be selected, and whether simulation can be leveraged to alleviate the cost of real data collection. We study these questions on a task that requires both precise manipulation and spatial understanding of the scene. In this task, an SO-101 arm moves a chess king between two squares that are given only by their names, such as e4 and a5. We fine-tune $π_{0.5}$ with various dataset sizes and update budgets, using teleoperated demonstrations as well as simulated demonstrations from a randomized scripted expert that imitates the variability of human demonstrations. In simulation, the success rate scales with both data and updates. Importantly, we find that a short learning-rate cool-down adds up to 31 points and is necessary for comparing checkpoints, and that validation loss and action error are unreliable for model selection. On the real arm, we fine-tune on up to 256 teleoperated demonstrations and evaluate ten policies in 25 trials each. Partial results indicate that pretraining on the simulated demonstrations helps substantially, most clearly when real data is scarce. We also present a negative result, in which training on the policy's own rollouts with filtered behavior cloning, RECAP-style advantage conditioning, corrections from an automatic coach, or residual reinforcement learning did not yield a measurable improvement over plain behavior cloning in our setting. We release the simulator, the expert and the real demonstrations.

SWAM: Scaling World Action Models with Cross-Modal Alignment

2026-11-09cs.CVcs.RO👤 Xinyuan Xia (h=1)
Yu Zhou, Xinyuan Xia, Xuankun Yang, Yinghao Yang, Wendi Chen
Core Contributions
  • Questions an assumption most world-action models make: that language-video alignment from video pretraining survives action learning. The paper shows action training can erode it and studies how to prevent that.
  • Builds a ~47,000-hour pool across general video, egocentric video, UMI and robot demos and runs controlled ablations of language-video vs language-video-action supervision, annotation quality, mixing and architecture.
  • Under a fixed budget, mixing egocentric video with UMI demos is what most improves control generalisation - evidence that cheap human-centric data substitutes for robot time.
  • The resulting recipe hits 88.10% / 61.56% on RoboTwin Easy/Hard (trained on Clean only) and 40.0% vs 23.3% on three real tasks; data, code and weights released.
Show abstract
World action models jointly predict video and actions conditioned on language. Existing studies focus primarily on architectures and training mechanisms, typically treating language-video alignment acquired through video pretraining as an established foundation. However, introducing action learning may impair this alignment. Principled guidance on data and supervision design for maintaining language-video alignment during action learning remains limited. To investigate this, we curate a pool of approximately 47,000 hours of general videos, egocentric videos, UMI demonstrations, and robot demonstrations. Building on this, we systematically study how language-video supervision, language-video-action supervision, and the joint use of these two forms affect WAM performance. In particular, we examine how language annotation quality affects pretrained language-video capabilities, and how action supervision from UMI and robot demonstrations extends these capabilities to language-video-action modeling. To better scale the model, we further conduct detailed studies on joint training recipe and model architecture. Under a fixed training budget, mixing egocentric videos and UMI demonstrations improves control generalization. Model architecture affects how effectively these experiences are used, while scaling pretraining under the selected configuration brings further gains. The resulting recipe, post-trained on RoboTwin Clean demonstrations only, achieves success rates of 88.10% and 61.56% under Easy (Clean-to-Clean) and Hard (Clean-to-Random) conditions, respectively. Our model achieves 40.00% SR across three real-world tasks, compared with 23.33% for the strongest baseline, indicating better generalization under unseen conditions. We release the curated data, training code, and model weights.

World In-Context Learning for Agentic Robot Manipulation

2026-11-09cs.CVcs.RO👤 Heng Fang (h=0)
Heng Fang, Zehui Guo, Yuehan Zhang, Mingyang Du, Dingkang Liang
Core Contributions
  • Proposes World In-Context Learning: a frozen foundation model rehearses a task in a simulator from one demo and writes what it learns into a reusable Task Memory (context), not into weights.
  • Key insight is that in-context knowledge is semantic (steps, object roles) rather than visual, so lessons from a simulated world transfer to a different real one - which is why ICL can finally compete with trained policies.
  • On all 50 RoboTwin 2.0 tasks it reaches 83.4% mean success with one demo per task and zero gradient updates, over 20 points above the strongest trained action policy, with strict data isolation for deployment inputs.
  • Improves with reflection and with stronger foundation models and transfers to a physical dual-arm robot via cross-embodiment memory adaptation - a direct contrast to the weight-editing route of #3.
Show abstract
Trained action policies write manipulation skill into network weights, tying what is learned to the distribution it is learned from. In-context learning (ICL) avoids this coupling but has not matched trained policies, because a demonstration recorded in another scene carries no feedback from the world the robot currently faces. We observe that what an agent learns in context is semantic, recording task steps and object roles rather than visual appearance, so experience gathered in one world transfers to another. Based on this insight, we propose World In-Context Learning (WICL), a paradigm in which a frozen model starts from a demonstration, rehearses the task in a world such as a simulator, and writes the feedback into its context rather than its weights. We build RoboScript, a modular infrastructure that implements WICL for agentic manipulation. From a single demonstration, RoboScript learns each task through rehearsal in simulation, freezes the learned knowledge as a Task Memory, and deploys the frozen memory on new scenes with data isolation that ensures every deployment input is available on a real robot. On all 50 tasks of RoboTwin 2.0, RoboScript reaches 83.4% mean success across clean and randomized settings with one demonstration per task and no gradient updates, exceeding the strongest trained action policy by over 20 points. The method generalizes across visual distributions, improves through reflection, scales with the foundation model, and transfers to a physical dual-arm robot through cross-embodiment memory adaptation. With a world in the loop, a robot can learn a manipulation task in its context as well as it learns one in its weights. All code is available at https://github.com/ArcheOne-AI/RoboScript

LHNav: Long-Horizon Humanoid Navigation via Multirate Coordination and Physics-Grounded Action Data

2026-11-09cs.ROcs.CV👤 Hu-Jun Bao (h=18)
Xueyang Wang, Zehong Shen, Dingkun Wei, Huaijin Pi, Changpeng Wang
Core Contributions
  • Splits humanoid navigation into a Brain (task reasoning), Cerebellum (VLA motion-token prediction) and Brainstem (whole-body control) running asynchronously at different rates, so planning the next goal never stalls locomotion.
  • Unlike waypoint-following stacks, progress and arrival feedback close the loop between layers, which is what lets a policy trained only on single-goal demos chain successive destinations - 75.56% success on long-horizon tasks.
  • A physics-grounded data engine re-tracks motion references along collision-aware routes before rendering observations, so the VLA learns from motions that are physically executable rather than kinematic hallucinations.
  • Beats OmniNav and DualVLN on single-goal success, smoothness, collisions and falls in simulation, and runs zero-shot long-horizon tasks on a Unitree G1 with rearranged layouts.
Show abstract
We study humanoid navigation across multiple destinations under long-horizon language instructions. This task is challenging because robots must maintain progress across successive goals while continuously producing natural, stable whole-body motion through confined spaces. However, task decomposition in existing methods does not by itself ensure progress throughout execution, while waypoint-based navigation does not guarantee natural, route-faithful whole-body motion. Our key idea is to organize navigation into three complementary roles operating at different time scales: a Brain for task reasoning, a Cerebellum for local motion generation, and a Brainstem for whole-body execution. We realize this idea in LHNav, an asynchronous system that uses progress and arrival feedback to coordinate navigation goals, VLA motion-token prediction, and whole-body control. To train the Cerebellum for natural, route-faithful motion, we develop a physics-grounded data engine that improves physical tracking of motion references along collision-aware routes and pairs the resulting demonstrations with rendered visual observations. Together, these designs allow task planning to proceed alongside continuous movement and extend single-goal navigation to successive destinations with natural, route-faithful whole-body motion. In simulation, our navigation VLA outperforms OmniNav and DualVLN in single-goal success and motion smoothness, with fewer collisions and falls. Using a VLA trained only on single-goal demonstrations, LHNav achieves a 75.56% success rate on long-horizon navigation tasks. Deployment on the Unitree G1 further demonstrates single-goal navigation and zero-shot long-horizon task execution in real-world environments, including layouts with repositioned objects. Project website: https://robbyant-research.github.io/LHNav/.

Compass 1: Towards Open-World and Persistent Embodied Navigation from Monocular RGB Videos

2026-11-09cs.CVcs.RO👤 Yu Qiao (h=6)
Hai Zhang, Siqi Liang, Yanjun Guo, Shoufeng Zhang, Yunhao Wang
Core Contributions
  • Positions itself as a navigation foundation model that unifies language understanding with video generation and can backtrack through ~2 minutes of visual history, versus a handful of recent frames for typical VLN policies.
  • Memory is added without architectural change: sparse keyframes plus an explicit History Representation Map, trained with structured VQA supervision on task-stage grounding, exploration consistency and spatiotemporal target retrieval.
  • Scale is the other lever - an annotation pipeline for monocular RGB video yields 500+ h of real interactions and 2,000+ h of annotated web video, open-sourced.
  • Trained mostly at 1 m camera height yet transfers to wheeled (0.35 m), quadruped (0.5 m), humanoid (0.85 m) and drone (1.5 m) views; 79.4% overall open-world success vs 17.8% for the best baseline, and 0% to 58% on history-backtracking tasks.
Show abstract
Embodied navigation requires agents to achieve persistent spatial awareness and long-horizon execution stability in open-world environments. However, existing navigation architectures struggle to sustain both capabilities, remaining effective only over a handful of recent frames. We present Compass 1, the first embodied navigation foundation model to unify language understanding and video generation while enabling memory backtracking through nearly 2mins of visual history. Without altering the underlying architecture, we introduce History Representation, which integrates sparse visual keyframes with an explicit History Representation Map to preserve long-term spatiotemporal context. To effectively leverage this history, we further introduce History Utilization, a structured VQA supervision protocol comprising three categories: task-stage grounding, exploration consistency, and spatiotemporal target retrieval, enabling map-grounded reasoning and stable persistent long-horizon navigation. To fuel this system, we establish a complete annotation pipeline for monocular RGB videos and open-source a massive dataset featuring over 500 hours of high-quality real-world interactions and over 2,000 hours of annotated web videos. Trained mainly on the distribution of 1m height camera, Compass 1 generalizes seamlessly across diverse embodiments and scenarios, ranging from indoor environments to pedestrian-dense streets, spanning wheeled robot at 0.35m, quadruped dog at 0.5m, humanoid at 0.85m, and aerial drone at 1.5m. Compass 1achieves a dominant overall success rate of 79.4% across open-world evaluations, compared to 17.8% for the strongest baseline. It demonstrates exceptional zero-shot execution exceeding 90% on foundational tasks, a 250% boost in exploration, and an unparalleled leap from 0% to 58% in challenging persistent history backtracking.

CSG-Nav: Confidence-Aware Semantic-Geometric Fusion for Robust Exploration and Accurate Zero-Shot Object-Goal Navigation

2026-11-09cs.RO👤 Qing-Quan Li (h=2)
Shoubin Chen, Rui Xu, Zehao Wu, Kai Zhang, Tao Huang
Core Contributions
  • Targets three failure modes of foundation-model object search - unreliable long-range detections, hallucination-driven direction bias, and stopping on semantically plausible but geometrically wrong targets - with one fusion mechanism for each.
  • Semantic-source fusion combines open-vocabulary detection confidence with scene and relational reasoning to score directions; a Gaussian distance decay down-weights far-away views so evidence accumulates only where it is reliable.
  • Termination is grounded by fusing detection with depth-based 3D localisation, so the agent stops where the object physically is rather than where a VLM thinks it should be.
  • Zero-shot success of 58.9% (HM3Dv1), 71.6% (HM3Dv2) and 44.3% (MP3D), with indoor/outdoor real deployments.
Show abstract
Zero-shot object-goal navigation requires autonomous agents to explore unseen environments and locate target objects without task-specific training, posing challenges in perceptual reliability under observation uncertainty and physically grounded goal termination. Although foundation-model-based approaches improve generalization, they remain vulnerable to unreliable long-range perception, hallucination-induced directional bias, and semantically plausible but geometrically incorrect stopping decisions. To address these limitations, we propose CSG-Nav, a zero-shot navigation framework centered on confidence-aware multimodal information fusion. CSG-Nav integrates three complementary fusion mechanisms. First, semantic-source fusion integrates open-vocabulary detection results, detection confidence, scene observations, and relational reasoning to produce more reliable direction-level semantic scores and suppress hallucination-induced directional bias. Second, spatial-confidence fusion weights multi-view semantic evidence using a Gaussian decay function, suppressing unreliable distant observations while accumulating spatially consistent exploration preferences. Third, semantic-geometric decision fusion combines semantic target detection with depth-based 3D localization to support physically grounded stopping decisions. Experiments on HM3Dv1, HM3Dv2, and MP3D demonstrate competitive zero-shot performance, achieving success rates of 58.9%, 71.6%, and 44.3%. Fusion-level ablations and real-world deployments in indoor and outdoor environments further verify the effectiveness and practicality of CSG-Nav for robust navigation under uncertain perception. Code is available at https://xr-ui.github.io/CSGNav.github.io/

V3DSAM: Semantic-Geometric Loop Verification and Geometry-Anchored Persistent Instance Mapping for Feed-Forward Dense SLAM

2026-11-09cs.ROcs.CV👤 Dong-Qing Zhao (h=0)
Maolin Ye, Dongqing Zhao, Linyang Li, Shuai Xue, Yuan Liu
Core Contributions
  • Combines feed-forward geometry from VGGT-SLAM 2.0 with SAM3 open-vocabulary semantics to fix unreliable cross-view association in long-sequence feed-forward dense SLAM.
  • Dual-scale loop verification (pixel-level bidirectional reprojection + object-level 3D correspondence) rejects false loop closures without touching backbone-internal features, keeping the system backbone-agnostic.
  • Instances are defined by class-constrained 3D connectivity in the optimised global frame, assigning global IDs without frame-to-frame identity propagation that drifts over long runs.
  • Rejected all four false loop candidates on TUM RGB-D and improved instance identity consistency on long ScanNet sequences.
Show abstract
To address unreliable cross-view association in long-sequence feed-forward dense SLAM, this letter presents V3DSAM, an open-vocabulary semantic dense SLAM system that establishes physical correspondences at both the scene and object levels. The system combines feed-forward geometric estimates from VGGT-SLAM 2.0 with open-vocabulary semantic observations from SAM3. We introduce semantic-geometric dual-scale loop verification, which identifies false loop closures using pixel-level bidirectional reprojection and object-level 3D spatial correspondences, without accessing backbone-internal features. We further propose geometry-anchored persistent instance mapping, which defines physical instances through class-constrained 3D connectivity in an optimized global coordinate system and assigns global IDs without inter-frame identity propagation. The proposed verifier rejects all four false loop candidates in our TUM RGB-D evaluation, while long-sequence ScanNet experiments show improved instance identity consistency. Thus, V3DSAM reduces dependence on internal backbone features and temporal ID propagation for robust long-term cross-view association.

AutoVLA-D: Learn a Trajectory-Geometric Condition from Reasoning for Autonomous Driving VLA

2026-11-09cs.ROcs.CV👤 Yuguang Yang (h=n/a)
Yuguang Yang, Zhewen Tan, Canyu Chen, Linlin Yang, Cheng Chi
Core Contributions
  • Notes that driving instructions describe actions ("slow down, merge left") while VLAs output ego-state trajectories, weakening conditioning; TrajCond adds an intermediate discrete trajectory-geometric token sequence.
  • Encodes it as a 1D differential action trajectory learned in a two-stage scheme, then generates discrete and continuous trajectories autoregressively together (hence 'Double').
  • Finding: discrete trajectories are worse as final outputs but better as a condition, consistently improving continuous prediction on NAVSIM navtest, nuScenes and NeuroNCAP in open and closed loop.
Show abstract
Autoregressive VLA planning usually predicts continuous trajectories as the final output, but driving instructions often describe actions rather than ego states, which weakens direct conditioning on the final continuous trajectory. We address this gap with TrajCond, which introduces a discrete trajectory-geometric condition for VLA planning. Specifically, we represent TrajCond as a 1D-Brohan differential action trajectory and learn it with a two-stage training framework before final continuous trajectory prediction. With the simultaneous autoregressive generation of discrete and numeric trajectories, we name our method AutoVLA-D (Double). Experiments on NAVSIM navtest, nuScenes, and NeuroNCAP show that discrete trajectories are weaker as direct outputs but more effective as TrajCond, consistently improving final continuous trajectory prediction across closed-loop and open-loop driving benchmarks. Code is available at https://github.com/ShivaTeam/AutoVLA-D.

Rethinking Radar 3D Perception: Representation Learning from Raw Spectra

2026-11-09cs.AIcs.CVcs.LGcs.RO👤 Miao Zhang (h=0)
Miao Zhang, Mohamed Abdelsamad, Bin Yang, Sherif Abdulatif, Benedikt Loesch
Core Contributions
  • Challenges the consensus that raw radar cubes are too noisy to learn from: the problem is the missing objective, not the representation, since handcrafted reductions irreversibly discard weak structure.
  • Defines signal as what remains reconstructable under localised corruption, and instantiates this as a Leakage-aware Spectral MAE whose masking mimics cross-bin spectral-leakage energy spreading.
  • Shows that organising data as x-y-Doppler with height as features substantially beats naive channel stacking for dense 4D spectra.
  • Outperforms state-of-the-art radar detectors and point-cloud SSL counterparts, positioning raw spectra as a first-class input for all-weather 3D perception.
Show abstract
Raw radar spectra are widely viewed as too cluttered and noisy for direct representation learning, motivating handcrafted reductions, sparse point clouds, or compact Doppler descriptors in radar perception. We challenge this assumption and argue that the main limitation lies not in the raw representation itself, but in the absence of a learning objective that separates structured signal from noise. Handcrafted reductions partially address this problem but impose rigid assumptions about what constitutes signal, discarding weak yet informative structure while retaining artifacts that fit the reduction model. Once removed, such information cannot be recovered by supervised learning. We propose an alternative: structure is the component of a radar spectrum that remains reconstructable under localized corruption, while noise is the component that does not. In ultrasound-guided femoral access, probe pressure deforms the tissue and changes the vascular anatomy a robot observes. A simulator must reproduce these mechanics fast enough for robotic interaction, yet the computational effort needed for a clinically meaningful signal like vein collapse before artery is unclear. We test the feasibility of a physics-based simulation in NVIDIA's Isaac Sim for Healthcare, with Stable Neo-Hookean tissue solved by Vertex Block Descent on one GPU. A simulation of a low-fidelity cylindrical benchtop phantom with known geometry and mechanical properties, pressed by a robot, predicted its artery within 5 percentage points at every depth. It matched the vein within 3 points through 11 mm of a 25.4 mm press and followed the collapse behavior of the low-fidelity physical phantom. Across element size, time step, and solver iterations, wall clock time per frame scaled linearly with solver work, but accuracy did not improve with more work. The chosen setting reduced solver time per frame by a factor of 976, from 29,700 ms to 30.4 ms, while remaining within 1.03 points of the finest reference setting. The physics solver alone took 30.4 ms per frame, while the full stack, including ultrasound and graphics rendering, took 56.4 ms per frame. Finally, when seeded with realistic literature-based mechanical properties, the digital leg model compressed the vein far more than the artery at every setting, reproducing the expected clinical signal of vein collapse before artery, which is used to distinguish them. In total, these results suggest that the NVIDIA Isaac Sim platform may be suitable for augmenting reinforcement learning training or online simulation for autonomous femoral access with ultrasound.
