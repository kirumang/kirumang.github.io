Research Landscape

The clearest signal in this batch is that World Action Models (WAMs) are moving from "bolt a video model onto a policy" to engineering the interface between imagination and action. Three papers each target a different seam. LaST0.5 (#1) argues that global world-model features are the wrong thing to hand to an action head and inserts an interaction-centric latent expert supervised by robot-object contact cues, reaching 99.5% on LIBERO after pretraining on 14k hours. MetaWAM (#4) reframes how proprioception and actions enter a pretrained video diffusion prior as a distribution-alignment problem, packing all robot signals into one DCT-band "meta-frame" and cutting latency by 39%. Elastic-WAM (#16) attacks the cost side, learning where and how long to keep denoising the imagined future. Around them sit two papers that adapt pretrained models without touching their weights: residual corrections for frozen latent world models under physics shifts (#8, 90-100% real-robot recovery from three episodes) and ExRL (#29), which RL-tunes only how long to execute each action chunk. UGG (#5) extends the same generative-completion logic to 4D geometry, treating depth and pose prediction as conditional generation.

A second theme is human data as the scalable supervision source, approached at three levels of abstraction. DexSeed (#12) keeps human grasps but re-synthesises everything else in physics to produce executable dexterous VLA demos; Ego Guidance / SPAR (#28) skips actions entirely and uses future-frame prediction on egocentric video as an auxiliary signal that scales with data; and the Ego2Robot survey (#22) provides the taxonomy that places these on a spectrum from explicit embodiment transfer to latent dynamics modelling. Read together, the open question is how much physical grounding must be added back after leaving the human video - DexSeed adds a lot, SPAR adds almost none.

Finally, several papers converge on a sobering point: what predicts well is not necessarily what decides well or acts safely. The deformable-rope study (#19) shows estimated physical parameters improve next-shape prediction but give no control gain, while interaction history does the reverse. Safe Depth (#27) finds an off-the-shelf Depth Anything V2 stack collides in 63% of episodes and fixes it with conformally-calibrated one-sided bounds (3% collisions). LEASH/RoboRisk (#24) reports high harm rates for every frontier foundation model driving robots and adds task/action/commit gates; AST (#17) monitors whether information gathering is actually progressing; and L2P-DR-Forest (#7) offers distributionally robust guarantees for nonlinear planners. On the systems side, real-time budgets dominate: HiPFuse (#2) on a Jetson, FAME-QP's CUDA-graph QP solver (#20), a 25 Hz GP-augmented MPCC on an AUV (#10), and a 10 ms excavator state estimator (#21).

LaST0.5: A Generalist World Action Model with Interaction-Centric Latent Reasoning

2026-11-10cs.RO👤 Pheng-Ann Heng (h=105)
Xuheng Zhang, Yiming Zhang, Jialin Gao, Hao Chen, Jiaming Liu
Core Contributions
  • Identifies a representation mismatch in existing WAMs: features optimised to predict how the whole scene evolves under-weight the local robot-object contacts that actually determine grasp and placement success.
  • Inserts a third 'latent expert' between world and action experts in a Mixture-of-Transformers, supervised by visual interaction cues, so action generation conditions on a compact contact-centric state instead of raw world features.
  • Adds closed-loop self-checking: jointly predicted progress values are compared with imagined futures, and inconsistency triggers re-sampling or adjusted execution - a cheap form of plan monitoring inside the policy.
  • Pretrained on a 14k-hour robot corpus; hits 99.5% on LIBERO and beats prior SOTA on ten RLBench tasks, with real-world long-horizon and dexterous demos.
Show abstract
World Action Models (WAMs) have recently emerged as a promising paradigm for robotic manipulation, endowing policies with strong capabilities for modeling temporal and physical dynamics. Existing WAMs, encompassing both video-first action prediction and joint video-action modeling, directly transfer global physical representations from world models to action generation. However, global scene representations may underemphasize local robot-object interactions, creating a mismatch between representations optimized for world evolution and those required for manipulation. To bridge this gap, we propose LaST0.5, a novel WAM that introduces an interaction-centric physical representation between world modeling and action generation. Specifically, LaST0.5 learns a compact interaction-centric latent space under the supervision of visual interaction cues between the robot and the environment. This reasoning space is instantiated as a latent expert that bridges the world expert and the action expert within a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture. Furthermore, LaST0.5 performs adaptive world-to-action transfer by jointly predicting future representations and progress values, using progress consistency to adjust action execution and trigger future resampling. Finally, we pretrain LaST0.5 on a large-scale robotic data corpus comprising over 14k hours of data, establishing interaction-centric physical reasoning as a scalable and effective paradigm. LaST0.5 achieves 99.5% on LIBERO, surpasses the previous state of the art on ten evaluated RLBench tasks, and demonstrates robust long-horizon execution and precise dexterous manipulation in real-world experiments.
#4 h=53

From Video Frames to Robot Meta-Frames: A Unified Latent Interface for World Action Models

2026-11-10cs.CVcs.RO👤 M. Shou (h=53)
Guoqiang Liang, Yiqi Lin, Yanzhe Chen, Kevin Yuchen Ma, Mike Zheng Shou
Core Contributions
  • Recasts how robot signals (proprioception, actions, values) are injected into a pretrained video diffusion model as a distribution-alignment question rather than a formatting choice.
  • Introduces a useful diagnostic: the frozen denoiser's relative reconstruction error measures how far an encoding sits from the video prior - spatial encodings are far off, frequency-domain packing is close.
  • MetaWAM packs every signal into disjoint DCT bands of a single 'meta-frame' via inverse DCT, needing no new encoders/decoders or architecture changes; slot-aware routing lets one frame serve policy, world-model and value heads.
  • Collapsing per-signal frames into one cuts inference latency by 39% while reaching 68.9% on RoboCasa and 85.0% on three real Franka tasks.
Show abstract
Pretrained video diffusion models (VDMs) offer a powerful foundation for World Action Models (WAMs), which jointly predict robot actions and future observations. Building such models requires injecting low-dimensional robot signals (e.g. proprioception, actions, and values) into a latent space shaped by natural videos, and a key design choice is the interface through which these signals enter: existing schemes treat it as a formatting detail, spending a separate latent frame on each signal or bolting on dedicated encoders and decoders. We argue that signal injection is instead a distribution-alignment problem: how well a representation matches the prior of the pretrained VDMs during denoising. Using the relative reconstruction error of a frozen VDM denoiser as a probe, we find that conventional spatial encodings sit far off this prior, whereas frequency-domain packing yields latents that the denoiser corrects far less. Motivated by this diagnostic result, we propose MetaWAM, which folds all robot signals into a single unified meta-frame: each signal is assigned to a disjoint frequency-domain channel band and lifted to the spatial latent by an inverse DCT, yielding a decodable interface aligned with the pretrained VDM prior without any added module or architectural change. A fixed band layout together with slot-aware routing lets the same meta-frame support policy, world-model, and value prediction over a shared set of robot variables. On RoboCasa, MetaWAM reaches 68.9% success while collapsing the per-signal frames into one and cutting inference latency by 39%, and attains 85.0% success on three real-world Franka tasks. Code and models are available at https://github.com/showlab/MetaWAM
#16 h=13

Elastic-WAM: Adaptive Spatiotemporal Imagination for Efficient Robot Control

2026-11-10cs.ROcs.CV👤 Hao-Nan Chen (h=13)
Haitian Li, Tianfu Li, Xuxi Zhao, Zizheng Liu, Yangcheng Yu
Core Contributions
  • Observes that the useful amount of future-video denoising differs across tasks and states, and that only some image regions matter for the next action - so fixed refinement budgets waste compute.
  • Learns a spatiotemporal 'refinement demand field' (shared temporal basis + spatial amplitudes) that decides when to stop refining, how far each denoising step jumps, and which regions to refresh.
  • Trains the field with a three-stage, action-grounded supervision so compute is allocated by its effect on action quality, not on visual fidelity.
  • Complements MetaWAM (#4): both reduce WAM latency, one by shrinking the interface, the other by sparsifying imagination; reported to improve success while lowering latency on sim and real long-horizon tasks.
Show abstract
World-Action Models improve robot control by predicting future visual dynamics, but iterative video denoising incurs substantial inference overhead. Recent approaches reduce this cost through partial future denoising, yet typically rely on fixed refinement budgets. Our empirical observations reveal that preferred refinement depths vary across tasks and states, while different future regions exhibit varying relevance to action generation. Motivated by these findings, we propose Elastic-WAM, which adaptively allocates world refinement computation across temporal denoising time and spatial future space. A unified spatiotemporal refinement demand field governs when to stop world refinement, how far to advance each denoising step, and which regions to refresh. We parameterize this field with a shared temporal basis and spatial amplitudes, and learn it through a three-stage action-grounded supervised procedure. At inference, the learned field guides recurrent sparse world refinement to improve task success while reducing inference overhead. Evaluations across diverse simulation benchmarks and real-world long-horizon manipulation demonstrate state-of-the-art control performance with reduced inference latency.

Few-Shot Residual Adaptation of Pretrained Latent World Models

2026-11-10cs.RO👤 Rudolf Lioutikov (h=23)
Jakub Suliga, Pankhuri Vanjani, Nisarga Nilavadi, Wolfram Burgard, Rudolf Lioutikov
Core Contributions
  • Asks whether a pretrained latent world model can survive physics shifts (centre-of-mass, friction) without fine-tuning its weights or explicitly identifying the changed parameters.
  • Learns self-supervised residual corrections from latent prediction errors and applies them at two sites - inside the predictor and on its output latent - leaving the base model frozen.
  • Frozen-model success drops to near zero under the shifts; with just three adaptation episodes the dual-site method recovers 90% (CoM) and 100% (friction) on a real robot, and both sites beat either alone.
  • Diagnostics suggest much of the correction fixes a generic sim-to-real prediction mismatch, while shift-specific information is what makes control succeed - a useful caution for interpreting adaptation gains.
Show abstract
Pretrained latent world models can support visual planning and control, but their predictions may fail when physical dynamics change at deployment. We study whether such models can adapt from only a few interactions without modifying their pretrained weights or identifying the changed physical parameters. We introduce a few-shot residual adaptation approach that learns self-supervised corrections from latent prediction errors. The corrections are applied both inside the latent predictor and directly to its predicted latent state, while the pretrained world model remains frozen. We evaluate the approach in simulation and on a real robot under center-of-mass and friction shifts. These changes reduce the success of the frozen model to near zero. Using three interaction episodes for adaptation, our dual-site approach recovers 90\% success under the center-of-mass shift and 100\% under the modified friction on the real robot. Ablations show that correcting both locations outperforms single-site adaptation. Our diagnostics further suggest that much of the learned correction reduces a general prediction mismatch, while successful control also depends on shift-specific information captured during adaptation.
#29 h=9

ExRL: Sample-Efficient Online Adaptation of Long-Chunk Policies via Execution Length Control

2026-11-10cs.RO👤 Zi-Jin Hong (h=9)
Xu Luo, Jiaying Yang, Hao Wu, Junlin Xie, Zijin Hong
Core Contributions
  • Shows that RL fine-tuning of long-chunk policies (dozens of steps per chunk) is sample-hungry because the optimised action space is huge.
  • Key reframing: adapt how long to execute each predicted chunk rather than the actions themselves, a low-dimensional control knob whose expressivity grows with chunk length.
  • Implemented as a lightweight adapter trained with off-policy RL on top of an untouched base policy, so it applies to many policy families with minimal per-task tuning.
  • Reports sample-efficient improvements in both simulation benchmarks and real-robot tasks - practical where online robot interaction is expensive.
Show abstract
Predicting long action chunks, often spanning dozens of timesteps, has become a key ingredient in increasingly capable robot policies. In this paper, we find that adapting these long-chunk policies with existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods can require many online samples due to the high dimensionality of the action chunks being optimized, making such adaptation impractical in real-world scenarios where interaction data are costly to collect. Our key insight is to shift adaptation from optimizing actions to optimizing how long to execute each predicted action chunk. This keeps the adaptation space low-dimensional while its expressivity grows with the chunk size. We thus introduce Execution at the Right Length (ExRL), a simple approach which augments the base policy with a lightweight adapter that predicts the execution length of each chunk and is trained using off-policy RL algorithms. Across simulation benchmarks and real-world robotic tasks, ExRL enables sample-efficient adaptation of a diverse range of long-chunk policies with minimal task-specific hyperparameter tuning.

UGG: Unified Generative Geometry Model

2026-11-10cs.CVcs.LGcs.RO👤 Yilun Du (h=50)
Amani Kiruga, Zhiyi Li, Ruojin Cai, Hansen Lillemark, Xiaoshan Wu
Core Contributions
  • Unifies two usually separate camps - feedforward geometry predictors and generative video models - into a single model over RGB video, depth and camera pose.
  • Assigns different noise levels per modality and per frame, turning generation, depth estimation and pose prediction into variants of one conditional-completion task.
  • This lets it train on heterogeneous datasets that each label only some modalities, sidestepping the need for fully annotated 4D data.
  • Generative sampling enables inference-time search over scene hypotheses scored against geometric evidence from observed frames, improving geometry without ground truth - especially under sparse views.
Show abstract
Understanding 4D scenes is a core challenge in visual intelligence, requiring models to jointly capture scene geometry, appearance, and dynamics from partial observations. However, existing approaches are subdivided into feedforward models for direct geometric prediction, and generative models for visual synthesis. We introduce UGG, a unified framework for 4D scene understanding that jointly models RGB video and geometric representations such as depth and camera pose. By assigning different noise levels across modalities and time, UGG formulates visual generation and geometric prediction as conditional completion within a single generative model. This flexible formulation enables the model to learn from diverse, heterogeneous datasets with different subsets of geometric and visual annotations. Furthermore, our generative formulation enables inference-time search over multiple plausible scene hypotheses, using geometric evidence extracted from observed RGB frames to improve geometric prediction without ground truth geometry. We demonstrate UGG's effectiveness over non-unified baselines across camera-controlled video generation, depth estimation, and camera pose prediction, including in challenging sparse observation settings. Code and visualizations are available at https://ugg-model.github.io/

DexSeed: Expanding Human Hand-Object Interactions into Robot Demonstrations for Dexterous Policy Learning

2026-11-10cs.RO👤 Taku Komura (h=17)
Huaijin Pi, Zehong Shen, Yong Wang, Kechun Xu, Changpeng Wang
Core Contributions
  • Targets the data bottleneck for dexterous VLAs: instead of costly teleop, it expands existing human hand-object sequences to new objects and locations rather than merely replaying them.
  • Groups sources by grasp mode with shared language, uses InteractionMesh retargeting plus arm-trajectory augmentation to preserve local hand-object relations across geometries.
  • Splits execution: an RL finger policy produces physically valid contacts while the arm tracks precomputed trajectories, so only successful, executable rollouts become training data.
  • A VLA fine-tuned solely on DexSeed data executes language-specified grasps in sim and on real hardware, with better contact fidelity than retargeting baselines.
Show abstract
We study how to generate diverse, physically executable robot demonstrations for dexterous vision-language-action (VLA) learning. Existing demonstration-generation methods typically rely on costly teleoperated examples. Meanwhile, human hand--object datasets provide a reusable source, but existing transfer methods mainly reproduce given interactions. Turning these interactions into VLA training data further requires coverage of different objects and manipulation locations, together with executable arm and finger actions, and visual observations. To achieve this, we present DexSeed, a framework for low-cost generation of dexterous robot data from human hand--object interactions. Our key idea is to expand human sequences while preserving their grasps and realizing the resulting motions under arm-tracking dynamics in physics simulation. Specifically, we organize source sequences by grasp mode with shared language descriptions. InteractionMesh-based retargeting and arm trajectory augmentation adapt these sequences to the robot hand, different object geometries, and pick-and-place locations while preserving local hand--object relationships. An RL finger-control policy produces executable rollouts, while the arm tracks precomputed trajectories. Rendered observations and recorded actions from successful rollouts are paired with grasp descriptions to fine-tune a VLA. Experiments show improved contact fidelity in retargeting and more reliable whole-arm execution than the evaluated baselines. Simulation evaluation and real-world deployment further demonstrate that a VLA fine-tuned solely on the generated demonstrations can execute language-specified grasps. Project page: https://robbyant-research.github.io/DexSeed/.

2026-11-10cs.ROcs.AIcs.CV👤 Qi-Xiang Ye (h=9)
Lin Long, Tianren Ma, Zhong Chu, Di Wu, Maosong Cao
Core Contributions
  • Studies how much egocentric human video helps robot policies when no action labels are extracted from it at all.
  • Proposes SPAR, a Mixture-of-Transformers on a pretrained VLM where future visual-state prediction runs alongside action prediction as an independent, complementary learning signal.
  • Finds representations shaped by egocentric video improve out-of-distribution robot tasks, and gains continue as video volume scales - evidence for action-free video as a scalable pretraining source.

Show abstract
In this paper, we investigate how human egocentric videos can benefit robot policy learning without action supervision. Building on a pretrained VLM, we propose Shared Prediction on Action and Representation (SPAR), an MoT architecture in which future visual state prediction can provide independent and complementary learning signals alongside action prediction. We find that representations learned from egocentric videos improve downstream performance on out-of-distribution robot tasks, with further gains as video data scales. We also study the effects of objective choices and data curation, providing insights into how to leverage action-free videos for scalable robot learning.

From Egocentric Human Video to Robot Manipulation in the Era of Embodied Intelligence

2026-11-10cs.RO👤 Zhao-Shuo Li (h=12)
Yi Wang, Jingqiu Wu, Lei Yao, Zhaoshuo Li, Haoyu Zhu
Core Contributions
  • Defines 'Ego2Robot' learning and organises the field into four paradigms: explicit embodiment transfer, explicit action/task supervision, egocentric representation pretraining, and latent action/dynamics modelling.
  • For each paradigm traces what is extracted from human video, how the robot policy consumes it, and when extra robot data is still needed - a useful lens for papers like #12 and #28 in this batch.
  • Surveys datasets spanning egocentric video, robot demos and simulated resources.
  • Flags open problems in physical feasibility, multi-agent coordination, in-context adaptation, controlled evaluation and predictive control.
Show abstract
Egocentric human video (EHV) records close-range hand-object interaction (HOI) and the temporal structure of everyday activities, offering a potentially scalable source of experience for robot manipulation. Using this experience for robot control requires recovering useful supervision without recorded robot actions and accounting for differences in morphology, viewpoint, and action space. We define Ego2Robot learning as the process of learning or adapting robot manipulation policies from EHV, organized into four learning paradigms: explicit embodiment transfer, explicit action and task supervision, egocentric video representation pretraining, and latent action and dynamics modeling. The four paradigms differ in what they derive from human video: robot-format demonstrations, interpretable supervision, visual representations, or predictive models of actions and dynamics. For each paradigm, we trace what EHV information is extracted, how robot policies use it, and when the additional data is required. Besides, we review the benchmark datasets that cover EHV, robot demonstrations, and synthetic and simulated resources. This paper links EHV data's contribution to the task information preserved and the robot-specific grounding used during training or deployment. Finally, we identify challenges in physical feasibility, agent coordination, in-context adaptation, controlled evaluation, and predictive control.

From Prediction to Action: Learning Physical Context for Deformable Linear Object Manipulation

2026-11-10cs.RO👤 Alessio Caporali (h=12)
Chengxiao Dong, Alessio Caporali, Hongyu Lan, Gianluca Palli
Core Contributions
  • Separates two notions often conflated: predictive utility (does a representation forecast DLO shape?) and decision utility (does it lead to better actions?).
  • Introduces Geometry-Pointer-Flow, which decouples grasp-point selection from grasp-conditioned motion; geometry-only GPF cuts executed shape error by 32.8% vs a DLO diffusion policy with 18.7x fewer parameters and ~193x faster inference.
  • Ground-truth physical parameters reduce next-shape error by 15.8%, and estimated parameters predict slightly better than interaction history - yet estimated parameters give no control gain, while history-conditioned GPF reduces executed error by 9.9% over 470 paired trials.
  • Trained only in simulation, the history-conditioned variant transfers zero-shot best to hardware - arguing physical context should be judged by the decisions it supports.
Show abstract
A representation that accurately predicts a deformable object's response is not necessarily the one most useful for choosing manipulation actions. We study this distinction between predictive utility and decision utility in planar single-grasp shape control of Deformable Linear Objects (DLOs). As a common decision backbone, we introduce Geometry-Pointer-Flow (GPF), a geometry-grounded policy that separates grasp-location selection from grasp-conditioned motion generation. Its geometry-only variant reduces one-step executed shape error by 32.8% relative to a DLO-specific diffusion policy, while using 18.7 times fewer parameters and achieving approximately 193 times faster inference. On this backbone, we compare physical context represented by ground-truth parameters, estimated parameters, and learned interaction-history representations, for both forward prediction and executed control. For prediction, ground-truth physical parameters reduce next-shape error by 15.8% over geometry alone, and estimated parameters are slightly more predictive than interaction history. This ordering does not carry over to control: estimated parameters provide no detectable one-step control gain, whereas history-conditioned GPF reduces executed error by 9.9% relative to geometry-only GPF across 470 paired simulation trials. In closed loop, interaction history improves strict-threshold attainment in simulation and yields the strongest zero-shot performance on hardware. All policies are trained exclusively in simulation and transferred to the physical robot without real-world training or fine-tuning. These results show that physical context should be evaluated by the decisions it supports rather than by predictive accuracy alone. Code will be released upon acceptance.

HiPFuse: Real-Time LiDAR Semantic Segmentation for Robotic Perception on Edge Devices

2026-11-10cs.CVcs.RO👤 G. Vosselman (h=63)
Haiyang Wu, George Vosselman, Ville Lehtola
Core Contributions
  • Addresses a known failure of BEV LiDAR segmentation: collapsing a vertical column mixes differently labelled surfaces (e.g. road under a car).
  • Fuses point features with height-partitioned BEV features across horizontal scales, with fixed height groups and auxiliary supervision from the same 3D cells, plus a coarser dual-scale context module.
  • Runs above the 10 Hz scan rate on Jetson AGX Orin: 63.7% mIoU at 14.2 FPS (SemanticKITTI) and 62.5% at 22.4 FPS (MCD).
  • Shows downstream value: swapping only the semantic front end cuts odometry ATE RMSE by 10.6% and raises accepted loop-closure recall by 8.7 pp.
Show abstract
Real-time robotic perception requires accurate LiDAR semantics under limited resources. Bird's-eye-view (BEV) aggregation is efficient but can mix differently labeled surfaces in columns. We present HiPFuse, a point-BEV fusion framework that exchanges point and height-partitioned BEV features across horizontal scales, combining point-wise detail with 2D spatial context from one LiDAR scan. Fixed height assignments preserve separate vertical aggregation groups, with auxiliary supervision of initial BEV features using targets from the same 3D cells. A dual-scale BEV context module adds broader context from a coarser grid. On SemanticKITTI and MCD, HiPFuse achieves 63.7% and 62.5% mIoU at 14.2 and 22.4 FPS on Jetson AGX Orin, respectively, exceeding the 10 Hz scan rate. Separate training and evaluation show indoor applicability. Replacing only the baseline semantic front end with HiPFuse in fixed robotic systems reduces odometry absolute trajectory error RMSE by 10.6% on average across two outdoor sequences and increases accepted-loop recall by 8.7 percentage points on SemanticKITTI. Qualitative semantic maps show more continuous road labeling. Our code is available at https://github.com/haiyang2022/HiPFuse.

From Depth to Safe Depth: Uncertainty-Aware Monocular Perception for Mobile Robot Navigation

2026-11-10cs.ROcs.CV👤 S. Ayat (h=9)
Saeid Firouzi Daghigh, Saeed Ayat
Core Contributions
  • Demonstrates a concrete hazard: Nav2 driven by off-the-shelf Depth Anything V2 (metric) collides in 63% of 120 episodes because it systematically overestimates distance to nearby obstacles.
  • Adds a 0.58M-parameter head on the frozen depth model that predicts a per-pixel log-depth correction and uncertainty, and feeds the planner a one-sided 'safe depth' bound whose margin is set by conformal calibration.
  • Halves AbsRel (0.262 to 0.134), reduces near-range overestimation from 59.8% to 12.0%, and lifts navigation success from 37% to 70% with collisions falling from 63% to 3% (ground-truth depth: 2%).
  • A constant margin with matched coverage reaches only 30% success because the robot freezes - uncertainty-aware margins win 52 of 56 discordant episodes, including on glass and textureless walls.
Show abstract
Monocular depth foundation models make it tempting to replace depth sensors on mobile robots with a single RGB camera. We show that, used off the shelf, this is unsafe: in a simulated indoor navigation benchmark, a standard Nav2 stack driven by Depth Anything V2 (metric) collides in 63\% of 120 episodes, because the model systematically over-estimates the distance to nearby obstacles. We propose a lightweight plug-in module that turns predicted depth into \emph{safe depth}. A 0.58\,M-parameter head on top of the \emph{frozen} depth model predicts, per pixel, a log-depth correction $μ$ and an uncertainty $σ$, trained with a $β$-weighted Gaussian negative log-likelihood. The navigation stack consumes the one-sided bound $\hat d_{\text{safe}}=d\,e^{μ-kσ}$, where $k$ is set by conformal calibration on a held-out world, and uncertainty also decides which pixels may be treated as floor. The correction halves the depth error (AbsRel $0.262\rightarrow0.134$) and reduces near-range over-estimation from 59.8\% to 12.0\%. In navigation, safe depth raises the success rate from 37\% to 70\% and lowers collisions from 63\% to 3\%, close to the 2\% of ground-truth depth. A constant safety margin calibrated to the same pixel coverage reaches only 30\% success because it freezes the robot; uncertainty-aware safety wins 52 of 56 discordant paired episodes against it. Gains hold on glass, texture-less walls and dark regions.

Differentiable Filtering for Inertial Odometry

2026-11-10cs.ROcs.CL👤 Ting Zou (h=15)
Yaru Gu, Ting Zou
Core Contributions
  • Unlike TLIO-style pipelines where the neural displacement network is trained separately from the filter it feeds, DFIO backpropagates through the error-state Kalman filter update.

Co-speech gestures can increase engagement and improve speech understanding. Yet, most data-driven robot systems generate either rhythmic beat-like motion or semantically meaningful iconic gestures, but not both. We present an emotion-aware rhythmic-semantic co-speech gesture system that generates iconic and beat behaviors in real time from text and the robot's internal emotional state. We formalize the task of beat-iconic gesture generation using emotion and propose lightweight models that predict iconic timing and intensity, then fill the remaining spans with emotion-conditioned beats at an appropriate intensity level. We train on the BEAT2 dataset and deploy the system on the social robot Haru. In an online user study with 60 participants, our method shows stronger speech-gesture alignment and is preferred over baseline methods.
