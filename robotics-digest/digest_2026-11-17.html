🔭 Research Landscape

The headline theme today is putting guarantees around generative policies without retraining them. Three of the top-ranked papers share a recipe: keep a learned flow or diffusion prior, and add a separate mechanism at sampling time that enforces what the data cannot. Task Convergent Flow Matching (#1) searches over flow source seeds with CMA-ES so each action chunk provably makes net task progress, lifting PushT success from 31.3% to 88.5%; Barrier-Guided Diffusion (#4) shows that team-level cooperation can be produced from single-agent demonstrations alone, because capability constraints determine which trajectories are possible while the data only decides how likely they are; and Sim2CBF (#12) builds implicit control barrier functions from forward rollouts of a simulator or world model, removing the need for model derivatives. WoRD (#24) applies the same steer-the-sampler idea in reverse, using guided denoising to infer an agent's goal. Together they suggest a maturing division of labor: generative models supply the motion prior, while physics, constraints or rollouts supply correctness.

A second thread asks what limits learned policies in practice, and the answers are increasingly about systems rather than architectures. The Maniformer team's study of hundreds of thousands of hours of real data (#21) finds that policies keep about 90% of operators' detours and that inference-time miscalibration causes all 30 trials to fail, so collection-system design directly shapes behavior. VoxelSBFL (#17) borrows fault localization from software testing to map failure-prone workspace regions of VLAs, and the cross-embodiment work (#25) supervises unseen robot-skill pairs through tool-space geometry instead of joint labels. On the compute side, VSpark (#29) brings speculative decoding to autoregressive VLAs (up to 2.10x, lossless) and the JEPA planner-accelerator co-design (#26) makes the planner manufacture bit-level sparsity for hardware to exploit, while JointGV (#2) corrects world-model drift with a parallel scene graph, halving pole-angle error on a real pendulum.

Software-engineering thinking is visibly entering robotics. The survey on agentic intelligence for the physical world (#15) argues that the world becomes the test suite and calls for a physical analogue of continuous integration; BehaVerify (#22) delivers formal LTL and CTL checking for behavior trees deployed on ROS; DailyBrick (#23) uses a meta-reviewer agent that replays and repairs construction programs; and CueClash (#28) shows that frontier VLMs including Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-5.5 lean on 2-D picture cues for 3-D relations, with 18 to 37 point shortcut gaps. In driving, CaFe (#16) makes a similar causal-evaluation point by showing that 23 to 83% of nearby agents in language-conditioned simulators react to instructions they could not have observed. The field is investing as much in how robot behavior is checked as in how it is generated.

Task Convergent Flow Matching for Generative Robotics

2026-11-17 cs.ROcs.LG 👤 D. Rus (h=139)
Lorenzo Paiola, Maximilian Stölzle, Giorgio Grioli, Daniela Rus, Cosimo Della Santina
Core Contributions
  • Targets a failure that imitation metrics miss: generative policies can emit plausible chunks that stall or undo earlier progress, so the paper requires each chunk to satisfy a task-level 'performance funnel' that tolerates bounded regressions but demands net progress.
  • Proves sufficient conditions under which repeated funnel satisfaction implies convergence, and uses interval-wise Lipschitz bounds to certify continuous-time satisfaction from discrete rollouts, turning a heuristic search into a certified one.
  • Searches over the flow's source noise with CMA-ES inside radial quantile bands, so the learned motion prior is preserved: only 11.3% of executed windows are out of distribution versus 34.1% for unconstrained search.
  • Inference-time only, works for Euclidean and Riemannian (pose-valued) flow policies, and raises PushT success from 31.3% to 88.5% and three-cube stacking from 77.0% to 86.0%.
Show abstract
Successful manipulation requires the manipulated objects to progress toward a desired task outcome. Generative policies can produce plausible robot motions without ensuring this progress, allowing successive action chunks to stall or undo earlier changes to the scene. We introduce a task-centric inference-time refinement framework for flow matching policies that evaluates candidate action chunks through parallel physics-based rollouts and searches for motions satisfying sufficient conditions for task convergence. Task-level performance funnels bound temporary regressions while requiring net progress over each chunk. We establish sufficient conditions under which repeated funnel satisfaction yields convergence throughout execution, and use interval-wise Lipschitz bounds to verify continuous-time funnel satisfaction from sampled rollouts. To accommodate contact-rich hybrid dynamics, we refine the flow's source seeds using derivative-free Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES), constrained to radial quantile bands of the source distribution to help retain the learned motion prior. This formulation supports both Euclidean and pose-valued Riemannian Flow Matching (RMFM) policies. On PushT and three-cube stacking, refinement raises success from 31.3% to 88.5% and from 77.0% to 86.0%. On PushT, whose coverage score responds continuously to the motion of the block, a learned funnel lowers the average maximum loss of achieved progress by 19% relative to constraining the endpoint of each chunk alone, at a similar success rate. With the radial band, 11.3% of the executed motion windows are out of distribution, a rate within the range of held-out demonstrations, compared with 34.1% for an unconstrained search.
#4 h=71

Capability Composition in Heterogeneous Teams via Barrier-Guided Diffusion Sampling

2026-11-17 cs.ROcs.LGcs.MAeess.SY 👤 M. Egerstedt (h=71)
Diana Laura Morales, Ruoyu Lin, Magnus Egerstedt, Fabio Pasqualetti
Core Contributions
  • Addresses a data gap in multi-agent learning: cooperative demonstrations are expensive or nonexistent for new team configurations, but the physical conditions for combining capabilities are known in advance.
  • Encodes those conditions as barrier constraints enforced while sampling from diffusion models trained only on individual agents, with a proof that every constraint-satisfying team trajectory remains reachable.
  • Gives a clean separation of roles: constraints decide which team behaviors are possible, demonstrations decide how likely each is.
  • Agents can join or leave without retraining, and the approach is demonstrated on a physical multi-robot testbed (Egerstedt and Pasqualetti groups).
Show abstract
The agents in a heterogeneous team can cooperate to accomplish tasks that exceed their individual capabilities. The team trajectories for a cooperative task can be generated by a generative model, provided that the model has been trained on demonstrations of the agents cooperating. Yet, demonstrations of cooperation are costly and may not exist for tasks that the team has never performed. The conditions under which the agents can combine their capabilities, however, are physical properties of the team and are known before any demonstration is collected. We encode the conditions as constraints on the team trajectories, and we enforce the constraints while sampling from generative models trained on demonstrations of each agent alone. We prove that the proposed method can generate every team trajectory that satisfies the constraints, although the demonstrations contain no cooperation. Hence the constraints determine which team trajectories can be generated, and the demonstrations determine how likely each team trajectory is. Numerical results on two cooperative tasks show the effectiveness of the method, which generates cooperative team trajectories that satisfy the constraints and requires no retraining when agents join or leave the team. Finally, we implement the method on a multi-robot testbed.

Sim2CBF: From Forward Simulations to Implicit Control Barrier Function Safety Filtering without Model Derivatives

2026-11-17 cs.ROeess.SY 👤 Jana Tumova (h=28)
Luzia Knoedler, Yihan Liu, Shaohang Han, Yifei Dong, Jana Tumova
Core Contributions
  • Makes CBF safety filters usable with models that cannot be differentiated, such as physics simulators or learned world models, by solving the filter using only forward evaluations.
  • Approximates the CBF constraint via finite differences and local linear interpolation over sampled controls while keeping failure-avoidance guarantees.
  • The barrier itself is implicit, built from backup-policy rollouts, so no hand-designed safe set is needed.
  • Needs fewer samples than pure sampling-based filters and is less conservative than blending nominal and backup actions; shown on hardware box toppling and vision-based control.
Show abstract
Failure avoidance in complex robot-environment interactions is challenging because the underlying dynamics are difficult to model accurately, and available predictive models may not provide reliable derivatives. We present Sim2CBF, a failure-avoidance framework that solves the Control Barrier Function (CBF)-Safety Filter (SF) optimization problem using only forward-model evaluations, thereby avoiding differentiation of the predictive model. Specifically, we approximate the CBF constraint using finite differences and local linear interpolation over sampled control inputs while preserving failure-avoidance guarantees. The underlying CBF is implicit and constructed from backup-policy rollouts, enabling the use of expressive predictive models such as physics-based simulators and learned world models. Compared with purely sampling-based SFs, our formulation requires fewer control samples and yields less conservative behavior than direct nominal-backup blending. We evaluate Sim2CBF in simulation, hardware box-toppling, and vision-based control tasks, demonstrating its applicability to contact-rich and learned dynamics, as well as its adaptability to changes in the predictive model.
#24 h=14

WoRD: Diffusion-Based Posterior Inference for Online Goal Recognition

2026-11-17 cs.AIcs.LGcs.RO 👤 Sarah Keren (h=14)
Almog Anschel, Sarah Keren
Core Contributions
  • Brings learned trajectory priors to goal recognition, which previously relied on symbolic abstractions or hand-built dynamics models.
  • Runs a warm-started denoising chain per candidate goal, guided toward the observed prefix by a closed-form likelihood gradient, then reweights by Bayes' rule.
  • Keeps calibrated multimodal beliefs on ambiguous prefixes where filter- and classifier-based observers collapse or become overconfident, across robot, pedestrian and vehicle data.
Show abstract
Goal recognition is the task of inferring an agent's intended goal from its observed behavior. Most existing work focuses on high-level symbolic recognition, abstracting away the continuous motion through which agents reach their goal. Methods that do reason over continuous trajectories rely on an explicit dynamics model that is hard to acquire and rarely transfers across settings. Neither approach explicitly learns how agents move toward each candidate goal, limiting accuracy on ambiguous trajectory prefixes where such knowledge is most needed. We present WoRD (Waypoint Recognition with Diffusion), which uses a learned trajectory prior to infer the agent's goal from partial observations. At each step, WoRD runs one warm-started denoising chain per candidate goal, steers each chain toward the observed trajectory through a closed-form log-likelihood gradient, and reweights the resulting samples by Bayes' rule into a calibrated posterior over goals. We evaluate WoRD on simulated robot settings, pedestrian trajectories, and vehicle trajectories using four metrics targeting distinct failure modes. Across all three settings, the same inference procedure preserves calibrated multimodal beliefs through ambiguous prefixes where filter- and classifier-based observers collapse to a single mode or grow overconfident.

JointGV: Structure-Aware Correction of Visual World Models with Parallel Graph Dynamics for Long-Horizon Modeling

2026-11-17 cs.ROcs.AIcs.CV 👤 P. Lio' (h=82)
Tian Lyu, Yichen Jiang, Ziyuan Cheng, Yunda Yan, Xiangyu Pan
Core Contributions
  • Rather than redesigning visual world models, JointGV attaches a task-conditioned scene graph that rolls out in parallel and corrects the visual state with a bounded residual, a plug-in that leaves the original dynamics untouched.
  • Rests on a cross-granularity hypothesis: graph-node motion and visual-token change describe the same physical transition, so their predicted changes can be coupled even with no shared feature space.
  • Works on both spatial-token (DINO-WM) and global-latent (DreamerV3) models, cutting joint-position error by 11-28% over 32-step rollouts and roughly halving real-pendulum pole-angle error (12.56° to 6.69°).
  • Downstream payoff is stark: an actor trained in JointGV-corrected imagination finishes 46/50 inverted-pendulum episodes versus 0/50 with the uncorrected model.
Show abstract
Visual world models predict future visual states conditioned on actions, but recursive errors in entity poses and spatial relations limit their long-horizon prediction for robot planning and control. We ask whether a separately predicted relational state can correct this drift without restructuring the visual model. This is challenging because graph nodes and visual states share neither elements nor a feature space. We propose Joint Graph--Visual Dynamics Correction (JointGV), a plug-in based on a cross-granularity dynamics hypothesis: despite their different representations, graph-node and visual-state changes describe the same physical transition. JointGV rolls out a task-conditioned scene graph alongside the visual world model, couples their predicted changes, and combines the resulting graph-motion context with graph-predicted node positions to compute a bounded residual. Graph-learning and correction gradients do not propagate into the original visual dynamics. We attach JointGV to DINO-WM (spatial tokens) and DreamerV3 (a global latent state), evaluating both on DMC Cheetah Run and Walker Walk across three seeds and extending the evaluation to contact-rich manipulation with DMC Finger Turn Hard. Across 32-step rollouts, JointGV improves structural and visual-prediction accuracy, reducing joint-position error by 11--28\%. For spatial tokens, learned graph--visual change coupling gives lower error than broadcasting the same mean graph-motion context to every token, particularly when non-adjacent parts appear close together in the image. On real-robot inverted-pendulum sequences, JointGV reduces DINO-WM's 32-step pole-angle error from $12.56^\circ$ to $6.69^\circ$ and improves DreamerV3 and LeWorldModel as well. In simulated inverted-pendulum control, an actor trained with JointGV-corrected rollouts completes 46 of 50 episodes, compared with none using the original rollouts.

Manufacturing Differential Sparsity: Planner-Hardware Co-Design for Efficient World Model Planning

2026-11-17 cs.ARcs.AIcs.LGcs.ROeess.SY 👤 Christopher Torng (h=14)
Wenbo Zhu, Paul Yi-Chia Chen, Yuanhan Li, Zishen Wan, Christopher Torng
Core Contributions
  • Attacks the bottleneck of JEPA world-model planning, where sampling-based MPC calls the predictor tens of thousands of times and one step can take tens of seconds on a GPU.
  • Observes that nearby actions give nearby latents, which becomes differential bit-level sparsity in a quantized predictor, something hardware can exploit.
  • Uniquely, the planner manufactures that sparsity: reference-forest sampling ties candidates to shared roots with little loss in planning quality.
  • The co-designed accelerator is 1.28-1.80x faster with 38-52% less energy than a dense design, and beats prior bit-sparse accelerators at equal area.
Show abstract
JEPA-style latent world models let embodied agents plan toward new goals with model predictive control, but they are still too slow for real-life embodied control: sampling-based MPC repeats predictor inference tens of thousands of times per plan, and even on high-end GPUs one planning step can take tens of seconds. We observe substantial latent locality in this repeated-inference workload: the predictor maps close actions to close latents, and inside a quantized predictor this closeness takes a form that hardware can exploit, differential bit-level sparsity. Unlike an input stream, whose temporal similarity is given, a planner itself decides which candidates it evaluates together, so the planner can choose its own references and, with reference forest sampling, manufacture more sparsity: it couples the sampled action sequences of each group to a shared root, with competitive planning quality. We build the accelerator on the insights of the rich line of bit-level sparsity accelerators and design it and the planner tightly coupled as a single co-design: the planner increases sparsity and exposes structure for hardware scheduling, and the bit-exact hardware effectively uses the sparsity that the planner creates. On three JEPA world models, our co-design is 1.28-1.80x faster than a dense accelerator and uses 38-52% less energy, and it is 1.22-1.58x faster than prior bit-sparse design and uses 16-36% less energy, with the same area. To our knowledge, our planner is the first planner that manufactures differential bit-level sparsity across its candidates and exposes its structure for hardware scheduling, and our co-design is the first algorithm-hardware co-design for JEPA-style world-model planning.

VSpark: Speculative Decoding for Autoregressive Vision-Language-Action Models

2026-11-17 cs.CVcs.RO 👤 Youngjoon Jang (h=12)
Suho Yoo, Youngjoon Jang, Hyebin Cho, Taeyeop Lee, Jaewook Jeong
Core Contributions
  • Applies speculative decoding to autoregressive VLAs, and explains why naive drafts underperform: many tokens encode near-identical motions, so close drafts are still rejected.
  • Trains the draft on rollouts with calibrated action noise and on VLA hidden features rather than output tokens alone, raising acceptance.
  • Continuation reuse keeps the VLA's already-computed predictions along rejected drafts as candidates, recovering wasted compute.
  • Lossless 1.28-2.10x speedup across four VLAs with different action tokenizers and four benchmarks.
Show abstract
Our aim is to accelerate autoregressive vision-language-action models (VLAs), which generate robot actions one token at a time. Speculative decoding makes this possible: a lightweight draft model proposes several tokens at once, and the VLA verifies them in a single pass, accepting only those that match its own predictions, so decoding remains lossless. The speedup, however, is limited in robot control, where many different tokens encode nearly the same motion and a draft close to the correct action is still rejected. To address this, our approach, VSpark, is built upon three design choices: (i) rollouts collected with calibrated action noise, which expose the draft to a wider range of robot states; (ii) feature alignment, which trains the draft to match the VLA's hidden features rather than its output tokens alone; and (iii) continuation reuse, which keeps the predictions the VLA has already computed along rejected drafts as candidates for the next verification step, instead of discarding them. On four VLAs with different action tokenizers and four manipulation benchmarks, VSpark achieves 1.28x to 2.10x speedup while keeping the VLA's decoding lossless.

Hidden Variables Behind Real-Robot Data Quality: Lessons from Hundreds of Thousands of Hours

2026-11-17 cs.RO 👤 Guanghui Ren (h=16)
Maniformer Real-World Manipulation Team, Che Tsang Calvin, Xingyu Chen, Bowen Jiang, Naixiang Gao
Core Contributions
  • Moves the data-scaling debate from quantity to quality, drawing on hundreds of thousands of hours of real dual-arm data and fine-tuned pi-0.5 policies.
  • Finds that position-only reachability overstates usable workspace, that parallel body-part teleoperation cuts task time by 43-53%, and that compliant control reduces contact forces.
  • Policies inherit operator inefficiency, keeping about 90% of extra path length from detour-laden demos, and active-search demos raise arm-selection accuracy from 50% to 100%.
  • Mixed-calibration training data is tolerable if an inference-aligned subset is kept, but inference-time miscalibration fails all 30 trials, giving a concrete pre-scaling checklist.
Show abstract
As real-robot data collection scales, collection-system design shapes both operator efficiency and the behaviors learned by robot policies. Workspace limitations, control-interface friction, and latency can introduce pauses, detours, and corrective motions into otherwise successful demonstrations. We investigate these effects across embodiment, teleoperation control, and data consistency, combining a kinematic study of 25 dual-arm robots, controlled teleoperation experiments, and real-robot evaluation of fine-tuned $π_{0.5}$ policies. The embodiment study shows that positional reachability overstates orientation-aware task coverage and that modest shoulder motion can substantially expand the effective workspace. Teleoperation experiments show that parallelizing body-part control reduces task completion time by 43-53%, while compliant control improves success and reduces peak contact forces on constrained and contact-rich tasks. Policy-learning experiments show that demonstrations must capture the visual information underlying action choices: active-search demonstrations improve arm-selection accuracy from chance (50%) to 100%. Calibration experiments reveal that mixed-calibration training data remain usable in our tested configurations when an inference-aligned subset is retained, whereas inference-time miscalibration causes all 30 trials to fail. Policies also retain approximately 90% of the extra path length in detour-contaminated demonstrations, showing how inefficient operator motions persist in learned behavior. Together, these findings connect collection-system design to demonstration quality and downstream policy execution. They motivate evaluating effective workspace, control efficiency, observation completeness, calibration alignment, and motion quality before scaling collection, and addressing system-induced compensations at their source.

Zero-Shot Compositional Generalization for Embodied Manipulation

2026-11-17 cs.RO 👤 Wei Sui (h=14)
Baicheng Li, Wenkang Qin, Youqi Pan, Dongxiao Mao, Yukun Zhou
Core Contributions
  • Tackles missing robot-skill combinations in pooled multi-embodiment datasets: a skill shown on one arm should transfer to another arm that has never performed it.
  • Key insight: a demonstrated command defines a tool-space target that any robot can realize, so calibrated forward kinematics provides supervision for a foreign robot without its joint labels.
  • Reachability checks filter which cross-robot targets are valid, and actions run through each robot's native decoder.
  • Gains on unseen robot-task pairs are verified with matched-supervision controls across three backbones and two simulators.
Show abstract
Robot demonstrations are collected on different embodiments, leaving many robot-skill combinations unobserved. Pooling these data shares visual knowledge, while joint labels remain tied to the demonstrating kinematic chain. We propose a geometry-supervised training framework for zero-shot composition of familiar robots and skills. The central insight is that a demonstrated command provides a tool-space target for every robot that can realize it. Calibrated forward kinematics supplies this supervision to foreign native action interfaces without their joint labels for that skill. Native reconstruction retains demonstrated configurations, and positive reachability checks select targets for cross-robot supervision. A visual policy learns to predict shared actions and executes through the target robot's native decoder. During policy learning, one prediction receives geometric supervision through both source and foreign native realizations. Matched supervision controls, repeated policy training and evaluations across three backbones and two simulators demonstrate improved transfer to missing robot-task combinations.

VoxelSBFL: Voxel-Level Spectrum-Based Failure Prediction for Vision-Language-Action Models

2026-11-17 cs.ROcs.SE 👤 Aitor Arrieta (h=20)
Arusa Kanwal, Pablo Valle, Paola Roggero, Miren Illarramendi, Shaukat Ali
Core Contributions
  • Transfers spectrum-based fault localization from software testing to VLAs, with workspace voxels replacing program statements as the unit of analysis.
  • Fully black-box: needs only recorded end-effector paths and pass/fail outcomes, producing suspiciousness maps that score new trajectories without model internals.
  • Large-scale study of 34 SBFL formulas over 96,000 Pick-Up trajectories from four VLAs reaches 0.704 macro ROC-AUC, beating eight action- and trajectory-based baselines.
  • Points to runtime monitoring and targeted data collection in failure-prone regions as practical uses.
Show abstract
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models integrate visual perception, language understanding, and action generation to control robots. Testing these systems produces trajectories and task outcomes, but existing approaches provide limited guidance on using these recorded trajectories to assess subsequent executions or inform policy repair. We conjecture that, for a given policy and task, traversing certain workspace regions is associated with a higher likelihood of failure. To investigate this conjecture, we propose VoxelSBFL, a black-box adaptation of Spectrum-Based Fault Localization (SBFL) in which workspace voxels replace program elements as units of analysis. Recorded tool-center-point (TCP) trajectories provide binary voxel coverage, which is combined with execution outcomes to construct suspiciousness maps using established SBFL formulas. These maps identify failure-associated regions and score unseen trajectories without requiring internal model access. We evaluate 34 spectrum-based techniques across four VLA models using 96,000 Pick-Up trajectories in simulation and compare the leading techniques against eight action- and trajectory-based metrics. VoxelSBFL obtains an overall macro ROC-AUC of 0.704, significantly outperforming state-of-the art baselines. Sensitivity analyses show that performance varies with spatial granularity and training-set size. These findings support spatial spectra for VLA execution assessment and motivate future research on runtime monitoring, self-healing and targeted policy improvement.

When the World Is the Test Suite: Agentic Intelligence for the Physical World

2026-11-17 cs.ROcs.AIcs.SE 👤 Zhi-Heng Xi (h=21)
Kaixin Li, Meng Luo, Yanlin Li, Yuchen Tian, Xiang Liu
Core Contributions
  • Frames a new area where coding agents write programs for robots, labs and controllers but, unlike software, no test suite exists: correctness must be established by running in the world or in simulation.
  • Organizes the field around three questions (writing, testing, learning) and classifies programs by their distance from the actuator and what judges them.
  • Examines what traces, learned judges, simulators and formal checks can substitute for costly physical trials, and what safety requires beyond task success.
  • Central claim: code is becoming cheap while trustworthy evidence stays expensive, so progress hinges on a physical counterpart to continuous integration.
Show abstract
Coding agents built to repair software now write, test, and repair programs for robots, laboratories, and industrial controllers. Unlike in software engineering, no test suite comes with the code. Whether a program works must be established by running it on a robot, in a physics simulator, or through the policies it helps to train, and such evidence is costly to obtain and can itself be wrong. As these agents take on more physical tasks, how their programs are judged matters as much as how they are written. This paper examines this emerging area, agentic intelligence for the physical world, by treating the world as the program's test suite. We organize the discussion around three questions: how agents write programs for the physical world, how these programs are tested, and how agents learn from the results. We first trace how language models for robots and software engineering agents came together, ask which software testing practices carry over to physical tests, and describe each program by where it sits relative to the actuator and by what judges it. For writing, we cover programs that act as robot policies and programs that support policy learning by producing rewards, tasks, and training worlds. For testing, we examine what physical trials cost, what traces, learned judges, simulators, and formal checks can establish in their place, and what safety requires beyond task success. For learning, we discuss how tested programs are retained as experience, in skill libraries and in model weights. Finally, we turn to benchmarks and evaluation practice and outline open challenges. Writing programs is becoming cheap, while trustworthy evidence about what they do remains costly. Progress will therefore depend on a physical counterpart of continuous integration: verification cheap enough to run often, reliable enough to trust under search, and maintained alongside the experience it validates.

BehaVerify: A Formal Verification Tool for Behavior Trees

2026-11-17 cs.SEcs.LOcs.RO 👤 Diego Manzanas Lopez (h=15)
Serena S. Serbinowska, Ben Wooding, Diego Manzanas Lopez, Luke Nam, Anne M. Tumlin
Core Contributions
  • Delivers a usable toolchain for formally verifying behavior trees and neuro-symbolic BTs, which are spreading into safety-critical robots.
  • A DSL with formal semantics supports LTL and CTL specs, and a 'fastforwarding' nuXmv encoding keeps checking fast.
  • Generates verified Python and Haskell implementations and integrates with PyTrees and ROS, already deployed on several autonomous systems, including for runtime monitors.
Show abstract
This paper presents BehaVerify, a verification toolbox for behavior trees (BTs) and Neuro-Symbolic BTs. BTs are a graphical language for planning and sequencing behaviors - similar to extended state machines - and are increasingly used in safety-critical domains. BehaVerify enables users to specify a BT using a Domain Specific Language (DSL) with formal syntax and semantics. This language further includes Linear Temporal Logic and Computation Tree Logic specifications. BehaVerify uses an efficient encoding into nuXmv, called fastforwarding, to quickly verify if a specification holds over the BT. Additionally, BehaVerify can generate implementations of the BT in Python and Haskell. Through Python, BehaVerify has been integrated with PyTrees and Robot Operating System (ROS) and has been deployed to several autonomous robotic systems, including for verifying runtime monitors. While prior works have developed the underlying theory, this manuscript focuses on the toolbox usage, functionality and implementation. BehaVerify is open source: https://github.com/verivital/behaverify

DailyBrick: Language-Guided Synthesis of Brick Assembly Instructions

2026-11-17 cs.ROcs.CV 👤 Xin Tan (h=15)

Accurate shape estimation of Deformable Linear Objects (DLOs) is a fundamental challenge in robotic manipulation, often hindered by visual occlusions and the high-dimensional configuration space of flexible materials. While Force/Torque (F/T) and vision-centric methods have been proposed, they frequently struggle with grasp-site ambiguities and environmental interference. In this letter, we investigate the hypothesis that global shape estimation can be achieved through tactile sensing and proprioception, without any visual perception. We contend that dense, high-resolution tactile imprints capture critical boundary conditions and contact gradients that are sufficient to resolve the high-dimensional configuration of the DLO. To prove this, we introduce a Physics-Informed Vision Transformer (ViT) framework that maps tactile images to a 3D vertex chain. By leveraging self-attention mechanisms and a physics-weighted loss function, our model enforces structural priors to ensure that the predicted geometry adheres to the object's physical manifold. Experimental results demonstrate that our tactile-based approach achieves superior reconstruction accuracy across Euclidean and Chamfer metrics. Furthermore, we demonstrate the efficiency of this modality, achieving model transfer to novel DLO materials and geometries. Our findings suggest that tactile feedback provides a robust and computationally efficient alternative for DLO state estimation in visually degraded or occluded environments.

Levio: Smart Actuated Back-Exoskeleton via Retrofit of a Commercial Passive Device

2026-11-17 cs.RO 👤 H. van der Kooij (h=59)
Ionnais Kyriazis, Mohamed Irfan Refai, Herman van der Kooij
Core Contributions

  • Offers a cheap template for research labs to test active assistance strategies without years of custom hardware design.

Passive back exoskeletons show promising benefits for reducing occupational musculoskeletal disorders. However, their assistance cannot be modulated towards the task requirements. Active exoskeletons offer opportunities for pose-specific assistance, but, developing these systems from scratch for research can be time consuming. Therefore, here, we present an accelerated design pipeline that retrofits a Commercial Off-The-Shelf (COTS) passive back exoskeleton, Laevo Flex, with COTS actuation (CubeMars AK10-9) and sensing (IMUs and load-cell) paradigms. Levio was able to provide assistance based on the user's pose. Nine participants performed stoop lifting with the original Laevo and the retrofit Levio system, in transparent and assisted modes. Compared to Laevo, Levio modulated the assistance provided between the bending and lifting phases of the stoop task. The passive assistance offered by Laevo was $25.2 \pm 4.3$ Nm and $30.6 \pm 9.5$ Nm respectively during the bending and lifting phases of the stoop tasks. Levio reduced the resistive torque during bending to $17.9 \pm 4.6$ Nm and offered increased assistance of $37.7 \pm 6.4$ Nm during lifting. Moreover, participants reported higher usability scores and lowest mean global discomfort score while using the Levio when compared to Laevo. These benefits demonstrates retrofitting passive exoskeletons is a viable direction for rapid prototyping of wearable exoskeletons for lifting.
